**What it does** [checked]: radar features per point: 3D position, uncompensated vx, vy, RCS (no time offset mentioned); 5 sweeps aggregated; voxel-grid encoder at BEV resolution (random cap of P points per voxel, FC + max-pool, VoxelNet-like), CNN squeezes the height. Lifting: image features at stride 1/8 are projected along camera rays into a voxel space around the camera (8 height bins up to 10 m), height removed by a 1 x 1 conv, then deformable attention guided by the sparse radar points filters it to initial BEV queries; six deformable-attention lifting blocks and six fusion cross-attention blocks follow; ViT-B/14 (DINOv2) or ResNet-101 backbone.

**Results that matter** [checked]: nuScenes val vehicle IoU 58.4, drivable 83.3, lane 45.3, map mIoU 54.3 (ViT-B/14); ResNet-101 57.3 / 81.8 / 43.8 / 53.0. Ablations: learned radar encoding vs raw radar metadata +0.6 vehicle IoU / +0.9 map mIoU; radar-driven attention lifting vs parameter-free lifting +1.8 / +4.2 (largest on map classes). Forward pass on an A100: ViT-B/14 382 ms (2.6 FPS), ResNet-101 137 ms (7.3 FPS), Simple-BEV (ResNet-101) 18 ms.

**Transferable technique**: radar information can steer the camera lift (where along the ray the mass goes), which is the reverse direction of the splat and complementary to a radar density map: a radar density in BEV could multiply the depth distribution of each cone (a prior over range bins), without any attention. The +4.2 map mIoU from radar-guided lifting is the only controlled number found for this direction.

**Cost and fit**: 137 to 382 ms; far too slow; only the idea of the radar prior on the lift.

**Doubts**: the page read does not give the effect of radar on the lift alone separated from the encoder; dt unused; attention not a density.
