**Verification of our from-memory reference**: id 2003.12039, Zachary Teed and Jia Deng, submitted 2020-03-26, v3 2020-08-25 (v3 only fixes a formatting issue in Eq. 7); ECCV 2020 is not written on the arXiv page (memory). "All-pairs correlation volume" and "recurrent GRU refinement" are both correct. Correction to the shorthand "coarse-to-fine": the paper stresses that RAFT is NOT coarse-to-fine, it keeps one (1/8) resolution and refines a single flow field.

**What it does** [checked]: a feature encoder runs on both frames and a context encoder on the first only; the correlation volume of ALL pixel pairs is built once and pooled to a multi-scale pyramid (so large displacements stay reachable at coarse pyramid levels while the flow stays at one resolution); a weight-tied convolutional GRU starts from flow 0 and at every step looks up the correlation values around the current flow estimate (lookup radius 4 and 4 pyramid levels, 12 train / 24-32 test iterations, sequence loss with exponentially growing weights, gamma 0.8: all memory) and outputs a flow update.

**Results that matter** [checked on the ar5iv summary]: KITTI F1-all 5.10 % (previous best 6.10 %), Sintel final EPE 2.855 px (previous 4.098); trained on synthetic data only, KITTI EPE 5.04 px vs 8.36 for the best prior net; about 10 fps on 1088 x 436 on a GTX 1080 Ti, a small version with one fifth of the parameters 20 fps; accuracy keeps improving with more iterations up to 200 without diverging.

**Transferable technique**: (1) the correlation lookup around the CURRENT estimate is the learned analogue of scoring velocity hypotheses: our block scores M fixed-number hypotheses per cell by a learned score + Doppler likelihood, RAFT scores a (2r+1)^2 neighbourhood of displacements by feature similarity; (2) weight-tied recurrent refinement with a sequence loss gives a supervised gradient at every iteration (our block has one shot; an "iterate: re-centre the hypotheses on the posterior mean, score again" variant is the cheap port); (3) zero initialisation = our zero-initialised velocity head.

**Cost and fit**: the full all-pairs volume is (H W)^2; at our 120 x 104 BEV that is 156 M entries per level for ONE pair (fine for a research step, wrong for 12 ms); a local window (like ALOcc 2411.07725 and SelfOccFlow 2602.23894) is the BEV-sized form. Images with texture vs BEV maps with thin lines and empty cells: the feature correlation of an empty cell is uninformative, so the aperture problem is worse than in images.

**Doubts**: image optical flow with dense texture, brightness constancy; nothing about sparse sensors or about multi-modal (hypothesis) outputs: RAFT outputs ONE flow per pixel, and averages over ambiguous matches.
