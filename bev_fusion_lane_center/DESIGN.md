# DESIGN - lane-centre graph from camera + radar (lcr)

The standing design spec of this problem, made from the owner's instructions and the results of the experiments (introduced 2026-11-21, D131). The coder is given it together with the Design delta of the card. It says what to build and why; code, cards and ledger say the rest. The owner's original words are in the single decision files at the git tag `archive/pre-cleanup-2026-11-20` (`experiment_execution/decisions/D<n>.md`).

How to read it. **RQ** = a requirement or order of the owner (only the owner changes it; what it costs is written in the row). **DR** = a design rule found by experiments. Status: ORDERED (owner), HOLDS (a verdict by a rule written before the run), TIE (the card ended in a tie by its rule; kept as is), SINGLE (one seed, one card or in-sample), CHOSEN (by a mission or the lead, not by a number), CLOSED (answered no; closed by the lead or twice), CONTESTED (a card is open against it). **Attack** = the cheapest test that would overturn the row. Every row can be attacked by a card that names its id; no row is final.
Other files: `PROBLEM.md` (the owner's frame: goal, data, protocol, current best; protected), `experiment_execution/DECISIONS.md` (the owner's permissions and the id index), `HANDOVER.md` (where we stand).
VAL note: VAL (873 frames) was read by E365 - E373 (E373 card): the confirmation removes the selection of the head instance, not the reuse of VAL; every VAL number below is a development number. TEST has not been used for the token champion.

## 1 System of record: the token champion (D113 - D120)
Chain, drawing and current best are in `PROBLEM.md` ("The product chain", "Current best") and are not repeated. What a coder needs on top:
1. The tokens are the predicted nodes of B': the pyramid of B' is frozen, its node head (a 2 m grid of nodes with an existence score) is trained in stage 2; a hard top-K of 512 is a named compute budget (DR-9).
2. One token head: three self-attention layers with a relative-geometry bias and an allowed-pair gate; the wiring is a per-source softmax with a `none` slot (loss weight 0.25); a seven-way node-type softmax; links are short (<= 8 m) or long.
3. Two stages: the head on cached B' tokens, then the head and the B' node head together through the token loss (DR-2).
4. Read-out by four named thresholds (DR-3). S4 (the champion): topo F1 0.6682 / 0.6813, junction F1 0.2538 / 0.2481 (E372, D120). Cost: 2.323 M parameters and 16.21 ms whole path, measured on the stage-1 head (`experiments/E2E_cost_table.md`; S4 has the same layers and was not re-timed; D120 quotes 16.6 - 16.8 ms, HANDOVER 16.2 - 16.4 ms). No TEST check.

## 5 How the spec moves
1. **Card:** names the rows it attacks or changes in its Design delta (row ids and the changed text). The coder is given this file plus the delta (D131).
2. **Result:** the written rule gives KEEP / CUT / TIE / INCONCLUSIVE. KEEP: the delta becomes the row (basis = experiment id and its numbers; the old text stays in git). CUT / TIE: the row keeps its text and gets the result of the attack in its basis; answered the same way twice = CLOSED with a re-open condition.
3. **The owner's words:** an instruction that fixes how the system must be becomes an RQ row (his words verbatim, or the tag pointer, in the basis). An answer that only permits (TEST, a run, shutdown, push, install, a grant for a protected file) goes into `DECISIONS.md` and never into this file.
4. **Who writes (a default, not decided in D131):** the lead of the loop, at card time (the delta) and at close (the merge, in the commit of the verdict); a change of an RQ row only by the owner's recorded word.
