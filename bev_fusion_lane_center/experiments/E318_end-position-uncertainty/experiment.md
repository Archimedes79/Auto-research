The maps kill (worse than E307 by more than 0.05 keypoint F1) is report-only, flagged at -0.08, as in E317 / E324: the arm carries the same loss as E320, which measured -0.0457 / -0.0317.

Card of 2026-11-12 22:50, written by the lane chat. Second of Mirko's hypotheses of the evening: the position uncertainty the head is allowed to express is not large enough. Today every keypoint is a point with the same implicit sigma: the heat target has a fixed Gaussian (1.0 m since E307) and the pair head sees only the position, so a label offset of 1 - 2 m at a junction end (the largest single loss of the E283 junction analysis) is punished like a real error and nothing tells the pair head or the planner which end is soft. A Laplace NLL with a learned scale per keypoint (heteroscedastic regression, Kendall and Gal; the "uncertainty" head of the end heads of MapTR-like and CenterNet-like detectors) lets the net declare an end uncertain at the price of log sigma, and gives the planner an uncertainty per end for free.

**The uncertainty has to be worth something even when topo F1 does not move** (written before the run, definitions added 2026-11-14 with the tool `temp/e318/sigma_check.py`, which reads `runs/<run>/ends_val.pkl` after `jends_eval.py dump --run <run>` and the GT of `lane_gt_data.QData("val_clean")`; VAL 873 frames, 22 scenes). - **Keypoint end error.** Every valid keypoint of the dump (a real peak of the heat; entries are the first K of the 2K arrays, exits the rest) that has a GT end of its kind within 4 m and 30 degrees (`qendnet.MATCH_POS / MATCH_ANG`, the matching of `jends.geom_loss`: many to one, the NEAREST such end) gets the distance to that end in metres as its error. Keypoints with no such end (errors beyond 4 m, false peaks) are not in the statistic: the correlation is read over the matched keypoints, the ones the end term trains.
- **Clause 1, Spearman.** The rank correlation (average ranks for ties) between sigma = exp(log sigma) and the end error, pooled over entries and exits, **at least 0.3**.
- **Delivered pairs and precision.** A pair (entry i, exit j) is delivered when `allowed[i, j]` and sigmoid(logit) >= 0.1 (the read-out of `qends_eval.cmd_score`); it is TRUE when its two keypoints are matched, by the STRICT one-to-one matching of `jrule_control._match` (greedy by distance, 4 m / 30 degrees, every keypoint and every GT end used at most once, all valid keypoints taking part, i.e. `--kp-thr 0` as the read-out; `jpair_pr.py` uses 0.5), to the entry and the exit of ONE GT pair. Precision = TRUE / delivered.
- **Clause 2, fifths.** The delivered pairs of all 873 frames are pooled and sorted by the sum of the two sigmas of the pair; the most confident fifth is the 20 % with the smallest sums, the most unsure fifth the 20 % with the largest; **precision(confident) - precision(unsure) at least 0.03** (the original bar). The same split by the range of the same seed (+0.17 / +0.13 on the E312 dumps, null measurement below) is REPORTED next to it and is NOT a condition (revision of the sharpening after the second critic pass, 2026-11-14, still before any E318 run): a scale trained on the position error has no reason to beat the range at pair precision, the null's own interval is wide ([+0.05, +0.27]) and the rule compares point estimates; the train / VAL injection mismatch could also flip the sign. A failed clause 2 therefore means "no pair-precision value of the scale"; clause 1 (Spearman against the end error) is the claim about the end, and a scale that only reproduces the range is visible in the printed nulls.
- **Nulls (printed by the script for every run, same two statistics with the scale replaced by free information):** the range of the keypoint from the ego (pairs: the sum of the two ranges), the heat probability at the peak turned into an unsure score (`-kp_p`; pairs: `-(p_i + p_j)`), and for the fifths the pair logit (`-logit`: the head's own confidence, a reference, not a null of the scale). ## 1. Design and prediction
Single change against E317 (the control) in the card's words: the position scale; **claim boundary: the arm is NOT a single factor.** The head `JEnds(unc=True)` adds `logsig = Linear(d, 1)` on the self-attended keypoint token (zero-initialised, so sigma = 1 m at the start; clamped to [-3, 3], i.e. 0.05 - 20 m) and two inputs to the pair logit (the detached log sigma of the entry and of the exit keypoint, so the pair loss cannot shape the scale; checked in the self-test). The end term of the geometry loss of E317 (every valid keypoint within 4 m / 30 degrees of a GT end, nearest end) is the Laplace NLL |d| exp(-s) + s instead of the smooth L1; the shape term is unchanged. So u differs from g in three things at once: (a) the end loss (Huber -> Laplace NLL: the position gradient is exp(-s) per metre, up to e^3 = 20 where the net is confident, against at most 1 for Huber, under the trainer's gradient clip 3.0; outside [-3, 3] the clamp of `jends.py` (about line 237) gives log sigma no gradient any more), (b) two more inputs of the first layer of the pair head and of the scale head (their initial weights differ from g's even at the same seed; the sample order is the same), (c) the scale as an output. A topo F1 effect, up or down, cannot be attributed to the scale alone; the informativeness of the scale itself (clauses 1 and 2) is what the card reads about the scale. Self-tests (`python jends.py`): a 3 m error pushes log sigma up and a 0.1 m error pushes it down, a keypoint that is no end gets no gradient, the pair loss does not reach `logsig`, the flag-off model is bit-identical to the E312 code. Two seeds, VAL 873 frames, TEST untouched.

**Calibration caveat.** In training the GT ends are keypoint candidates (injected): the end error of the matched keypoints is about 0.6 m on TRAIN (E317 card, `t_real.py geom`; the logged `geom_end_d` of E320 is 0.62 - 0.72 m) against about 1.4 m on VAL (E310, E312's `kperr`). The learned sigma is calibrated on the first distribution and read on the second: the absolute level of sigma is not an error bar, only the VAL rank statistics of clauses 1 and 2 are read, and a systematically low sigma on VAL is expected rather than a defect.

Honest risk. A Laplace NLL on the position error of a keypoint whose position is a soft-argmax inside a 3 x 3 window can only reduce its error by shifting heat; the scale itself does not move the peak. So the expected benefit is not a better topo F1 (**prediction a number: +0.005 over E317, a TIE**) but the information: the Spearman correlation of sigma with the true end error of at least 0.3 (a head that has learned nothing gives 0; the free quantity `range` gives +0.12 / +0.10 on E312's two seeds, so the bar can fail) and a pair precision gain of 0.03 between the confident and the unsure fifth. If sigma ends in the clamp or at one value for all keypoints the kill applies (read on the VAL dump).
