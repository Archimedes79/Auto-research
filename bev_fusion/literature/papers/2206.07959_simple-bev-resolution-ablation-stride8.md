**What it does** (only what the existing note lacks) [checked]: the 2D encoder (ResNet-101 pretrained for COCO detection; the BEV ResNet-18 is trained from scratch) gives a C = 128 map at 1/8 of the input: the last-layer output is upsampled and concatenated with the third-layer output, then two convs. The 3D volume is 200 x 8 x 200 (Z, Y, X) over 100 m x 100 m x 10 m, i.e. 0.5 m x 1.25 m x 0.5 m voxels; each voxel centre is projected and bilinearly sampled, no depth is estimated ("Lift-Splat without depth estimation"). Loss: CE for segmentation, L1 for centreness and offset, uncertainty weighting.

**Results that matter** [checked]: resolutions swept from 112 x 208 to 896 x 1600 (train = test size). Vehicle IoU: 448 x 800 47.4 at 83 ms; 672 x 1200 49.3 (best) at 133 ms and "nearly twice the training time"; "resolutions lower than 448 x 800 drastically worsen performance"; the highest resolution drops again (the authors' guess: object scale no longer matches the backbone pre-training). Table I: bilinear sampling lift 47.4 against depth-based splatting 44.4. Batch size 2 -> 40 is worth nearly 14 IoU. Repo README: camera-only 47.6, camera + radar 55.8 mean IoU at 448 x 800 (paper 47.4 / 55.7).

**Transferable technique / what it means for us**: (1) input resolution is the largest knob in this family and the knee is at about 448 x 800 for a 100 m range; our 256 x 704 has 0.50 times the pixels of 448 x 800, so the sweep says a gain is expected from MORE PIXELS, but see the arithmetic below; (2) a stride-8 map with an upsample-and-concat of the stride-8 stage onto the last stage is the encoder recipe; (3) the parameter-free pull with no depth already beats a depth splat, so the depth weight of our lift must be tested against this control (the note 2403.05402 gives +1.4 NDS for the weight); (4) batch size is a confounder: compare lifts at equal batch.
Arithmetic from the brief's numbers (not from the paper; focal length of the nuScenes front camera about 1266 px at 1600 x 900 is from memory, camera height 1.5 m from the brief): the 40-60 m ground band spans f*h*(1/40 - 1/60) rows, 16 rows at full resolution, 8 rows at 448 x 800 (f about 633 px) and 7 rows at 256 x 704. So the whole-image step from 256 x 704 to 448 x 800 does NOT buy rows at the horizon (7 -> 8); a horizon strip at 2x vertical magnification would (14 rows). The resolution gain seen in Simple-BEV is therefore mostly about near / mid objects, and it does not argue against a strip.

**Cost and fit**: the model is 83 ms (448 x 800, their GPU not stated on the page read); MIT code and weights, the lift is `F.grid_sample` (see the existing note).

**Doubts**: vehicle segmentation IoU over 100 m x 100 m with six cameras, not lanes and not a front camera; no per-range IoU; the per-resolution numbers are in a figure; the drop at the top resolution is confounded by backbone pre-training scale; no resolution x stride interaction.
