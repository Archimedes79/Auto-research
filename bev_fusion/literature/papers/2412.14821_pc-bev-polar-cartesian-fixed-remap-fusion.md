**What it does** [checked]: Two BEV branches on a lidar scan, a Cartesian grid and a polar grid. Features move between them by a fixed correspondence: each Cartesian cell centre goes to (rho = sqrt(x^2 + y^2), phi = arctan2(x, y)), the index map is computed once, zero padding where there is no counterpart. Fusion = concatenation then conv / MLP. The "remap" replaces a point-based fusion (grid centres as points, grid sampling and scatter back).

**Results that matter** [checked]: nuScenes lidar segmentation val mIoU: Cartesian alone 71.4, polar alone 75.0, two branches with point-based fusion 76.2, with remap fusion 78.2 (+0.9 over the point-based line as stated, my arithmetic gives +2.0 against 76.2; the page text and table do not agree), transformer-CNN block +0.6 to 78.8. SemanticKITTI val: polar 61.2, Cartesian 56.5, PC-BEV 66.4; test 67.2. Latency: remap 0.04 ms against 6.8 ms (CPGNet scatter) / 8.0 ms (pytorch_scatter) for the point-based path on a 2080 Ti (170x); whole PC-BEV 31 ms vs 39 ms on a V100.

**Transferable technique**: a fixed index table per calibration for polar to Cartesian costs 0.04 ms and loses nothing against the learned or point-based alternative; keeping both domains and fusing in Cartesian beats either alone (+3.2 over the better single branch on nuScenes, as read). For us: the polar radar CNN result is pulled into Cartesian, and a Cartesian conv after the pull plays the part of the second branch.

**Cost and fit**: the remap is a gather; plain PyTorch via grid_sample or an index tensor; whether the paper uses bilinear or nearest is not stated.

**Doubts**: lidar, dense; interpolation type unspecified; the 0.04 ms is the remap op only; the gain over the point-based fusion has an inconsistency between text and table.
