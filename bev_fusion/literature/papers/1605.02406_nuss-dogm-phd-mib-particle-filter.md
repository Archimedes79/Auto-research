**Correction to the from-memory citation**: the title is "A Random Finite Set Approach for Dynamic Occupancy Grid Maps WITH REAL-TIME APPLICATION" (arXiv 1605.02406, v1 2016-05-09, v2 2016-09-10), authors Dominik Nuss, Stephan Reuter, Markus Thom, Ting Yuan, Gunther Krehl, Michael Maile, Axel Gern, Klaus Dietmayer. The arXiv page carries no journal reference; the IJRR 2018 record is from search results only.

**What it does** [checked, html]: the state of each grid cell is a random finite set; the filter is called PHD / MIB (probability hypothesis density with multi-instance Bernoulli birth). It is implemented as a particle filter in which each particle is x = [px, py, vx, vy] with a weight; the weight sum of a cell is its occupied mass (Dempster-Shafer variant). Prediction: constant velocity with process noise (transition density as proposal), weights scaled by the persistence probability, free mass decays with a time-step discount. Update per cell: particle weights times the true-positive probability and the spatial likelihood, then normalised. Resampling: constant particle count per step, equal weights afterwards, total weight (= posterior occupancy) preserved. Birth: new particles sampled from a birth distribution with weight = birth existence probability / number of birth particles; in the DS variant only in cells with measured occupied mass, with a count scaling with that mass. Laser and radar fused (abstract); the radar Doppler enters through the radar measurement likelihood and association probability (the html defers to a reference; not read).

**Results that matter**: abstract only (no numbers): process-model parameters act as the theory predicts; suitable models give consistent state estimates. Runtime and grid size in the part not read.

**Transferable technique**: map between the two: particle = (position, velocity, weight) ~ our (cell, hypothesis m, pi_m); constant-velocity prediction with noise ~ our chain of the coordinates over the ego steps with v_m (+ sigma_v |dt| blur on request); per-cell weight normalisation ~ the splat normalised by mass, mass = occupied mass; birth from measurement ~ hypotheses generated from the cell's own feature f_m(F(p)) (a LEARNED birth). What the classical filter has and we do not: a proper Bayesian update that multiplies the predicted weights with the new sensor likelihood each frame (our TemporalPropagation is a predictor; the update is the learned fusion conv / GRU), and resampling that keeps a fixed compute budget with the right statistics (we keep M hypotheses per cell and let pi_m carry the weight; no resampling means no particle depletion, but also no pruning).

**Cost and fit**: the filter itself is not differentiable (resampling, birth sampling); it is the model, not a block to port. The Doppler update of the classical filter is the reference for our Doppler evidence term (see Diehl 2008.03696 for a radar-only DOGM that states the weighting in words).
