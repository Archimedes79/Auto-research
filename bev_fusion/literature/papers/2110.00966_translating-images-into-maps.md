**What it does**: assumes an image column corresponds to a ray through the camera in the overhead map. Each column (length h_u, dim C) is a sequence; a transformer encoder-decoder translates it into a radial sequence of length r_u (polar map w_u x r_u x C), convolutional only along the horizontal direction. The polar map is resampled to the Cartesian BEV (C x Z x X, 100x100 at 0.5 m cells, upsampled to 200x200 for evaluation), followed by temporal fusion over T frames and a segmentation decoder. Depth is implicit: the decoder attention along the ray decides where in range a column's content sits; no explicit depth distribution or uncertainty.

**Results that matter** (nuScenes val IoU): spatial model drivable 72.6, walkway 32.4, car 37.4, truck 24.5, pedestrian 8.7, mean 25.0 (STA-S baseline mean 21.8); spatio-temporal mean 25.7. In Table IV it gets drivable 78.9 and vehicle 38.9 against LSS 72.9 / 32.0. Abstract: 15% relative gain on nuScenes, 30% on Argoverse. No lane/divider class, no latency in the page.

**Transferable technique**: columnwise (polar) lift gives a one-to-one structure for a front camera: the BEV is a fan of rays; far cells are thin in the image and the transformer can learn range from context. This is the cheapest "learned depth" alternative to a depth categorical. Could be combined with Gaussian splats: predict per-column radial Gaussian (mean range, variance) instead of a full transformer.

**Cost and fit**: transformer over columns (width ~ 50 to 100 sequences of length ~ 30), moderate; training time and ms not stated. Single front image works by design (monocular).

**Doubts**: speed not reported; uncertainty is not explicit; flat-ground assumption inherited from the ray model; the polar-to-Cartesian resampling is not described.
