# 2408.11447 GaussianOcc: Gaussian splatting instead of volume rendering for self-supervised occupancy

| | |
|---|---|

| verdict | use (the evidence for cheap rendering: splatting 3-8x faster than volume rendering at equal quality) |

**What it does**: Stage 1 learns scale from the surround cameras: a depth net predicts depth, scale, rotation per pixel, unprojects to Gaussians (opacity 1, colour = source image) and splats them into the adjacent camera; overlap regions are masked on one side (critical, naive splatting fails). Stage 2: each voxel vertex of the occupancy volume is a Gaussian (learned opacity and semantic features, fixed scale, identity rotation) rendered to depth and semantic maps. Losses: temporal photometric (SSIM + L1, beta 0.15), cross-view photometric, semantic with lambda 0.02. No ground-truth pose needed.

**Results that matter** (Occ3D-nuScenes, self-supervised mIoU / mIoU*): GaussianOcc 9.94 / 11.26 against OccNeRF 9.54 / 10.81, SelfOcc 9.30 / 10.54; RenderOcc (2D lidar labels) 23.93 / 24.53; supervised methods 20-29.5. Render time for six surround images at 180x320 on an A100: volume rendering 0.85 s, splatting 0.17 s; at 240x520 1.57 s vs 0.17 s; 360x640 volume rendering OOM, splatting still 0.17 s. Epoch time 2.68 h vs 1 h (2.7x). Accuracy: with learned pose occupancy mIoU* VR 11.19 vs SR 11.26; depth Abs Rel with learned pose VR 0.506 vs SR 0.197. Self-supervised pretraining then supervised fine-tuning (SimpleOcc backbone): 37.29 -> 38.45 mIoU (nuScenes pretrain), 37.40 (DDAD).

**Transferable technique**: render a BEV voxel grid by splatting each voxel as a Gaussian (cost scales with the Gaussian count, not the image size); orthogonal to the decoder choice and applicable to an SDF-derived opacity. Pretraining gain for a supervised model is about +1.2 mIoU.

**Cost and fit**: fits a 3060 better than volume rendering; the pretraining gain is small. Still self-supervised photometric quality (mIoU ~10) is poor.

**Doubts**: Table 6 and 7 compare against their own volume-rendering baseline; pretraining gain is a single run.
