**What it does**: The radar branch is a light Point Transformer (PT / PTv3 style): multi-sweep radar points (with RCS and Doppler features) are serialised along a space-filling curve, grouped into patches of 64 and processed by within-patch attention, as a 4-stage U-Net (channels 32 / 64 / 128 / 256, one block per stage, sample resolution 0.05 m, drop path 0.3). The output points live in the same positional-embedding space as the object queries. Fusion: sparse frustum fusion (radar points to image features along the frustum), range-adaptive radar aggregation (RAR, radar points near a query in a range-dependent radius), local self-attention among queries; no dense BEV grid.

**Results that matter** (nuScenes; checked): val camera baseline (Far3D) 48.6 NDS / 37.5 mAP; + LSA 49.8 / 38.9; + LSA + RAR 53.9 / 44.8; + SFF 54.9 / 46.4 (RAR is the radar-position part: +4.1 NDS / +5.9 mAP). Full model val R50 62.0 NDS / 54.5 mAP (R101 64.4 / 57.1); test V2-99 67.1 NDS / 60.0 mAP; tracking 63.1 AMOTA. Speed: Tiny (R18) 56.0 NDS at 32.9 FPS, Small (R50) 60.0 at 24.3, Base 61.7 at 19.1, Large 64.4 at 7.2, on one RTX 3090, batch 1, FP16 - the share of the radar encoder is NOT given.

**Transferable technique**: a radar encoder over RAW points (no pillars, no BEV grid) with a local radius whose size depends on the range (RAR) is what moves the camera baseline by +4 NDS; the idea that the neighbourhood radius should grow with range is in agreement with our polar window. The point-transformer part is not ablated against a pillar / conv encoder.

**Cost and fit**: whole detector 30 ms+ on a 3090; no isolated radar branch figure; no code.

**Doubts**: no radar-only result; no simple-encoder baseline for the point transformer; claims are for the detection benchmark with a 100 M-class camera model; code not released.
