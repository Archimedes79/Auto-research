**Verification**: id 1709.02371, Deqing Sun, Xiaodong Yang, Ming-Yu Liu, Jan Kautz; v1 2017-09-07, v3 2018-06-25 is the CVPR 2018 camera-ready (comment on the page); code link on the page. Matches the shorthand "pyramid, warping, cost volume".

**What it does** [checked abstract]: a learnable feature pyramid for both frames; at each level the CURRENT flow estimate warps the second frame's features, the warped features are compared with the first frame's features in a cost volume, and a CNN estimates the flow from the cost volume. [memory] the cost volume is local (search range d = 4 pixels at each level, correlation normalised by the feature length), a dense-connected estimator per level, a dilated context network at the end, and a multi-scale L2 loss with a weight per level; about 8.75 M parameters.

**Results that matter** [checked abstract]: about 17 times smaller than FlowNet2, easier to train; best published on the MPI Sintel final pass and KITTI 2015 at the time; about 35 fps on 1024 x 436 Sintel frames.

**Transferable technique**: warp-then-correlate is a residual cost volume: the search window only has to cover the RESIDUAL motion after the coarser estimate has been applied, which is how a small window covers 10-30 m/s at 0.5 m cells (a 0.5 s hop moves a car 5-15 m = 10-30 cells). Our block avoids the large search with explicit velocity hypotheses chained over the ego steps instead; a pyramid in velocity (coarse 4 m cells with all hypotheses, fine 0.5 m cells with residual hypotheses) is the PWC form of the same idea. The backward warp of PWC is a gather (our arm B "pull"); our splat is the forward form (see softmax splatting 2003.05534).

**Cost and fit**: the licence forbids commercial use, so take the idea only; the layer is cheap (one grid_sample and a local correlation per level).

**Doubts**: image-domain brightness constancy; the multi-scale loss weights are tuned for flow, not for velocity of sparse thin objects.
