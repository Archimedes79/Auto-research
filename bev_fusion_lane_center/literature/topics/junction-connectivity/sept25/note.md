# sept25 SEPT: SD-map prior and intersection keypoints for lane topology

- title: SEPT: Standard-Definition Map Enhanced Scene Perception and Topology Reasoning for Autonomous Driving
- relevance: 2
- summary: SEPT fuses an OpenStreetMap road skeleton into the BEV feature of OpenLane-V2 lane-topology networks (TopoNet, TopoLogic, LaneSegNet) through a raster branch, a vector branch and a gated fusion, and adds an auxiliary heatmap of SD-map intersection points. TOP_ll rises from 10.9 to 19.5 (TopoNet) and from 23.6 to 31.2 (TopoLogic) on the v1.1 metric, but the pair-scoring heads stay unchanged and no SD link-to-link connection is used, so it does not say which entry joins which exit. Useful as evidence for vector map tokens and a cheap keypoint auxiliary; no code released.

**What it does** (all from the arXiv HTML rendering, https://arxiv.org/html/2505.12246v1): the SD map (OpenStreetMap road, crosswalk and sidewalk polylines, cut to the ego frame) is encoded twice. Raster: occupancy canvas with one channel per road type, a CNN gives F_SD^R of shape H x W x C, then a FiLM-style "Feature Transformation" (FT) predicts per-channel gamma, beta from a max-pooled difference of BEV and SD features to reduce misalignment (F_B^R = gamma * F_SD^R + beta). Vector: polylines resampled to M segments, a SMERF-style transformer gives F_SD^V of shape M x C, and BEV cells attend to it by multi-head cross-attention (F_B^V). A "dual gated feature fusion" (DGFF) concatenates both, applies sigmoid gates, two projections and a weighted sum (mu = nu = 0.5). Fusion happens once on the final BEV feature (200 x 100 cells), which feeds the unchanged lane, area and topology heads. The auxiliary Intersection-aware KeyPoint Detection (IKPD) head predicts a 1-channel Gaussian heatmap of the SD map's merge, diverge and cross points from the fused feature, with focal loss; total loss L_DET + L_TOP + L_IKPD with no stated weights. The paper says it "does not modify the baseline loss functions": the lane-lane topology head and its supervision are those of TopoNet, TopoLogic or LaneSegNet and are not described.

**Results that matter** (OpenLane-V2 subset_A validation, ResNet-50; Tables I and II of the HTML page; improvements are against the same host without SD map; every OLS value recomputed by me from its four parts and matched; no seeds or intervals on the page). TopoNet to TopoNet+SEPT: TOP_ll 4.1 to 8.3 (v1.0), 10.9 to 19.5 (v1.1); DET_l 28.6 to 34.2; OLS 35.6 to 40.4 (v1.0), 39.8 to 45.2 (v1.1). TopoLogic to TopoLogic+SEPT: TOP_ll 18.0 to 25.1 (v1.0), 23.6 to 31.2 (v1.1); OLS 40.9 to 45.8, 43.4 to 48.4. Against the other SD method on the same host (SMERF): TopoNet TOP_ll 15.4 vs 19.5, TopoLogic 26.9 vs 31.2 (v1.1). LaneSegNet (OLUS metric): TOP_lsls 25.6 to 32.2, OLUS 36.7 to 42.6. Parameters grow by 7.8 to 8.6 M (computed from the tables, 61.8 to 70.4 M for LaneSegNet); no speed is given.

**Transferable technique** (no K id registered yet): (1) Vector SD tokens with cross-attention beat raster on topology in the paper's own ablation (LaneSegNet TOP_lsls +4.6 vector-only vs +1.9 raster-only, Table II); we already have both kinds (E104b raster tokens, E273 graph-transformer map tokens). (2) IKPD: a junction-node heatmap with free targets from the map graph, as an auxiliary on the shared feature. (3) DGFF-style learned gate when two map branches exist. (4) FT/FiLM for misalignment, a small gain (OLUS 38.2 to 39.9, Table III) next to our learned registration. Details and a port card: [details.md](details.md), [port_card.md](port_card.md).

**Why it performs** (the paper's ablation, Table II, LaneSegNet host, one run each): of the TOP_lsls gain +6.6, the hybrid SD fusion gives +5.4 (82 %, computed) and IKPD +1.2; OLUS +4.7 and +1.2. So the SD prior carries the gain and IKPD adds a smaller part. IKPD is ablated on LaneSegNet only, only as hybrid vs hybrid+IKPD, never without the SD map, never on TopoNet or TopoLogic. Table IV: DGFF TOP_lsls 31.0 vs addition 29.5, concatenation 29.9, cross-attention 30.0.

**Cost and fit** (for our attach step): the map branch is a prior on features, so it helps where the camera cannot see (far range, occlusion; qualitative rows in the paper), not at pair decisions as such. Cost: about +8 M parameters and a second map encoder in the paper; an IKPD-type head alone is a few conv layers (not measured by the paper). For us the vector side exists; what is new is the keypoint auxiliary (S effort), the gate (S to M) and nothing for pairs. Needs: junction nodes from the map graph or the GT lane graph in the grid frame. The paper's SD input is polylines and occupancy; lane numbers and road directions are listed as future work, while our E273 map tokens already carry successor / predecessor edges, lane count and lane index (experiment.md of E273, section 2.5), so on the input side we use more than the paper does.

**Doubts**: (a) Pair prediction is not shown: no junction or non-junction split of TOP_ll on the pages read, no per-pair analysis. (b) Table I baselines differ slightly from the originals (our local digest of the TopoLogic paper, TP01, lists TopoLogic at 29.9 / 47.2 / 18.6 / 21.5 for DET_l / DET_t / TOP_ll / TOP_lt in v1.0, SEPT lists 29.2 / 46.5 / 18.0 / 20.6), so the baselines were probably re-run, the paper does not say. (c) Standard split of subset_A with OSM maps in absolute place: the SMERF paper's own geographically disjoint split points to location leakage on the standard split (PL43 digest, Table V, not re-read here). (d) No SD-map noise, outdated or missing-map study; one qualitative outdated-map row. (e) Epochs, optimiser, learning rate, queries, image size, polyline points per segment, Gaussian radius, loss weights, CNN of the raster branch are not stated. (f) No code, so nothing can be reproduced; claims rest on single numbers.

**What it does NOT answer** (our question): which entry joins which exit is decided by the baseline heads (TopoNet MLP/GNN, TopoLogic distance plus similarity, LaneSegNet); the paper adds no pair feature, pair loss, matching or assignment, and does not use the SD graph's connections between roads to constrain pairs. IKPD reaches topology only through the shared feature. It also does not say how much of the TOP_ll gain sits at junctions.
