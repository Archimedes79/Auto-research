**What it does** [checked]: a particle-based DOGM on radar-only data; each particle carries x = [s v] (2-D position, 2-D velocity); per cell particles are sampled from a mixture of the prior (predicted previous population) and a distribution based on the current measurement; the number of STATIC particles sampled is driven by a radar pre-classification variable b_RSP (from direction, SNR, radial velocity and ego motion), not by the absolute radial velocity, because an object moving orthogonally to the sensor has zero radial velocity and would otherwise be called static; if b_RSP is below a threshold no static evidence is assumed and dynamic particles are drawn. Particles are weighted against the mixture, cell weights follow the maximum occupancy mass of the measurement grid with a decay function. No explicit likelihood formula is written out in the paper.

**Setup and results that matter**: 50 m grid edge, 0.2 m cells, at most 50 particles per cell; Berlin urban data, eleven Hella short-range radars and four Ibeo laser scanners (the lidar has blind spots), static ego vehicle in the quantitative test to avoid odometry errors. Velocity of one moving vehicle: mean square error 0.138 m/s for radar vs 1.350 m/s for lidar; NEES: lidar leaves the 95 % bound during braking (constant-velocity model), radar stays consistent; static / dynamic ROC better for radar (no AUC). C++, no runtime figure.

**Transferable technique**: (1) the orthogonal-mover problem is exactly the aperture problem of Doppler: a radar cell's evidence constrains only v . u, so "no radial velocity" must NOT be read as "static"; our evidence term -r^2 / (2 sigma^2) with J detections per cell is the right form (a tangential mover has r = v_m . u - d consistent with several v_m, so the learned score has to break the tie from the camera features), and the static hypothesis v_0 = 0 must be gated by the same ego-compensated Doppler; (2) birth particles are drawn from the measurement, i.e. hypotheses that are CONSISTENT with Doppler: for our block this is a sampling alternative to a learned f_m: add hypotheses on the Doppler ray (v = d u + t u_perp) with the tangential part t from the camera head.

**Cost and fit**: not differentiable; nothing to port as code.

**Doubts**: single vehicle, static ego, no comparison to a learned method; the 0.138 m/s figure is for one track.
