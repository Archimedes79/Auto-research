# 2411.13311 Resource-efficient camera + raw radar fusion in the polar BEV (range-azimuth) domain

**What it does**: The camera image is warped by fixed geometry (intrinsics, mounting height, pitch) to a Cartesian BEV grid (x 5-50 m, y -22..22 m), converted to polar coordinates and resampled with spline interpolation channel by channel; the transform is not learned. A camera FPN encoder gives range-azimuth (RA) camera features; the radar branch (RD tensor 512 x 256 x 32 channels, MIMO pre-encoder, ResNet-50-like encoder, Doppler / azimuth axis swap) gives RA radar features. Fusion: channel concatenation + four Conv-BN layers (144, 96, 96, 96). Outputs on a 128 x 224 polar grid (0.8 m x 0.8 deg): sigmoid occupancy and a 2-channel range / azimuth regression.

**Supervision**: focal loss + alpha-weighted smooth-L1 on positives (the FFT-RadNet recipe). RADIal, 8,252 labelled frames with 9,550 vehicles, 70 / 15 / 15 random split, Adam 1e-4, 100 epochs, batch 4.

**Results that matter** (RADIal test, IoU 0.5): AP 95.75 %, AR 91.35 %, F1 93.49 %, range error 0.11 m, angle error 0.09 deg; 6.58 M parameters, 58.91 FPS, 2.06 GPU GB. Camera backbone ablation: ResNet-50 53.67 M parameters and 18.55 FPS; EfficientNet-B2 14.47 M and 51.80 FPS.

**Transferable technique**: the fixed camera-to-polar warp (image to BEV to polar by geometry, no depth network) is the same idea as our virtual-camera + column-polar table, here published for a front camera; radar and camera features are fused on the common polar grid, which makes the radar's range and azimuth axes native. The 59 FPS figure is an existence proof that a polar fusion head can sit inside our budget.

**Cost and fit**: 6.6 M parameters; needs raw RD data for the radar branch (not nuScenes). The fixed warp assumes a flat ground, so height is lost.

**Doubts**: vehicle detection only; 70 / 15 / 15 random split (whether neighbouring frames leak is not discussed on the page); no map layers; no uncertainty output; the v1 text does not give FLOPs.
