**What it does**: The Augmenting-And-Purifying (AAP) pipeline: (1) superimpose multi-frame lidar (static points in world coordinates, movable objects through their boxes, linked by instance token) and voxelise; (2) train a camera+lidar baseline on these labels and generate pseudo labels; (3) fill only the empty voxels of the initial labels with the pseudo labels (about twice as dense); (4) purify by human annotation against projected multi-view images (about 4000 human hours). No mesh step.

**Format and coverage** [checked]: 0.2 m voxels, x, y in [-51.2, 51.2] m, z in [-5, 3] m, 512 x 512 x 40; 16 semantic classes (barrier ... vegetation) + empty, noise ignored; 850 scenes, 34 K keyframes (28,130 train, 6,019 val). Download v0.1 `nuScenes-Occupancy-v0.1.7z`: about 5 GB archive, about 130 GB unpacked (v0.0, deprecated, 24 GB / 270 GB); Google Drive and Baidu. Frame: lidar coordinates (z range suggests it; the page does not say).

**Results that matter**: none for radar; its adoption is large (Cam4DOcc builds its static-object layer on it).

**Transferable technique**: 0.2 m cells collapse to 0.5 m without loss of thin structure (barriers, poles); x reaches 51.2 m (85 % of our area, computed). A column rule over the structure classes gives the stationary map.

**Cost and fit**: the 130 GB unpacked size is the problem on our disks (C: is nearly full; D: holds the raw data); a streamed read of the 7z member by member would avoid it (not verified). Dense (mesh-free) stacking keeps the lidar sparsity visible at far range.

**Doubts**: the human purification covers an unknown share of frames; "pseudo-label fill" can add camera-network hallucinations to far cells; no visibility mask.
