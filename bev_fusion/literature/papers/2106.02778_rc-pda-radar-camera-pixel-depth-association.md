**What it does**: Radar hits are projected into the image on an assumed horizontal plane (height of the hit is unknown because of the wide beam) and the large radar-camera baseline plus occlusion make a hit's depth wrong for many neighbouring pixels. Stage 1 (RC-PDA, a U-Net) predicts for each radar pixel the probability that each pixel in a 5 x 36 neighbourhood shares its depth; this is converted to a 6-channel "multi-channel enhanced radar" (MER) at different confidence thresholds. Stage 2 is a standard image-guided depth completion net taking the image + MER. Attributes used: range, projected image position (azimuth), radial velocity (to compensate accumulated points) and radar scene flow with optical flow for occlusion; RCS is not used.

**Results that matter** (nuScenes, MAE in m, full image): image only 1.808; image + raw radar 1.569 (Ma et al. net) / 1.821 (Li et al. net); image + radar + MER 1.472 / 1.655. Where RC-PDA > 0.9: MER MAE 1.251 m against 7.369 m for raw radar. Raw radar even hurts one net (1.821 > 1.808). No per-attribute ablation.

**Transferable technique**: treat a radar point as a set of candidate pixels with a learned association probability, instead of a point with a hard position; output channels at several confidence thresholds are a soft substitute for a Gaussian footprint. For a BEV system the analogue is a learned footprint (elongated along the range/ray, wide in the unknown-height / lateral direction) per point - the same idea as GaussianCaR's covariance head, but trained with depth association labels derived from lidar.

**Cost and fit**: a U-Net on the sparse radar image in front-camera view: cheap, and it works with CAM_FRONT + one radar. Needs lidar-derived depth for training the association labels (we may have the nuScenes lidar at train time only).

**Doubts**: depth completion, not BEV; MER gains over image-only are modest (1.81 -> 1.47 m); evaluated on accumulated radar sweeps with a distance cap.
