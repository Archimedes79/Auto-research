## 1. Problem and claim
Given a black-box predictor and a post-processing family C_lambda(x) indexed by one scalar lambda (larger lambda = more conservative output, e.g. larger sets), choose lambda from n calibration points so that the expected value of a bounded loss on a fresh point is at most alpha. Split conformal prediction is the special case of the 0/1 miscoverage loss; the paper lifts it to any loss that is bounded above and non-increasing in lambda.

The claim that matters: with a one-line rule (a shifted empirical-risk threshold) the expected test loss is at most alpha at finite n, with no additive slack in the upper bound, and (under i.i.d. and no-jump conditions) at least alpha minus 2B/(n+1). The guarantee is marginal, in expectation, for exchangeable data; it is not a per-instance and not a high-probability guarantee.

## 2. Method as built

### 2.1 Setup and inputs/outputs
- Data: n calibration pairs plus one test pair, (X_i, Y_i), i = 1..n+1. A fixed model, a family C_lambda, lambda in a set Lambda with lambda_max = sup Lambda in Lambda.
- Loss: l(C_lambda(x), y) with values in (-inf, B], B < inf, non-increasing in lambda. Write L_i(lambda) = l(C_lambda(X_i), Y_i).
- Abstract form: L_1..L_{n+1} are exchangeable random functions Lambda -> (-inf, B], non-increasing in lambda.
- Output: one scalar lambda-hat, fixed before the test point arrives, used unchanged on every new point. Nothing is trained; the model is untouched.

### 2.2 The rule and the theorem (digest)
- Empirical risk: Rhat_n(lambda) = (L_1(lambda) + ... + L_n(lambda)) / n.
- E4 (paper Eq. 4): lambda-hat = inf { lambda : (n/(n+1)) Rhat_n(lambda) + B/(n+1) <= alpha }; if the set is empty, lambda-hat = lambda_max.
- E3 (paper Eq. 3): target E[ L_{n+1}(lambda-hat) ] <= alpha, expectation over calibration set and test point.
- **Theorem 1 (risk control):** if every L_i is non-increasing and right-continuous, L_i(lambda_max) <= alpha almost surely, and sup_lambda L_i(lambda) <= B almost surely, then E[ L_{n+1}(lambda-hat) ] <= alpha. Exact at finite n.
- Reading of the rule: the unseen test loss is replaced by its worst case B, so "empirical risk including the test point <= alpha" holds for any test loss; exchangeability moves this to the expectation. Proof sketch: build an oracle threshold lambda' from all n+1 losses, show lambda' <= lambda-hat, use monotonicity to bound the risk at lambda-hat by that at lambda', condition on the multiset of functions (the test function is then uniform over the n+1), apply total expectation.
- **Theorem 2 (lower bound):** additionally i.i.d. losses, L_i >= 0, and no jumps in the risk with probability one (for every lambda, P(jump of L_i at lambda > 0) = 0). Then E[ L_{n+1}(lambda-hat) ] >= alpha - 2B/(n+1). Hence alpha - 2B/(n+1) <= E[...] <= alpha. The empirical risk can jump by at most about B/n at a time (Jump Lemma), so the rule cannot overshoot far. The authors say the factor 2 may not be tight (conformal literature has 1/(n+1)).
- **Special case (Section 2.3, Eq. 13):** with L_i(lambda) = 1{s(X_i, Y_i) > lambda} the rule returns the usual split-conformal quantile, the ceil((n+1)(1-alpha))/n empirical quantile of the scores.

### 2.3 Not guaranteed (what is bounded and what is not)
- Bounded: the average of the loss over fresh points and over the draw of the calibration set (marginal). Not bounded: the loss of a given graph/frame, the loss conditional on the calibration set, or the realized average on one finite evaluation set. The paper calls concentration-based (high-probability) bounds excessively loose and compares to high-probability risk-control work (Bates et al. 2021; Learn-then-Test), which it says is technically unrelated and gives probability statements instead (Section 1.2).
- Not conditional on subgroups: no group-conditional guarantee is discussed (digest says the paper is silent on dependence between calibration points in practice).
- Spread of the realized risk: Section 3 reports the standard deviation of the realized risk over 1000 random calibration/evaluation splits per example (2.5); there is no formula for it.

### 2.4 Non-monotone losses and broken exchangeability
- **Non-monotone loss (Proposition 1):** for every epsilon there is a non-monotone loss with E[L_{n+1}(lambda-hat)] >= B - epsilon: the guarantee can fail arbitrarily badly. The paper stresses that monotonicity is the one assumption you must check.
- **Corollary 1 (monotonize):** replace L_i(lambda) by L~_i(lambda) = sup over lambda' >= lambda of L_i(lambda'), run the same rule on the average of these: E[L_{n+1}(lambda~)] <= alpha holds. It is valid always but loses power unless the risk is nearly monotone.
- **Theorem A.1 (Appendix A, asymptotic):** with the monotonized empirical risk of the average (running sup of Rhat_n) and lambda-up = inf { lambda : that <= alpha }, the limsup of the expected loss is <= alpha as n -> infinity; no finite-sample statement is known.
- **Distribution shift (Section 4.1):**
  - Known, bounded likelihood ratio w = dP_test/dP_train: apply Theorem 1 to the weighted losses (Eq. 25).
  - Covariate shift with access to the test covariate (transductive): E26: lambda-hat(x) = inf { lambda : ( sum_i w(X_i) L_i(lambda) + w(x) B ) / ( sum_i w(X_i) + w(x) ) <= alpha }; Proposition 2 gives E[L_{n+1}(lambda-hat(X_{n+1}))] <= alpha even for unbounded w. Estimating w is left open.
  - Proposition 3 (total variation): E[L_{n+1}(lambda-hat)] <= alpha + B * sum_{i=1..n} TV(Z_i, Z_{n+1}); under the Theorem 2 conditions the lower side is alpha - B * ( 2/(n+1) + sum_i TV(Z_i, Z_{n+1}) ). The sum grows with n, so the bound is vacuous unless the per-point TV distance is tiny (inference).
- **Dependent data (frames of one scene):** not treated by the paper. Theorem 1 needs the n+1 loss functions to be exchangeable; Theorem 2 needs i.i.d. Whole scenes drawn at random are exchangeable units; the frames inside a scene are not exchangeable with frames of another scene (inference, section 5).

### 2.6 Other extensions (Section 4, digest, not needed for us now)
Quantile risk (4.2): Quantile_beta of the loss <= alpha is the expectation of an indicator loss (Proposition 4); unconditional CVaR control is open. Multiple risks (4.3): for losses L(lambda; gamma) with levels alpha(gamma), take lambda-hat = sup_gamma lambda-hat_gamma (Eq. 30), then every normalised risk is <= 1 (Proposition 5); this needs all risks to move in the same direction. Adversarial risk (4.4): apply the rule to the worst case over a perturbation set (Proposition 6). U-risks (4.5): order-k statistics for ranking/metric learning (Proposition 7).

### 2.7 Calibration-set size
- The paper gives no formula for a required n. The guarantee holds for any n; what changes is (a) the B/(n+1) shift, which makes the rule more conservative when n is small, (b) the slack 2B/(n+1) between the lower and upper bound, and (c) the spread of the realized risk across calibration draws.
- Examples: n from 1000 to 30000 with sd from 0.0011 to 0.0150; the sd also depends on the loss and the model, so n alone cannot be read from these (the tool said the same). The paper remarks that the gap between 1/(n+1) and 2/(n+1) is small already for moderate n.
- My arithmetic: with B = 1 the shift is 1/(n+1): 0.048 for n = 20 units, 0.0099 for n = 100, 0.0011 for n = 873 (rounded). The unit count n matters, not the number of frames (section 5.2).

### 2.8 Cost
Zero at inference: lambda-hat is one constant, the model is unchanged and not retrained. Calibration cost = one pass of the pipeline per calibration point and per lambda on a grid, plus a mean and a threshold. The released function is four statements (section 4). Nothing about training schedule, hardware or time is given beyond the experiments (not found).

### 5.1 Which loss (my construction on top of the paper's examples; inference)
The paper's relevant examples are FNR (Fig. 1, 2: fraction of true items missed, a set that grows with lambda), the best-of-set F1 (Fig. 4) and the graph distance to the nearest member of a set (Fig. 3). A whole-graph F1/Q is not usable (ratio, not monotone). Two losses fit our output as a *set of hypothesis branches* whose size is steered by the threshold:
1. **Pruning-induced miss (recommended first):** L_miss(tau) = (GT lane length covered by the unpruned hypothesis tree but not covered by the graph kept at threshold tau) / (GT lane length covered by the unpruned tree), or divided by a fixed constant. Denominator from GT/tree, not from the output. It is 0 at tau = lowest threshold (no pruning), so L(lambda_max) = 0 <= alpha holds by construction; this is a pruning loss relative to the tree, not an absolute recall (absolute recall is capped by the model and cannot be guaranteed up to alpha). It is the FNR example of Fig. 1/2 applied to the branches. With lambda = 1 - tau (larger lambda = keep more) it is non-increasing.
2. **False length (the "short graphs" side):** L_false(tau) = length (or vertex count) of kept graph not within the match tolerance of GT, divided by a fixed constant such as the length budget (not by the number of kept vertices). It is non-increasing in the pruning strength and 0 for the empty graph. Opposite orientation to loss 1, so the two cannot be combined with Section 4.3 (needs one orientation); instead compute tau_false (smallest pruning strength with false loss <= alpha_f) and tau_miss (largest pruning strength with miss loss <= alpha_m) and report whether tau_false <= tau_miss. If not, the two targets conflict and that itself is a finding.
3. Graph distance (Fig. 3 type): a Chamfer-style distance from each GT lane to the nearest kept vertex, normalised by a fixed cap, is monotone under nested pruning (more vertices cannot increase the nearest distance); a smoother alternative to the binary found-or-not. Optional second loss.
Losses per frame are bounded; the check "per-frame loss curves non-increasing" is an empirical test on the table, and Corollary 1 repairs small violations.

### 5.2 Unit of exchangeability and calibration split (inference)
- **Unit = scene.** Frames of one scene are strongly correlated (given), so frame-level n = 873 would overstate the information; the B/(n+1) shift and the realized spread should be computed with n = number of scenes. Define L_i(tau) = mean over the frames of scene i of the per-frame loss; B bounds this mean. A new scene is then exchangeable with the calibration scenes if scenes are sampled alike; the time recursion inside a scene is harmless (the scene loss is a function of the whole scene). The VAL scene count was not looked up (PROTOCOL.md says TEST = 21 scenes; scene lists are in `data/splits.json`); the count decides everything: with about 20 units the shift alone is about 0.05 of B (my arithmetic from 2.7), comparable to an alpha such as 0.1.
- **Which split.** TRAIN cannot be used (the heads were fitted on it: losses are biased low, exchangeability with new scenes fails). VAL is the only candidate, and it is also the set on which the 0.3 / 0.4 were tuned. Use the conformal rule *instead of* that tuning (the rule is the selection), and judge it on disjoint scenes: repeated random scene-level splits of VAL (as the paper does with 1000 splits of n and the rest), reporting mean realized risk, sd and the share of splits above alpha; then TEST once as a single realization (21 scenes: sd is large, one number).
- **alpha and the grid are fixed on the card before the curve is looked at.** Choosing alpha because the curve looks good turns the guarantee into an in-sample statement; label it so if it happens.
- **Failure series and map errors.** Calibration on clean scenes, test on dark / fog / cam-off halves breaks exchangeability; Proposition 3 gives no usable bound. Options: (a) calibrate per known condition (smaller n); (b) weighted rule Eq. 25/26 with the known mixture weights w = condition frequency ratio, which needs calibration losses for each condition (the failure series exists on VAL, E193); (c) just measure the violation and report it. (b) is the paper's route for a discrete, known shift; an estimated or unobserved condition is listed by the paper as open.

## 8. Testing recipe for us (proposal; card with numeric prediction before the run)
- **Layer 0 (self-test, numpy):** (a) toy known answer: n = 9, scores 0.1 .. 0.9 in steps of 0.1, miscoverage loss 1{s > lambda}, alpha = 0.2, B = 1: the rule needs (count of scores above lambda + 1) / 10 <= 0.2, so lambda-hat = 0.8 (my arithmetic), equal to the ceil((n+1)(1-alpha))-th smallest score. (b) Monte-Carlo: i.i.d. uniform scores, n = 50, alpha = 0.1, 5000 repetitions: mean realized risk in [alpha - 2B/(n+1), alpha] (the paper's bounds; the lower side needs continuous scores). (c) empty-set case returns lambda_max and a non-monotone toy loss visibly violates alpha (Proposition 1 behaviour). (d) the port raises when the first grid point already violates the bound.
- **Layer 1:** reproduce the champion's operating point: report the pruning-induced miss and false-length risk curves at tau = 0.3 on VAL from `evaluate.py` outputs; the stored Q 0.735 must come back from the same graphs.
- **Layer 2 sanity:** shuffled scene pairing of loss and GT (rule then returns the trivial threshold), frame-level versus scene-level calibration (frame-level should under-cover on held-out scenes; this is the demonstration of the unit argument), a null threshold (no pruning).
- **Layer 3-4:** 1000 random scene-level splits of VAL into calibration and evaluation parts; report mean realized risk, sd, share of splits above alpha, chosen tau with its spread, and Q / topo F1 / lanes found / R@1 far of the graph at the calibrated tau against the hand-tuned champion on the same evaluation scenes (paired scene bootstrap, noise band 0.010).
- **Prediction (mine, to be put on the card):** the calibrated threshold for a pruning-induced miss target lands within the hand-tuned 0.3-0.4 neighbourhood for a moderate alpha and moves by more than the grid step across random scene splits when the VAL has few scenes; realized risk on the held-out half is at or below alpha on average with a visible tail of splits above it; Q at the calibrated tau is inside the 0.010 band of the hand-tuned value.
- **Decision rule:** KEEP the calibrated rule as the documented selection procedure if (i) the mean realized risk on held-out scenes is <= alpha plus one standard error and (ii) Q (and topo F1) at the calibrated tau is not worse than the hand-tuned value by more than the band; TIE if the calibrated tau is within the band of the hand-tuned one (take the cheaper variant: both cost zero at inference, keep the one with the guarantee); CUT if the loss curves are not monotone beyond a Corollary-1 repair, or if realized risk exceeds alpha on more than the nominal share of splits for reasons not explained by the small unit count; INCONCLUSIVE means more scenes are needed (TRAIN losses cannot be added, see 5.2; options are the failure-series scenes or a new split decided by Mirko).
- **Order:** numpy rule and Layer 0 first (hours), then the loss tables from cached trees, then the split study.

## 9. Novelty and relevance for our problem
The novelty is the extension of the conformal quantile to any bounded monotone loss with a one-line rule, a finite-sample upper bound with no slack, a matching lower bound up to 2B/(n+1), and handling of covariate shift and non-monotone losses by weighting and monotonization. For us it is the only candidate we have found that turns "pick the fork-pruning threshold on VAL" into a rule with a statement, at zero inference cost, with a hard requirement list (monotone loss, nested pruning, scene as the unit, enough scenes). Its limits are exactly ours: the guarantee is marginal and in expectation, small scene counts make the shift term and the spread large, and it can only choose among graphs the model already offers; it does not close the coverage gap or improve Q. Value: a calibration and robustness tool for A8 (and a clean way to report the operating point), not a quality lever.
