**What it does**: BEVFormer with the learnable BEV queries replaced by a radar-derived embedding. Points of all five radars are put into a common ego frame and counted per BEV cell (a saliency grid); an embedding layer maps each count to a learnable vector, a gated unit (sigmoid-linear times tanh-linear) filters it, and the result plus the BEV positional embedding forms the queries of temporal self-attention and spatial cross-attention. A multi-task head predicts rain / night as binary labels. The page does not state which per-point attributes (RCS, Doppler) are used; only the point COUNT per cell enters, so it is a radar occupancy encoding.

**Results that matter** (nuScenes val, NDS): night: baseline 0.1913, multi-task only 0.2742, radar backbone only 0.2744, both 0.2812; rain: 0.3877, 0.5060, 0.5087, 0.5091. Full val: 0.4787 -> 0.4863 NDS. Headline 46.99 % relative night gain and 31.31 % rain gain over BEVFormer. Note: multi-task learning alone gives almost all the gain, so the radar occupancy contribution beyond the weather flags is small (+0.007 NDS at night).

**Transferable technique**: a one-channel radar-occupancy prior is enough to seed BEV queries; and the weather / time-of-day auxiliary labels are an inexpensive way to teach the fusion when to trust the camera (A8 failure series).

**Cost and fit**: needs a BEVFormer-class trained encoder; the radar backbone itself is trivial. Not a model for a 12 GB small system.

**Doubts**: baseline at night is very low (NDS 0.19), so relative gains look large; the attribution of the gain to radar is weak (MTL alone is as good).
