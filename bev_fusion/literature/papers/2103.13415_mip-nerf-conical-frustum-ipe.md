**What it does** [checked]: NeRF casts one ray per pixel; Mip-NeRF casts a cone through the pixel centre o, direction d, with radius at unit distance r = pixel width x 2/sqrt(12) (so the cross-section variance matches the pixel footprint variance). The interval [t0, t1] along the cone is a conical frustum, approximated by a Gaussian: with t_mu = (t0+t1)/2 and t_delta = (t1-t0)/2, the mean along the ray is t_mu + 2 t_mu t_delta^2 / (3 t_mu^2 + t_delta^2), the axial variance is t_delta^2/3 - 4 t_delta^4 (12 t_mu^2 - t_delta^2) / (15 (3 t_mu^2 + t_delta^2)^2), and the radial variance is r^2 (t_mu^2/4 + 5 t_delta^2/12 - 4 t_delta^4 / (15 (3 t_mu^2 + t_delta^2))). In world space: mu = o + mu_t d, Sigma = sigma_t^2 d d^T + sigma_r^2 (I - d d^T / |d|^2). The positional encoding is replaced by its expectation under this Gaussian (IPE): sin / cos of P mu times exp(-0.5 diag(P Sigma P^T)), so frequencies that are finer than the frustum width are damped to zero.

**Results that matter** [checked, abstract + ar5iv]: multiscale Blender, average error about 60 % lower than NeRF; single-scale Blender about 17 % lower; about 7 % faster than NeRF; one MLP with 612 k parameters instead of 1191 k; equals a brute-force supersampled NeRF at about 22x less compute. All rendering numbers, none for driving or BEV.

**Transferable technique** [inference from the checked formulas, not from the paper]: for a feature cell (pixel group) with footprint w pixels at stride s, take the cone radius r(t) = t * (w*s/f) * 2/sqrt(12) (f = focal length in pixels) and the depth-bin interval [t0, t1]. The closed-form Gaussian (mu, Sigma) of each (cell, depth bin) frustum can be marginalised to the BEV plane by taking the xy sub-block of Sigma (true for any Gaussian), giving an anisotropic 2D footprint that grows with range and is elongated along the viewing direction: exactly the "cross-section integrated over the BEV cells it covers" of the brief. It can be fed to a Gaussian rasteriser (GaussianLSS / GaussianCaR use diff-gaussian-rasterization) or to an erf-based per-cell integral. The depth-distribution variance (GaussianLSS) can be added to sigma_t^2, so the footprint has a geometric and a learned part. Spec test for a port: for d along x and r = 0 the covariance must reduce to sigma_t^2 on the axis; for a cone of known radius the radial variance must match a Monte Carlo sample of the frustum (numpy, few lines).

**Cost and fit**: a few elementwise ops per (cell, bin), no learned parameters; IPE itself is not needed unless a positional code of the position is used.

**Doubts**: Gaussian approximation of the frustum is exact only in its first two moments; for thin far frustums fine, for wide near ones the true shape is not Gaussian; 2/sqrt(12) is a choice for a square pixel and a symmetric radius, a rectangular multi-pixel group gives an elliptical cone (not covered by the paper). No driving evidence that a footprint beats a point on a 0.5 m BEV grid.
