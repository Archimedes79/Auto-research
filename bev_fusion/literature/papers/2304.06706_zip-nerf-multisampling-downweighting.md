**What it does** [checked]: Grid features (Instant-NGP) have no notion of scale, so cone rendering with a plain lookup at the frustum centre aliases. Zip-NeRF replaces the Gaussian-IPE by (a) multisampling: each conical frustum becomes six points in a hexagonal pattern around the ray whose mean and variance match the frustum, each an isotropic Gaussian with sigma_j = 0.5 r t_j / sqrt(2); and (b) downweighting: the trilinear lookup at grid level l is scaled by erf(1 / sqrt(8 sigma_j^2 n_l^2)), where n_l is the grid resolution, so a Gaussian much wider than a cell contributes less; the six weighted lookups are averaged, and the weights are also given to the MLP.

**Results that matter** [checked]: versus mip-NeRF 360, about 11 % lower RMSE, 17 % lower DSSIM, 19 % lower LPIPS and about 24x faster training on the 360 benchmark; versus a naive iNGP + mip-NeRF 360 combination 55 % to 77 % lower error at the coarsest scale; ablations: removing multisampling or downweighting both hurt, naive 6x6 supersampling does not reach it.

**Transferable technique**: the erf weight is the closed-form fraction of a Gaussian that fits into one grid cell; for a BEV grid with cell size c and a footprint with sigma along a direction, erf(c / (2 sqrt(2) sigma)) is the mass inside one cell per axis (inference, not in the paper). Together with Mip-NeRF this gives a per-cell integral of the footprint without sampling, which is the cheap version of "integrate the cross-section over the BEV cells it covers". Hexagon multisampling is the sampling version if the cell must be looked up in a learned grid rather than splatted.

**Cost and fit**: designed for per-scene optimisation on a 3D hash grid; cost per ray is six lookups. Only the idea transfers; the code would have to be re-derived (no official repo seen).

**Doubts**: no 2D / driving use; a camera-BEV test would need our own ablation (point vs cone vs cone + erf). The transfer to 2D is the scout's extrapolation.
