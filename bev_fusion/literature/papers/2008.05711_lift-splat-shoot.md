**What it does** [checked]: each image yields H x W x D frustum points; per pixel a context vector c (C channels) is scaled by the depth probability alpha_d (outer product alpha x c); depths are fixed 4 to 45 m in 1 m steps (about 42 bins), images 128 x 352. The splat is a sum pool of all points into the BEV pillar of their nearest cell (200 x 200 at 0.5 m, +-50 m); the cumulative-sum trick (sort by cell id, cumsum, subtract at boundaries) with an analytic gradient gives about 2x faster training; the forward pass runs at 35 Hz on a Titan V. One point per (pixel, depth bin): no footprint, nearest-cell assignment.

**Results that matter** [checked]: BEV IoU nuScenes car / vehicles: CNN 22.78 / 24.25, OFT 29.72 / 30.05, Lift-Splat 32.06 / 32.07; map drivable / lane boundary 72.94 / 19.96 (OFT 71.69 / 18.07). The paper does not ablate bin count or pooling.

**Transferable technique**: the frustum tensor and the cumsum pooling define the interface that a cone lift has to match (features, depth probabilities, camera geometry in, C x H x W BEV out). A cone lift changes only the splat step: from nearest-cell (one cell per point) to a footprint weight over several cells. BEVPoolV2 (already collected) is the fast version of the same step.

**Cost and fit**: cheap on a 12 GB card at 128 x 352; the point splat is exactly what aliases at far range (one point per bin, gaps between rays).

**Doubts**: licence is the practical blocker for code reuse (see above); the in-paper comparison baselines are weak by today's standards.
