# 2210.06006 BEV-LaneDet - monocular front-camera lane detection at 100+ FPS

**What it does**: a ResNet34 backbone; two front-view feature maps (1/32 and 1/64 of the 576x1024 input) go through the Spatial Transformation Pyramid. In it the View Relation Module is an MLP that maps flattened front-view positions to flattened BEV positions (a fixed learned mapping, no depth, no calibration at test time). A "virtual camera" first warps every image to mean-of-training intrinsics/extrinsics by one homography, which fixes the geometry the MLP has to learn. Output on a BEV grid of 0.5 m cells, 200 x 40 (x about 3 to 103 m, y -10 to 10 m): four heads (confidence, embedding, offset, height). Depth uncertainty: none; ambiguity is absorbed into the learned MLP, heights come from the height head.

**Results that matter**: OpenLane F-score 58.4 vs PersFormer 47.8; Apollo balanced scene 98.7 vs 92.9 (table; the abstract and the intro quote different Apollo gains). Speed 185 FPS TensorRT / 102 FPS PyTorch on a Tesla V100; ResNet18 variant 272 FPS.

**Transferable technique**: for a fixed front camera, (1) the homography warp to a virtual camera removes extrinsic variation and makes the lift a function of image position only; (2) a learned MLP view relation (H*W -> X*Y) as a tiny lift; (3) 0.5 m cell BEV with offset+height heads is our grid and fits lane centre points. Pair with a Gaussian-variance map so that far cells carry an uncertainty channel.

**Cost and fit**: 3060 will reach well over 50 FPS (estimate from V100 numbers, not measured). Lane-specific. Forward range 100 m in a narrow 20 m strip: not a wide BEV.

**Doubts**: OpenLane/Apollo, not nuScenes; the MLP relation is dataset-specific (breaks if the camera moves); flat-ground homography assumption; no uncertainty output.
