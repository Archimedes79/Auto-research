### 3.18 Loop 12: calibrated outputs and failure monitors for the hand-over to the planner (how the literature entered; what each element gave in our experiments)

The question of loop 12 is not a new block but whether the numbers on the interface can be believed. The four works below were looked up for this loop (abstract pages, 2026-10-18; (abs) = abstract only, nothing from the full text); the last column is ours (nuScenes TEST, camera + radar BEV features, 21 scenes, simulated navigation map, synthetic failures; sections 7.31 - 7.35).

| Work | Idea | Where it entered | What came out in our experiments |
|---|---|---|---|
| Guo et al., On Calibration of Modern Neural Networks (ICML 2017) (abs) | modern networks are mis-calibrated; temperature scaling, a single-parameter variant of Platt scaling, is the proposed remedy | expected calibration error (ECE) and one temperature per output, fitted on the VAL rows (E109) | the route probability of the assignment ensemble is calibrated without any scaling (ECE 0.006 clean, map error 2 m); the keep probability gains from a temperature on clean data (0.023 -> 0.011) and loses under failure (section 7.32) |
| Ovadia et al., Can You Trust Your Model's Uncertainty? (NeurIPS 2019) (abs) | under dataset shift, post-hoc calibration falls short; methods that marginalise over models do better | the failure sources of the protocol are the shift (E109); the seed ensemble of the assignment networks was not compared with a single network | the temperature of the clean VAL rows raises the keep ECE under every failure type; a temperature fitted on the failure type helps for six of seven types, one fitted on the other types only partly (section 7.32) |
| Gu et al., Producing and Leveraging Online Map Uncertainty in Trajectory Prediction (CVPR 2024) (abs) | online map estimators extended to output uncertainty; the uncertainty is fed to a trajectory predictor; reported on nuScenes: up to 50 % faster training convergence and up to 15 % better prediction | the interface of section 4 of the decision report (probabilities and trust signals next to the graph) follows the same idea for a lane graph with a route attribute | whether a planner gains from the probabilities was not tested (open loop, section 9.1); what we measured is that the route probability and a frame-level confidence are usable (E109, E110) |
| MetaBEV, ICCV 2023 (abs) | BEV queries that pull features from whatever sensors are present; trained for sensor failure (LiDAR or camera missing); reported for LiDAR + camera | the failure protocol (camera and radar sets switched off, E57 and later) | MetaBEV trains for the missing sensor; our frozen foundation does not, and the hold (E108, E112) only covers the time while the remembered area stays in view; the sources we looked up contain no radar-camera comparison |

Takeaway: the route attribute of the modular system is calibrated where a right map is given (ECE 0.006 clean and at most 0.016 under the seven failure types at map error 2 m; 16 conditions = eight sources at the map errors 0 and 2 m, all below 0.02); the keep probability over-predicts under failure, clearly only with an extrinsics error (ECE 0.164); a failure-conditioned temperature repairs part of it but needs the failure type, which is what a monitor would have to tell. The literature we looked up offers calibration and uncertainty for single outputs; a frame-level confidence from the interface alone, as measured in E110, was not found in these four abstracts (this is not a claim of novelty).
