**What it does** [checked]: camera-radar fusion in two spaces: in the perspective view a Height Association Transformer uses radar features to improve the depth prediction (height ambiguity of 2+1D radar); in BEV a radar-weighted depth consistency refines the sparse initial BEV. Radar points are voxelised into pillars at one height level; the head is CenterPoint-style (anchor-free, centre-based), tracking uses CenterPoint's greedy velocity matching. The summary says the text does not say how Doppler channels enter the model and describes no dedicated velocity branch. Temporal: the last eight history frames are fused; the back-projection runs after temporal fusion; no explicit BEV warping or alignment is described in what was read (the base codebases BEVDet / SoloFusion do warp).

**Results that matter** [checked on html]: nuScenes val R50 256 x 704: NDS 58.5, mAP 49.4, mAVE 0.227; R101 512 x 1408: 61.7 / 53.6 / 0.231; test (V2-99) NDS 64.2, mAP 57.4 (abstract: 64.2 NDS +1.8 and 58.4 AMOTA +1.5 over the previous camera-radar best); occupancy on Occ3D +3.7 mIoU over previous camera-based methods (abstract). FPS and hardware not found.

**How it handles velocity**: not by flow or warping in the part read: regression by the detection head from the stacked eight-frame BEV history plus radar features; mAVE 0.227 on val is the best camera-radar velocity number found in this scan (RCBEVDet R50: 0.220, see its note; CRN 0.344).

**Transferable technique**: nothing for the velocity block; the radar-weighted depth consistency is an A1/A2 item.

**Doubts**: SOA numbers are detection-box numbers (mAVE is per box, evaluated at matched true positives), not a dense per-cell velocity field; they do not test our block's claim.
