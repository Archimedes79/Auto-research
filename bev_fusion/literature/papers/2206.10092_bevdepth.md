**What it does** [checked]: (1) Camera-aware depth: intrinsics and extrinsics are flattened, passed through an MLP and used as a Squeeze-and-Excitation gate on the image features, then also concatenated into DepthNet. (2) Explicit depth supervision: lidar points are projected into each view, min-pooled to the feature stride, one-hot over the depth bins, binary cross-entropy. (3) Depth Refinement Module: stacked 3x3 convs on the depth-width plane of the frustum features, +0.8 mAP in the ablation. (4) Efficient Voxel Pooling: one CUDA thread per frustum feature adds into its BEV cell (replaces sort + cumsum), 80x faster pooling than the LSS baseline per the paper. Still a point lift: the feature goes to the single BEV cell containing the point.

**Results that matter** [checked]: nuScenes val BEVDepth-R50 256 x 704: 35.1 mAP / 47.5 NDS; R101 512 x 1408: 41.2 / 53.5; test with TTA 60.0 NDS (60.9 with ConvNeXt). Ablation vanilla 28.2 mAP / 32.7 NDS to full 33.0 / 44.2; depth loss alone +2.2 mAP. Bin count and range not on the page (112 bins over 2-58 m is from memory, same as the MatrixVT row).

**Transferable technique**: the camera-aware gate is the cheapest way to make one depth head work for a fixed-calibration rig and a changed one; the lidar depth loss is the training signal that makes the depth distribution (and hence the cone's axial extent) meaningful. For a cone lift the pooling kernel is the part to replace, depth supervision and DepthNet stay.

**Cost and fit**: needs lidar depth labels (available in nuScenes); the CUDA voxel pooling is a point scatter-add and would have to be extended to footprint weights.

**Doubts**: text and table disagree on the total gain (text: +4.0 mAP / +4.0 NDS; table: +4.8 mAP / +11.5 NDS) on the page read; detection only; heavy backbones in the headline numbers.
