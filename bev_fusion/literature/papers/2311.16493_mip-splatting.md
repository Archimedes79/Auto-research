**What it does** [checked abstract]: 3D Gaussian splatting shows artifacts when the sampling rate changes (focal length, camera distance). Two fixes: a 3D filter that limits the size of each Gaussian by the maximum sampling frequency implied by the training views, and a 2D Mip filter that replaces the screen-space dilation with a 2D box filter simulating the pixel integration. Works when training at one scale and testing at several.

**Results that matter**: no numbers on the abstract page.

**Transferable technique**: our BEV splat is the same problem at a different scale: a BEV cell of 0.5 m integrates the footprint, and a Gaussian smaller than the cell aliases (the rasteriser's fixed dilation hides this). The 2D Mip filter variance rule (the Gaussian's covariance gets a cell-sized box variance added, with an opacity compensation so that the integral is preserved: [memory], not on the abstract page) is the principled way to keep the splat mass constant when the footprint is below one cell; the 3D filter ties the minimum Gaussian size to the sampling rate of the source camera, i.e. to the pixel-group footprint of block 1 (my reading of the abstract, formulas not read).

**Cost and fit**: no extra parameters; needs a change in the CUDA rasteriser (GaussianLSS's diff-gaussian-rasterization is a modified 3DGS rasteriser, MIT-licensed repo per its page, base-rasteriser licence not checked here).

**Doubts**: shown for novel-view rendering only; formulas and code were not read, so the exact filter has to be taken from the paper before any claim.
