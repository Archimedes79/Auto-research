**What it does**: Each radar point has 5 channels: 3D coordinates, ego-compensated velocities and a time offset (up to 2048 points, 6 earlier sweeps); RCS is NOT used and the paper explicitly argues against relying on it. An MLP builds pillar features that are scattered on a 128 x 128 BEV (more than 90 % empty). The Radar Dense Encoder downsamples to 1/2, 1/4, 1/8, applies self-attention at the smallest scale with a 2D position embedding to fill empty cells, and restores the resolution with skip connections. Radar tokens use a 2D BEV position embedding (height ignored); image tokens use PETR-style 3D embeddings. Elevation ambiguity (radar height unknown, so azimuth and depth noise) is handled by fusing radar and image tokens in separate transformer stages and re-positioning queries after each layer.

**Results that matter** (nuScenes val, NDS / mAP, R50 256 x 704): radar only 12.3 / 1.6; camera only 49.3 / 38.9; radar + camera 51.7 / 43.0; SECOND BEV encoder 55.1 / 46.6; Radar Dense Encoder with skips + self-attention 56.4 / 48.2 (vs SECOND +1.3 NDS, +1.6 mAP); full decoder 58.6 / 50.9 at 52.2 ms on an RTX 3090 (ResNet18: 56.0 / 47.4 at 39.8 ms). Test (V2-99): 64.7 NDS / 57.8 mAP.

**Transferable technique**: densify the sparse radar BEV with a small U-shaped conv + attention at 1/8 scale before fusion; the radar-only network is almost useless (1.6 mAP) yet adds +2.4 NDS-points to camera-only, which says radar acts as a depth/velocity prior. Dropping RCS is a data point against the RCS-aware scatter of RCBEVDet (the two papers disagree; neither ablates RCS alone).

**Cost and fit**: the dense encoder is small; the decoder is query-based and not needed for a dense BEV head.

**Doubts**: no per-attribute ablation; the time offset channel could carry some of the gain; detection only.
