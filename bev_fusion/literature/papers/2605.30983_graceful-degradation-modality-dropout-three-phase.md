**What it does** [checked]: on BEVFusion-MIT it adds a TrustGate router that scores each modality's trust and a fusion block (FFB), trained in three phases: Phase 1 pure-vision warm-up, 1 epoch at lr 1e-4; Phase 2 with the image backbone and one expert frozen, the router and FFB trained 3 epochs at lr 1e-5 WITH modality dropout (each iteration: both kept p = 0.5, LiDAR dropped p = 0.25, camera dropped p = 0.25; the dropped features are zeros before the experts and router); Phase 3 end-to-end fine-tuning without dropout, 3 epochs at lr 1e-7. 4 x A100.

**Results that matter** [checked, nuScenes val, clean / LiDAR-drop / camera-drop mAP]: BEVFusion without dropout 66.9 (NDS 69.7) clean and 0.0 mAP when LiDAR is dropped; the same with plain modality dropout 56.1 / 12.1 / 44.1 (NDS 62.2 / 14.4 / 50.1): dropout costs 10.8 mAP of CLEAN accuracy and still leaves the LiDAR-drop case at 12.1; proposed 68.3 / 32.8 / 58.4 (NDS 71.2 / 39.9 / 65.1).

**Transferable technique**: (1) with a camera branch and a radar branch fused by concatenation a naive dropout schedule can cost clean accuracy; our plan's dropout (p = 0.15-0.2 from epoch 2) is a milder variant but needs a clean-accuracy gate: compare against the no-dropout control on VAL, same budget; (2) the staged schedule (single-sensor warm-up, then fusion with dropout and frozen backbones, then a low-lr no-dropout fine-tune) is the pattern; its Phase 3 lr is 100 x smaller than Phase 1: dropout off before the end keeps the full-input case sharp; (3) a per-modality trust score as input to the fusion (our mass and dt channels are the geometric analogue).

**Cost and fit**: schedule only (3 + 1 + 3 epochs); the router is small.

**Doubts**: camera-LiDAR, where a missing LiDAR is catastrophic (0.0 mAP), a much larger gap than camera vs radar; the 10.8 mAP clean loss is one run on BEVFusion-MIT; no code to check; the same 3-phase gives gains from staging as well as from the router, they are not separated in the numbers read.
