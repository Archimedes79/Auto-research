# E282 The node head learns the sparse row itself: a zone-matching existence loss (baseline B, D55 / D58)

Card of 2026-11-08, written by the lane chat at Mirko's word ("Yes, write it"), revised the same day after a fresh critic pass (`ar-critic`, mode cards: REVISE, five blocking points, all checked against the sources and taken, section 8). Status: **step 1 (the code) is written, tested and has passed a code critic pass (section 8, second table); the CPU mini-pilot has not run; no GPU job queued.** This card is stage 1 of a longer road (the node head alone). After the code, the first job is a CPU mini-pilot (section 3) that can close the card before any GPU hour. GPU only in Mirko's windows. No TEST. Nothing pushed.

**Why this card.** Two ways to a thin node row exist today: a rule step on the output (E275, hard step, KEEP) and a trained soft step inside the head (E277 / E279, KEEP at 1.25 m). Both thin a DENSE row (a node in every 2 m cell: 31.5 nodes per 100 m of GT lane in B; 19 - 21 vertices per 100 m after thinning). A thinner row than 1.5 m destroys B's graph (hard step 2.0 m: topo F1 0.238, lanes found 0.218, E275 curve); why (links trained between neighbouring 2 m cells, or the node recall of 0.545 at 2.0 m) is not separated by that test. The point density was Mirko's own complaint ("the point density is very high", E276 P1). E271 went a pixel way (a differentiable sparsification of the 1 m centre-lane head to a vertex budget). E276 arm s tried the direct node way, a SPARSE node target (Douglas-Peucker on the dense nodes, 0.3 m / 7.5 m chords, 14.4 nodes per 100 m; a perfect graph from it is exactly as good: P1 oracle), with the per-cell loss, and failed its phase-3 gate. **The diagnosis of E276 7b is that the head knows where the lane is (recall 0.91 at e >= 0.3) but a per-cell loss asks it to pick one SPECIFIC cell out of about three on a straight stretch; the existence probability smears and stays below 0.5.** The critic pass measured the cause on the arm s checkpoint: in 1,030 free zones the cell with the highest existence logit is the GT cell in 37 % of the zones against 41 % by chance, and the mean logit spread inside a zone is 0.35: the head has no preference inside a zone. E277 section 6 and E276 7b named the remedy, a set-based node loss (one-to-one matching inside the loss, allowed by D55) so that a node may sit ANYWHERE along a straight lane. This card builds it and tests only whether the node head can then learn the sparse row.

**What this card does not promise.** A sparse row makes the output thin by construction and without a rule step, but the link head, the sections and the assignment are trained on the dense row; a sparse row needs its own link labels (stage 2, a separate card, only if this one passes). The perfect-network `vert100_dp` is 11.4 for any node target (P1): the geometry noise of B (28.9) is not the node grid, so the sparse row alone does not fix it. The expected payoff of the whole road is small (about one in twenty, section 3); the case for it is the node density, the missing rule step and the possibility of the old reference's thin, accurate graphs (10.9 vertices per 100 m at topo F1 0.74).

**Two readings.** **H1** the failure of arm s was the loss: with any cell of a segment allowed to carry the node, the head learns a regular placement ("one node per 4 - 7 m along a straight lane") because the winner is chosen by the head's own existence output. **H0** the head cannot do it: the segment boundaries (the Douglas-Peucker anchors) are invisible locally, so it delivers two nodes in some segments and none in others whatever the loss. **H2** (found by the critic pass) the failure of arm s was the tier switches it was trained with (E276 weights `tier_w` 3 / 1.5 / 1, `neg_w` 2 / 1.5 / 1 / 1), not the loss: the pilot's arm s runs without them to say.

**D55 status.** Matching inside the loss is allowed (D55). The winner of a segment is chosen from the detached existence output (an index choice without gradient, named like the index choices of `qlinks.py`); the loss on the winner and on the others carries the gradient. Nothing is added to the forward path; the delivered row is read at e >= 0.5 as before. No thinning step is needed or used.

## 2. Design
- **Zones** as defined above, computed from the GT in `qtargets.py` (a new function next to `node_table`, about 30 lines, tested against hand cases and against the sparse table: every zone has exactly one sparse node, every dense node is in at most one zone, the dense nodes that are in no zone are the nodes nearer to an anchor than to any free vertex, a zone's cells are connected along the piece).
- **The existence loss of arm m.** Per batch element: the positive of a zone is the cell with the largest `e_logit.detach() + 0.05 * [the cell holds the sparse GT node]` among the zone's cells (a segmented argmax; exact ties go to the lowest cell index; the 0.05 only breaks ties toward the sparse target: the critic pass found that a bias of 0.5, larger than the logit spread inside a zone of 0.35, makes the winner the GT cell in 91 % of the zones, i.e. arm s again); the target map `exists_m` has a 1 at the winners and 0 elsewhere; the focal BCE of E273 runs on it unchanged, normalised by the number of zones; the dense cells of a zone that are not the winner are negatives (hard negatives on the lane), the cells outside every zone are negatives as before. The terms for offset and direction use the winner's DENSE GT node and the winner mask; flags, section start and the long-link ends are targets of the anchors, whose zone is their own cell (their cell is the positive, as in the per-cell loss), so they are unchanged; the loss weights of E273 stay; the tier switches stay off in every arm. Logged per step: the share of free zones whose winner is not the GT cell.
- **Not changed:** the pyramid (frozen in phase 3), `NodeHead`, phases, learning rates (1e-3, 6000 steps on the GPU), the read-out at e >= 0.5, B and every stored checkpoint. `--node-loss` defaults to the E273 loss; the dense and the per-cell sparse arms run exactly as before.
- **Stage 2 (a separate card, only after this one passes):** the link labels of a candidate pair become 'the zone of u is joined to the zone of v in the sparse GT', the forced candidate list of the training uses the winners instead of the GT cells, the sections and the assignment follow the nodes; then phases 4 - 7 and the graph metrics against E279. Not designed here.
- **Alternatives not in this card:** Hungarian matching on a distance and probability cost (a stricter one-to-one, slower); a soft step with a 5 x 5 neighbourhood on the sparse target (still fixes the cell); a heat-map with peak extraction (a rule step on the output); a larger positive weight on the per-cell sparse loss.

## 6. Risks and fallbacks
| risk | effect | what we do |
|---|---|---|
| H0: the zone boundaries (the Douglas-Peucker anchors) are invisible locally | two nodes in some zones, none in others | the pilot gate stops it (pilot-level) |
| H2: the failure of E276 arm s was the tier switches | the matching loss is not needed | outcome S-PASSES, the pilot's s runs without the switches |
| the winner rule is a feedback loop (the head's own output decides the target) | the head never commits, or it commits to the tie-break bias only | the bias is 0.05; the share of winners off the GT cell is logged and gated; m5 shows the large-bias case |
| the anchors (34 % of the zones, 92 % of them event nodes) stay on the per-cell loss and are hard | the head misses events whatever the free zones do | fixed_zone_recall is gated only against s, and reported; a lever for events (a weight) is a separate card |
| free placement moves the nodes from frame to frame (flicker) | stage 2 inherits unstable links | node persistence reported next to the gate; a drop of more than 0.03 against B (0.975) is named in the summary |
| the pilot is small and on the CPU | a wrong conclusion in either direction | the validity condition on arm d; relative floors; the bootstrap; the GPU stage repeats the gate at full size before anything is built on it |
| the metrics are new | a metric bug decides the gate | promoted with tests; Layer 1 reproduces the E276 counts and the reference rows |
| a rule already reaches an exact of 0.58 (s at e >= 0.3 plus a greedy 3 m step) | the learned row is not better than a rule step | the references are written down; the GPU stage compares with the s twin at matched density; the summary names the rule alternative |
