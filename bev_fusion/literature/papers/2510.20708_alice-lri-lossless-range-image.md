**What it does**: Projecting a spinning-lidar cloud into a range image is normally many-to-one (several points per pixel, only one kept, usually the nearest) and has geometric inconsistencies, so the image cannot be turned back into the cloud. ALICE-LRI infers the sensor intrinsics from the point cloud alone (beam layout, angular distributions, per-beam corrections) so that every point gets its own pixel and the cloud can be reconstructed. The abstract names no equations.

**Results that matter** (checked, abstract): zero points lost on the complete KITTI and DurLAR data sets ("perfect point preservation"); geometric error "well within sensor precision"; "real-time"; a compression case study with "significant quality improvements"; no numbers for the last three on the page.

**Transferable technique**: a sensor-grid image is invertible exactly when the projection is injective (one point per cell) and the residual to the cell is stored. For the nuScenes radar list in a polar image: the measured steps are about 0.2 m in range for all radars and the azimuth is continuous with 1 percent / 5 percent quantiles of the spacing of 0.03 to 0.2 deg / 0.3 to 0.85 deg (radar_encoding.md section 3a); a 0.5 m x 1 deg bin is therefore coarser than the sensor's own resolution, two detections can share a bin, and the sub-bin offsets (dr, dphi) restore the position only for ONE detection per bin. The lossless variant for radar is the K-slot cell, not the lattice inference.

**Cost and fit**: not applicable; no code.

**Doubts**: abstract only; lidar with a fixed beam structure; no timing.
