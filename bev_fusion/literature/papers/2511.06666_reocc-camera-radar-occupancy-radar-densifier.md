**What it does**: Radar Densifier: a fixed formula (Eq. 1), Gaussian-weighted sharing of features from non-empty neighbouring pillars with the spread set by RCS; no learnable parameters and no loss. Radar Amplifier: an MLP with softmax re-weights the channels (Eq. 2). Both feed a camera-radar occupancy network on Occ3D-nuScenes (16 semantic classes + free). The paper names no loss; training is AdamW, lr 4e-4, 24 epochs, batch 4 on 8 GPUs. Lidar is not used, and lidar-supervised radar methods (RadarDistill, DenseRadar) appear only as related work.

**Results that matter** (Occ3D-nuScenes val, ResNet-50): mIoU +5.46 (15.02 %) over the camera-only baseline; dynamic-class mIoU +6.46, of which Densifier +1.99 and Amplifier +1.72 in the ablation; no static-class breakdown is given.

**Transferable technique**: the RCS-controlled Gaussian spreading of sparse pillars is a parameter-free densifier (compare the RCS-scaled scatter of RCBEVDet++); the observation that the radar gain is reported on dynamic classes while static classes are not shown means there is no evidence from this paper for the static part.

**Cost and fit**: both modules are tiny.

**Doubts**: the page does not say how the Amplifier is trained (presumably end-to-end); gains measured with the Occ3D camera mask and 40 m range (see OccFusion 2403.01644 on why that can hide the radar).
