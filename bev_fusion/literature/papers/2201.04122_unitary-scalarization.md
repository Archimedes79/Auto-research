**What it does** [checked]: shows that "unitary scalarization" (the plain sum of the task losses) plus single-task regularisation and stabilisation matches or beats the specialised multi-task optimisers on the benchmarks where those were introduced. Benchmarks: Multi-MNIST (2 tasks), CelebA (40 tasks), Cityscapes (segmentation + depth), Meta-World MT10 / MT50 (RL). Compared: MGDA, IMTL, PCGrad, GradDrop, RLW (random loss weighting). Regularisation used for the scalarised runs: dropout (one layer p 0.5 on Multi-MNIST, 0.25-0.5 on CelebA, one in the Cityscapes heads), L2 weight decay (grid {0, 1e-4, 1e-3} on CelebA, 1e-3 best; 1e-5 on Cityscapes), early stopping on the best average validation accuracy; RL: larger replay buffer, actor L2, raw rewards normalised per mini-batch. Argument: several specialised optimisers act partly as regularisers, which explains their small gains.

**Results that matter** [checked, text only]: on Multi-MNIST, CelebA and Cityscapes none of the optimisers clearly beats unitary scalarisation; on MT10 it is on par, on MT50 PCGrad is slightly above, not significantly. IMTL crashed on all RL runs from numerical overflow. Overheads: memory a factor m lower than most specialised methods (m tasks); CelebA PCGrad about 35x slower than scalarisation, GradDrop / MGDA / IMTL 1.05-2.4x, RLW about 5 %; Cityscapes IMTL / MGDA / GradDrop 1.6-2.3x, PCGrad about 30 % slower; Meta-World one scalarised run about 15 h against more than a week for PCGrad, MGDA, GradDrop. Nash-MTL and CAGrad are not compared (Nash-MTL is mentioned in passing; CAGrad did not appear in the text read).

**Transferable technique**: our `task_weighting="fixed"` with count-normalised terms IS unitary scalarisation. Take the protocol, not a method: before any learned weighting, the control gets its own tuned learning rate and weight decay, dropout in the heads, a validation-based stop, and the same budget; report per-group metrics, not one averaged number. The record also says to expect that a balancing method fails to beat the control on 2-3 task dense problems.

**Cost and fit**: nothing to build; it is the zero-cost arm.

**Doubts**: result numbers only in figures; at most 2 dense tasks (Cityscapes) and no detection; 2022 optimisers only (no FAMO, no UPGrad; no Nash-MTL / CAGrad); the "regularisation" explanation is an interpretation. See 2209.11379 for the same message with an explicit Pareto-front protocol and 2408.07985 for a weighting that beats plain equal weights by 2-3 points Delta_m on NYUv2.
