## 1. Problem and claim
Online mapping (lane dividers, road boundaries, pedestrian crossings) from camera images needs many labelled sequences. The paper treats the task as multi-label BEV segmentation and asks how far unlabelled sequences can substitute for labels, using a plain student / EMA-teacher scheme (mean teacher) with FixMatch-style pseudo-labels. Its own contribution is small: the teacher's predictions for a frame are combined with the teacher's predictions on a few other frames of the same drive, moved into the current frame with the ego motion, so that the pseudo-label is more complete than a single-frame prediction.

The claim that matters (abstract, digest): with 10 % of the labels, mIoU is 3.5 times the labelled-only result and 3.5 mIoU below full supervision on Argoverse 2 (AV2); adapting to a new city with unlabelled target data only closes a 5 mIoU gap to 0.5. The gain is large in the low-label regime and disappears from about half of the labels (section 3.2). Everything is segmentation; nothing about graphs, vector maps or lanes as polylines.

## 2. Method as built

### 2.1 Pipeline and architecture (Sec. 3.1, 4.1; digest)
```
 6 camera images -> ResNet50 encoder -> LSS lift to BEV -> light convolutional bottleneck decoder (Simple-BEV style)
                  -> classification head: per-cell probabilities for 3 classes (crossing, divider, boundary)
 ROI +-45 m longitudinal, +-15 m lateral, cell 0.3 m  (grid size in cells: not stated; 300 x 100 from my arithmetic)
 Metric: mean IoU over the 3 classes
```
Encoder, lifting and decoder are trained (not frozen). Variants tested only in Fig. 5 (CVT, IPM, GKT, LSS) and Table 4 (DINOv2 instead of ResNet50).

### 2.2 Teacher / student scheme (Sec. 3.2; digest)
- Student S: trained by gradient. Teacher T: EMA of the student, theta_T <- alpha theta_T + (1 - alpha) theta_S after each step. The keep rate alpha is **not given**. The teacher is not a separate, stronger or pre-trained network.
- Per unlabelled sample x: the teacher sees a weakly augmented x, the student a strongly augmented x~; the teacher output is turned into a pseudo-label phi(F_T(x)) and the student is trained to match it.
- Labelled samples train the student with the supervised loss.

### 2.3 Losses (Sec. 3.2, 3.4, 3.5, 4.1; digest)
- Total, per the paper: sum over labelled samples of l_sup + sum over unlabelled samples of (w_cls * l_cls + w_feat * l_feat). Sum or mean normalisation: not stated.
- l_sup: focal loss on the three class maps (gamma, alpha not stated). l_cls: the same loss with the pseudo-label as the target. Weight w_cls = 1.
- l_feat: similarity between teacher and student BEV features, either MSE or cosine, at an early BEV feature (z_bev) or a late one. Final choice: late features with cosine, w_feat = 0.25.
- Both weights are ramped linearly over the first third of training.
- No matching, no assignment: per-cell classification only.

### 2.4 Pseudo-label construction (Sec. 3.4; digest)
- FixMatch-style confidence threshold: a cell contributes only if the teacher probability passes the threshold; best value 0.6. Hard labels (argmax) or sharpening by dividing logits by a temperature T before the sigmoid are the two variants. Sharpening was marginal (Tables 6, 8) and the authors do not like the extra hyper-parameter.
- Whether "threshold" is applied to the probability of the positive class only, and whether cells below the threshold count as background or are masked, is not stated. Multi-label with sigmoid, so classes are independent.

### 2.5 Augmentation (Sec. 3.3; digest)
Photometric (brightness, contrast, saturation, hue, colour conversion, channel swap), CamDrop (drop one or more cameras and mask their field of view in the loss), CutOut (remove 25 % of the image), BEVDrop (drop 50 % of the BEV features). Weak augmentations of the teacher branch are not specified. Best set: photometric + CutOut + BEVDrop (Table 1); CutOut beat CamDrop.

### 2.6 Teacher fusion over samples (Sec. 3.6, 4.2; digest, the part that matters)
- Teacher predictions of past and future frames are transformed into the current frame with the known ego motion; the pseudo-label keeps "the most confident" teacher predictions among these frames. There is no equation. A per-cell maximum over the warped probability maps fits the description, but that reading is mine.
- Extra frames: sampled at random, "within a maximum range of 30 m" (spatial range, not a time window). Count: the text of Sec. 4.2 says 2 additional samples, but the caption of Table 13 is described as naming 4 as the best result, and the Table 13 numbers (below) do put 4 above 2: **text and table disagree**. Frame rate, time offsets, whether the frames are always from the same sequence: not stated ("nearby / sequential frames").
- The student sees only the augmented current frame; the fused frames only affect the target (inference from Sec. 3.2 / 3.6, not an explicit statement).
- Feature fusion (average the overlapping teacher BEV features before the head) was also tested and gave no significant benefit.
- Regions of the current frame that no warped frame covers get only the current frame's prediction.

### 2.7 Training recipe (Sec. 4.1, 4.3; digest)
Optimiser, learning rate, weight decay, batch size, labelled-to-unlabelled batch ratio, epochs, EMA keep rate, GPU type, training time: **not found**. Stated: nuScenes and AV2 use the same hyper-parameters except learning rate and weight decay (values not given). Model selection on the validation split, numbers reported on test (Sec. 4.3). Results are mean +- spread (number of seeds not extracted).

### 2.8 Data and label regimes (Sec. 4.1, 4.3, Fig. 6, Tables 14-15; digest)
- AV2: 700 labelled sequences in the full set; 2.5 % = 18 sequences, 10 % = 70 sequences. Label fractions used: 2.5, 5, 10, 25, 50, 100 %. Geographically disjoint splits (from the authors' earlier paper). The number of unlabelled AV2 sequences in the main runs is **not stated**; the unlabelled data comes from areas separate from the labelled set with the same camera setup.
- nuScenes: "all data is used as unlabelled for all runs" (Fig. 6 caption); labelled counts of nuScenes, frame rate, sequences: not found. Boston to Singapore adaptation uses a published split.
- City adaptation: labelled source cities plus 0, 25, 50, 100, 200 unlabelled sequences of the target city, kept geographically separate from the test set; how the separation is enforced is not described.
- Ground truth: rasterised vector map at 0.3 m cells; line thickness not stated.

### 2.9 Inference
Student (or teacher) forward pass; no pseudo-labelling at test time; no post-processing described. Compute (parameters, ms): not stated.

### 3.6 Caveats
- Teacher = EMA of the student, so the paper does not test a stronger, frozen or slower teacher, which is our case.
- No SSL run at 100 % labels; the benefit with all labels present plus extra unlabelled data is unknown. On nuScenes the gain at 50 % is +0.4 inside a +-2.8 spread.
- Single architecture family (LSS) for the ablations at 2.5 % and 10 %; spread is large at 2.5 %, the sweeps of Tables 5-13 are one run per cell (validation) and swing by 2 to 3 points between neighbouring settings, so only differences above about 3 points in these tables mean anything. The test-set ladder (Table 3) has smaller spreads (0.1 to 0.7 at 10 %).
- Fusion gain (+2.7 / +2.1 in Table 3) is the paper's novelty; it is about 4 printed spreads at 2.5 % (2.7 against 0.7) and 10 spreads at 10 % (2.1 against 0.2), single ladder run per row as far as seen, and it sits on top of a very strong augmentation stack; it is untested on a model with frozen features.
- Two internal inconsistencies noted: number of fusion samples (2 in the text, 4 best in Table 13) and the level of Table 13 against Tables 11-12.
- Task and metric: segmentation mIoU at 0.3 m cells, maximum range 45 m longitudinal; no graph, no connectivity metric.

### 5.2 What is agnostic and what needs a matching or rule step
- **Agnostic (no matching needed):** the EMA teacher, the weak / strong view split, the per-cell focal loss against a pseudo-label, the confidence threshold on a per-cell probability, the multi-frame warp and per-cell maximum, the schedule (linear ramp of the unlabelled weights over the first third), the feature cosine loss.
- **Needs a matching or rule step the moment the target is a graph, not a raster:** pseudo-labels as vertices / edges would be compared with student predictions by assignment, which our rule forbids. Remedy: make the pseudo-target a raster (dense lane-centre field or the dense targets of `make_dist_gt` / `make_dir_gt` kind, by file name only), train the dense head and the existing differentiable builder with their existing losses, and put the multi-frame fusion on the raster before the student sees it. Fusing teacher graphs across frames (merging vertices that coincide) would be a rule step, but it runs offline once, outside the training path (a data-preparation script, as `make_graph_gt.py` and `oof_infer.py` appear to be by name; not read).
- **Not transferable as is:** image-space augmentations and the trainable-encoder feature loss.

### 5.3 Design sketch for our setting (proposal; the card with a prediction number and rule is written before any run)
Question: does a student trained on TRAIN labels plus fused teacher pseudo-labels on the extra scenes close part of the far-range gap to the 628 ms teacher at the same 25 ms?

Pipeline:
1. Teacher = `control` (E94 trace network, trained on TRAIN only, never on VAL / TEST / extra scenes). Run on every keyframe of the extra scenes (about 1.8 h per pass).
2. Convert each frame's teacher graph into a soft raster in the student's target format: per-cell confidence, not a binary mask.
3. Fusion: warp the rasters of neighbouring keyframes of the same scene into the current ego frame with the poses; per-cell maximum of confidence (the paper's reading); take neighbours within a spatial range (the paper used 30 m; we have keyframes of about 2 Hz so a range of 30 m is a few frames, to be checked from the poses) and 2 to 4 of them (Table 13: 6 hurts). Start with all neighbours inside the range deterministically, then random sampling per epoch as in the paper.
4. Filter: keep cells above a confidence threshold (start 0.6 as in the paper; the sweep in Tables 5 and 7 says the choice is not sensitive at 10 % labels) and mask the rest from the loss instead of calling them background (a missing lane then is not punished); optional extra rule: keep a lane cell only if at least two frames support it.
5. Student training: standard TRAIN labelled loss + w * pseudo-label loss on the extra scenes, w ramped linearly over the first third of training (paper), mixed batches. Strong view: BEVDrop-like cell masking of the cached features and, where available, cached failure-variant features (dark, blur, fog); no image augmentation.
6. The teacher stays fixed (no EMA); an EMA of the student as a second teacher is a later arm.

Arms (2 seeds each, same steps and same parameter count; chain and dense head as in the champion recipe):
- C0: champion recipe, TRAIN only (null).
- C1: + extra scenes with single-frame teacher pseudo-labels (isolates the unlabelled data).
- A: + extra scenes with fused pseudo-labels (the paper's contribution).
- C2: + extra scenes with shuffled pseudo-labels (pseudo-label from another scene; must give no gain, otherwise the gain is regularisation or more steps).
- C3: equal-step control on TRAIN only (more epochs with the same schedule).
- Oracle (a sanity level, not a bound): extra scenes with real labels, if the labels can be built (see D7); this also answers whether SSL is worth the effort.
- Optional: teacher fused on the labelled TRAIN scenes too, replacing or mixing with GT, to see whether the fused teacher improves coverage where GT is incomplete (not in the paper).

Prediction (mine, a guess to be replaced on the card): A raises lanes found by +0.02 to +0.04 and R@1 far by +0.02 to +0.04 over C0 (about a fifth to two fifths of the 0.105 gap to the teacher); Q and topo F1 stay within the noise band 0.010 and 0.010; C1 is half of A; C2 gives no gain. Reasoning: the paper's fusion adds about 2 to 3 mIoU on top of single-frame pseudo-labels; the gain from unlabelled data shrinks when labels are plentiful.

Decision rule (proposal): KEEP if A beats C0 and C1 on lanes found and on R@1 far by more than the 0.010 noise band, with the paired scene bootstrap interval excluding 0 on VAL, while Q and topo F1 do not drop by more than 0.010 and false-lane metrics (FL, dup_frac) do not rise; TIE inside the band (take the cheaper variant: no extra data); INCONCLUSIVE means add a seed; CUT if C2 matches A. ms per frame must not change (the student is unchanged), so the cost of the method is data preparation only.

### 5.4 Leakage risks
- **Spatial overlap with VAL / TEST (the main risk):** nuScenes drives repeat the same roads in Boston and Singapore, and the paper's own city-adaptation result shows that unlabelled data of the target area raises the score (Table 15). Our VAL and TEST are scene-disjoint from TRAIN, but scene-disjoint is not location-disjoint. If extra scenes cover the same road stretches as VAL / TEST scenes, a student trained on teacher output for those roads gains an unlabelled-adaptation advantage on VAL / TEST that real deployment would not give. Check before use: compute the distance between ego tracks of every extra scene and every VAL / TEST scene (global poses; the nuScenes log / location field), report the share of extra frames within, say, 30 m of a VAL / TEST track, and run arms with and without the overlapping scenes; label the result as transductive if overlap is kept. This is the same issue as the paper's "geographically separate from the test set" requirement (digest).
- **Same-log overlap:** make sure no extra scene belongs to the same log as a VAL / TEST scene (the scene names against `data/splits.json`; not checked here); the official nuScenes validation scenes may sit inside parts 4-6 or inside the parts we already have (memory, not checked).
- **Teacher contamination:** the teacher must be trained on TRAIN only. If the trace network was ever fitted or selected on VAL, the pseudo-labels carry VAL information; the E94 provenance has to be confirmed from the registry.
- **TRAIN pseudo-labels:** using the teacher output on its own training scenes is over-confident (the teacher saw those labels); the out-of-fold teacher (the `oof_infer.py` name suggests one exists; not read) is the proper source for that arm.
- **TEST rule:** none of this touches TEST labels; TEST once per finished design, as before.

## 8. Novelty and relevance for our problem
The paper's own novelty is small: it applies the standard mean teacher with FixMatch-style thresholds to BEV map segmentation and adds one idea, fusing the teacher's predictions from a few nearby frames, moved by ego motion, into a more complete pseudo-label (worth about 2 to 3 mIoU in its ladder, Table 3, below the strong augmentations at 13). It matters for us because that fusion attacks the same weakness as our far-range gap: a far lane is seen near in another keyframe, so a slow, accurate teacher can label the far part of the current frame, and the student on frozen features can then learn it at 25 ms with no matching. The evidence is weaker than it looks: teacher is the student's own EMA, the gain is concentrated at 2.5 to 25 % labels and vanishes by 50 %, nuScenes gains are noisy (Table 14), everything is segmentation, and the image-space augmentations that carry most of the gain do not exist on cached features. Treat it as a cheap recipe worth one three-arm test on the extra scenes (after the leakage check), not as a promised gain.
