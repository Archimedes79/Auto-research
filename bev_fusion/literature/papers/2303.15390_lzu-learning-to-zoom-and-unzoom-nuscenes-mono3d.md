**What it does** [checked]: zoom the input with a non-uniform warp, compute spatial features on the warped image, "unzoom" the features with the inverse warp so that outputs are in original image coordinates. The warp is an invertible piecewise-bilinear map on a small grid; the experiments use the SEPARABLE variant (one monotone 1D map per axis, combined by Cartesian product; the paper says non-separable warps are no better and slower, inverting one takes about 12.6 ms). The saliency that drives it, per task (Appendix A.6): Argoverse-HD 31 x 51 grid, KDE of all training boxes ("fixed") or of the previous frame's detections ("adaptive"); Cityscapes 45 x 45, average ground-truth boundaries; nuScenes 27 x 48 grid (FWHM 10), KDE of the 3D boxes projected into the image. The fixed warp is a pure function of image coordinates, can be cached, and then costs almost nothing.

**Results that matter** [checked]: nuScenes monocular 3D detection, FCOS3D R50-FPN, 1600 x 900 images at scale 0.25 / 0.5 / 0.75 / 1 (Table 5, NDS / mAP, uniform vs LZU fixed): 21.8 / 11.4 vs 23.4 / 13.1; 27.5 / 17.5 vs 29.3 / 20.1; 30.5 / 21.0 vs 31.8 / 22.4; 31.2 / 22.4 vs 32.6 / 24.8. Latency at scale 1 on an RTX 2080 Ti, batch 1: 88.7 ms uniform, 89.4 ms LZU ("less than 11 ms" worst case). Argoverse-HD (RetinaNet R50-FPN, 1920 x 1200): at 1x AP 31.9 -> 32.6 and small-object AP 11.4 -> 13.2; at 0.25x AP 10.5 -> 12.4, AP small 0.3 -> 1.0; extra latency "less than 44 ms" (cached fixed warp: about 1 ms, 98.3 -> 99.3 ms). Cityscapes PSPNet mIoU at 128 x 128: 39.3 -> 41.7. NO distance breakdown anywhere.

**Transferable technique**: for a fixed forward camera the saliency is a static function of the image, so the warp is a lookup table. The one-line recipe for us: magnify the image rows that carry the far BEV band. The saliency does not have to be learned: the ground-plane projection of the BEV cells at 30-60 m tells which image rows they occupy (a derived prior), and the share of rows per metre of depth falls as 1/z^2. Our inference (not in the paper): in a PULL lift the unzoom step is not needed; project the BEV point to the original (u, v), apply the cached forward warp (two 1D lookups) and bilinear-sample the feature map of the warped image at that position; the footprint of a BEV cell in the warped map is stretched along the warped axis, which the depth-weighted footprint has to account for. At equal compute the table shows +2.6 mAP at scale 0.5 (17.5 -> 20.1), the size of effect to compare with the strip.

**Cost and fit**: separable cached warp: about 1 ms, no new parameters; the warp changes the aspect ratio of image features (anisotropic receptive fields for the trunk); the BEV head stays dense and differentiable (the warp is a fixed resampling).

**Doubts**: FCOS3D monocular box detection, not lanes, not BEV, no per-distance result, so "helps far objects" is inferred from the small-object AP gains; the nuScenes saliency is fitted on training boxes (dataset prior; the FOVEA paper reports that such a prior failed to generalise to BDD100K); thin lines may be distorted by a strong anisotropic warp (not tested); the canvas size and the code licence are not on the pages.
