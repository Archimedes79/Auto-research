**What it does**: Point-wise segmentation of 4D radar returns into road-boundary points, then boundary curve fitting. Own data: 50 clips of about 40 s (30,424 radar frames; 24,291 training frames doubled by horizontal flip, 3,076 val, 3,057 test), highways, urban, mountain roads. Labels were inferred from lidar point clouds with PointPillars; no manual or map-based labels are described. Boundary examples named: fences, bushes, roadblocks.

**Results that matter**: point-wise accuracy 93 % overall (94 % highway with overpasses, 91.2 % mountain roads); median Chamfer distance 0.023 m overall, 0.11 m on mountain roads; median Hausdorff 2.34 m; no F1 reported. The page shows one frame where the lidar-based ground truth missed the right boundary and the radar method found it.

**Transferable technique**: radar boundary / stationary-structure targets in the literature are lidar-built; none found uses an HD-map edge. The test of a map proxy therefore has to be done on our own data (precision / recall of the proxy against static radar returns).

**Cost and fit**: own sensor and data, not nuScenes; the PointPillars-from-lidar labelling step is not reproducible from the page.

**Doubts**: no breakdown by guardrail, curb, wall, vegetation; single private data set; no comparison of radar-visible and map boundaries (the paper says so itself by omission).
