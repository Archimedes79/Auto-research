## 1. Problem and claim
Sequence models for long sequences (language, RL trajectories, copy tasks). Classic LSTM / GRU cannot be trained in parallel over time because their gates read the previous hidden state, so training needs backpropagation through time. The paper removes that dependence (and the tanh range limit) so that the recurrence becomes linear in the state with input-only coefficients, which a parallel prefix scan evaluates in log-space. Claim that matters: the resulting minLSTM and minGRU train much faster than the originals on long sequences (the digest gives factors of 175x to 1361x per training step), use fewer parameters, and match Mamba and a Transformer on the paper's tasks. The abstract gives no benchmark number.

Everything in the paper is about length 100 to 4096 sequences; nothing is about sets, geometry, vision, or sequences of fewer than about 100 steps.

## 2. Method as built

### 2.1 Cell equations (digest; symbols: x_t input, h_t hidden state, sigma sigmoid, Linear_d = one linear layer to width d)
GRU as given in the paper (for reference):
- G1: z_t = sigma(Linear([x_t, h_{t-1}])), r_t = sigma(Linear([x_t, h_{t-1}]))
- G2: h~_t = tanh(Linear([x_t, r_t * h_{t-1}]))
- G3: h_t = (1 - z_t) * h_{t-1} + z_t * h~_t
- parameters of order 3 d_h (d_x + d_h)

LSTM (reference): f, i, o = sigma(Linear([x_t, h_{t-1}])); c~ = tanh(Linear([x_t, h_{t-1}])); c_t = f * c_{t-1} + i * c~; h_t = o * tanh(c_t); parameters of order 4 d_h (d_x + d_h).

**minGRU** (two removals: (a) the previous state leaves gate and candidate, and the reset gate r disappears with it; (b) the tanh on the candidate goes):
- M1: z_t = sigma(Linear_{d_h}(x_t))
- M2: h~_t = Linear_{d_h}(x_t)
- M3: h_t = (1 - z_t) * h_{t-1} + z_t * h~_t
- parameters of order 2 d_h d_x. With d_h = alpha d_x the ratio to GRU is 2 / (3 (1 + alpha)) (my derivation): 33 %, 22 %, 17 %, 13 % for alpha = 1 to 4, the same values the digest gives.

**minLSTM** (removals: previous state out of all gates and candidate; tanh on candidate and output out; output gate out so that h_t = c_t and the separate cell state is gone; then the two gates are normalised):
- L1: f_t = sigma(Linear(x_t)), i_t = sigma(Linear(x_t)), h~_t = Linear(x_t)
- L2: f'_t = f_t / (f_t + i_t), i'_t = i_t / (f_t + i_t)
- L3: h_t = f'_t * h_{t-1} + i'_t * h~_t
- parameters of order 3 d_h d_x; ratio to LSTM 3 / (4 (1 + alpha)): 38 %, 25 %, 19 %, 15 % (digest, same as my derivation).
- Reason for L2 (footnote in the paper, digest): with unnormalised gates the state scale can grow with sequence length; the normalisation makes the scale independent of time. Without L2 the cell is a free linear recurrence with two separate coefficients. minGRU gets the same property for free because its two coefficients are 1 - z and z.

Common: both are the linear recurrence v_t = a_t * v_{t-1} + b_t with a_t, b_t functions of x_t only (minGRU: a = 1 - z, b = z * h~).

### 2.2 Parallel scan in log-space (training mode; digest)
- The scan computes all h_t at once. Log-space version (credited to Heinsen 2023): a*_t = cumulative sum of log a (with a leading zero), then log h = a* + logcumsumexp(log b - a*), output exp(log h) without the initial entry.
- Logs need positive numbers, so the candidate is made positive: g(x) = x + 0.5 for x >= 0 and sigma(x) for x < 0, with log g(x) = log(x + 0.5) for x >= 0 and -softplus(-x) for x < 0. The hidden state and candidate are therefore positive in the paper's log-space implementation (g appears in the candidate; my reading of the digest is that the same g is applied in sequential mode so that both modes agree; the digest does not say this explicitly).
- Gate logs without evaluating sigma then log: log z = -softplus(-k), log(1 - z) = -softplus(k) for z = sigma(k). For minLSTM with k the input-gate pre-activation and p the forget-gate pre-activation, diff = softplus(-p) - softplus(-k); then log f' = -softplus(diff) and log i' = -softplus(-diff). This avoids the division in L2.
- Inference runs the plain sequential recurrence M3 / L3 one step at a time. Training uses the scan. The appendix gives about a dozen lines of PyTorch for each mode (Listings 1 to 10 per the digest, not seen).
- Implementation in plain PyTorch, no custom kernel; the authors say chunking and similar tricks from other scan models could carry over (appendix E, digest).

### 2.3 Stacking, convolution, initialisation (digest)
- Layer 1 has input-only gates. From layer 2 on, the input is the previous layer's output, which already depends on the past, so the gates become time-dependent. Table 1 shows that this matters (section 3.1).
- A temporal convolution with kernel 4 in front of the recurrent unit is used in several tasks, following Mamba and xLSTM (footnote 4); an MLP and a conv helped on ListOps (Table 6).
- Initialisation: the paper states that the default PyTorch initialisation is stable (in contrast to state-space models); the one exception is minLSTM, where a larger forget-gate bias pushes f towards 1 early and gives faster, steadier convergence (appendix D.4, Figure 5).
- Not given in the digest: learning rates, layer counts, widths, expansion factors, optimiser; the paper follows the xLSTM and Decision-Transformer configurations for the tasks. h_0 handling (positive start state in log-space) not seen.

### 2.4 Losses and training recipe
Task losses only (cross-entropy for copy and language, the Decision-Transformer action loss for RL). No auxiliary loss. Hardware named: a T4 GPU, batch 64, for the runtime study (digest). Nothing else on schedule or budget was seen.

### 3.7 Caveats
- Speed-ups are measured on sequences of 512 to 4096 steps against a sequential BPTT baseline. At a few steps there is nothing to parallelise (section 5.2).
- Quality is equal to Mamba / Transformer within spreads on small benchmarks (character-level Shakespeare, D4RL, copy tasks); the paper does not show large-scale language modelling or any non-sequence data.
- Selective copying needs three stacked layers (Table 1), i.e. depth restores what the removed hidden-state dependence takes away.
- LRU (Orvieto et al., arXiv 2303.06349): the digest found no direct LRU experiment; the related-work part describes it as a linear gated RNN with complex diagonal recurrences and a special exponential parameterisation (appendix D.1 is about the Chomsky tasks, not LRU). The point the paper makes against such models is that minGRU / minLSTM need no special initialisation distribution. LRU was not read separately.

### 5.2 Which claims matter at 5 steps (honest answer)
- **Training speed (175x to 1361x):** irrelevant. These factors come from replacing BPTT over 512 to 4096 steps. We unroll 3 to 5 frames; the sequential loop over 5 steps of elementwise arithmetic costs less than the several kernels of a cumulative-sum and logcumsumexp scan, so the scan would probably be slower (inference by me, not measured).
- **Log-space scan, positivity function g, memory +88 %:** irrelevant and partly harmful (positive-only state; extra memory). Use the plain sequential form M3 / L3.
- **Parameters and FLOPs:** true but tiny. Example (my arithmetic, d_x = d_h = 16): GRU about 3 x 16 x 32 = 1536 multiply-adds per vertex and step, minGRU 2 x 16 x 16 = 512; for 300 vertices that is about 0.46 against 0.15 million multiply-adds, microseconds on a GPU and below launch overhead. The ms per frame of the chain (24 ms with, 17.5 ms without the time recursion; PROBLEM.md) is not set by the cell; the difference is the prior from the previous frame (p16_time.py runs the network on the previous frame; the runtime file captures it in CUDA graphs). A cheaper cell does not move ms per frame.
- **Sequential-inference speed (19.6 % / 41.5 % against GRU / LSTM at batch 64, long sequences):** irrelevant at batch 1 with hundreds of vertices.
- **What does carry over:** (a) the structural idea that an input-only gate is a valid and cheap parameterisation of "how much to trust the new observation"; (b) the gate-normalisation remark (L2): our present rule is not convex, it is the unnormalised linear recurrence l_t = 0.9 l_{t-1} + 1.0 l_obs (log-odds evidence accumulates), which is minLSTM before normalisation with constant coefficients; (c) the initialisation lesson (bias the forget path to stay near 1 at the start); (d) Table 1 as a warning: with input-only gates one layer is not enough for content-dependent memory.

### 5.3 Is dropping the hidden-state dependence harmful for us? (inference)
Yes if applied literally, no if the observation side carries the match evidence.
- In a language model, "input" is the token and the state is private to the cell. In our per-vertex update the quantity that decides how much to trust the memory is the relation between the warped previous state and the new observation: do they agree in position, direction and validity, how old is the memory, how large is the pose uncertainty. A pure minGRU gate z = sigma(W f_obs) sees only the new observation, so it cannot lower the memory weight when memory and observation disagree (fork appeared, lane ended, ego pose drifted). That information is exactly what GRU's recurrent term supplied, and what `p17_gate.py` supplies through its mismatch map (mean absolute feature difference of memory and observation) and its use / valid flags.
- Resolution: nothing prevents feeding match descriptors as the input x_t of a minGRU-style cell. The cell then still has "no recurrent weight matrix on the d-dimensional state", but the gate is no longer input-only in the sense of the scan. At 5 steps this costs nothing, because we do not scan. So the useful object is "GRU cell without reset gate and without tanh, with the match evidence as input", which is a mild simplification, not a different capability class.
- Removing tanh is harmless for a logit (unbounded) and harmless for a small feature that is not re-normalised; it removes a saturation that could otherwise cap an accumulated logit.
- Removing the reset gate costs the ability to ignore the old state when forming the candidate. Our candidate is the new observation only, so there is nothing to reset: no loss.

### 5.4 Design sketch (cell on the vertex memory)
Per current-frame vertex v after association to the warped previous vertices (soft or by the existing coincidence rule; the association itself is unchanged and not part of this proposal):
- Inputs: obs feature f_v (d), obs logit l_v, warped previous state (logit lp_v, feature hp_v), match descriptor m_v = [distance to the matched previous vertex, direction difference, validity of the match, age of the memory, pose-uncertainty scalar] (5 values), all per vertex.
- Arm 0 (control): the current fixed rule, l_t = 0.9 lp + 1.0 l_obs on the logit; the present handling of the small feature.
- Arm A, pure minGRU (the paper's cell): z = sigma(W_z f_v), candidate = W_h f_v; h_t = (1 - z) * h_prev + z * candidate on logit and feature. Expected to be worse than Arm 0 on frames where memory and observation disagree (section 5.3).
- Arm B, evidence-conditioned minGRU: z = sigma(W_z [f_v, l_v, lp_v, m_v]); candidate = W_h [f_v, l_v] (unbounded); h_t = (1 - z) * h_prev + z * candidate. Convex combination: the logit cannot exceed the larger of old and new, so confidence cannot accumulate over frames.
- Arm C, unnormalised minLSTM on the logit (log-odds accumulation kept): a = sigma(W_a [f_v, l_v, lp_v, m_v]), b = softplus(W_b [same]); l_t = a * lp + b * l_obs; for the feature use the convex form of Arm B. Initialisation reproduces Arm 0: last-layer weights zero, bias of a at log(0.9 / 0.1), bias of b at the inverse softplus of 1.0 (0.5413, my arithmetic), so the untrained cell equals the fixed rule exactly (the same trick as `p17_gate.py`, whose last layer is zero-initialised and which has an "init only reproduces the hold" test).
- Output shape unchanged: logit [N], feature [N, d] per frame; the cell has about 5 x 16 + 16 x d parameters per head (hundreds), no extra memory buffer beyond the previous state.
- Training: the builder loss of frame t with an unroll of 3 frames and the memory detached before the first (same as `p17_gate.py`); for 5 steps of history unroll 5 on VAL-free folds as there.

### 5.5 Prediction and decision rule (to be placed on the experiment card before a run)
- Prediction (numbers are my guesses, to be set by the lead): Arm C and Arm B change Q by 0.000 +- 0.010 on the clean series and by at most +0.01 S on the failure series against Arm 0; Arm A is 0.01 to 0.03 worse on the failure series; ms per frame unchanged within 0.3 ms.
- Rule: use `autoresearch/tools/decide.py` with the project's noise band 0.010 in S, 2 seeds, paired scene bootstrap. KEEP only if the failure-series Q or S gain of Arm B or C over Arm 0 exceeds the band with an interval that excludes 0, and the clean half stays within the band, and ms does not rise. A TIE goes to the cheaper variant, which is Arm 0 (zero parameters). INCONCLUSIVE means add a seed. Arm A falsifies the claim that input-only gates suffice; it is a diagnostic arm, not a candidate.
- Controls to add: the existing raster gate of `p17_gate.py` as the learned-gate baseline; shuffled association (match descriptors from another vertex) to show that the cell uses the match evidence.

## 9. Novelty and relevance for our problem
The novelty is the observation that dropping hidden-state dependence from GRU / LSTM turns them into linear recurrences with input-only coefficients that a scan trains in parallel, at a third to a half of the parameters and near Mamba quality on small sequence benchmarks. For us the scan and the speed claims do not apply: we have about 5 steps, a re-indexed vertex set and a signed logit, so the cost of the cell is already negligible next to the 24 ms chain. What stays is a small design lesson: an input-gated update with an initialisation equal to the present fixed rule (0.9 / 1.0 log-odds) is a cheap learned gate, and the paper's own one-layer result (Table 1) warns that the gate must be fed the match evidence between warped memory and observation, otherwise it cannot do what a GRU does. Value for the product: low (a possible small gain in A5 on the failure series, no gain in ms, no effect on the known gaps of range coverage and connectivity); one S-effort arm at most.
