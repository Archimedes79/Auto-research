## 1. Problem and claim
- Predict, from covariates x, the whole survival curve of an individual (probability of still being alive after each time) when follow-up is incomplete (right censoring), with an ordinary neural network and plain minibatch SGD. Time is cut into n intervals; the network emits one conditional probability per interval.
- The gap closed (Gensheimer and Narasimhan): Cox-type neural survival models (Cox-nnet, DeepSurv) need the whole training set in every update because the partial likelihood couples individuals through risk sets, and an earlier discrete-time network (Brown et al. 1997) used a heuristic squared-error loss that is not the likelihood. A discrete-time likelihood is a sum over individuals and intervals, so it trains with minibatches and allows non-proportional hazards.
- Kvamme and Borgan add: the same likelihood is a binary cross-entropy over intervals ("logistic hazard"), a PMF (softmax) parametrisation is the alternative, the grid should be tuned (finer grids: less bias, more variance), interpolation and a piecewise-constant continuous-time hazard (PC-Hazard) reduce the sensitivity to the grid.
- The claim that matters for us: the log-likelihood of a right-censored individual is an exactly specified sum of masked Bernoulli terms (event interval counted, later intervals not, censoring interval handled by an explicit convention). This is the algebra of our ray verification (nu_k = 1 - h_k). Neither paper is spatial, neither tests the calibration of the product of conditionals, neither treats occlusion.

## 2. Method as built

### Pipeline / architecture (Nnet-survival; digest)
```
covariates x (SUPPORT: 39 predictors) or image (MNIST digits 0-4, 28 x 28)
  -> any network:  SUPPORT = 1 hidden layer of 7 units + L2;
                   MNIST   = 2 conv (3 x 3) -> max-pool -> FC 4 units
  -> last hidden layer
  -> flexible version:  Dense(n) + sigmoid  -> surv_pred[j] = 1 - h_j,  j = 1..n   (one output per interval)
  -> survival curve:    S_j = cumprod_{i<=j} surv_pred[i]
proportional-hazards version:
  last hidden layer -> Dense(1, no bias) = X beta -> output j = sigmoid(w_j) ^ exp(X beta)   (w_j: n free baseline weights, zero-initialised in the code)
```
Number of intervals n: 15 - 40 in most experiments, 39 (0 - 1780 days) in the one-predictor simulation, 19 (to 6 years) for SUPPORT; cut-points spaced wider at late times so that every interval holds roughly the same number of events; other authors are cited for using at least ten intervals to avoid bias; the authors report robustness to the exact cut-points.

### Inputs, outputs (digest)
- Per individual: covariates, follow-up time t, event flag. Cut-points t_1 < ... < t_n are the upper limits, intervals are left-closed and right-open, [t_{j-1}, t_j), t_0 = 0.
- Targets: two length-n vectors surv_s ("survived") and surv_f ("failed in") per individual (Eq. 9 - 12 below). Output: surv_pred[j] = 1 - h_j.
- Predictions exist only up to t_n; the authors advise extending the last interval beyond the follow-up of interest. The repository code interpolates the cumulative product linearly in time (section 4).

### Equations (numbering of the Nnet-survival paper; digest, LaTeX from the tool)
- Eq. 1 (Brown et al., heuristic, NOT used): 0.5 sum_{i=1..d_j} (1 - h_j^i)^2 + 0.5 sum_{i=d_j+1..r_j} (h_j^i)^2. Gives the Kaplan-Meier estimate d_j / r_j for a null model only.
- Eq. 2 (likelihood contribution of interval j, used): `sum_{i=1..d_j} ln h_j^i + sum_{i=d_j+1..r_j} ln(1 - h_j^i)`; r_j = individuals at risk at the start of interval j, the first d_j of them fail in j.
- Eq. 3: `S_j = prod_{i=1..j} (1 - h_i)`; h_j = P(fail in j | alive at the start of j), may depend on the individual.
- Eq. 4 / 5, event in interval j: `lik = h_j prod_{i<j} (1 - h_i)`; `loglik = ln h_j + sum_{i<j} ln(1 - h_i)`.
- Eq. 6 / 7, censored: `lik = prod_{i<j} (1 - h_i)`; `loglik = sum_{i<j} ln(1 - h_i)`, where j is the interval index assigned by the half-interval rule: an individual censored at t_c with `(t_{j-2} + t_{j-1})/2 <= t_c < (t_{j-1} + t_j)/2` is credited with survival of intervals 1 .. j-1 (second half of j-1 or first half of j).
- Eq. 8 (proportional-hazards variant, after Harrell): `1 - h_i = (1 - h_base,i)^exp(X beta)` (complementary log-log link).
- Eq. 9 / 10, uncensored, failure at t: `surv_s(j) = 1 if t >= t_j else 0`; `surv_f(j) = 1 if t_{j-1} <= t < t_j else 0`.
- Eq. 11 / 12, censored at t: `surv_s(j) = 1 if t >= (t_{j-1} + t_j)/2 else 0`; `surv_f(j) = 0`.
- Eq. 13: `loglik = sum_{i=1..n} [ ln(1 + surv_s(i) (surv_pred(i) - 1)) + ln(1 - surv_f(i) surv_pred(i)) ]`. The loss is its negative, summed over the individuals of a minibatch.
Check (own, algebra): surv_s = 1 gives ln p, surv_f = 1 gives ln(1 - p), both 0 gives nothing, so Eq. 13 = sum of masked Bernoulli log-terms, and the two label vectors are weights x targets: surv_s = w a, surv_f = w (1 - a) with weight w in {0,1} and alive-target a.

### Equations of Kvamme and Borgan used here (numbering of that paper; digest)
- Eq. 1 - 4: f(tau_j) = P(T* = tau_j), S(tau_j) = P(T* > tau_j); hazard h(tau_j) = f(tau_j) / S(tau_{j-1}); f = h S(tau_{j-1}); `S(tau_j) = prod_{k<=j} (1 - h(tau_k))`. Grid 0 = tau_0 < tau_1 < ... < tau_m. Intervals here are (tau_{j-1}, tau_j].
- Eq. 5: per individual `L_i = f(t_i)^{d_i} S(t_i)^{1-d_i}` (assumes T* independent of the censoring time C*, no shared parameters; C* <= tau_m; an event and a censoring at the same time are recorded as the event).
- Eq. 6 (PMF-form loss), Eq. 7 (hazard / BCE form): `loss = -(1/n) sum_i sum_{j=1..kappa(t_i)} [ y_ij log h(tau_j|x_i) + (1 - y_ij) log(1 - h(tau_j|x_i)) ]`, `y_ij = 1{t_i = tau_j, d_i = 1}`, kappa(t_i) = grid index of the (discretised) time. The BCE reading is attributed to Brown (1975).
- Logistic hazard: `h(tau_j|x) = 1 / (1 + exp(-phi_j(x)))`; the authors say it follows Gensheimer and Narasimhan with a more numerically stable implementation.
- PMF parametrisation, Eq. 8 - 11: m + 1 classes, `f(tau_j|x) = exp(phi_j) / (1 + sum_k exp(phi_k))`, `S(tau_m|x) = 1 / (1 + sum_k exp(phi_k))`, loss `-(1/n) sum_i [ d_i log softmax_{kappa}(phi) + (1 - d_i) log sum_{k>kappa} softmax_k(phi) ]`. Unlike DeepHit it allows survival past tau_m.
- Continuous-time part: Eq. 13 - 15 (h = f/S, S = exp(-H), `L_i = h(t_i)^{d_i} exp(-H(t_i))`); CDI: S linear inside an interval, `f = (S(tau_{j-1}) - S(tau_j)) / Delta tau_j` (Eq. 16); CHI: H linear inside an interval, `h = eta_j = (H(tau_j) - H(tau_{j-1})) / Delta tau_j`, `S(t) = exp(-eta_j (t - tau_{j-1})) S(tau_{j-1})`.
- PC-Hazard: Eq. 17 `L_i = eta_kappa^{d_i} exp(-eta_kappa (t_i - tau_{kappa-1})) prod_{j<kappa} exp(-eta_j Delta tau_j)`; Eq. 18 `rho(t) = (t - tau_{kappa(t)-1}) / Delta tau_{kappa(t)}`; Eq. 19 the network outputs `eta~_j = eta_j Delta tau_j = log(1 + exp(phi_j))`; loss `-(1/n) sum_i [ d_i log eta~_kappa - eta~_kappa rho(t_i) - sum_{j<kappa} eta~_j ]`; Eq. 20 `S(t|x) = exp(-eta~_kappa rho(t)) prod_{j<kappa} exp(-eta~_j)`.

### Step-by-step derivation of likelihood and loss (assembled from the equations above; the algebra check is own)
1. Discretise and define the conditional hazard `h_j = P(fail in j | alive at the start of j)`. Then the probability mass of failing in j is `f_j = h_j S_{j-1}` and `S_j = (1 - h_j) S_{j-1}`.
2. An individual who fails in interval j contributes `f_j = h_j prod_{i<j} (1 - h_i)` (Eq. 4). A right-censored individual contributes the probability of surviving what was observed, `S_d = prod_{i<=d} (1 - h_i)` (Eq. 6), where d is the last interval for which survival is credited (the convention, next block).
3. Taking logs: `ln f_j = ln h_j + sum_{i<j} ln(1 - h_i)`, `ln S_d = sum_{i<=d} ln(1 - h_i)`. Read each summand as a Bernoulli term (fails in interval i, given alive at its start) with target y_i = 0 for survived intervals and y_i = 1 only at the event interval: the sum over i = 1 .. kappa of `y_i ln h_i + (1 - y_i) ln(1 - h_i)` is ln f_kappa for an event and ln S_kappa for a censored individual (K-B Eq. 7). This is a binary cross-entropy on the logit of h_i.
4. Regrouping the same terms by interval over individuals gives the life-table form Eq. 2: in interval j only the r_j individuals still at risk contribute, d_j with ln h_j and r_j - d_j with ln(1 - h_j). Maximising `d ln h + (r - d) ln(1 - h)` gives h = d / r (own algebra); this is why the null model reproduces the Kaplan-Meier / life-table estimate and why the conditional labels are the risk-set labels.
5. The network supplies h_j(x) per individual. Every term depends only on the individual's own outputs and labels, so a minibatch gives an unbiased gradient (the Cox partial likelihood does not have this property). The repository sums the terms over intervals per individual.
6. The labels surv_s / surv_f implement the masks: intervals after the event or after the credited censoring interval carry neither label and drop out; the risk-set indicator is the alive-mask (all earlier intervals survived).

### Censoring-interval conventions across formulations (the question of this extraction)
Interval j means [t_{j-1}, t_j) in Nnet-survival and (tau_{j-1}, tau_j] in K-B; a censoring time strictly inside interval j is meant.

| formulation | rule | terms in the loss | where seen |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nnet-survival | interval j credited as survived iff t_c >= midpoint of j (surv_s(j) = 1), no failure label. Equivalent to rounding t_c to the nearest interval border and crediting everything up to that border (own check: the midpoint of an interval is the midpoint of its two borders, also for unequal widths). Reason given: individuals censored in the second half of an interval get credit; without it survival would be biased downward (paper, citing Brown et al. 1997) | ln p_i for credited intervals | Eq. 11 - 12; code `make_surv_array`: first half of the vector = `t >= breaks_midpoint` |
| K-B logistic hazard (paper) | a censored time in (tau_{j-1}, tau_j] is moved to the end of the previous interval, an event time to the end of its own interval; justification: tau_{j-1} is the last point where the individual was recorded alive, tau_j the first where it was recorded with the event. Quote: "censored times are moved to the end of the previous interval". Alternative justification: continuous event times inside intervals, censoring only at interval borders | BCE with target 0 for i <= kappa of the moved time, i.e. the interval containing t_c is NOT counted | K-B section 4.1 |
| pycox LogisticHazard | `LabTransDiscreteTime` (the usual entry point) wraps `IdxDiscUnknownC(cuts)`, whose `censor_side` defaults to 'left' (class docstring: censorings are moved to the left cut point; underlying class `DiscretizeUnknownC`, docstring "scheme 2"): an interior censoring time gets the index of the cut below; a time exactly on a cut keeps its index; events use side 'right'. The event interval is treated the same in all formulations: its failure term (target 1) is counted, later intervals are not. So our `prev_ok` mask (sample counts if all earlier samples were alive, the first failed one is the last counted) is exactly the uncensored case of Eq. 4 / 5, K-B Eq. 7 and `nll_logistic_hazard` with events = 1. The conventions differ only for censored rays. Our `cens=True` branch (samples outside the raster get weight 0, section 5) is the K-B / pycox "floor" convention: alive through the last in-range ring, the censoring ring and later rings not counted. It is not the Nnet-survival rule, and for point rings it should not be (section 5.1a).

### Unequal interval widths (digest)
- Nnet-survival: cut-points are free; the flexible version has one output neuron (own weights and bias) per interval, so a width is absorbed into the learned h_j; widths are not an input. In the proportional-hazards version the baseline conditional survival of each interval is a free weight. The width experiment (uniform 1 year, 1 month, 1 week, and growing widths with a 1-year half-life) gave the same C-index 0.66 in all four, i.e. discrimination did not depend on the grid (calibration was not compared across grids).
- K-B: Logistic-Hazard also has one free output per interval. For the equal-event grid they build a Kaplan-Meier quantile grid (each step lowers the KM curve by the same amount). Only PC-Hazard models the width explicitly: the network outputs `eta~_j = eta_j Delta tau_j`, so the raw widths are not needed, and rho(t) handles the partly observed last interval. The paper does not relate the logit hazard to a per-unit-time hazard when widths vary (digest: not analysed).

### Right censoring and left truncation
- Right censoring: Eq. 6 - 7 and the half-interval labels (Nnet-survival); moved-to-previous-border (K-B). K-B state that the event time T* is independent of the censoring time C* and that the two laws share no parameters (Nnet-survival states no such assumption in the text read).
- Left truncation / delayed entry, time-varying covariates, competing risks, repeated events: not treated in Nnet-survival (digest: "not stated"; the encoding of one failure interval plus survived intervals implies one event per individual without saying so). K-B treat single-risk data. pycox has `nll_pmf_cr` for competing risks (seen on the loss page, not in the paper read).
- Standard theory (memory, not seen): delayed entry is handled by dropping the terms of the intervals before entry, so that the risk set of an interval contains only individuals who have entered; this is exact for hazard models because every term is conditional on being at risk.

### Training recipe (digest)
- Nnet-survival: Keras / TensorFlow, custom loss (negative Eq. 13), minibatch SGD; Adam on MNIST, RMSprop on SUPPORT; SUPPORT: 70 / 30 split (6,373 train / 2,732 test), L2 strength by 10-fold cross-validation on the training set (log-likelihood); learning rate, batch size, epochs, early stopping not stated (1,000 epochs only in the timing test).
- K-B simulations: ReLU + batch norm + dropout, equal widths, grid search over 1 / 2 / 4 / 8 layers, 16 / 64 / 256 nodes, dropout 0 / 0.5, batch 256, AdamWR (cycle length 1, doubling, lr x 0.8 per restart), initial lr by Smith's range test, 10 repetitions; real data: details of the architecture not stated in appendix C (which only covers numerical stability: log-sum-exp for PMF, BCE for the hazard, a linear approximation of softplus for PC-Hazard).

### Inference (digest)
- Survival curve = cumulative product of the interval survival probabilities; the code reads it at a given time by linear interpolation between the border values. Nothing is thresholded or matched; no post-processing step. K-B: discrete estimates, or CDI / CHI interpolation for continuous time (the interpolation does not affect training, only test-time predictions).

Evaluation tools and what "calibration of the survival curve" means:
- Concordance (Harrell's C in Nnet-survival; time-dependent concordance of Antolini et al. in K-B): fraction of comparable pairs ordered correctly by predicted risk; discrimination only, insensitive to the grid (0.66 for all four schemes).
- Brier score (Graf et al., modified for censoring): squared error of the predicted survival probability at a fixed time against the observed status, censoring handled by weighting; mixes calibration and discrimination; the integrated version averages over time.
- Calibration plot (the only calibration evidence in Nnet-survival): at a chosen time t, group individuals by decile of predicted S(t | x), compare the mean prediction with the Kaplan-Meier estimate in the group (the Kaplan-Meier estimate takes care of censoring); the diagonal is perfect. In the simulations the check is the group-wise Kaplan-Meier curve against the mean predicted curve. So "calibrated" means: among individuals to whom the model gives survival probability s at time t, the fraction really surviving past t is s. It is a marginal statement about the product S(t), checked at a few times; it does not check the conditional hazards one by one. Neither paper reports a calibration number (no ECE, no slope).
- D-calibration (Haider et al., digest of ar5iv): for uncensored individuals S(d_i | x_i) at the observed event time should be uniform on [0,1] (probability integral transform); B = 10 equal bins, Pearson chi-square, p > 0.05 counts as passed (Eq. 9 - 12, Table 3 of that paper). Censored individual with censoring time c and S(c): the bin that contains S(c), [b1, b2), receives weight (S(c) - b1) / S(c), every bin below it receives (1/B) / S(c); the weights sum to 1 (appendix B.5, Eq. 24 - 26). Theorem B.2: expected bin shares equal the bin widths when censoring is independent of the event time and the survival curves are strictly decreasing. 1-calibration is the single-time version (Hosmer-Lemeshow on S(t*)); a model can pass one and fail the other. Not discussed in the text read: ties and discrete time grids. Weakness noted by the authors: many individuals censored at time 0 put equal weight on every bin and make a model look D-calibrated.

Caveats: no number of any of these papers is comparable with ours (medical tabular data, event times in days, hundreds to thousands of individuals); Nnet-survival has one split and no spread; the Brier differences are in the third decimal.

## 4. Paper versus code
- No code extraction is made; the pycox library is not needed (our loss is a few lines in `p16_loss.py`).
- Nnet-survival repository (github.com/MGensheimer/nnet-survival, URL given in the paper; MIT licence; Keras version for TensorFlow 2, PyTorch version in `nnet_survival_pytorch.py`; digest of the pages): `make_surv_array(t, f, breaks)` builds the 2n label vector exactly as Eq. 9 - 12 (censored: first half `t >= breaks_midpoint`; failed: first half `t >= breaks[1:]`, second half one-hot at the first interval whose upper border exceeds t; an event time beyond the last border sets no failure label, i.e. it acts as a censoring at the last border). `surv_likelihood(n_intervals)` = Eq. 13, clipped from below by the Keras epsilon before the log, summed over intervals. `PropHazards` layer: `sigmoid(kernel) ** exp(x)`. `nnet_pred_surv`: cumulative product then linear interpolation at the requested time. The clip, the interpolation and the beyond-last-border rule are not in the paper text. The README does not mention the half-interval convention.
- pycox (read only as pages): the hazard loss is BCE with logits; the censoring convention sits in the label transform / discretiser (`LabTransDiscreteTime` -> `IdxDiscUnknownC`, `censor_side='left'` default), not in the loss, which is why the loss page alone looks like "censoring interval counted". The paper text (K-B section 4.1) and the discretiser agree. `nll_pc_hazard_loss` uses softplus hazards and the `interval_frac` argument for the last interval (name on the page: `nll_pc_hazard_loss`). `nll_pmf` pads one extra logit column for survival past the last grid point (the padding helper is not shown on the page; K-B Eq. 9 fixes that logit to 0).

### 5.0 Notation and what the code does today (checked, nothing executed)
Rays of a tip: samples k = 1..K_ray at radii r_k (r_0 = 0 is the tip), gap D_k = r_k - r_{k-1}; nu_k = 1 - h_k = P(sample k verified | k - 1 verified); T_k = prod_{j<=k} nu_j, T_0 = 1; endpoint law P_k = P(last verified = k) = T_k (1 - nu_{k+1}), k = 0..K, nu_{K+1} := 0 so P_K = T_K. In survival terms the "event ring" is k + 1 and P_k = f_{k+1} = h_{k+1} S_k (Eq. 2 - 4).
- `p16_loss.level_losses`: target `tgt`; `prev_ok = cumprod([1, tgt_1 .. tgt_{K-1}])`; weight `w = pat.mask * valid * (0.3 + 0.7 on)` times `prev_ok`, times the in-range factor if `cens`; loss = sum(BCE-with-logits * w) / sum(w). One global mean over all counted terms (each term has the weight of the likelihood; a per-ring normalisation would change the objective).
- `p16_geom.survival`: v multiplied by the ring mask, `T = cumprod(v)`, `P = T * (1 - v_next)` with v_(K+1) = 0.
- Ring sets: from `Pattern("teardrop")` (reading the code): 12 rays, phi = 0, +-9, +-20, +-50, +-90, +-125, 180 deg; envelope radius r(phi) = max(4, 24 / (1 + (phi/20)^2)); samples = the shared radii (4, 8, 12, 18, 24) that are smaller than r(phi), plus r(phi) itself as last sample. So phi = 0 has 4 / 8 / 12 / 18 / 24 m (gaps 4 / 4 / 4 / 6 / 6), phi = +-9 has 4 / 8 / 12 / 18 / 19.96 m (last gap 1.96 m), phi = +-20 has 4 / 8 / 12 m, all others a single sample at 4 m: 28 real samples per tip in a padded 12 x 5 = 60 slot tensor (my count from the code, not executed; `Pattern.n_samples` would confirm it). The last ring of a ray is therefore not on a shared ring, and the output index k means different distances for different rays.

### 5.1 The recipe: (a) - (e), with the source of each statement
Tags: [paper] = in Nnet-survival or K-B; [theory] = standard survival analysis, not seen on a page; [own] = my derivation.

**(a) Loss weight with an in-range multiplier; out-of-range rings read as alive.**
- [paper] Per ray, with failure ring f (first unverified) the loss is `-sum_{k<=f} [ (k<f) ln nu_k + (k=f) ln(1 - nu_k) ] = -ln P_{f-1}` (Eq. 4 - 5; the event interval is counted); a ray verified through all rings gives `-sum ln nu_k = -ln T_K`.
- [own] General form with weights: `loss = - sum_k m_k c_k [ a_k ln nu_k + (1 - a_k) ln(1 - nu_k) ]`, `m_k = prod_{j<k} a_j` (risk-set indicator, includes the first failure), `c_k` in {0,1} the in-range multiplier (1 if ring k is read inside the raster, else 0). It equals the Nnet-survival label form with surv_s = m c a, surv_f = m c (1 - a). With `c_k = 0` for k > c (c = last in-range ring) and no failure up to c this is `-ln T_c`: the K-B / pycox censored term, i.e. "alive through the last in-range ring, no event term".
- [own] Why the floor convention and not the Nnet-survival half-credit for rings. Nnet-survival's credit repairs a bias that arises when a failure can be seen anywhere inside the interval but censored individuals are dropped from it: people who fail early in the interval stay in, people who would survive it are removed, so the hazard is overestimated. A ring is a single reading at the end of its gap. Whether ring k is in range is a function of the tip, the ray and the raster only, not of the outcome; the ray is simply not in the risk set of ring k. Dropping it is unbiased given x, and crediting ring c + 1 as alive would assert a label nobody read. K-B's own justification (the censoring point is the last time the individual was recorded alive) is the ring situation exactly.
- [own] Inference side. "Read as alive" for the product means: drop the unreadable rings from the ray (the existing ragged ring mask) and put the leftover mass at the last readable ring, `P_c = T_c`, which is the right-censoring lump (PonderNet gives the remainder to the last step, VS04). Do NOT set nu_k = 1 for k > c and keep the last ring K as the endpoint: the endpoint law would then place all mass outside the raster. Mark the endpoint at c as censored (a lane that continues beyond view) so that a downstream rule can tell it from a lane end. The present default (`cens` off, border-padded reads) corresponds to "the edge cell is a normal sample": plan notes say the verifier then learns "edge of map = lane ends".
- [own] Soft version: if a ring lies within one cell of the edge, a bilinear read is partly padded; use `c_k = fraction of the bilinear footprint inside the raster` instead of a hard 0 / 1. Not needed for a first test.
- [own] Occlusion / unobserved rings in the middle of a ray (absorbing death assumed): do not mask the chain, merge the gap. If rings o_prev < j <= o are unobserved and ring o is observed: alive at o gives `+sum_{j in gap} ln nu_j` (alive at o implies alive at the unobserved rings); dead at o (first failure) gives `ln(1 - prod_{j in gap} nu_j)`, the probability that the lane died somewhere in the gap (= S(o_prev) - S(o), interval censoring). Both terms keep every nu_j in the graph with gradient and need an observability flag as input or label.
- [own] Left truncation: a tip spawned at ring k0 of another ray is alive at its start by construction; with conditional labels nothing special is needed (terms before entry do not exist) [theory].

**(b) Per-metre hazard form for changing ring sets.**
- [paper] K-B PC-Hazard: constant hazard eta_j inside an interval, network output `eta~_j = eta_j D_j = softplus(phi_j)`, survival of the interval `exp(-eta~_j)`, partial interval `exp(-eta~ rho)` (Eq. 17 - 20). Logistic-Hazard and Nnet-survival have one free output per interval and no notion of width.
- [own] For our rays: head output `phi_k`, rate `sigma_k = softplus(phi_k)` in 1/m, `nu_k = exp(-sigma_k D_k)`, `ln nu_k = -sigma_k D_k`, `ln(1 - nu_k) = ln(-expm1(-sigma_k D_k))`, `T_k = exp(-sum_{j<=k} sigma_j D_j)` (cumulative hazard). The same sigma means the same lane-ending rate per metre on every ring ladder, so the 4 / 4 / 4 / 6 / 6 m gaps, the ragged last gap of 1.96 m of the +-9 deg rays, and a shorter or longer ladder (`reach`) share one output. Example (own arithmetic): sigma = 0.0128 / m gives nu = 0.950 on a 4 m gap, 0.926 on a 6 m gap, 0.975 on 1.96 m, T over 24 m = 0.736; equal nu = 0.95 on a 6 m gap would mean sigma = 0.0085 / m, a different hazard.
- [own] The discrete BCE is the exact likelihood here, not an approximation: a ray gives us only "alive at r_{k-1}, dead at r_k", an interval-censored event in (r_{k-1}, r_k], whose probability under a piecewise-constant hazard is `S(r_{k-1}) - S(r_k) = T_{k-1} (1 - exp(-sigma_k D_k))`. (K-B section 4.1 gives the same reading: the discrete loss also follows from continuous event times inside intervals when censoring happens only at the interval borders.) PC-Hazard's own event term `ln eta~ - eta~ rho` would be right only if the death position along the ray were observed.
- [own] Link function: nu = exp(-x) means h = 1 - exp(-x), the complementary log-log link (as in Nnet-survival's proportional-hazards variant, Eq. 8), against h = sigmoid(phi) for the logit hazard; they agree for small hazards. To keep `binary_cross_entropy_with_logits` one would pass logit(nu) = -x - ln(-expm1(-x)); computing the two log-terms directly is more stable. Gap merging in (a) becomes a sum of sigma D.
- [own] A constant sigma is an exponential length law with mean 1/sigma, the continuous counterpart of PonderNet's geometric prior.

**(c) Per-ring ECE of nu_k and what ECE of the product means.**
- [paper] Only visual: decile plots of predicted survival against Kaplan-Meier at three times (Nnet-survival Fig. 6), Brier score, and D-calibration in VS05's paper. No ECE, and no check of a product of conditionals in any paper read.
- [own] Three different objects, three populations:
  1. Conditional ECE of ring k: rows = rays in the risk set of ring k (alive through k - 1, ring k in range); label a_k, prediction nu_k; equal-mass bins (e.g. 10 - 15), `ECE_k = sum_b (n_b/n) |mean(a_k) - mean(nu_k)|` with n_k and a bootstrap interval over scenes. It tests the hazard itself. Rays outside the risk set must be excluded (a marginal "near a lane at ring k" label is a different object) and the training weights `0.3 + 0.7 on` should be reported both ways.
  2. Cumulative / product ECE at ring k: rows = all rays with ring k in range; prediction T_k; label A_k = prod_{j<=k} a_j. This is the survival-curve calibration of Nnet-survival Fig. 6 at "time" r_k. For range censoring, which is a function of x, complete cases at each ring are unbiased if the model is calibrated given x, so no Kaplan-Meier estimate is needed; with random or outcome-dependent censoring (occlusion) one would need a Kaplan-Meier or inverse-probability weighting.
  3. Law-level calibration: D-calibration of the endpoint law. Event ring E = first unverified ring, `S(m) = T_m`; randomised probability integral transform for a discrete law, `u = T_f + V (T_{f-1} - T_f)`, V ~ U(0,1) [own; Haider et al. do not treat discrete grids]; censored rays (alive through c) spread over bins as in Haider et al. appendix B.5; exclude rays with c = 0 (they put weight on every bin). It checks the whole law P(k) in one test, and the node-level validity ECE in `evaluation/loops/loop16/p16_diag.py` (5 equal-width bins, node validity against "within 1 m of a lane") is a fourth, coarser object.
- [own] Relation: if nu_j(x) is calibrated given x and the history, then T_k is calibrated. Binned (weak) calibration of each ring does not imply calibration of the product; elementary bound for numbers in [0,1]: `|prod a_j - prod b_j| <= sum |a_j - b_j|`, so errors may add up over the 5 rings. Worked example (own arithmetic): a head that says 0.95 on every ring where the truth is 0.93 has a ring-wise error of 0.02 but T_5 = 0.774 against 0.696, an error of 0.078. Report ECE_k for k = 1..5, the product ECE at each ring and the D-calibration, plus the calibration of the validity at the SELECTED endpoint (`T[k*] * prior` in `p16_geom.candidates`), because choosing k* by argmax of P is a selection on the outputs and does not inherit the calibration of fixed-k products.
- [own] What the ECE of the product means for us: the path validity `nu_val = T_{k*}` is compared with thresholds (`alive 0.3`, the final node threshold) and multiplied into later stages; a calibrated T makes these thresholds mean "probability that the chord is on the lane", a miscalibrated one makes them arbitrary numbers. Low per-ring ECE with high product ECE is the case to watch.

**(d) Expected loss over the endpoint law (PonderNet style).**
- [paper] The hazard NLL is `-ln P` at the observed endpoint (Eq. 4 - 5 with P_k = f_{k+1}): the log score of the endpoint law, not an expectation. PonderNet (VS04, digest) trains `sum_n p_n L(y, y_n) + beta KL(p || geometric prior)`, beta = 0.01, and gives the remainder to the last step. Neither is in the survival papers.
- [own] `L_exp = sum_{k=0..c} P_k l_k` with `P_k = T_k (1 - nu_{k+1})`, nu_{c+1} := 0 (censoring lump), and l_k the cost of declaring k the endpoint: position error of sample k to the downstream ground-truth set (the quantity `dist` of `level_losses`, a smooth-L1) plus a missed-length term, e.g. `lambda_short * relu(L_true - r_k)` for k before the true end. The missed-length term is mandatory: without it k = 0 (stop at once) has zero cost and `P_0 = 1` minimises the loss. A larger weight on false length than on missed length expresses "better short than false". Gradient: `dP_k/dnu_j = P_k / nu_j` for j <= k and `dP_k/dnu_{k+1} = -T_k`, so every ring j gets a signal from every candidate k >= j - 1, instead of only from the selected endpoint as in the present child loss.
- [own] Use it next to the NLL, not instead: the NLL is a proper scoring rule (its minimiser is the true law), the expected loss is linear in P and its minimiser is a point mass on the cheapest endpoint, so alone it makes the law over-confident and destroys the calibration that (c) measures. Suggested: `L = NLL + lambda L_exp`, optional KL to an exponential length law with the per-metre rate of (b). Gap to the claim "no argmax": P's argmax is used only at inference to pick k*, as today.

**(e) What breaks with dashes, occlusions, crossings: the single-event assumption.**
- [paper] The label scheme has one failure interval per individual and drops everything after it; it does not state this as an assumption (digest). The statistical model is an absorbing state: once dead, dead. Kvamme-Borgan add independence of event time and censoring time.
- [own, from `p16_loss` / `p16_geom`] Dashes (broken markings) do not change the ground-truth label (the centre line exists) but remove the evidence at a ring: the head must bridge the gap from context. If it cannot, nu drops at the gap and the ray ends early (a false short lane). `survival_tolerant` (one missing sample at factor 0.5, a max-product rule) is a heuristic outside the survival model; the principled version is the gap likelihood of (a) with an observability flag, or a three-state chain (alive, in gap, dead) [own, not worked out here].
- [own] Occlusion is a different thing from range: the lane continues behind the occluder. If the model is told "unobserved" it can mask the term and keep the chain (a); if not, it learns "occluded = lane ends". The assumption "censoring independent of the event" is doubtful: occluders (traffic) correlate with where lanes are. Camera plus radar has no lidar visibility labels (VS43), so the flag has to be geometric or predicted.
- [own] Crossings and forks: the label `tgt` is "a lane within 0.6 m whose direction is within 60 deg of the ray", not "the same lane". A crossing or merging lane that is not nearly perpendicular gives a false alive label at or beyond the true end (a revival). Directly at the true end ring it turns the failure label into an alive label there, so the hazard of that ring is under-estimated and the lane is drawn too long; further out the revival is removed from the loss by the alive-mask (rings after the first failure are never trained), so the model is never taught that a later ring can live again, and T_k cannot increase at inference either. A fork is not an event for a ray (the rays along the branches live on); a ray between two branches dies. Different terminal causes (end, fork, merge, veering off) are competing risks: not covered by Nnet-survival or Logistic-Hazard; pycox has a PMF competing-risk loss (`nll_pmf_cr`) if cause-specific endpoints are ever needed.
- [own] A lane that ends and restarts (an intersection box without centre line, a hidden stretch that really has no lane) cannot be one spell: the second part must be a new tip, which is what the link head does.

## 8. Corrections to the level-1 note (VS01)
1. Censoring convention of Kvamme-Borgan / pycox (main error). The note says they "count the censoring interval as survived". That holds for the loss line only (BCE cumsum through idx inclusive). The paper (section 4.1) moves a censored time to the end of the PREVIOUS interval, and pycox's label transform does the same by default (`LabTransDiscreteTime` -> `IdxDiscUnknownC`, `censor_side='left'`; a censoring time exactly on a cut keeps its index). Net effect: the interval that contains the censoring time is not credited; the individual is counted alive through the last grid point where it was recorded alive.
2. PMF versus logistic hazard. The note lists "PMF form excludes it (mass strictly after)" as a different convention. It is not: the PMF censored term is log S(tau_idx) = sum_{j<=idx} log(1 - h_j), the logistic-hazard censored term for the same idx. They differ in the network output, not in the censoring convention. The same three-way contrast appears in `sources/lit16/verify_sampling.md` section 3b.2 and in the cluster card.
3. Nnet-survival rule: correct as written; add that it equals rounding the censoring time to the nearest interval border (credit through that border), that intervals are [t_{j-1}, t_j) there but (tau_{j-1}, tau_j] in K-B, and that the repository implements it as `t >= breaks_midpoint`.
4. "Nnet-survival repo URL from memory, not opened": the URL is in the paper (github.com/MGensheimer/nnet-survival); opened now. MIT licence, Keras and PyTorch versions, files `nnet_survival.py` (`make_surv_array`, `surv_likelihood`, `PropHazards`, `nnet_pred_surv`), `support_study.py`.
5. Kvamme-Borgan "journal version from memory": the arXiv page lists v1 (2019-10-15) only and no journal reference; the journal version remains unchecked.
6. "PMF slightly worse and less stable across grids": the paper text says PMF generally trails Logistic-Hazard by a small margin (and that interpolation makes results more stable); "less stable across grids" was not seen. In Table 2 PMF has the higher concordance on SUPPORT (0.627 against 0.625) and NWTCO (0.710 against 0.704); Logistic-Hazard leads on FLCHAIN, METABRIC, Rotterdam / GBSG. The difference is small either way.
7. "Calibration shown only qualitatively": confirmed (decile plots at 6 months, 1 and 3 years, Fig. 6; no number). The SUPPORT numbers (C 0.732; Brier 0.181 / 0.184 / 0.177; Cox PH 0.734; 0.183 / 0.186 / 0.178) and the K-B numbers quoted in the note (Tables 2 and 3: 0.792 / 0.658 / 0.625; PMF 0.786 / 0.632 / 0.627; IBS 0.0918 / 0.172 / 0.213) agree with the pages read.
8. "Tried in our experiments: none" is out of date: the alive-mask hazard BCE is the default verification loss in `p16_loss.level_losses` (K41, M4), the `cens` option exists (default off), and E132 compares the survival read-out with a one-score baseline (section 7).
9. "Fit to our project" says the convention at the BEV edge or in occlusion "must be fixed". My analysis (section 5.1a, own) points to a single choice for ring samples at the raster edge: floor (mask the unreadable ring, keep the chain, put the leftover mass on the last readable ring); occlusion needs the gap likelihood and a flag. Unequal gaps are handled exactly by the per-metre form; and the ring ladder of the +-9 deg rays ends at 19.96 m, not at 24 m (section 5.0), so the "shared rings" are not shared by every ray.
10. Level-1 note VS05 (not asked, short): its "from memory, unverified" definition of D-calibration is confirmed by the digest of the ar5iv text (uniform S(d_i | x_i), 10 bins, chi-square, censored individuals spread over the bins by a stated rule, assumes independent censoring); ties on discrete grids are not treated there.
