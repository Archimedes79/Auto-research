**What it does** [checked]: re-runs MGDA, GradNorm, PCGrad, IMTL and RLW (random loss weighting) against plain scalarisation (weighted sum, proportional sampling) at scale: neural machine translation (Pre-LN Transformer, 3 + 3 layers, about 120 M parameters; En->Fr/Zh, En->Fr/De, En->Fr/Ro), Cityscapes (7-class segmentation + depth, ResNet-50 shared encoder) and CelebA (40 attributes, about 200 K images). Claim: multi-task optimisers give no improvement beyond what traditional optimisation (a well-chosen scalarisation) reaches.

**Results that matter** [checked]: NMT and Cityscapes: the methods land on the scalarisation Pareto front, never beyond it; on Cityscapes the scalarisation front is the test front and the specialised solutions are significantly below it; for En->Ro/Fr the best test trade-off is the scalarisation with weights (0.3, 0.7). CelebA average error (lower is better): scalarisation 9.23 test / 8.73 val, MGDA 9.53 / 9.24, PCGrad 9.35 / 8.89 (their own runs); earlier papers' scalarisation baselines vary from 8.71 to 9.99, i.e. baseline tuning moves the number as much as the method does. A sparse learning-rate grid gives a spread 6-7x the seed standard deviation, so seeds alone do not make a comparison. Rankings change with the weighting used at evaluation. Larger models shift the front towards better values (3L-3L to 12L-12L; steps/s 11.52 to 5.48). The paper recommends exploring the scalarisation weights ("more reliable and effective") but notes grid search is expensive and efficient search is open.

**Transferable technique**: the evaluation protocol for any balancing arm (Kendall, UW-SO, FAMO, UPGrad): (1) tune the learning rate on a log grid per arm, (2) compare at several scalarisation weight vectors (at least the fixed 1:1:1:1 and one hand-set weighting such as the BEVerse-style up-weighted map), (3) judge the whole trade-off between groups, not one scalar, (4) do not call a difference real inside the learning-rate-grid spread.

**Cost and fit**: costs only runs: with four groups a full grid is out of reach on one 3060, so use 3-4 hand-picked weight vectors on quarter-length runs (the same trick as the T search in 2408.07985).

**Doubts**: NMT-dominated; CAGrad, Nash-MTL, FAMO are not in the text read; Cityscapes only 2 tasks; the paper itself notes it has no efficient search for scalarisation weights.
