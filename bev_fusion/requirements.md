# requirements - what the camera + radar BEV system must satisfy

Set by Mirko. Every design and every code change is checked against this file; a change that breaks a requirement is not accepted. Only Mirko changes or removes a requirement (a session may propose one, marked as a proposal). Each requirement has an id, its statement, its source (date and the words it came from), how it is checked (a gate in `tests/`, a rule, or a review) and its status:
`met` = a gate passes; `by construction` = the code allows it, no gate yet; `open` = not checked or not known; `violated`.

## 1 Camera geometry (Mirko, 2026-10-30)
> "the camera projection must use values from calibration data. The projection should also still work, if the camera is rolled, thus the method should build on top of a general camera projection. This shall not be learned. And not rely on idealized virtual cameras."

| id | requirement | how it is checked | status (2026-10-30) |
|---|---|---|---|
| R-GEO-1 | **Calibration drives the projection.** The mapping between image and vehicle coordinates uses the calibration of the camera as input: intrinsics K, extrinsics (vehicle to camera) and the 2D transform applied to the image, per sample. No camera constant (focal length, mounting height, pitch, image size, a dataset's scale or crop) is hard-coded in a block; dataset constants live in the data adapters only. | the lift takes `K`, `T_cam_car`, `ida` as arguments (`training/lift_pd.py build_table`, `fallbacks.build_s_table`), one calibration per sample (`s1_camera.lifted`, `model.camera_bevs`); `lift_pd.py` self-test: a camera 1 m forward shifts the BEV by exactly 2 cells; `tests/test_projection_general.py` A | met for the lift. Open: a window of frames shares the calibration of its first frame (static mounting assumed); dataset constants (`camera_encoder.nuscenes_front_ida`, the 256 x 704 size) sit in adapters and the DualBEV preprocessing |
| R-GEO-2 | **General projection.** The method builds on the general pinhole projection x ~ K [R \| t] X with any rotation (roll, pitch, yaw), any position, fx != fy, an off-centre principal point and skew; a camera rolled about its optical axis works without a special case. (Images are assumed rectified, as nuScenes' are; a lens-distortion model would be an added, calibrated, non-learned step: not built.) | `tests/test_projection_general.py`: A the table of every lift voxel equals an independent 3 x 4-matrix projection (roll up to 35 deg, pitch 12, yaw 20, fx != fy, skew, an image transform with 10 deg rotation, scale and crop; image position and depth coordinate, error below 1e-4 px and 1e-4 m); C an image rotated by 90 deg together with its calibration gives the same table; B the oracle lift on rendered images with exact depth for roll 0 / 20 / -25 / 35 deg at a level and at a pitched, yawed, moved pose ranks the hit cells at 0.96-0.99 (lanes) and 0.76-1.00 (vehicles), no more than 0.15 below the same pose without roll | met for the projection geometry. Open: (1) the thresholded lane recall of the stride-8 oracle falls from 1.0 to about 0.6 with roll while the ranking stays at 0.97 (the mass spreads differently over axis-aligned 8 x 8 pixel groups): a representation effect, to keep in mind for far thin lines of a rolled camera; (2) the pre-trained image heads (DualBEV depth net, camera-aware through a 27-value vector) were trained on six near-level nuScenes cameras: their depth for a rolled or much higher camera is untested; (3) lens distortion |
| R-GEO-3 | **Not learned.** Which image position and depth a 3D point corresponds to is computed from the calibration, never learned. Learned parts decide what is at those positions (features, depth and height distributions). *Clarification (proposal, Mirko to confirm):* which 3D points are sampled (for example the height levels) may be fixed or learned; how a sampled point projects is fixed. | `tests/test_projection_general.py` D: the lift tables (PD, P, S) have no parameters, and with learned heights switched on `z_levels` is the only one | met |
| R-GEO-4 | **No idealised virtual cameras.** No virtual upright, level or zero-roll camera, no re-rendering of the image into a canonical view, no assumed camera height or horizon. Resizing, cropping, rotating or flipping the image (input size, augmentation) is allowed only as an explicit 2D transform folded into the projection. | by construction: no virtual camera in the code; the draft with the virtual upright camera (`plan_draft2.md`) was dropped on 2026-10-25; the image transform is the `ida` argument of the table (tests A and C); review rule for every new lift or encoder | met (by design) |

## 4 Goals of the first message (scope; to confirm or strike)
BEV grid at 0.5 m; the transform encodes distance uncertainty (lift PD keeps the depth distribution); temporal fusion for moving and non-moving targets and for lane markings; radar encoded with sensor direction, Doppler, RCS and position; a pre-trained image backbone is allowed; a semantic segmentation head and an object detection head; later an SDF decoding into 3D with a reconstruction loss in the image. Decisions taken since: front camera first, 60 m range, no horizon strip for now (a strip stays a later option, plan 2.7), lift PD as the first fixed building block (its value against the plain pull is the S1a experiment).
