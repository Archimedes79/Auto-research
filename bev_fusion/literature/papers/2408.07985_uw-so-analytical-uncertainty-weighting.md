**What it does** [checked]: Kendall's uncertainty weighting has the closed form sigma_k = L_k at its optimum (shown for L1 loss), i.e. weights proportional to 1 / L_k. UW-SO uses exactly that with a stop-gradient on the current batch losses and normalises with a softmax of temperature T: w_k = softmax_k( (1 / sg[L_k]) / T ), total = sum_k w_k L_k. The weights sum to 1 and cannot blow up; no learned parameters, no EMA, no extra pass. T is the only hyperparameter, searched coarse to fine; the best T is visible after about a quarter of the training (so a short run decides it).

**Results that matter** [checked; Delta_m % over single-task models, lower is better, mean +- std over seeds; "Scalar" = brute-force grid search of fixed task weights]: NYUv2 (3 tasks), ResNet-50: equal weights (EW) -7.1 +- 0.4, UW -7.2 +- 0.2, UW-SO -9.8 +- 0.2, Scalar -9.7 +- 0.2, IMTL-L -7.7 +- 0.3, DWA -6.6 +- 0.3, RLW -6.6 +- 0.3. ResNet-101: EW -10.1, UW -10.4, UW-SO -12.3, Scalar -12.5. Cityscapes (2 tasks), ResNet-50: EW 5.3 +- 0.7, UW 0.5 +- 0.8, UW-SO 0.3 +- 1.3, Scalar 2.1 +- 1.4, IMTL-L 0.7 +- 0.9. CelebA (40 tasks, ResNet-18): EW -2.4, UW -0.1, UW-SO -4.0, IMTL-L -1.2. Tuned T: 20 / 28 / 48 / 22 (Cityscapes, four architectures), 3 / 2 / 3 / 2 (NYUv2), 100 (CelebA). The authors also report: larger networks shrink the effect of the weighting method; the learning rate matters much more than weight decay [checked]. Gradient-based methods (PCGrad, CAGrad, Nash-MTL, FAMO) are not in the benchmark.

**Transferable technique**: a bounded, parameter-free-at-run-time weighting of the four groups, `w = softmax((1/L.detach())/T)`, `total = (w * L).sum()`. Together with the finding that Kendall's own optimum is the same 1/L rule, it tells what our clamp [-3, 1] on s_g does: it caps the 1/L weight (e^-1 .. e^3). T has to be found on our scale: T ranges from 2 to 100 across benchmarks, so it depends on the size of the losses; our terms are count-normalised (order 1), so start with a log-spaced search T in {1, 2, 5, 10, 20} on a quarter-length S1 run.

**Cost and fit**: zero extra passes and memory [checked: weights from detached batch losses]. Fits four groups trivially. Watch: weights follow 1/L, so a group whose loss has an irreducible floor (thin lines with registration noise) gets a LOW weight while an easy group that is already near zero gets a high one; add a floor on w_g (the same role as our clamp) and log the weights per group.

**Doubts**: only 2-3 task dense benchmarks (NYUv2 / Cityscapes) and CelebA, SegNet/MTAN/ResNet backbones, no detection or BEV; no code found; no seed variation of T search reported on our kind of loss (focal + Dice + Huber); the "Scalar" reference is a grid search the authors ran themselves; per-batch losses are noisy at batch size 2-4 (the paper uses per-batch values, no EMA), which may matter on a 3060 with small batches.
