**What it does** [checked]: learns one weight lambda_k per task by a bilevel step: the network is updated on the lambda-weighted training loss, lambda is updated by the validation loss of the primary tasks through a finite-difference approximation of the second-order term. One theta update and one lambda update per iteration; training and validation batches come from the same dataset (no separate split); lambda starts at 0.1 with a hand-picked learning rate per dataset; a random subset of K tasks is used per lambda update, so memory is constant in the number of tasks. Also used for auxiliary learning (help one primary task).

**Results that matter** [checked; here Delta MTL is "higher is better", the opposite sign convention of the Delta_m % tables elsewhere]: NYUv2 (3 tasks): equal +3.57 %, DWA +4.58, uncertainty +6.50, Auto-Lambda +8.21; with gradient methods: PCGrad +5.09 with equal weights, +8.50 with Auto-Lambda; CAGrad +7.05, +11.07. Cityscapes: equal +0.92, uncertainty +0.86, Auto-Lambda +2.56. Tested up to CelebA (40 tasks), CIFAR-100 (20), RLBench (10).

**Transferable technique**: the idea that task weights can be driven by the validation loss of the primary task (our primary groups are lines and objects; areas and motion could be auxiliaries). Too costly in this form.

**Cost and fit** [checked]: "only two forward passes and two backward passes" extra per approximation step; about 3x the training time of equal weighting on CelebA; the first-order variant about 2.3x faster; the paper calls it about an order of magnitude cheaper than PCGrad / CAGrad (no timings given). On a 3060 with a long camera + radar + temporal stack this triples a run.

**Doubts**: the licence forbids commercial use (this project's use is not decided; do not copy code); needs a validation signal in the loop; 3 dense tasks at most.
