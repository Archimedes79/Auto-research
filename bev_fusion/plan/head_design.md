Removed 2026-11-10 as dead (never in a run): `w_radar_slot`, the splat correction (`CorrectionHead`, `w_correction`), `area_overlap` "semimetric", `line_boundary`, beta-NLL.

## 2. What the loss supervises today (the masks)

All masks come from layers of the GT dict (`autogt`): `undefined` (neither sensor), `occluded` (behind a GT box seen from the camera), `camera`, `radar`, `ignore` (a box without any lidar or radar point).

| weight | map areas and lines (`w_map`, `w_lines`) | objects and velocity (`w_obj`) | camera aux (`w_cam`) | radar aux (`w_radar`) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| rule today | 0 on `undefined`, 0.5 on `occluded`, x registration weight on lines | max(camera and not occluded, radar), x 0 on `ignore`, x 0.5 on interpolated labels | camera and not occluded | radar |
| with `fusion_area_only` (D38 (b) first step) | x fusion area (camera x radar) | x fusion area | unchanged (own sensor area, D36) | unchanged |

On 40 real frames the option lowers the mean map weight 0.878 -> 0.546 and drops 46.6 % of the supervised object centres (369 -> 197): the objects beside the car, outside the camera's view, are supervised by the radar alone today (`../experiments/t14_dontcare/results/fusion_area_real_gt.txt`, `tests/test_fusion_area_real.py`). The option does not reach the refiner's box mask (`losses.refine_gt_boxes`), so `startable.py` refuses it together with `model.refine`.

In every row a cell that is "unknown at the target time" (splat mass < 0.5, dt != 0) counts 0.5. An object whose centre cell has weight 0 or whose box is unobserved is **ignored**: its heatmap peak region gets a constant logit, so it is neither a positive nor a negative.

## 6. D43 (2026-11-02): Mirko's directions and answers to his questions

His message and my reading are recorded as D43 in `../../bev_fusion_lane_center/experiment_execution/decisions/`. What each point means for the heads:

1. **Centre-line GT (sparse and dense with splits and merges).** The lane project already has the sparse one: `bev_fusion_lane_center/training/data/prep/make_graph_gt.py` builds per sample the lane and lane-connector polylines (0.5 m, nuScenes map arcs) with their `incoming` / `outgoing` links, the directed edges, splits (a piece with >= 2 successors) and merges (>= 2 predecessors), in its own window (x right, y forward, +-50 m); its metric is `evaluation/sparse_lanes/graph_eval.py` (split / merge F1 at 3 m). autogt has the dense side only (mask, one averaged direction, the polylines without tokens or links). To close it: port the graph builder into autogt in the BEV frame (x forward 0..60 m, y left +-26 m), keep the arc tokens and links, and derive the dense fields from the graph: distance field, direction bins with several modes per cell, split / merge heat maps. The lane project's metric then scores the same graphs.
2. **The radar aux head.** It is a training-only side head on the radar branch's BEV (not on the trunk features S): objects on the radar cells plus a stationary-structure channel with no target. Its job (D38 (a), Mirko: "a test and debug tool"): show that the radar branch keeps the information. The final network does not need it, and no stored run compares aux on / off. In the incumbent its loss is about 5 % of the total (0.65 of 12.5 at step 1000 of `t11_vel_hurdle_acc2`, by difference from the logged groups, Kendall weights and total), but its gradient share is not logged.
3. **Forecast.** The data is there: autogt generates every keyframe, and the GT cache of a token holds cur / prev / next. Only the sample builder (`autogt.training_sample`, `losses.SIDE_DT`) stops at +-1 keyframe. dt = +-1.0 s is the keyframe k +- 2: add `prev2` / `next2` from the cache entries of the neighbouring tokens (no regeneration), add +-1.0 to `SIDE_DT`; the loss weight (1.0: 0.25), the time mover and the ego steps of a longer gap already exist. Longer sequences (the clips of 3 keyframes) already contain k + 2 for their first frame.
4. **The registration weight.** The nuScenes map is drawn in the localisation frame and sits 0.3 - 1 m beside the paint in places (Boston worst, Singapore mostly inside the noise); the GT layers inherit it, boxes do not. Mirko's rule of 2026-10-26: stationary GT never moves per frame; a correction is allowed only per map element in world coordinates (`map_offsets`). So a registration estimate can only act as a loss **weight** per frame (`w_reg` in [0.2, 1], lower where the estimate says the map is off): the loss reads `meta["w_reg"]` if it exists, nothing makes it. Today the loss tolerates the offset instead (dilated Dice, flat Huber region). The lane project's graph-ICP (`training/data/prep/reg_precompute.py`) registers a disturbed map to the extracted lane graph, for the map-pose noise of its route head: another question (map against our prediction, not against the paint), so it does not seed this estimator. The cheapest first step is to measure how much the tolerance already absorbs before building one.
5. **Dense outputs are a main goal.** The first losses on them have to be matching-free: (a) the soft line map against the GT line cells (soft Dice on `soft_line_map`), which ties the distance field and the mask together; (b) for the dense boxes, a peak-weight map against the GT centre heatmap plus the box parameters at the weighted cells (the CenterPoint loss, read from the dense output instead of the raw maps), so that one set of tensors is trained and delivered. A card with a prediction comes before the code.
6. **Aux heads must not dominate.** Proposal (not built): (i) the aux heads read **detached** branch features, so they diagnose and cannot shape the branches (D38 (a): "probes on detached features"); (ii) their weights stay outside the Kendall groups but are logged with the gradient share they get, so a dominating head is visible; (iii) an optional decay to 0 over the run, as the radar slot encoder's side loss already does (`radar_aux_floor`); (iv) off in the final end-to-end arms. (i) and (ii) are small; (iii) and (iv) are config. Nothing is built while the training stop of 2026-11-01 holds, and (i) changes training, so it enters as an option, default off.

### 7.1 The rule for a loss in the end-to-end system
A loss makes sense when (a) its output is read at inference or by the metric, (b) it supervises what its input can know (D36: no camera loss where the camera cannot see, no radar loss where the radar cannot see), and (c) a stored comparison pays for its cost. Side losses (aux heads, the radar reconstruction) are scaffolds or probes: they may start the training, they are not goals, and they decay or are off in the final arms.

| loss | verdict | why, with the evidence |
|---|---|---|
| areas: BCE | keep | the probability of each cell |
| areas: 0.5 x (1 - soft IoU) | test dropping | about half of the areas cost (10.7 -> 5.4 ms forward + backward per dt on the CPU); the rare layers (crossing, carpark) may need it |
| lines: dilated Dice on the mask + Huber on the 2 m distance field | keep both | **both are read**: `bev_task` scores the line mask in the area meters (headline `lane_divider_iou`, `road_divider iou@1`) and the distance field for the zero-crossing F1 and the field error; the lines family is the most expensive map loss (14 ms per dt; Dice and Huber are not timed apart). A cheaper variant (distance field only, headline read from it) is a test, not a verdict |
| dense objects: BCE + IoU | keep for now | decision (2) keeps it as free space for the planner; the suite scores `vehicle_iou` (`BevSegMetric.HEAD`), only BOARD.md omits it |
| object head: focal + offset, size, rotation, radius, velocity, direction, moving | keep | standard CenterPoint terms, all read by the metric; the velocity L1 (weight 1.0) is 0.34 of the object group in the first fifth of the `t20_base_s0` steps and 0.14 in the last fifth (dt 0 only against the dt-weighted group: not like for like) |
| motion field L1 | keep until the temporal redesign (D51) decides | with the hurdle the object head's velocity works; the dense field still feeds the time mover |
| camera aux head, radar aux head | off in the end-to-end arms (D38 (a), D43) | together 19 - 25 % of the loss time (CPU ranking, two runs); their stationary channel has no target; a diagnostic probe on detached features if wanted |
| radar reconstruction (`radar_aux`, delta slots) | scaffold only | it shows that the encoder keeps the information, not that the network uses it; the weight goes 1.0 -> 0.05 asymptotically (half-life 0.2 of the run: 0.08 at the end of 6000 steps) |
| Kendall task weights | keep, but look at the clamp | the learned weights at 6000 steps are far from 1: `t20_base_s0` s = (-0.08, 0.47, 0.94, -0.62), weights exp(-s) = (1.09, 0.62, 0.39, 1.86); t16 / t17 / t18 give objects 0.38 - 0.39 and motion 1.5 - 1.9. The 3000-step runs (t10, t11) ended near (1.0, 0.95, 0.6, 1.2). **The objects weight sits at the upper clamp s = +1** (0.94 - 0.96): the clamp holds it. Test: s up to 2, or fixed 0.3. The t6 maps-only run (objects 0, motion 0 and the radar aux head off) gained +0.015 ... +0.051 map IoU at 3000 steps, inside its 0.054 band and called a minor factor in its own report |
| dead options (`w_correction`, boundary, semimetric, UW-SO, consistency x2, beta-NLL, refine loss, old centre-line terms) | remove from the main path | none is in the incumbent; D43 puts refinement into its own stage. Done 2026-11-10 for `w_correction`, boundary, semimetric and beta-NLL (deleted); UW-SO and the two consistency terms stay (`train.py` uses them), the refine loss and the old centre-line terms are in use |

### 7.2 The radar branch: what the stored runs show
- **The radar is used, and for velocity it is the main source.** Same recipe, 3000 steps (t2): camera + radar mAP2 0.339, camera only 0.210 (-0.129), walkway -0.084, drivable -0.028, lane divider -0.017; radar alone mAP2 0.062. Removing an input from the trained `t20_base_s0` (6000 steps; relative change of the stage output): the radar removed changes the velocity field by 0.72, the fusion output by 0.43, the maps by 0.10, the objects by 0.078; the Doppler alone removed: velocity 0.61, fusion 0.18, maps 0.018, objects 0.012; the camera removed: fusion 0.84, maps 0.25, objects 0.25, velocity 1.92. In the 400-step `t16_small_s0` the same probe gave velocity 0.22 for the radar: its role in the velocity grew with the training.
- **No strong sign of neglect in the gradient either, with a caveat.** Median `grad.<module>` of `t20_base_s0`, steps 5000 - 6000: radar 1.15; camera reduce 3.33 (radar/this 0.35), camera reduce 2 2.58 (0.45), fusion 1.64 (0.70), camera temporal 1.30 (0.89), head reduce 1.08 (1.06). Update-to-weight: radar 2.0e-5 against camera reduce 3.1e-5, fusion 4.2e-5, trunk 3.9e-5, camera temporal 4.6e-5 (0.44 - 0.64) but head reduce 2.1e-5 (0.96), lane 2.3e-5 (0.86), camera merge 1.7e-5 (the radar is faster); every module's update-to-weight falls about 40 x over the run (cosine schedule). The radar's gradient fell 27 -> 20 -> 6.8 -> 1.15 over the four stages of the run (steps 20 - 500, 500 - 1500, 2500 - 3500, 5000 - 6000) while the camera reduce fell 6.4 -> 3.3. **Caveat:** `grad.radar` is the gradient of the TOTAL loss: it contains the radar reconstruction side loss (weight 1.0 at the start) and the radar aux head (0.3). Mirko's concern that the early radar gradient is the reconstruction is plausible and untested, because no per-loss gradient split is logged.
- **Where the radar should matter and may be under-used:** pedestrians' velocity (skill 0.014 in B2), objects at range or occluded, and robustness when the camera fails (radar alone: mAP2 0.062). The maps are mostly camera information, but the radar still helps them (walkway -0.084 without it), through the fused features and without any radar-specific map loss.

### 7.3 What keeps the radar from becoming the neglected brother (ranked; each is an experiment, none is built)
1. **Measure first, per run.** The two instruments exist: `probe: true` (the gradient and update per module) and `response.json` (the zero-radar and zero-Doppler response). The numbers of 7.2 are the baseline. Missing: the gradient per loss term, so that the reconstruction can be told from the task gradient (the loss audit of the open list).
2. **Modality-aware masks in the dropout samples.** `fusion.drop_modality(cam, rad, p)` zeroes the camera planes of p/2 and the radar planes of p/2 of the samples (p = 0.2: 10 % each) and the loss stays the same, so a camera-dropped sample is asked for lane lines that only the camera can know. Mask the map terms of camera-dropped samples (D36's rule applied to the dropout), keep objects and velocity. The radar then gets a clean task gradient where it is the better sensor, and the camera-failure case is trained. An asymmetric drop (more camera drops) needs a code change: the function takes one p.
3. **A Doppler consistency loss.** At every radar detection the predicted velocity (object or field) projected on the ray must give the ego-compensated radial velocity: `|u_ray . v_hat - v_r|` on non-static detections. It uses the sensor's physics, needs no GT, and rewards use of the Doppler, where the reconstruction only rewards keeping it. The velocity already depends on the Doppler (0.61); the expected gain is in the weak spots (pedestrians) and in robustness.
4. **Rate and scale of the branch.** `TrainConfig.lr_groups` exists: the radar module at 2 x the learning rate evens its update-to-weight against the fusion (0.48); a per-branch norm before the concat keeps the sparse radar features from entering the fusion smaller than the camera's. Config-level; weaker evidence than 2 and 3.
5. **The reconstruction stays a decaying scaffold,** it is not extended (7.1).
Not recommended: a radar loss on the map outputs.
