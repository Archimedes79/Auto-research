**What it does**: Proposes a spatial-temporal fusion on top of CenterPoint. Its ablation compares, with the same detector, a single frame, "data fusion" (the points of several frames concatenated directly, time lag as a point attribute), "feature fusion" and its own ST-Fusion.

**Results that matter** (checked, Table III and Table VI, nuScenes val, CenterPoint): mAP / NDS: single frame 57.28 / 65.58; data fusion 58.86 / 66.43; feature fusion 59.79 / 67.48; ST-Fusion 60.48 / 68.32. Number of frames k for data fusion: NDS 65.58 (k = 1), 66.19 (2), 66.43 (4), 66.07 (8): it saturates at 4 and falls at 8. The authors attribute the weak data fusion to "spatial misalignment caused by the object motion". The page does not describe an ego-motion step for the data fusion (the time lag is listed as an attribute), and reports no latency.

**Transferable technique**: for objects that move, concatenating older points without a motion correction saturates after a few sweeps and falls behind feature-level fusion by 1.0 NDS; the correction that radar has and lidar has not is the Doppler (shift of each old point along its ray by the dynamic radial velocity times dt, 2508.12330). So: point-level alignment must include the Doppler shift, and the number of sweeps is a design variable with an optimum (here 4), not "as many as possible".

**Cost and fit**: not reported.

**Doubts**: lidar; the "data fusion" arm may be uncompensated for ego motion (not stated) which would exaggerate the gap; one detector, one split, no seeds or confidence intervals stated on the page read.
