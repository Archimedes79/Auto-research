**What it does**: a dual-resolution BEV encoder for 3D occupancy (Occ3D-nuScenes, single frame). For the lift it studies the depth target: the usual one-hot LiDAR depth puts all mass on the first visible surface along the ray; the authors soften it with a Gaussian kernel along the depth axis (sigma = 1.5 bins, normalised to sum 1) and finally drop the depth loss altogether.

**Results that matter** (Occ3D-nuScenes val mIoU, ablation without the spatial enhancer): one-hot depth supervision 35.96; no depth supervision 36.92; Gaussian sigma 1.5 36.94. Final model 37.37 mIoU (ProtoOcc 37.04, FlashOcc 32.08).

**Transferable technique**: a one-hot target on the minimum LiDAR depth costs about 1 mIoU against no depth loss in a task where the BEV is supervised densely; a Gaussian along the depth axis removes the damage. For our soft-target fine-tune of the DualBEV depth head (plan 10.1) the cheap order of trials is: Gaussian-smoothed per-block histogram (sigma 1-2 bins) vs raw block histogram vs none, judged by the oracle test and the lane recall at 40-60 m.

**Cost and fit**: a one-line change of the target (a 1-D convolution of the one-hot along D); needs LiDAR depth for any supervised variant.

**Doubts**: occupancy (volume) is more forgiving of a smeared depth than a thin road surface; a difference of 0.02 mIoU between none and Gaussian is a tie; the numbers are from one run; no code.
