# Camera + radar BEV system - plan for discussion (draft 3, 2026-10-25)

Status: DRAFT for the discussion with Mirko; nothing is decided. Draft 2 (front camera, virtual upright camera, sigma points) is in `plan_draft2.md`; this draft replaces it after Mirko's comments of 2026-10-25:
- the problem is the real world, nuScenes is only one dataset: the design takes the sensor set, the calibration and the timestamps as inputs, never as constants of one dataset
- radar: a learned distribution per detection, f(x, y; detection, r(x, y; calibration), phi(x, y; calibration), time), not sigma points
- camera: frustums (rays of pixel groups) in the perspective space instead of a virtual upright camera
- every block founded on papers and their code; the ground truth observable; a test for every step

Paper ids are arXiv ids; "(lit)" = from a scout note in `literature/papers/` (all ids checked on their abstract pages, 2026-10-25). Code licences from `literature/code/front_bev_code_sources.md` (scout, read on the repository pages).

## 0. The problem, not the dataset
- Product: a BEV environment model for a vehicle with any number of cameras and radars at any mounting: semantic map layers (road, markings, lanes), objects, later detection, at 0.5 m, fast.
- Input contract (the only thing the system knows about a sensor): its model (pinhole / fisheye intrinsics, radar field of view and resolution), its pose in vehicle coordinates (calibration), the timestamp of each measurement, and the ego motion. Nothing is tuned to one calibration.
- nuScenes is the first training and validation set (front camera + five radars as the first rig; all six cameras are on disk once the download finishes). A second dataset or a changed rig is the test that the design does not depend on nuScenes.

## 1. One principle for every sensor
Every measurement becomes a probability mass over world positions, written in the sensor's own measurement space (where its uncertainty is simple), mapped by the sensor's model and calibration into vehicle coordinates, evaluated at the BEV cells it can reach, and added into one BEV grid. The measurement spaces differ (Mirko, 2026-10-25: a camera projection is not a polar system):
- camera: a perspective (projective) space - image coordinates (u, v) plus depth z along the optical axis; the model is the projection (pinhole: x = K [R|t] X; fisheye: its own unprojection). A pixel group is a rectangle in the image, so its preimage is a rectangular frustum (pyramid) from the camera centre, not a circular cone, and its angular size changes across the image (smaller towards the edges for a pinhole).
- radar: a polar space - range r, azimuth phi (elevation unknown for 2D radars), plus Doppler; the model is the polar-to-Cartesian map of the sensor pose.

| | measurement space | what the sensor knows well | what it does not know | what is learned |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| camera pixel group | perspective: (u, v) region x depth z | the image region (a narrow frustum) | the depth along it | the distribution over depth |
| radar detection | polar: (r, phi) + Doppler | the range (and radial velocity) | the azimuth (wide), the height | the spread around the detection |

Time is part of the position: each measurement is moved to the output time t_ref before it is evaluated (exact for static content, by Doppler for the radial motion of moving content, otherwise as a wider spread).

```
cameras ──> backbone + FPN ──> pixel-group frustums ──> learned depth distribution ──> frustum-slab splat ──┐
                                                                                                           ├──> fusion ──> temporal state ──> BEV trunk ──> heads
radars ──> time alignment ──> point encoder ──> learned density f(cell; detection, calib, dt) ──> splat ───┘
```
Block diagrams of draft 2 were shown in the chat; the drawing will be redrawn for this draft after the discussion.

## 2. Camera: image backbone + lift through lookup tables, height kept as channels (Mirko, 2026-10-25)

Mirko's proposals are the two standard lift families of the literature; both are built, on the same backbone and the same output interface, and compared.

### 2.1 Common parts
- Image backbone, ImageNet-pretrained (ResNet-34 default; ResNet-18 / EfficientNet-B0 as speed variants), FPN to one feature map at stride 8 of the half-resolution image (decided: 60 m, no horizon strip).
- Output interface of every lift: a voxel feature volume V[x_car, y_car, z_car, C] in vehicle coordinates - 0.5 m cells, x 0..60 m, y -26..26 m, z_car from -1 to 3 m in 8 slices of 0.5 m - then height into channels: reshape to BEV[x, y, Z * C] and a 1 x 1 conv to C ("feat[z_car]"; Simple-BEV 2206.07959 and Fast-BEV 2301.12511 do this; FlashOcc 2311.12058 is the same reshape in the other direction).
- All geometry is a lookup table computed once from the calibration (intrinsics + extrinsics in vehicle coordinates), rebuilt in milliseconds when the calibration changes; the network only reads the table.

### 2.2 Lift P ("pull", backward): world points at elevations, projected into the image
1. For every BEV cell (x, y) and every elevation z_k: the 3D point (x, y, z_k) in vehicle coordinates is projected into the camera with the calibration -> image position (u, v) on the feature map, plus a valid flag (in front of the camera and inside the image).
2. Lookup table: for every voxel (x, y, k) its (u, v) - a sampling grid of size 120 x 104 x 8.
3. Run time: bilinear sampling of the feature map at those positions (grid_sample) -> V[x, y, k, C]; then height into channels.
4. Learned elevations (Mirko): the 8 heights z_k are learnable parameters (initialised at the slice centres); the projection is then recomputed in every forward pass (a 100 k-point matrix product, negligible), so the gradient moves the heights. A later variant: per-cell height offsets predicted by the network (the deformable reference points of BEVFormer 2203.17270, without its attention stack).
- What it does not know: depth - a feature is copied to every voxel on its ray; the stack of heights and the trunk must sort out where along the ray the content is (radar helps most here).
- Basis: OFT 1811.08188 (orthographic feature transform, the first pull lift), Simple-BEV 2206.07959 (parameter-free bilinear sampling at multiple heights, height reshaped into channels; argues that resolution and batch size matter more than the lift; camera + radar 55.8 vs camera 47.6 mean IoU on its README (digest)), Fast-BEV 2301.12511 (the table as a gather: view transform 0.006 ms on CUDA at 256 x 704; uniform fill along the ray), BEVFormer (learned sampling offsets).
- Code: aharley/simple_bev (MIT) - the closest match, readable plain PyTorch; Fast-BEV (licence not stated: ideas only).

### 2.3 Lift S ("splat", forward): each feature pixel along its ray, with Mirko's intermediate coordinates
1. For every feature pixel (u, v) and every depth bin d: the point in camera coordinates is P_cam = z_d * K^-1 [u, v, 1] = (x_n z_d, y_n z_d, z_d) - the "stretched" coordinates (x_cam * z_cam, y_cam * z_cam, z_cam); then P_car = R P_cam + t with the calibration, and its voxel index (ix, iy, iz) in the volume above, plus a valid flag.
2. Lookup table: for every (u, v, d) its voxel index.
3. Run time: the network predicts per feature pixel a feature c (C channels) and a depth distribution alpha over the D bins; each (u, v, d) adds alpha_d * c into its voxel (scatter-add with the precomputed index, no frustum tensor kept: the BEVPoolV2 idea in plain PyTorch); then height into channels.
- Depth bins: D bins over 1-60 m (linear-increasing width, CaDDN 2103.01100); depth supervision possible later (lidar, not downloaded; BEVDepth 2206.10092: the depth loss alone +2.2 mAP) and a camera-aware gate on the depth head (BEVDepth: intrinsics / extrinsics through an MLP as a squeeze-excitation gate - needed for "any rig").
- Basis: LSS 2008.05711 (frustum points, categorical depth, sum pooling; nuScenes: vehicles 32.07 vs OFT 30.05 IoU, drivable 72.94 vs 71.69, lane boundary 19.96 vs 18.07), BEVDepth (camera-aware depth, depth loss), BEVPoolV2 2211.17111 (precomputed indices, no frustum tensor).
- Code: BEVDepth (MIT) for the depth head and its camera-aware gate; LSS (NVIDIA research-only licence: re-implemented, never copied); our own scatter-add with the table.

### 2.4 Lift PD ("pull with depth weight"): keeps the uncertainty and is a pure gather (recommended, Mirko 2026-10-25: "what maintains uncertainty and is fast")
| lift | keeps the distance uncertainty | inference cost | gaps at far range |
|---|---|---|---|
| P (pull, Simple-BEV) | no: the feature is copied to every voxel on its ray | one gather per voxel | none (every voxel samples) |
| S (splat, LSS) | yes: a depth distribution per pixel | scatter-add over pixels x bins (atomics); fast only with a custom kernel (BEVPoolV2 0.82 ms at 640 x 1600, GPU not named) | yes: one voxel per (pixel, bin), far voxels between rays stay empty |
| PD (pull with depth weight) | yes: the same depth distribution as S | two gathers per voxel and a product | none |
1. The image head predicts per feature pixel a feature c (C channels) and a depth distribution over the D bins (categorical, as S; it can be supervised like BEVDepth).
2. Lookup table per voxel (x, y, z_k): its image position (u, v) AND its depth along the optical axis z_cam, expressed as a fractional bin index - pure geometry from the calibration.
3. Run time: V(x, y, z_k) = c(u, v) * p(z_cam | u, v) - bilinear gather of the feature and linear interpolation of the depth probability at the voxel's own depth. Then height into channels.
- The depth distribution is the explicit distance uncertainty: a voxel receives the feature only as far as the pixel believes its content lies at that depth. A multi-modal distribution (road behind a car top) is kept.
- Cost for one camera: 120 x 104 x 8 = 100 k voxels x 64 channels, about 6 M multiply-adds plus the gathers - well below a millisecond (estimate, to be measured), no scatter, no custom CUDA, easy to export (TensorRT has grid sample).
- Learned elevations (Mirko's proposal) work unchanged: the table is then recomputed per forward pass from the learned z_k.
- Parametric variant PD-G, in INVERSE depth (Mirko, 2026-10-25): the depth distribution per pixel as a mixture of 2-3 Gaussians in u = 1/z_cam (e.g. ground, object, far / sky with mu_u near 0). Why 1/z: monocular cues are linear in 1/z (a ground point's row offset below the horizon is f h / z, an object's image size f S / z), so their noise is about constant in 1/z and grows as z^2 in z. Measured on the loaded DualBEV depth head (`temp/setup/check_inverse_depth_std.py`, 40 nuScenes mini front images, median per depth group): std_z 0.19 m at 2-5 m to 9.95 m at 30-40 m (a factor 52 over the groups), std_u 0.0057-0.0116 1/m (a factor 2.0). The 40-60 m group is truncated by the head's last bin at 60 m.
  - voxel weight: the exact mass of the mixture over the voxel's depth extent, Phi((u_hi - mu)/sigma) - Phi((u_lo - mu)/sigma) with u_lo, u_hi = 1/(z + d/2), 1/(z - d/2) precomputed in the table (no Jacobian to handle, no aliasing between bins, cheap: erf per voxel and component)
  - not uniform bins in 1/z over 1-60 m: 64 such bins would spend most of them at 1-3 m and make the last bin about 55 m wide; the mixture avoids the choice; if bins are needed, log-spacing or 1/z-uniform from about 4 m
  - first version needs no training: the moments of the pre-trained categorical give mu_u and sigma_u per cell (unimodal); a small head then learns the mixture (multi-modal: road behind a car top)
  - gains over the categorical: continuous (no 0.5 m quantisation, no cut at 60 m: far mass stays a tail), 3-9 numbers instead of 118, a smooth gradient for learned elevations, and an explicit sigma for the fusion: sigma_z = z^2 sigma_u of the camera against the radar's about constant sigma_r, so camera-near / radar-far follows from the uncertainties
  - basis (from memory, to verify): inverse-depth parametrisation of monocular SLAM (Civera, Davison, Montiel, IEEE T-RO 2008), disparity outputs of self-supervised depth (Monodepth / Monodepth2), inverse-depth hypothesis sampling in multi-view stereo; GaussianLSS 2504.01957 for variance as the footprint.
- Basis (scout of 2026-10-25, ids checked; all published numbers are 3D detection on nuScenes val, none is a segmentation or map result):
  - DualBEV 2403.05402 (ECCV 2024), HeightTrans: fixed heights per cell projected, feature sampled bilinearly and multiplied by the depth probability (trilinear sample) - the closest published form of PD. Sampling operators on a BEVDet base, one RTX 3090: depth-weighted sampling 30.6 mAP / 39.0 NDS at 0.32 ms vs unweighted pull 30.5 / 37.6 at 7.15 ms vs FB-BEV's attention version 29.5 / 36.6 at 12.5 ms. Code Apache-2.0.
  - FastBEV++ 2512.08237: the pure-gather form, Gather(feature) x Gather(depth prob), TensorRT-native without a plugin; R50 0.478 -> 0.488 NDS with the depth term; 134 FPS INT8 on a Tesla T4 (whole network). Code Apache-2.0 (built on BEVDet). The page is partly inconsistent.
  - FB-BEV 2308.02236 (ICCV 2023): the exact weight at a fractional depth by interpolating the two adjacent bins; depth-aware vs not +0.8 to +1.1 NDS; view transform 2.6 ms at 128 x 128 BEV (GPU not named). NVIDIA licence, non-commercial: re-implement, never copy.
  - DFA3D 2307.12972: the depth-weighted aggregation without a frustum tensor, 29 MB and 5.2 ms vs 3204 MB and 161.9 ms for the naive volume (GPU not named); with lidar depth the lift gains +15 mAP (supports the oracle test of 2.6). IDEA licence, non-commercial: re-implement.
  - PointBeV 2312.00703: the depth-free sparse pull on map classes (lane 49.6 IoU), MIT, plain PyTorch - a reference for lift P and for per-camera visibility masks.
  - the pieces: LSS / CaDDN / BEVDepth (depth head), Simple-BEV / Fast-BEV (gather table), GaussianLSS and FisheyeGaussianLift 2511.17210 (variance as the footprint, ray tables for any camera model).
- Gap: no published depth-weighted pull is evaluated on map layers or with a single front camera - our S1 is the first such number.

### 2.5 Which one, and what the literature says
- In the literature the families are close for segmentation (LSS vs OFT: +2.0 vehicles, +1.3 drivable, +1.9 lane boundary for the splat); Simple-BEV argues that the parameter-free pull is enough once resolution, batch size and radar are right.
- Plan: S1 compares PD (recommended), P (no depth: the null of the depth weight) and S (splat: the classic) on the same backbone, trunk, head and budget; PD-G and learned elevations as variants of PD; the cheaper wins a tie.
- Shelved for later (draft 3): the frustum-slab footprint (exact area weights instead of one voxel per point) and the ground hypothesis; they are refinements of S and come back only if S wins and far range is the weak point.

### 2.6 Tests (what it must do -> how we prove it)
| requirement | test |
|---|---|
| the tables are geometrically right | synthetic: a point at a known (x, y, z) in vehicle coordinates; P: its voxel's (u, v) must equal the projection of the point; S: the pixel of its projection at its true depth bin must index its voxel; for every calibration and for random synthetic rigs (roll, pitch, yaw, height, other intrinsics) |
| P, PD and S agree on geometry | a one-hot depth and a feature map that is 1 at one pixel: S's voxel must be the voxel where PD is non-zero, and one of those where P samples that pixel |
| the depth weight is right | PD with a uniform depth distribution must equal P / D; with a one-hot depth at a known bin only the voxels at that depth receive the feature |
| rig independence | move the camera 1 m forward in the calibration and shift the scene: the BEV moves 2 cells, otherwise identical; rotate the camera with a synthetic image rendered for that pose: same BEV |
| the lift can carry the truth (oracle) | S with one-hot depth from a known depth (lidar if downloaded, else rendered synthetic scenes) and the GT semantics of the image: the lifted BEV must reproduce the GT layers; this is the upper bound of the lift |
| learned heights learn | the gradient reaches z_k (non-zero, finite); on a synthetic scene with content at one height the heights move towards it |
| differentiable | torch.autograd.gradcheck on small tables; the gradient reaches the depth head and the backbone |
| fast | ms per frame per lift, batch 1, idle GPU; Fast-BEV's 0.006 ms gather as the reference for P |

## 3. Radar: a learned density per detection (variant; the first version is the simple block of section 12 "Decided": hard raster + properties + 2 convs)

### 3.1 What it does, in plain words (your formulation)
For each detection d with position (x_d, y_d) in vehicle coordinates, its own sensor calibration c_d (radar position and direction in vehicle coordinates) and its time t_d:
1. Time alignment: the sensor pose is expressed at the output time t_ref (ego motion between t_d and t_ref applied to c_d); this moves static detections exactly to where they are at t_ref. dt = t_ref - t_d is kept.
2. Support: the BEV cells around the detection (a window in the sensor's range / azimuth, e.g. +-3 sigma of the sensor's nominal accuracy).
3. For every support cell (x, y): its range r(x, y; c_d) and azimuth phi(x, y; c_d) relative to the (moved) sensor - pure geometry from the calibration.
4. A small network gives the weight of that cell: w_d(x, y) = softmax over the support of g(r(x, y; c_d) - r_d, phi(x, y; c_d) - phi_d, r_d, e_d, dt), with e_d the detection's embedding (RCS, Doppler, static residual, rms fields, sensor type). The detection's feature, possibly modulated by the same inputs, is added to those cells with these weights; the total mass of a detection is 1 (or a learned confidence).
5. Doppler: the radial part of the motion is shifted by v_r dt along the line of sight; the tangential part is unknown, so g gets dt and the moving probability and can widen in azimuth for moving returns. The Doppler constraints (u u^T, v_r u) are added with the same weights into the A, b channels.

The function g works in the sensor's polar frame, so it learns range-like and azimuth-like spreads; because the polar coordinates are computed from the calibration, the same g serves every radar at any mounting.

| requirement | test |
|---|---|
| geometry right | synthetic radar at a random pose: a detection at (r_d, phi_d) must put its mass centre at the right cell; same detection seen by two radars at different poses -> same cell |

## 4. Fusion (after the temporal fusion of each branch, at camera time; Mirko, 2026-10-25)
- Main: concatenation of [camera planes (t_c); radar planes moved to t_c; their mass and dt channels] -> 3 x 3 conv (the BEVFusion ConvFuser style); modality dropout; radar-only auxiliary heads so the radar branch must learn on its own.
- Fallback 1 (proven camera-radar fusion, GaussianCaR, Apache-2.0, `literature/code/gaussiancar_fusion.md`): its FeatureRectifyModule - per-channel and per-cell sigmoid cross-gates between the camera and radar maps (about 30 lines of plain torch) - instead of the plain concat; our mass / dt channels as an extra input of the gate as the ablation.
- Fallback 2 (strong reference, CRN, MIT, `literature/code/crn_fusion.md`): deformable cross-attention between camera and radar BEV (multi-modal feature aggregation), ported with grid_sample.
- Basis: BEVFusion-style concat + conv (lit, SN20 of the lane project), GaussianCaR CMX fusion (lane divider 50.1 / drivable 82.9 IoU on nuScenes val, 75.6 ms whole network on an RTX 4090), CRN (fusion 6.8 of 44 ms); comparison table `literature/code/fusion_blocks_comparison.md`.
- Tests: camera off -> radar-only result, radar off -> camera-only result (no collapse); shuffled radar (from another frame) -> the gain over camera-only disappears (null control); gate maps plotted over range (radar should dominate far, camera near).

## 5. Sensor timing
- Every measurement has its own timestamp and ego pose (nuScenes: per sample_data; a real vehicle: per message). Output time t_ref = the newest camera image (or a fixed output clock).
- Static: exact pose change between t_meas and t_ref. Moving: radial part by Doppler, the rest as uncertainty (3.1). Camera images from several cameras with different times: each camera's frustums use its own pose at its own time.
- Basis: BEVDet4D 2203.17054 (lit: alignment matters, unaligned concat is worse than no history), RadarNet (lit).
- Tests: the time-alignment tests of 3.3; a camera test with a synthetic static scene and a time offset; latency budget per block.

## 6. BEV network after the fusion (standard flow, compute first)
- Grid around the vehicle (front-only first: x 0..60 m, y -26..26 m, 120 x 104 cells; full surround, also to 60 m, once all cameras are used), 0.5 m; coordinate channels (x, y, range, azimuth per sensor coverage) appended to the fused map.
- Flow (the standard of the best camera(-radar) BEV networks - BEVDet, BEVDepth, CRN, DualBEV all use the same `CustomResNet` + `FPN_LSS`; code `training/bev_trunk.py`, layer names as in the DualBEV checkpoint):
  1. fused map (c_in channels, 80 for the pre-trained shape) at 0.5 m, 120 x 104
  2. ResNet pyramid, 3 stages of two BasicBlocks, stride 2 each: 160 w channels at 60 x 52 (1 m), 320 w at 30 x 26 (2 m), 640 w at 15 x 13 (4 m)
  3. FPN_LSS decoder: the 4 m stage upsampled x4 and concatenated with the 1 m stage, 3x3 conv, 3x3 conv (512 w), upsample x2, 3x3 conv (256 w), 1x1
  4. semantic head: 3x3 conv BN ReLU + 1x1 to the classes at the full grid (section 8)
- Compute, measured as FLOPs (conv / matmul, one sample, hardware independent; `temp/checks/trunk_flops.py`, `chain_flops.py`; 2 x multiply-adds):

| T0 standard trunk, width 1 (DualBEV shape) | 100.9 | 27.3 | backbone 17.3, FPN neck 68.8, head 14.8; the 98 tensors of the DualBEV BEV encoder load exactly (0 missing / unexpected / mismatched) |
| T0 + light up-sampling | 74.7 | 26.6 | the full-resolution 3x3 conv replaced by a sub-pixel conv |
| T1 width 1/2 | 25.2 | 6.8 | |
| **T2 width 1/2 + light up-sampling (default)** | **18.7** | 6.7 | |
| T3 width 1/4 + light up-sampling | 4.7 | 1.7 | speed variant |
| T2 + stride-1 detail path (32 ch) | 21.3 | 6.8 | +2.6 GFLOPs; two 3 x 3 convs at 0.5 m on the trunk input, concatenated before the head (`detail=32`) |
| T3 + stride-1 detail path (32 ch) | 6.4 | 1.7 | |

  For the whole chain (one camera): ResNet-50 backbone 29.4, neck 4.4, DepthNet 35.6, lift PD + output conv 2.1 = 71.4 GFLOPs (the pre-trained camera heads, frozen), splat heads 0.2 + 0.1 per branch and step. So the trunk decides the total: T0 gives about 172 GFLOPs, T2 about 90.
  Milliseconds on the RTX 3060 are NOT measured yet: the GPU is shared with the lane project's training jobs (the indicative fp16 numbers of 7-16 ms are an upper bound dominated by that load); timing needs an idle GPU.
- Decision (compute first): T2 is the default - the standard flow at half width with the cheap up-sampling, 5.4 x fewer FLOPs than the original trunk; the FPN neck (two 3x3 convs at 512 channels and the full-resolution conv) is where the original spends its compute and where the width and the light up-sampling save it. Arms of the first trunk experiment: T2 (default), T3 (speed), T0 initialised from the pre-trained DualBEV BEV encoder (an ablation: it was trained for detection on a different grid and costs 5.4 x more, so it must earn it).
- Fallback (proven camera-radar trunk, GaussianCaR, `gaussiancar_fusion.md`): its 4-stage two-stream fuser (FeatureRectify + cross-attention fusion at each of 4 scales, stride-2 convs + ResBlocks between them) and DPT-style decoder - fusion inside the pyramid instead of once before it; used if a fused map in front of a single-stream trunk loses to it.
- Basis: BEVDet / BEVDepth / CRN / DualBEV BEV encoders (`literature/code/dualbev.md`, `crn_fusion.md`), GaussianCaR fuser + DPT head (`gaussiancar_fusion.md`), BEVFusion / CVT decoders (lit), CenterPoint trunk (lit, MIT).
- Stride-1 detail path (Mirko approved 2026-10-26; BEVTrunk(detail=32), DetailPath): the pyramid's first stage is already stride 2, so the 0.5 m output is rebuilt from 1 m features, and lane dividers are one or two cells wide. ## 7. Temporal fusion (ego motion, stationary environment, moving targets)
### 7.1 Main design T2: temporal fusion per branch, one splat function (Mirko, 2026-10-25)
- camera branch, own time axis (frames t_k, 12 Hz): past camera BEVs -> current camera time t_c; radar branch, own time axis (sweeps t_j of all radars): past detections / radar BEVs -> radar time t_r (the newest sweep), radar memory and radar-only aux heads there; then the radar BEV hops from t_r into t_c; finally [camera planes (t_c); radar planes (t_c); mass and dt channels] are concatenated -> fusion convs
- three prediction steps, all with the same splat (ego speed v and yaw rate omega as inputs: a vehicle reports them, for nuScenes `ego_speed_yawrate()` derives them from consecutive poses):
  1. vision: the camera temporal state of the previous camera frame -> t_c, dt_v = t_c - t_c,prev (about 1/12 s)
  2. radar: the radar temporal state of the previous sweep -> t_r, dt_r = t_r - t_r,prev (per radar sweep; or per detection at point level)
  3. radar to vision: the radar temporal state at the newest radar time -> t_c, dt = t_c - t_r,last (either sign)
  each branch then fuses its own current planes with its predicted state (concat + conv; ConvGRU as the variant), the result is the new state of that branch and feeds the next frame (recurrent, frame by frame)
- block diagrams: the splat function and this chain were shown in the chat of 2026-10-25 (to be saved as the architecture drawing of the S4 card)
- fallback for the temporal part (proven): FIERY's ego warp (pull by the SE(2) of the poses, `literature/code/fiery_ego_warp.md`) + a ConvGRU per branch = arm A
- one function for all three moves (`training/splat.py`): v_hat = f_theta(F(x, y)) - a per-cell MLP as 1 x 1 convs with 2 outputs (object velocity, m/s, source axes; last layer zero-initialised, so training starts as pure ego compensation; bounded by v_max tanh); position p' = R(-omega dt) (p + v_hat dt - d), d = (v dt, 1/2 v omega dt^2) - ego speed v and yaw rate omega (what a vehicle reports), translation first order in omega (no division by omega), rotation exact (one cos / sin per frame); dt either sign
  - accuracy (`temp/setup/check_ctrv_taylor.py`, static points up to 60 m): a first-order ROTATION would cost distance x (omega dt)^2 / 2 - chained over camera frames 0.30 m in a city turn (8 m/s, 0.5 rad/s, 0.5 s), 1.24 m in a tight turn; with the exact rotation the rest is of order v omega^2 dt^3 (millimetres per frame); long histories are carried frame by frame
- the splat: forward warp of each source (grid cell or radar detection) to the 4 cells around p', bilinear, normalised, optional importance z (softmax splatting: the more important source wins a collision); mass = sum of the kernel weights is the confidence channel (0 where nothing landed)
- v_hat is supervised with the GT velocity per cell (CRT-Fusion's recipe: box footprints, ground velocity in the source axes, 0 below 1 m/s, loss on object cells) and fine-tuned end to end through the splat (the bilinear splat alone only gives a position gradient within one cell); the radar head sees Doppler and the A, b channels, so it mainly learns the tangential part
- self-test of splat.py: exact 2-cell shift for 1 m of ego motion, landing point = formula (centroid exact) and within v omega^2 dt^3 of the exact constant-turn-rate motion, object velocity, forward-then-back, mass conservation, collision by importance, gradcheck, the velocity head learns, a moving blob lands at x + v dt (without v_hat it lags)
- learned correction (Mirko, 2026-10-25): p' = R(-omega dt)(p + v_hat dt - d) + delta, delta = d_max tanh(g_phi([F; v; omega; dt; v dt; omega dt; x; y]) / d_max) - `CorrectionHead` in splat.py, 1 x 1-conv MLP, zero start, d_max = 1 m (the range in which the bilinear splat gives a position gradient); meant for small corrections (acceleration, odometry / time-stamp / model errors)
- synthetic checks (2026-10-25, `temp/checks/check_splat_scenes.py`, `temp/checks/train_splat_correction.py`; objects with their own velocity + ego motion, expectation from the exact motion):
  - ONE splat is right: straight 15 m/s over 0.5 s exact (0.000 m, IoU 1.00); turning 10 m/s, 0.4 rad/s over 0.5 s: cars 0.02-0.09 m, lane lines 0.07 m; radar -> vision hop (0.04 s) 0.08-0.21 m; backwards in time (-0.3 s) 0.02-0.15 m
  - CHAINED splats of a map (6 frames, 0.5 s, no new observation in between) smear and lag: moving objects 0.4-0.8 m (turning) with importance 6; without importance 2-3 m (the velocity, being a feature, is averaged with the background at the edges, so edges slow down); stronger importance (30) or a weight = occupancy fatten or do not help; static content chains well (lane lines 0.00-0.10 m)
  - the learned correction did NOT fix chained lag (40 held-out scenes, 155 moving objects): chained 0.74 / 0.67 m (mean / median), chained + correction 0.83 / 0.81 m, each frame splatted once from its original 0.14 / 0.09 m (IoU 0.63 vs 0.23); 400 training scenes, the 1 x 1 head sees no spatial context, and a position offset cannot undo the blur of repeated bilinear resampling - an honest negative of this setup, not a proof that no learned correction can help
- consequence for the design: chain the COORDINATES, not the maps - the per-frame transforms (each with its own v, omega, dt) are applied to the point positions one after the other and the source is splatted ONCE from its original; moving content: a window of the last K original frames (features and their own v_hat), each splatted once with its accumulated motion into the current time; static memory may stay recurrent (ego only chains well); radar: detections splatted from their original measurements; the correction delta stays as a small in-cell term of that single splat; a learned temporal fusion (conv / GRU) refreshes the state with each new observation, which this prediction-only test does not have
- coordinates as appended channels (Mirko's idea: append an x, y map where a stage needs positions, splat features and coordinates together, strip x, y again so no learned layer sees absolute positions), tested with `temp/checks/check_splat_coordinates.py` (same scenes, 6 and 12 frames): it helps against plain chained maps (IoU of the cars 0.50 -> 0.83 straight, 0.30 -> 0.52 turning; mass gate alone already removes part of the lag) but the centre error stays 0.2-1.1 m over 6 frames and 2-4 m over 12 frames in a turn, against 0.00-0.14 m and 0.2-0.3 m for ONE splat from the original positions. Likely cause (hypothesis, not isolated): the splat averages the coordinates of up to four neighbouring sources, which pulls the stored position back towards the cell centre, and the velocity read from averaged feature channels is mixed with the background at object borders. Decision: the exact route is the analytic chain of the original source positions (above); coordinate channels stay as a side-channel option for anything that must pass through a splatted map, with the mass gate (features times min(mass, 1)) always on; the strip-before-learning rule holds for both.
- implemented and checked (2026-10-25): `chain_positions` (per-frame steps (dt_i, v_i, omega_i) applied to the coordinates, the object velocity vector rotated along) and `splat_window` (each of the last K original frames splatted once into the current time; returns (B, K, C, X, Y) + masses); `temp/checks/check_splat_window.py`, 6 frames at 12 Hz, constant turn (10 m/s, 0.4 rad/s) and changing motion (8 -> 14 m/s, 0.5 -> -0.1 rad/s): moving objects 0.06-0.13 m for every frame age (IoU 0.61-0.69) against 0.38 m (IoU 0.31-0.36) for chained map splats; lane lines 0.06-0.18 m (thin-line rendering level) against 0.18 m
- unknown cells (Mirko: "the resampling should lead to unknown pixels where no source pixel resides"): mass = 0 marks them - the share grows with the frame age (2 % at 0.08 s, 12-15 % at 0.42 s): the area the car drove into that the old frame never saw, and the ground behind each moving object (hidden under it in the old frame); 0 never-seen cells were reported as known. The mass goes into the temporal fusion as its own channel ("unknown" is not "empty"); cells with 0 < mass < 1 (bilinear tails) are scaled up to full size by the normalisation - the mass gate (feature x min(mass, 1); `splat(..., mass_gate=True)`, on by default in `splat_window`) helped in every chained variant of `check_splat_coordinates.py` (other session) and is about neutral for the window (one splat per frame): moving objects 0.08-0.15 m / IoU 0.62-0.81 with the gate against 0.06-0.13 m / 0.61-0.69 without (`check_splat_window.py [--no-gate]`)
- built (2026-10-26): `training/temporal_fusion.py` - the per-branch state update, ONE class for camera and radar (Mirko: the same code on both sides, separate only where explicit data is propagated). Per frame: pose advanced by the step (dt, v, omega) and re-centred by whole cells; velocity head -> static weight exp(-(|v_hat| / 1 m/s)^2); window = `splat_window` sources aggregated with mass x exp(-age / tau) x learned relevance (tau learned), summed mass and mean age as channels; static memory in a WORLD-ALIGNED grid, READ once before and WRITTEN once per frame by one splat (the memory itself is never resampled: no compounding blur, no lag), running mean with confidence and decay, moving content kept out; conv3x3 over [cur, agg, mass, age, memory, confidence]. Explicit data (radar Doppler information, counts, age): `n_explicit` trailing channels, `vec2` / `sym2` rotated with the ego rotation of their source frame, kept out of the memory, still evidence for the velocity head. Self-test: lossless re-centring; memory vs the world pattern 1.00 -> 0.97 over 36 frames straight, >= 0.95 turning (no compounding); a moving object leaves no ghost (0.002 vs 0.157 deviation from the static world without the velocity); window sources agree with the current frame; gradients; explicit rotation (a forward vector after a 90 degree left turn points right); 3.0 GFLOPs and 115 k parameters at 64 channels, 120 x 104, K = 3, per branch. `model.py` (the chain assembly) already constructs it as `window_memory` for the camera and optionally the radar.
- arms: T2 (main) vs B (7.2, one joint state with pulls) vs A (ego warp only + ConvGRU, the control)

### 7.2 Earlier design B: one joint state, pulls into the camera time (now a comparison arm)
Design agreed with Mirko (2026-10-25): three BEV maps with their own times are fused in the camera time - the camera BEV (t_cam, the reference), the radar BEV (t_radar; per cell its mean age) and the past state (t_past); each first gets its own 3 x 3 conv. The past yields the pull positions:
- per cell of the reference grid and per source s: x_s = T_ego(t_s <- t_cam) (x - v_c(x) dt_s), dt_s = t_cam - t_s, with v_c the cell's velocity in camera-time axes (equivalently T x - R v_c dt; "T x - v dt" holds only if v is expressed in the t_s axes); grid_sample pulls the source at x_s (a gather like lift PD: fast, differentiable). At 10-30 m/s and dt = 0.5 s the displacement is 5-15 m, so v is read at the pulled position once more (one fixed-point step) before the final pull
- ego motion is exact (poses); the object motion v (2 channels per cell, plus a moving probability and its uncertainty) comes from a motion estimator on the ego-aligned past state and the current sensors (incl. the radar's Doppler channels); order: ego-align the past -> estimate v -> pull
- no double counting: the radar encoder already moves each detection to t_cam by the ego motion and its radial Doppler part; the BEV pull adds only the remaining object motion, with the cell's mean radar age as its dt
- static memory: ego warp only, long, confidence-gated update (lane markings sharpen as the car approaches); moving memory: ego + flow, short
- every pulled source carries a confidence (age, moving probability, flow uncertainty); the fusion gates by it (holes and ghosts where content moves)
- training signal for v: GT box velocities rasterised per cell; a consistency term between the pulled past and the current BEV
- arms: B (this design, main) against A (ego warp only + ConvGRU, the control); later options: C (learned deformable pull offsets, BEVFormer temporal self-attention) if an explicit v does not learn, D (event-driven filter: the state predicted to each sensor's own time and updated there) if the time alignment is the weak point; E (static memory + tracked objects) not planned
- Basis: CRT-Fusion 2411.03013 (camera + radar + time: per-cell velocity and occupancy from a motion feature estimator, past features shifted by v dt above 1 m/s, recurrent; R50 nuScenes val 57.2 NDS vs CRN 56.0, night 33.0 vs 30.4 mAP; 14.5 FPS on an RTX 3090). Its code (Apache-2.0, `literature/code/crt_fusion.md`): a forward PUSH (scatter to 4 neighbours, colliding sources overwrite), ego motion separate (velocity offset first, then one ego matrix), fixed dt 0.5 s, 6 history frames stacked in channels and sparsified by predicted occupancy (would drop lanes); the velocity shift is assigned through `grid[mask][:, 0:2] = ...`, which in PyTorch writes into a copy (checked: `temp/setup/check_crt_mask_assign.py`), so - if the released line is as read - the shift does nothing, and the paper has no isolated ablation of it (only motion estimator + temporal fusion together, +1.1 NDS): the gain of a velocity warp is unproven, our arm B vs A is the test. We take its velocity GT recipe (box footprints rasterised, ground velocity in ego axes, zero below 1 m/s, loss on foreground cells) and head shapes, and re-write the pull, FIERY 2104.10490 (exact ego warp, ego-motion channels, spatial GRU; MIT, plain PyTorch), BEVDet4D 2203.17054 (alignment matters), VideoBEV 2303.05970, MemFusionMap 2409.18737 (lit).
- Tests: pure ego motion over a static scene: the pulled past must align exactly with the current map (warp test against the GT map in world coordinates); a target moving at known v must land at x + v dt (the control A must lag); radar with a known age: pulled to t_cam once, not twice; holes and ghosts carry low confidence; gain per range bin (far range should gain most).

- Pyramid, coarse to fine (PWC-Net 1709.02371; RAFT 2003.12039 is NOT coarse-to-fine: one resolution, all-pairs volume pooled to a pyramid, GRU, zero start): the velocity is estimated at 2 m cells first (30 x 26), refined at 1 m and 0.5 m. At 2 m a large motion is small: dt = 0.5 s, +-3 cells = +-12 m/s. Per level:
  1. hypotheses: a small cost volume - the current feature F0 against the ego-aligned past F_k at displacements d in a window (+-3 cells at 2 m, +-1 at the finer levels around the upsampled velocity), score = feature correlation (RAFT / PWC-Net); plus DOGMa's carried-over survivors (below), the static hypothesis and the Doppler-radial hypothesis; weights pi = softmax of the scores
  2. Doppler update (radar cells; a per-cell Kalman measurement update in information form - the A, b channels of the radar encoder): with the prior v_p (pi-weighted mean of the hypotheses, covariance P), the detections' rays u_j and radial velocities d_j: A = sum u_j u_j^T / sigma_d^2, b = sum u_j d_j / sigma_d^2 (d_j unwrapped towards v_p . u_j first: aliasing), v = (P^-1 + A)^-1 (P^-1 v_p + b), P' = (P^-1 + A)^-1 - the radial part comes from the measurement, the tangential part stays with the prior and keeps its uncertainty (closed form, differentiable)
  3. iterative refinement (RAFT-like update operator, n = 1-3 iterations, shared weights): a small ConvGRU sees [features; correlation lookup around the current v; Doppler residual r_j = wrap(v . u_j - d_j) with u_j; mass; P] and outputs dv and score corrections
  4. splat with the refined hypotheses (temporal.py), mass kept; the velocity map and P are upsampled x2 to the next level
- DOGMa ideas (dynamic occupancy grid maps with particles, Nuss et al. 1605.02406; classical with Doppler: Diehl 2008.03696; learned on lidar: Schreiber 2011.08659, dynamic-cell EPE 0.106 vs 0.200 m for particles), mapped onto the block:
  - particles = the per-cell velocity hypotheses with weights; prediction = the splat (ego motion + particle velocity, the same chain); update = likelihood weighting by the measurements: occupancy evidence of the sensors and the Doppler likelihood exp(-r^2 / 2 sigma_d^2)
  - persistence and birth: the top-M survivors of the previous frame are carried as a prior into the next frame (recurrent; differentiable as soft weights, no hard resampling in training), new hypotheses are born from the current measurements (correlation peaks, the Doppler-radial hypothesis, static)
  - free / occupied / unknown: the mass channel already marks unknown; radar's free space along its rays (no return before the detection) as an extra evidence channel - later
- Doppler residual, used three times: as the likelihood of the hypotheses (done), as the measurement of the Kalman update (step 2), as an input of the refinement (step 3); and as a self-supervised loss sum_j valid rho(wrap(v . u_j - d_j)) (radar scene flow: RaFlow 2203.01137, MIT code; CMFlow 2303.00462 uses Doppler only as an input and a pseudo label)
- Training signals: GT velocity per cell where available (synthetic world, nuScenes boxes), the warp loss at every pyramid level (the gradient locality of the bilinear sampler: SfMLearner 1704.07813 sec. 3.4, Monodepth2 1806.01260 sec. 3.2 - not Monodepth 1609.03677) masked by mass, the Doppler loss, temporal consistency of the survivors
- Where: step 1 of the temporal block in every branch - camera branch: correlation + refinement (no Doppler); radar branch: Doppler update + correlation + refinement; fusion after the radar -> camera hop: the camera's hypotheses get the radar's Doppler update in the same cell (camera gives the tangential, radar the radial part)
- Gates (synthetic world, tests/test_temporal.py extended): (a) the coarse level alone recovers +-12 m/s without labels; (b) each finer level / refinement iteration lowers the error; (c) the Doppler update brings the radial error to the noise level and leaves the tangential part and its variance unchanged (known answer); (d) over several frames the survivors resolve the tangential ambiguity; (e) against arm A (supervised velocity head + ego warp, the SOA-like control) and against v1 (current temporal.py)
- Cost (estimate): cost volume at 2 m: 780 cells x 49 displacements x 64 channels = 2.4 MMAC; the finer levels with 3 x 3 windows and 1-3 GRU iterations - a few ms in total

### 8.2 Decoding at any time dt with the SAME splat block - inside EVERY head (Mirko, 2026-10-25 / 2026-10-26)
- Structure (Mirko 2026-10-26: "decode at any delta t needs to be part of each head; a velocity head makes sense as well, but is implicit part in the rest"; built: `training/timed_head.py`, self-test passes):
  - one shared `TimeMover`: the explicit `MotionHead` (velocity field v_hat (B, 2, X, Y), m/s in the ego axes at t_c, zero start, bounded) + the splat of the state with ego motion (v, omega) over dt and a mass channel (0 = unknown cell); it is an explicit, supervised output: `MotionHead.loss` = L1 to the GT velocity on object cells (GT set to 0 below 1 m/s: a parked car does not move) + a weak L1 to 0 on the background (weight 0.1; the background is static)
  - one `TimedHead` wrapper per head (semantic, object, sensor auxiliary heads, any future head): refine block on [S(t_c + dt); mass] with FiLM by (dt, |dt|, sign dt), a residual, zero start; then the head. Every head decodes the SAME moved state with its OWN refine block (what fills an unknown cell differs: the road continues, an object does not)
  - the velocity is the same quantity in three places: the motion field (used by the mover), the object head's per-centre `vel` regression (8.3) and the radar Doppler channels that feed both; an optional consistency term ties the object head's `vel` at the GT centres to the motion field there
  - at dt = 0 the wrapper is exactly the plain head (zero-started refine, identity splat), so the main output is unchanged
  - self-test (`training/timed_head.py`): dt = 0 = plain head; ego motion moves static content to x -+ v dt (centroid within 0.1 m, either sign of dt); the unknown share is v dt / 60 m (0.083 at 0.5 s, 0.167 at 1.0 s) and none of it is reported as known; a hand-set velocity head of 4 m/s lands the blob at x + v dt; the motion loss is 0 for the right field; the semantic and the object head share ONE mover (parameters counted once); gradients reach the FiLM, the refine block and the velocity head
- One state, any time: the trunk's output feature map S(t_c) (0.5 m, C channels) is moved to t_c + dt by exactly the block of the temporal fusion (`training/splat.py`: velocity head v_hat = f(S), ego motion from v and omega over dt, coordinates chained per step where the ego motion is known per frame, the bounded correction delta with dt as an input, one splat with mass), then refined and decoded by the same semantic head:
  - dt = 0: the current BEV map (the main output; the splat is the identity)
  - dt > 0: future BEV maps (motion forecast for the planner); in training the future ego motion is known from the poses (non-causal data), at run time it is the planned motion (v, omega of the intended trajectory) - so the forecast is conditioned on what the car will do
  - dt < 0: past BEV maps (reconstruction; a self-consistency signal)
- Unknown cells: the splat's mass is 0 where no source landed (what the car drives into, what a moving object uncovers); a refine block - a few 3 x 3 convs (or a small U-Net) on [splatted features; mass; dt embedding (dt, |dt|, sign)] - fills them from the context (the road continues, a lane line continues) and cleans the bilinear tails; the loss is masked only by what is visible / defined at the TARGET time, so the refine block learns the in-filling; IoU is reported separately on known (mass >= 0.5) and unknown cells.
- Same logic, same code, same tricks as the temporal fusion (7.1): temporal fusion = many sources -> one target time; prediction = one source -> many target times. Only the feature depth differs (trunk channels instead of branch channels). The splat kernel may widen with |dt| (sigma(dt) = sigma_0 + sigma_v |dt|): the forecast becomes honestly blurrier with time and the position gradient reaches further (the bilinear kernel alone only gives a gradient within one cell).
- Training with past and future keyframes (non-causal, training only): per sample a current frame t_c and a set of dt from {0, +-0.5, +-1.0, +-1.5 s} (keyframes: full labels; 12 Hz sweeps in between: map labels exact, boxes interpolated, flagged); loss sum_dt w(dt) L_seg(head(refine(splat(S, dt))), GT(t_c + dt)) + the velocity supervision of 7.1 (GT box velocity per cell; needed because the splat's position gradient is local); w(dt) puts most weight on dt = 0.
- Why it helps beyond the forecast: an object's appearance at t_c +- 0.5 s is only explained by the right velocity, so the past / future targets supervise v_hat with the scene itself (complementing the GT velocity), and the decoder learns to keep unknown and known apart.
- Run time: causal - dt = 0 always, dt > 0 on request (planner); the past targets exist only in training.
- Basis: FIERY 2104.10490 (lit: future instance segmentation and flow in BEV, 2 s ahead, IoU 59.4 / 36.7 short / long range vs a static model 47.9 / 30.3; probabilistic future, recursive prediction beats predicting all futures at once); BEVerse 2205.09743 (motion prediction head on a shared BEV); PowerBEV and StretchBEV (BEV instance prediction; from memory, to verify); softmax splatting for the forward warp (Niklaus & Liu 2020, from memory).
- Tests (synthetic world first, `tests/synthetic_world.py`): dt = 0 reproduces the current map; objects with known velocity appear at x + v dt for dt = +-0.5 s; the share of unknown cells grows with |dt| and none is reported as known; the refine block learns to continue a straight road into the unknown area; +dt then -dt returns to the start (consistency).

### 8.3 Object head: centre points -> oriented boxes (vehicles) and circles (pedestrians), at any dt, with deformable refinement (Mirko, 2026-10-25)
Pixel-based head (every cell is classified as an object centre or not; the box is regressed from the centre cell); the selection of centres is a detached top-K, everything after it is differentiable.
- Input: the state at the requested time S(t_c + dt) from 8.2 (same splat + refine, in the ego frame of t_c + dt) and a dt embedding (dt, |dt|, sign) as FiLM (per-channel scale and shift) on the head's first conv, so the head knows the time it predicts for; dt = 0 is the current detection.
- Stage 1, plain CenterPoint head (built first; also the fallback):
  - heatmaps per class group - vehicle (car, truck, bus, trailer, construction vehicle), two-wheeler, pedestrian - with a focal loss on Gaussian targets around the GT centres (radius from the box size, CenterPoint's rule)
  - regression at the centre cell: sub-cell offset (2); vehicles and two-wheelers: oriented box - log length, log width (2), yaw as sin, cos (2); pedestrians: circle - log radius (1); velocity (2) for all; L1 losses at the GT centres
  - decoding: local maxima (3 x 3 max-pool, CenterNet), top-K, score threshold
- Stage 2, iterative refinement with n = 1 (the loop allows n > 1 with shared weights):
  - per candidate (training: top-K predictions, detached, plus GT centres with jitter; run time: top-K) 4 deformable sampling points: initialised at the centres of the 4 box faces (for circles: 4 points on the circle at 0 / 90 / 180 / 270 deg), learned bounded offsets from the centre feature in the box frame (they rotate with the box; zero start = the face centres), bilinear samples of the state's features
  - coordinates: the sampling points' positions relative to the centre in the box frame are always an input; absolute x, y (or range, azimuth) channels only as an ablation arm - no learned layer sees absolute positions unless that arm shows a gain
  - an MLP on [centre feature; 4 sampled features; relative offsets; dt embedding] gives residuals (dx, dy, dlog l, dlog w or dlog r, d yaw, dv) and an IoU-quality score; loss: L1 on the residual targets (GT - stage-1 box) and BCE of the score against the BEV IoU of the stage-1 box (CenterPoint's IoU-guided score)
- The scene as a function of time (Mirko: force the network to learn it): per training sample several dt from {0, +-0.5, +-1.0 s} (keyframes: annotated boxes; sweeps: interpolated boxes, flagged, training only); GT boxes at t_c + dt in the ego frame of t_c + dt; sum_dt w(dt) (L_stage1 + L_stage2), most weight on dt = 0; an optional consistency term: the dt prediction of a constant-velocity object equals its dt = 0 box moved by v dt
- S5a built (`training/object_head.py`, self-test: perfect targets decode to the GT boxes) and gated on the synthetic world (`tests/test_object_head.py --quick`, 2026-10-26: 200 training / 20 held-out scenes, 400 iterations): one scene overfit AP 1.0, IoU 0.991; held-out car AP@1 m 0.96, BEV IoU 0.83, centre 0.15 m, yaw 4.5 deg modulo pi (7.7 deg modulo 2 pi: static boxes are ambiguous by 180 deg from footprints alone); truck AP@1 0.86 (only 6 matches); pedestrian circles AP 1.0, IoU 0.76, radius 0.002 m; velocity about 1 m/s from one past frame 0.5 s earlier

The information is in the input; the gap is learning (400 iterations, 200 scenes: the same net reaches IoU 0.991 on one scene). Whether more steps / scenes close it (then it is optimisation) or not (then loss / decoding / capacity) is the learning-curve part (configs 200 / 800 scenes x 400 ... 6000 iterations), queued for the first free GPU slot (`tests/run_when_gpu_free.sh`; the GPU was saturated by other sessions, one smoke step took 34 s)

- Basis: CenterNet "Objects as Points" (1904.07850, from memory), CenterPoint 2006.11275 (lit: centre heatmap + regression; its second stage samples the BEV features at the box centre and the 4 face centres; MIT, `det3d/models/bbox_heads/center_head.py` for targets and loss), Deformable DETR (2010.04159, from memory: learned sampling offsets), Fast and Furious (Luo et al., CVPR 2018, from memory: detection and forecasting jointly from a temporal BEV), FIERY / BEVerse (lit: future prediction on a shared BEV)

- Metrics: nuScenes detection metrics in the front field of view up to 60 m (AP by centre distance 0.5 / 1 / 2 / 4 m, ATE, ASE, AOE, AVE), per range bin and per dt; ms of head and refinement

### 8.4 Loss specification - PROPOSAL for review (2026-10-26, not decided)
Why: before this section the losses were scattered (the object head's complete in code; the semantic head one sentence; motion, dt, auxiliary and the total undefined). Everything here refers to GT layers that exist (`training/gt_bev.py`) and to heads that exist or are specified (8.2, 8.3).

**Principles**
- every term is normalised by its own count (valid cells, positives, objects), so all terms are of order 1 and comparable; fixed ratios inside a head, learned weights only between task groups
- masks decide what is supervised; "undefined" (neither camera nor radar sees it) is never supervised
- every loss is evaluated at the TARGET time t_c + dt against the GT of that time (8.2), dt-weighted
- the GT is not exact (map registration offset of 0.5-1 m in places, 9.5): lines are supervised with a tolerance and per-frame weights, never at the exact cell alone

**Masks (what each mask does)**
| mask (from the GT) | map layers (areas, lines, centre-lines) | objects, velocity |
|---|---|---|
| undefined | weight 0 | weight 0 (also no negatives) |
| occluded (behind a GT box seen from the camera) | weight 0.5 (the map is inferable from context and the planner needs it; arm: 0 / 1) | the centre is supervised if in (camera and not occluded) or radar coverage; otherwise ignored (neither positive nor negative) |
| camera / radar coverage | all defined cells; the camera aux head only camera and not occluded, the radar aux head only radar coverage | as above |
| unknown at the target time (splat mass < 0.5, dt != 0) | supervised with weight 0.5 (this is what teaches the refine block to fill in); reported separately | same |
| unobserved box (annotation with no lidar and no radar point: `observed == False`; GT layer `ignore`; 16.6 % of the supervised boxes on nuScenes mini, bug-hunter finding 2026-10-26) | the footprint is IGNORED in the dense layers (neither object nor background) | not a positive of the heatmap; its peak region joins the heatmap ignore set; no regression, velocity or motion loss; stage 2 matches only observed boxes |
| interpolated labels (12 Hz sweeps, training only) | map exact: weight 1 | boxes: weight 0.5; evaluation only on keyframes |

Measured (`temp/checks/occluded_map_stats.py`, nuScenes mini, 135 keyframes, 0.5 m front grid): of the cells the front camera reaches, 37.6 % are occluded by a GT box (9.5 % at 0-20 m, 33.0 % at 20-40 m, 44.2 % at 40-60 m); among the map layers 36.6 % of the drivable area, 33.2 % of the lane dividers, 18.8 % of the road dividers and 28.6 % of the centre-lines. (The column "in radar coverage" is 100 % because the approximate radar fan of `gt_bev.py`, half angle 60 deg to 70 m from memory, covers the whole grid: that mask does not discriminate yet.)

**Losses per output**
| output (head) | loss | note |
|---|---|---|
| areas (5 layers; semantic head) | BCE on the soft coverage fraction (targets in [0, 1]) + 0.5 x (1 - soft IoU), per layer | |
| lines (lane divider, road divider; semantic head) | (a) soft Dice between the predicted mask and the GT mask DILATED by 1 cell (tolerance +-0.5 m: a +-1 cell shift costs nothing, a 3-cell shift does); (b) Huber (delta 0.5 m) on the truncated distance field (metres), weight 1 for cells with GT distance < 1 m, 0.25 elsewhere; the line is decoded sub-cell from the field | thin structures: the field gives position below the cell size, the dilated Dice avoids punishing registration noise |
| centre-lines (mask + direction; semantic head, later stage) | the same dilated Dice for the mask + L1 on the unit direction (cos, sin) at mask cells, weight 0.5 | link to the lane project; off until the S1 baseline exists |
| objects, dense (3 groups; semantic head) | BCE on the footprint coverage + soft IoU | free space for the planner; may be dropped once the object head runs (ablation) |
| object head stage 1 (8.3) | as implemented (`object_head.head_loss`): CenterNet focal loss (alpha 2, beta 4) / peaks + L1 on offset, log size, yaw (sin, cos normalised), log radius, velocity x 0.2 | complete, tested |
| object head stage 2 (8.3, not built) | L1 on the residuals (GT - stage-1 box) + BCE of the quality score against the BEV IoU of the stage-1 box | |
| motion head (8.2; `MotionHead.loss`) | L1 to the GT velocity per cell on object cells (GT set to 0 below 1 m/s; boxes whose devkit velocity is NaN, from memory: boxes without a neighbouring annotation, are masked) + 0.1 x L1 to 0 on the other cells | the radar Doppler channels are in the state; optional consistency: 0.1 x L1 between the object head's `vel` at the GT centres and the motion field there |
| splat correction (7.1) | no own loss: trained through the dt != 0 losses; an L2 penalty 0.01 x |delta|^2 keeps it a correction (bound 1 m) | |
| camera aux head (training only) | the main losses on areas, lines, objects, only camera and not occluded cells, weight 0.3 | catches sensor-specific failures |
| radar aux head (training only) | (a) objects as the main loss on radar coverage; (b) STATIONARY STRUCTURE map: target = footprints of stationary annotated objects (boxes with speed < 0.5 m/s: parked vehicles, standing pedestrians) + a 1 m band along the edge of the drivable area; BCE + Dice, radar coverage, weight 0.3 | nuScenes has no guard-rail or kerb GT and no lidar: this is a PROXY (drivable-area edge ~ road boundary); the earlier "occupied by something that does not move" target is not available |
| radar slot decoder (training only, optional) | valid BCE + L1 on properties and offsets at valid slots (as `radar_recon_test.py`), weight 0.1 | only if the 32-channel radar features are used without the raster skip (section A of `radar_encoding.md`) |
| depth | none | the camera heads are frozen and there is no depth GT (no lidar); if unfrozen, the pretrained depth head stays frozen |

**Radar stationary-structure target - evidence (2026-10-26; scout `literature/radar_stationary_gt.md`, test T0 `temp/checks/t0_stationary_proxy.py`)**

**Time (the dt-weighted multi-time loss)**
- total(sample) = sum over dt in {0, +-0.5 s (the neighbouring keyframes), +-1.0 s where the keyframes k +- 2 exist} of w(dt) x sum over heads of L_head(dt), with w(0) = 1, w(+-0.5) = 0.5, w(+-1.0) = 0.25 (per sign); GT of the keyframe at that time (annotated, exact), ego steps from the poses
- warm-up: dt = 0 only for the first 20 % of the steps, then the dt != 0 terms ramp in linearly over the next 20 %; modality dropout (p = 0.15-0.2) from the second epoch
- optional consistency (arm): the dt prediction of a constant-velocity object = its dt = 0 box moved by v dt, weight 0.1

**Weighting between tasks**
- four groups: map areas, lines + centre-lines, objects (dense + instances), motion; learned uncertainty weights (Kendall et al., 1705.07115, lit): L = sum_g exp(-s_g) L_g + s_g with s_g = log sigma_g^2 clamped to [-3, 1] (the clamp keeps the thin lines from being starved), start s_g = 0; GradNorm (1711.02257) only if one group starves
- aux heads and the radar decoder: fixed weights (0.3 / 0.1), not learned: they are scaffolding
- gradient clipping at norm 10, lr and BatchNorm precautions from the lane project (clip, roll-back guard)

**Registration robustness (lines, area edges)**
- (i) tolerance in the loss (dilated Dice, Huber's flat region) - always; (ii) a per-frame weight w_reg in [0.2, 1] from a registration estimate (lateral peak of the image contrast along the projected dividers within +-0.25 m: 1, within +-0.75 m: 0.5, otherwise 0.2; frames without an estimate: 1) once the estimator exists; (iii) metrics at tolerance 0 / 0.5 / 1.0 m, not only at the exact cell; (iv) arm: shift the GT layers per frame by the estimated offset where the peak is clear, flag the rest

**Metrics reported next to the losses** (not used for training): IoU per layer per range bin (0-20, 20-40, 40-60 m) at the three tolerances; known / unknown cells for dt != 0; detection AP and AVE per range and dt; ms per frame

**Tests (known answers, before any training)**: BCE on soft targets = the analytic value; the dilated Dice is 0 for a 1-cell shift and > 0 for 3 cells; Huber zero for the exact field; cells with weight 0 give exactly zero gradient; doubling the map area leaves every normalised term unchanged; the Kendall weights stay inside the clamp; the total is finite and decreases when overfitting one synthetic scene

(2) the dense object coverage (vehicle, pedestrian, two-wheeler) STAYS in the semantic head next to the object head (Mirko, 2026-10-26); dropping it is an ablation for later (comparability with the published BEV vehicle IoU, free space for the planner, in-filling at dt != 0).

- regression: beta-NLL (2203.09168; `beta_nll`, a log-variance channel on the head) only as an arm against the Huber loss: a plain Laplace NLL lowered StreamMapNet's AP (0.779 -> 0.704) in the scout's source, so it is not adopted without the control
- time: bidirectional cross-time consistency of the predictions at -0.5 / 0 / +0.5 s (`cross_time_consistency`; MapTCL 2608.05209, FIERY / ST-P3, UnFlow 1711.07837 for the forward-backward occlusion check): the prediction at 0 is moved to +-0.5 s with the ego-exact chain and the motion field and compared with the prediction there, both directions, each with a stop-gradient on the target side, a cell without counterpart costs a constant (so 'push everything out of the grid' is not rewarded), invisible or occluded cells are ignored; own warm-up (hold 20 %, ramp 20 %), weight 0 = off. Arms: w(+-0.5) = 0.5 (above) against the published 0.9 per 0.5 s step; window of 7 frames reported better than 5 (not applicable to 3 keyframes)
- recipe (scout of the BEVDet family and the nuScenes fusion papers): AdamW, lr 3e-4 (published 2e-4 .. 4e-4, not scaled with the batch), weight decay 0.01 on weights only, effective batch 16 by accumulation (2 x 8), 500 optimiser steps warm-up from lr / 3, cosine to 1e-3, clip 5, EMA 0.999, bf16 with positions, mass and v dt in fp32. BEV augmentation (rotation +-22.5 deg, scale 0.95 .. 1.05, no flip, applied to the GT and the sensor data alike) is NOT built yet. Plain modality dropout is held back until a control exists
- derived-data path: a feature cache of the frozen camera heads (`training/feature_cache.py`, 0.28 MB per frame in float16) lets training skip the 71 GFLOPs camera encoder; cached camera BEVs equal the standard model's within 2.6e-4 of their range (self-test), so cached and uncached training are the same model; the GT cache (`data/cache/gt`) is written on first use

## 9. Ground truth: how it is made and how you can look at it

### 9.1 Generation (CPU, cached per frame)
1. For every camera frame (keyframes and, after the download, 12 Hz sweeps): the vehicle pose at the image time t_ref.
2. Map layers from the map expansion, transformed into the vehicle frame at t_ref: polygons (drivable area, ped crossing, walkway, stop line) rasterised with fractional coverage per cell; lines (dividers) drawn 1 cell wide plus a truncated distance field; lane centre-lines from the discretised lane arcs.
3. Objects: the annotated boxes, interpolated per instance to t_ref (position, yaw, size) between the 2 Hz keyframes; footprints rasterised; velocity per box. Interpolated labels are flagged (training only; evaluation on keyframes).
4. Masks: coverage = cells reached by any camera frustum (from the calibration, generic for any rig); occlusion = ray casting from each camera centre through the GT box footprints (cells behind a box are occluded); range bins.
5. Stored per frame (compressed arrays) with an index of provenance (map version, interpolated or annotated, timestamps).

### 9.2 Observing it (a viewer tool, local files, not in git)
- Per frame a three-panel picture: (1) the camera image with the GT projected into it (dividers and centre-lines as lines, areas translucent, boxes as 3D wireframes, radar detections as dots with Doppler arrows) - if the projected lines do not sit on the painted markings, the GT or the transforms are wrong; (2) the BEV raster with each layer in its colour, coverage and occlusion masks hatched, the camera frustums and radar fields of view outlined, the ego vehicle drawn; (3) a text strip with the timestamps, dt of each sensor, the interpolation flag and the class cell counts.
- Per scene a 12 Hz video of those pictures, and an interactive page (scene / frame slider, layer toggles) to browse a scene; key frames sent to you.

### 9.4 GT pixel maps: one generator for any time and resolution (Mirko, 2026-10-25)
- One function `gt_bev(scene, t, grid)` (to build, `training/gt_bev.py` + a cache writer): the ego pose at time t (any camera frame, keyframe or 12 Hz sweep, and t_c + dt for the future / past targets of 8.2), the grid (0.5 m or 0.25 m, front 0-60 m or surround), and it returns the layers below in the ego frame at t. The synthetic world (`tests/synthetic_world.py`) produces the same layers, so training and evaluation code is the same for synthetic and real data.
- Layers per cell:
  - areas (drivable area, ped crossing, walkway, stop line, car park): polygon coverage fraction from the map expansion, rasterised with 4 x 4 supersampling (`NuScenesMap` polygons transformed into the ego frame, filled per sub-cell)
  - lines (lane divider, road divider): a 1-cell mask and a truncated unsigned distance field (2 m, in metres) from the polylines; lane centre-lines from the discretised lane arcs with their direction (cos, sin) - the link to the lane project
  - objects: footprint coverage per class group (vehicle, pedestrian, two-wheeler), instance id, velocity (vx, vy in the ego axes at t, from the devkit's box velocity), boxes interpolated per instance to t between the 2 Hz annotations (a flag per cell: annotated or interpolated)
  - masks: camera coverage (cells any camera frustum reaches, from the calibration), occlusion (ray casting from the camera centre through the GT box footprints), radar coverage; "undefined" = outside every coverage: excluded from the loss
- Storage: derived data of this project (rule: project folder on C:, `bev_fusion/data/gt_cache/`, not in git): per frame one compressed array file with uint8 coverage / distance and bit masks, plus an index with provenance (map version, interpolation flag, timestamps); estimate about 150 KB per frame at 0.5 m (12 layers), 4 x at 0.25 m: keyframes of the front camera (20,418) about 3 GB at 0.5 m; the 12 Hz sweeps (98,029) about 15 GB - generated on demand for the training samples rather than all at once.
- Checks: those of 9.3 (reprojection into the image, world consistency between frames, interpolation error) plus: the 0.25 m map pooled to 0.5 m equals the 0.5 m map; the future target t_c + dt generated directly equals the target generated at t_c and moved by the exact ego motion for static layers; viewer pictures of 9.2 for a few scenes before any training.

- Rule (Mirko, 2026-10-26): **stationary GT is temporally stable, it does not move.** Map layers come from the fixed world geometry in every frame; a correction is allowed only per map element in world coordinates (`NuScenesGT(map_offsets={element token: (dx, dy)})`, the same in every frame, from a multi-log estimate), never per frame (no per-frame pitch or registration shift of the GT). Stationary objects (speed < 0.5 m/s over the whole track and < 1 m from the median position) get one world pose for all their keyframes (median position, mean yaw, median size) and velocity 0 (`stationary_fix=True`, box flag `stationary`). Self-test (mini scene): 177 stationary instances, raw annotation jitter median 1.1 cm -> 0; every stationary box seen in k and k+1 has the same world pose.

### 9.3 Checks of the GT itself
- Reprojection: image edge strength along the projected dividers must be higher than along the same lines shifted by 0.5 m (catches calibration, pose and time errors automatically).
- World consistency: the map layers of frame t warped by the ego motion to frame t+1 must equal the layers of t+1 (IoU near 1 except at the coverage edge).
- Interpolation error: leave one keyframe out, interpolate it from its neighbours, compare with its annotation (position / yaw error per class and speed) - an upper bound for the 12 Hz labels.
- Statistics: cells per class and range bin, per city; empty or broken scenes listed.

## 12. Decided and open questions

### Decided (Mirko, 2026-10-25)
- range: the system is built for 60 m (grid and depth bins end at 60 m)
- no horizon strip for now: one image stream, half resolution, stride 8 (pixel groups of 16 x 16 full-resolution pixels); the full-resolution horizon strip (stride 4 over the rows whose ground lies at 15-100 m) stays a later option
- what that means (geometry for a camera like CAM_FRONT, f ~ 1266 px, h ~ 1.5 m): across the frustum is 0.25 m wide at 20 m and 0.76 m at 60 m (fine for 0.5 m cells); along the range a group covers 3.4 m of road at 20 m, 10 m at 35 m, 21 m at 50 m - beyond about 20 m single-frame road positions come from the learned depth distribution inside the frustum and from temporal fusion (each far cell is seen again closer), which section 7 must therefore deliver and the per-range evaluation must show

- camera lift: image backbone + lookup tables, height kept as channels (feat[z_car]); lifts P / S / PD compared in S1, PD recommended because it keeps the depth uncertainty and is a pure gather (section 2)
- first building block FIXED (Mirko, 2026-10-25): the camera block produces the world voxel map with lift PD (pull with depth weight); P and S stay in S1 only as controls; the next design topic is the radar
- radar block FIXED, simple (Mirko, 2026-10-25): every detection goes into the BEV cell where it resides (hard raster, 0.5 m, same grid and index order as the camera BEV) with its properties - distance from the sensor, direction from the sensor in the vehicle frame (circular), Doppler as a circular value (sin / cos of k pi v_r / v_max, k = 1, 2, 4), RCS, age - and sub-cell offset; then two 3 x 3 convolutions (`training/radar_bev.py`, self-test passes). This replaces the learned density g of section 3 for the first version; section 3 and `radar_encoding.md` stay as the variants (polar image + soft splat + pull, Cartesian soft footprint, learned sigma, height profile), each to be justified against this simple block in the radar stage S2 (hard raster = R0).
- radar path logged and made information-preserving (Mirko, 2026-10-25): 
adar_encoding.md section A records the chain adapter -> point-level time alignment -> raster -> 2 convs -> radar BEV with its interfaces for the temporal fusion; the raster has an information-preserving form (K = 3 exact slots per cell + remainder mean + count, exact inverse decode_slots); measured on nuScenes mini: the per-cell mean merges 56 % of the detections of a 0.5 s window (errors small for most, large in rare mixes), 3 slots keep 91 %; learned conv features alone recover only 46 % of the detections within tolerance after 4,000 steps (raster oracle: mean 63.5 %, slots 95.0 %) - keep the raster as a lossless skip and train the features with a slot decoder as auxiliary loss; rule for the temporal fusion: align detections as POINTS and rasterise once, do not resample a finished radar raster
- camera and radar blocks are settled: both deliver a BEV on the same grid (camera 128 channels, radar 32) for the fusion; next design topics: fusion, temporal state, heads
- kept for later (Mirko, 2026-10-25): PD-G in inverse depth (mixture in 1/z, exact voxel mass, section 2.4); the categorical lift PD on the pre-trained head stays the working version until then
