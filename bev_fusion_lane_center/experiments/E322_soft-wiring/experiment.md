## 1. Card text
- **Statement:** choosing the delivered piece a connector end is wired to with a learned softmax over the pieces in reach (plus a dustbin) beats the nearest-piece rule of the snap by more than the noise band in topo F1. **Closed at the gate for the imitating design:** an oracle that applies the rule at the matched GT end gains -0.0007 / +0.0013 over the rule (section 2); a head that is trained to imitate that label cannot do better than the label. What the gate does not cover is listed in section 3.
- **The rule is a named selection on the OUTPUT (D55):** the one-piece nearest-end rule is a hard selection at the output of the system, named as such, which D55 allows (hard selection only on the output and named); it is not a rule step on the training path (the training path of the pair head ends in the curves; the snap acts after it). D84 point 3 asks for differentiable solutions: the end geometry of the snap has a learned replacement (E320), the selection a replacement that ties the rule at best (this card). **One question for Mirko when he is back (D86 names both weights: end-to-end and simple):** is an end-to-end replacement of the output selection worth one more module when it ties the rule? D86's default, applied here: not now.
- **Mechanism (why it was asked):** on E312 the snap's gain over the unsnapped read-out (0.0387 / 0.0383 topo F1) is 69 % / 73 % WIRING (one nearest piece per end instead of every piece in reach: `--snap --snap-nogeom` 0.6706 / 0.6760 against 0.6438 / 0.6481 unsnapped) and 27 - 31 % end geometry (E320 replaced that part inside the network). The keypoints are 1.2 - 1.4 m off the GT ends (`jdiag.py kperr`) and the median gap between the nearest and the second nearest piece end is 1.15 m, so a wrong choice by the rule looked plausible.
- **What the gate measured instead:** (a) the rule at the keypoint picks the same piece end as the rule at the GT end in 84.2 % / 84.2 % of the ends where both find one, and in 75.0 % / 74.5 % of the ends that have two or more PLACES to choose from (59.6 % / 58.8 % of those ends; 7424 / 7620 ends); the differing choices do not change topo F1 (o - c is within the band) but move junction F1 by +0.0067 / +0.0063 (intervals above 0, 0.006 against a guard of 0.03). (b) The metric CAN see a wrong choice: the positive control (every end with a second place in reach joined to the second nearest piece end) loses 0.0311 / 0.0301 topo F1. (c) The reach of 4 m is the best of 2 / 3 / 4 / 6 m (c - r6 +0.0026 / +0.0036); r2 / r3 also shrink the cover radius of B's long links (`add_connectors(wire_pos=)`), so they are not a pure wiring effect. (d) Dropping the pairs with an end that matches no GT end lowers topo F1 (o_drop - o -0.0022 / -0.0047): the matcher's truth is not the scorer's truth, so o is not an upper bound for a dustbin. The snap's wiring gain is the COUNT 'one piece instead of every piece in reach' (E323 family), which the rule already does.
- **Inputs:** `evaluation/jwiring.py` (committed, selftest in `run_gates.sh`), the dumps of E312 (`runs/e312_p_s<S>/ends_val.pkl`), B's full dump (`temp/e276/predfull_e273_p_s<S>.pkl`), the GT ends of the VAL frames (`jwiring.py gt-ends`).
- **Starts from:** E312 (the system of record), E320 for the attached-end reading. **Lens / group / status:** E2E differentiability (D55) of the delivered graph; closed at the gate, 2026-11-13.
- **Referee:** first fresh critic pass 2026-11-13 08:25, REVISE (over-claims, dustbin half of the target not in the oracle, the 84 % population, no positive control, rounding, post-hoc statements, design gaps, the exit side untested); this version answers it, see the end of section 3.

### The design of the learned wiring (recorded, NOT built: what a later card would have to say)
Only to be taken up if the end-to-end principle is weighted above simplicity (the question above).
- **Base and confound:** the frozen E312 checkpoints, not the E320 recipe. E320's geometry gain is real (the recipe is +0.0075 / +0.0089 over u' through the geometry loss, the attached ends add +0.0022 / +0.0040), its CUT came from the duplicate guard and E323 showed that this is not the number of curves (E312 at E320's count: dup_frac +0.0011 / +0.0005; E320 keeps +0.0172 / +0.0089 at an equal count), so a head on top of E320 would carry that excess. E320's PRIMARY read-out (rule-wired) stays the geometry-recipe reference.
- **What is trained:** a softmax over the delivered piece ends in reach (the same gate: 4 m / 45 degrees, at most 8, an index choice) plus a dustbin, scored by a small MLP (about 10 k parameters like `Attach`: 9602 parameters, 3.9 MMACs) from the distance, the along / across offset and the heading difference of the piece end, the length and kind of the piece (lane / link), B's existence and link probabilities of it, the keypoint's kind and probability and the partner end's geometry. Target: the piece end the rule picks at the matched GT end, a dustbin for an end that matches no GT end or has no piece at its GT end (untested as a target: see o_drop), cross entropy. The delivered pieces are a frozen function of B, computed per frame (B's read-out on CPU); the keypoints stay live, so joint training with the pair head is not excluded by caching the pieces. Data caveat: B's dumps exist for the VAL frames only, TRAIN pieces would have to be dumped (B on 8414 frames, about 5 GPU minutes, an estimate) and are in-sample for B, so the piece quality at TRAIN is better than at VAL (a mismatch to be handled by a keypoint jitter and checked on VAL).
- **Read-out:** the softmax weights are the edge probabilities that go to the output (planner-ready); the hard step that stays, named, is the argmax per end (the dustbin = the end is not wired); the p >= 0.1 threshold, the keypoint peak choice, the candidate gate, B's close-node step and the section solve stay as named hard steps.
- **Controls:** c (E312 snapped, rule), u' (snap without the end geometry, the floor), o (the label), `second` (the positive control), E320 PRIMARY (rule-wired, attached ends) as the geometry-recipe reference.
- **Rule it would carry:** equal accuracy, not a gain: topo F1 of the learned selection at least c - 0.005 on both seeds with the guards F1@1.0, dup_frac (+0.01), vertices, junction F1 held. **Kill:** the head's agreement with the oracle label on the VAL ends with a real choice (two or more places) not above the rule's own 75 % (a head that imitates worse than the rule is not worth its cost), or topo F1 below c - 0.01 on either seed. **Budget:** CPU minutes for the pieces cache, a TRAIN dump of B (about 5 GPU minutes), the head itself seconds to minutes (about 10 k parameters on cached features; an estimate, not measured); cost to state when built: about +0.01 M parameters, about 4 MMACs, the host time of the candidate lists (not measured; the pair head is already host-bound, 9.7 ms for 0.02 GMACs).
- **A zero-parameter variant as an INTERFACE option, not an accuracy experiment (lane lead, 2026-11-13):** soft-distance edge weights: for every end the candidates in reach (the same gate) get the weights softmax(-d^2 / (2 sigma^2)) over the candidates and a dustbin whose logit is -R^2 / (2 sigma^2), with the temperature fixed here at sigma = 1.2 m (about the mean keypoint error, `jdiag.py kperr`) and R = 4 m (the reach), so that the dustbin never wins while a candidate is in reach and the hard argmax IS the nearest-piece rule: topo F1 is identical to c by construction. The weights are delivered to the planner as edge probabilities. It is built only when the planner interface wants edge probabilities; no run now; **when it is built a selftest must assert that the argmax equals `rule_end` on random scenes (the identity is not tested today)**.

## 3. What it says and what it does not
The nearest-piece rule is at the ceiling of what choosing a SINGLE piece by the geometry of the GT end can give: the oracle label and the rule are exchangeable in topo F1 although they differ in a quarter of the real choices, while a wrong choice on purpose costs 0.03. The snap's wiring gain is the effect of wiring ONE piece instead of all of them, which the rule already does. What the topology of the delivered graph is limited by is not shown by this gate; the descriptive shares are: 28.7 % / 26.5 % (seed 0; seed 1 29.3 % / 24.7 %) of the entry / exit ends have no delivered piece end in reach (a lane that B does not deliver cannot be wired), 26 % of the ends match no GT end (false keypoints) and 25 % of the matched ends find no piece at their GT end; these shares overlap and no intervention was made, so they are where to look, not a measured limit. The levers that the numbers point to are the pieces themselves (lanes found 0.74: the pixel / node / link stages of B), the number of curves and pieces at the output (E323), and the hard steps of B's own read-out (the close-node step: E292).
**Not covered by the gate (untested, stated so that nobody reads more into it):** (1) a different notion of 'correct' than the rule at the GT end: the piece whose lane continues the GT lane, a heading-aware choice among the 12 - 14 % tied ends (co-located ends of a fork or merge: `add_connectors` takes the first in list order, the oracle takes the same), wiring BOTH tied pieces (a `k = 2` weighted wiring, the first member of the next family card); (2) a dustbin: the oracle cannot leave an end unwired (the 26 % unmatched ends and the 25 % ends without a piece at the GT end keep the keypoint rule), and o_drop shows that the matcher's truth is not the scorer's truth, so o is not an upper bound there; testing it needs a per-end option in `add_connectors` (shared code, flag-off identical) and is not done; (3) one threshold (p >= 0.1) and two networks; (4) the pieces are B's hard read-out (the close-node step included), so 'the piece' is itself a product of a rule; (5) the gate was measured before the card was written.
**Referee answered (first pass):** 1 over-claims narrowed (title of the finding, 'ceiling', 'falsified'); 2 dustbin stated as untested; 3 the 84 % population and the real-choice rate (75 %) added, 'do not change the score' corrected (junction F1 +0.006); 4 the positive control added (`second`); 5 the r2 / r3 confound stated; 6 differences rounded as the bootstrap prints them (-0.0007 / +0.0013); 7 post-hoc items stated, the hypothesis of the stub parked, a ledger note on the E312 rows; 8 the descriptive shares labelled and both seeds given; 9 kill and budget and the data caveat added in the design, the selftest of the interface option stated as a requirement; the exit side and a tie case are now in the selftest of `jwiring.py`.
