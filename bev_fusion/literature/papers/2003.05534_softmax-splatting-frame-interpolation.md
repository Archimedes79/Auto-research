**Verification**: id 2003.05534, Simon Niklaus and Feng Liu, submitted 2020-03-11, CVPR 2020; project page http://sniklaus.com/softsplat. Matches the from-memory citation.

**What it does** [checked]: forward warping ("splatting") of an image and its feature pyramid by an optical flow, with four variants. Summation: each source pixel adds its colour to the target pixels around its moved position, weighted by a bilinear kernel b(u); overlaps just add up (brightness inconsistency). Average: sum divided by the splatted all-ones image (normalised, but a car in front of grass is averaged with the grass). Linear: weight by an importance map Z, normalise by the splatted Z (separates foreground, but not invariant to a shift in Z). Softmax: weights exp(Z), normalised by the splatted exp(Z), so only differences in Z matter. Z is an importance metric (brightness constancy / a small network, memory); gradients of the splat with respect to the input is the bilinear weight and with respect to the flow involves the slope of the kernel; the authors supply a CUDA implementation because scatter-style ops are poorly supported in autodiff frameworks. The flow, the features and Z can all be fine-tuned end to end; interpolation at arbitrary t by splatting with t * flow.

**Results that matter**: states first rank on the Middlebury interpolation benchmark (intro); only softmax splatting handles the occlusion in the paper's Figure 2 (qualitative). The PSNR ablation table of the four variants was not in the characters read (digest of the abstract only): no number here.

**API** [checked README]: `softsplat(tenIn, tenFlow, tenMetric, strMode)` with modes sum / avg / linear / soft; the example scales the metric by -20 (the paper's alpha), noted as learnable.

**Transferable technique**: our `gaussian_splat` is average splatting with a hypothesis weight pi_m (and a bilinear kernel) plus a mass channel and a mass gate `min(mass, 1)`; the paper's softmax weighting with an importance Z is the "collision winner" rule (plan.md section 7: importance 6 improved chained moving objects from 2-3 m to 0.4-0.8 m). Differences worth knowing: (1) the paper normalises by the splatted weight everywhere, we additionally keep the raw mass as a "known/unknown" channel (the paper has no such channel, holes are filled by the synthesis network); (2) in the paper Z is the SAME for all candidates of a pixel, we multiply the importance by the hypothesis probability pi_m, so the LEARNED score doubles as importance.

**Cost and fit**: CuPy kernel is not needed; in torch `index_add_` on flattened indices does the same forward pass with autograd for weights and positions.

**Doubts**: designed for dense frames, where nearly every target pixel receives a source; in BEV with 90 % empty cells the normalisation divides empty by empty (eps) and the mass gate is what keeps it honest.
