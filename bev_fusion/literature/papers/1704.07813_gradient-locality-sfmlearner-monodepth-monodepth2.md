**CORRECTION to the from-memory reference**: the exact statement "multi-scale loss against gradient locality of the bilinear sampler" is NOT in Monodepth (1609.03677, CVPR 2017). A search of its text finds "locally fully differentiable" (Sec. 3.3) and four output scales chosen for "more stable convergence" (Sec. 4.1), but neither "locality" nor "local minima". The statement is in
- SfMLearner (1704.07813, Zhou, Brown, Snavely, Lowe), Sec. 3.4 "Overcoming the gradient locality": "the gradients are mainly derived from the pixel intensity difference between I(p_t) and the four neighbors of I(p_s)" (quoted, one sentence); remedy: explicit multi-scale and smoothness loss (as an alternative to a small-bottleneck encoder-decoder).
- Monodepth2 (1806.01260, Godard, Mac Aodha, Firman, Brostow, ICCV 2019), Sec. 3.2 "Multi-scale Estimation": the clause "Due to the gradient locality of the bilinear sampler" is in the text, followed by the reason that existing models use multi-scale depth prediction and reconstruction "to prevent the training objective getting stuck in local minima"; its own remedy is to upsample the low-resolution depth maps to the input resolution and compute the photometric error there (against holes in low-texture regions and texture-copy artefacts).

**What it means for us** [from the statements]: a bilinear kernel gives a position gradient only inside one source cell neighbourhood (4 pixels), so a mis-placed object further than one cell away gets no useful gradient: the standard remedies are (a) several scales (SfMLearner, Monodepth), (b) evaluating every scale at full resolution (Monodepth2), (c) our two: a Gaussian splat kernel whose width is annealed (training/temporal.py `sigma`) and a softmax over far-apart velocity hypotheses (non-local credit). A multi-scale loss on the splatted map (the loss on the splat output pooled 2x, 4x, 8x) is the cheapest copy of (a) and costs nothing at inference.

**Cost and fit**: nothing to port as code; the multi-scale pooled loss is three lines.

**Doubts**: both methods use it for photometric self-supervision where the target is texture; for a supervised BEV loss the target is a smooth heatmap and the locality problem is milder; no ablation in the cited papers isolates the multi-scale effect on a forward splat (all use backward bilinear sampling).
