**What it does**: Each pixel is back-projected along a unit ray from a precomputed fisheye lookup table, scaled by the centres of 64 depth bins (1-30 m), and moved with the extrinsics: that is the Gaussian mean. The covariance is Sigma = sigma^2 I + Delta Delta^T, where sigma is a predicted scalar and Delta the depth-bin quantisation error. The Gaussians are splatted differentiably into a 64 x 48 BEV (24 m x 18 m, 0.375 m cells). Four fisheye cameras at 1024 x 864, about 190 degrees. Uncertainty is predicted, not evaluated.

**Results that matter** (proprietary dataset, about 4790 traces, parking / low-speed urban): IoU 87.75 percent drivable, 57.26 percent vehicles. No ablation, no latency (about 3 h per epoch on a Quadro RTX 8000, batch 4).

**Transferable technique**: the camera model enters only through a per-pixel ray LUT, so the same lift serves pinhole and fisheye (our "any rig" requirement); a covariance term that includes the bin width (the quantisation error of the depth discretisation) so the footprint cannot be thinner than the bin.

**Cost and fit**: no speed data; splatting as in GaussianLSS, not a gather.

**Doubts**: single results row, no baseline against pinhole LSS or a pull, private data, no code; "uncertainty" is never checked for calibration.
