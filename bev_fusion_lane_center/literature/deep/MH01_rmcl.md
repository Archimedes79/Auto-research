## 1. Problem and claim
- Task: regression of a **set of targets per input** (conditional distribution with several modes, 1 to 3 targets per input) with K output heads trained by winner-takes-all (WTA), without matching and without assuming an order of the targets.
- Gap: classical (single-target) MCL / WTA has no way to tell which hypotheses are meaningful; at inference the K hypotheses are over-confident, because hypotheses in rarely used regions look as reliable as the others (their weight is uniform). Relaxed WTA and top-n WTA repair this only by letting the non-winners move, which biases them towards the mean and needs tuning.
- Claim: add K scalar **score heads**, trained by binary cross-entropy to say "this hypothesis is the winner of at least one target of this input". After normalisation the scores are the probability mass of each Voronoi cell of the hypotheses, the hypotheses are the cell conditional means, and the output is a weighted point set (a mixture of Diracs, or a piecewise-constant density). Validated on a synthetic 2-D example and on sound source localisation (SSL); **no vision, no lane, no tree**.
- The audio task of rMCL is source **localisation**, not separation; speech separation (WSJ0-mix) is the aMCL paper.

## 2. Method as built

### Architecture (digest)
```
x_s (input) -> shared trunk (any regression net) ---+--> K hypothesis heads f^k(x_s) in R^d   (last layer split K ways)
                                                    +--> K score heads    gamma^k(x_s) in [0,1] (separate last layers, sigmoid)
toy:   t in [0,1] -> 3-layer MLP, 256 hidden units (ReLU) -> 20 hypotheses (2-D points) + 20 scores
audio: 4-ch first-order Ambisonics, 44.1 kHz, 30 s files cut into 0.5 s chunks, STFT (Hann 0.04 s, 50 % overlap, 2048 bins, amplitude and phase stacked)
       -> SELDnet-style CRNN (about 1.5 M parameters) -> T = 25 output frames per chunk, final FC layers duplicated per hypothesis (5 hypotheses: about +4 k parameters)
```
- Hypotheses and scores are trained **jointly** (total loss = WTA term + beta x scoring term, beta = 1). Whether the score loss is stopped from reaching the trunk is not stated; the text says "jointly", so by default the score gradient reaches the trunk.
- Targets: toy, 1 or 2 points per input (2 with probability q(t), piecewise affine, q(0) = q(1) = 1, q(1/2) = 0); audio, the source directions (azimuth / elevation) active in a frame, 1 to 3 sources. The number of targets varies per input; there is no padding rule in the text (the appendix says only that metrics are taken on active frames).

### Equations (numbered as in the paper; written in plain text)
Notation: x_s input, Y_s the set of targets of x_s, f^k hypothesis k (k = 1..K), gamma^k its score, l a per-target loss.
- **E1 (single-target WTA, sMCL):** L(f(x_s), y_s) = min over k of l(f^k(x_s), y_s).
- **E2 (multi-target WTA, "Firman et al."):** L(f(x_s), Y_s) = sum over y in Y_s of sum over k = 1..K of 1[y in Y^k(x_s)] * l(f^k(x_s), y). Each target is assigned **independently** to the hypothesis whose cell it falls in, so two targets may go to the same hypothesis and some hypotheses get none. No normalisation by |Y_s| or K is written.
- **E3 (Voronoi cell):** Y^k(x) = { y : l(f^k(x), y) < l(f^r(x), y) for all r != k } (strict inequality, so a tie belongs to no cell; training tie handling is not stated).
- **E4 (risk, from Rupprecht et al.):** the integral over x of the sum over k of the integral over Y^k(x) of l(f^k(x), y) p(x, y); for squared L2 it is minimised, for cells of non-zero mass, when f^k is the conditional mean of y inside its cell.
- **E5 (winner and loser sets):** K+(x_s) = { k : there is y in Y_s with k in argmin_r l(f^r(x_s), y) }; K-(x_s) = {1..K} minus K+(x_s). Ties: every tied index is in K+ by this definition.
- **E6 (scoring loss):** L_scoring = - [ sum over k in K+ of log gamma^k(x_s) + sum over k in K- of log(1 - gamma^k(x_s)) ]. A **sum** over all K heads (not a mean), target 1 for winners, 0 for losers.
- **Total:** L = L_WTA (E2) + beta * L_scoring (E6), beta = 1 in both experiments.
- **E7 (reading of the outputs):** gamma^k(x) = P(Y in Y^k(x) | x) and f^k(x) = E[Y | Y in Y^k(x), x] (cell probability, cell conditional mean). **E8:** total expectation = sum over k of gamma^k f^k.
- **Proposition 1** (equation numbers 9 - 10 not seen): if inside cell k the target follows a law pi_k with mean f^k, then P(Y in A) = integral over A of sum_k gamma^k pi_k. Assumptions: finite-volume output space, hypotheses at the geometric centre of their cells, non-zero cell probabilities, train and test from the same joint law. **E11** (pi_k uniform inside the cell, maximum-entropy argument): p(y | x) = sum_k gamma^k 1[y in Y^k(x)] / Vol(Y^k(x)). **E12** (pi_k a Dirac at f^k): p(y | x) = sum_k gamma^k delta_{f^k(x)}(y).

### The score target, step by step (what E5 and E6 mean in an implementation)
1. For every target y in Y_s compute the K distances l(f^k(x_s), y); take the argmin hypothesis w(y). No gradient flows through the choice.
2. Regression term: the sum over targets of l at the winner (E2); the gradient reaches only the winners, each as many times as it wins.
3. Winner set K+ = the distinct values of w(y); loser set K- = all others.
4. Score target: 1 for k in K+, 0 for k in K-; BCE on the K sigmoid outputs, summed (E6). The target is a **multi-label** vector, not a one-hot: with two targets won by two different hypotheses both scores are pushed to 1.
5. **rMCL\***: instead of all of K-, use **one** loser drawn uniformly from K- per training sample (all other negative score heads get no update that step). Memory saving, and relief of the class imbalance |K-| >> |K+|. Whether the draw is per sample or per batch and whether the negative term is re-weighted: not stated. On the toy task it performs comparably to rMCL.
6. Special cases (derived by me from E2, E5, E6): |Y_s| = 1: E2 equals E1, K+ has one element (two with a tie), the score target is (1 for the winner, 0 for the K-1 others) and the score head learns "am I the nearest hypothesis"; |Y_s| = 0: K+ is empty, all scores are pushed to 0, the sum of the raw scores is 0; |Y_s| >= K: every hypothesis may be a winner, no negatives. K = 2 with one target: one positive and one negative, balanced, and rMCL* is identical to rMCL.
7. The sum of the **raw** (un-normalised) scores estimates the number of sources (Appendix A.2 / A.3); the sum-to-one normalisation is an inference-time step for the mixture reading, it is not part of training.

### Inference (Algorithm 1, digest)
Forward pass; build the Voronoi cells from the hypotheses (E3); normalise gamma^k <- gamma^k / sum_j gamma^j; output the mixture of E12 (or E11). Hypotheses with score 0 drop out. No NMS, no top-k, no threshold in the main text; Appendix B.2 mentions zeroing "very low-score hypotheses given an arbitrary threshold" for the outlier experiment only. Metrics: **EMD** = Wasserstein-1 between the predicted mixture sum_k gamma^k delta_{f^k} and the uniform mixture on the target points (cost = spherical distance in audio, l2 in the toy); **oracle error** = per target the minimum distance over all hypotheses, averaged (the score plays no role). Metrics are computed on active frames only. The unit of the audio errors was not seen (spherical distance; presumably degrees).

### Variants and combinations
- eps-WTA (relaxed WTA, Rupprecht et al.): the non-winners get their gradient scaled by a small eps (weights (1 - eps) for the winner and eps / (K - 1) for each other head are the usual definition: **memory**, not seen in this paper's text). Baseline setting eps = 0.5. top-n WTA: the n best heads are updated (n = 3); the idea of a decreasing n is attributed to Evolving WTA. PIT variant (Schymura et al.) as a baseline.
- **top-n rMCL and eps-rMCL** (Section 4.4) are only named; how the score target is built in them (does K+ change?) is **not found** in the text I could query. The paper only says the rMCL improvement is in theory orthogonal to the other WTA variants.

### Training recipe
- Toy: Adam, 100,000 training / 25,000 validation samples, batch 1,024, 20 epochs, checkpoint with the lowest validation loss, l = l2, beta = 1; methods compared: sMCL, independent ensemble (IE, 20 single-hypothesis models), rMCL, rMCL*. EMD evaluated at 50 equally spaced t with 1,000 ground-truth samples; the cell centroids from 35,000 samples per input. Number of seeds: not stated.
- Audio: AdamW, batch 128, initial learning rate 0.05 with the Vaswani warm-up schedule (details not given), beta = 1, 300 recordings of 30 s per training split of which 60 are validation, NVIDIA A100, about 2,000 GPU hours in total including failed runs. Epoch count, weight decay, checkpoint rule, the training loss l for audio: **not stated** in the text I could query. Metrics: mean +- std over test samples (not over seeds), three training runs mentioned in B.3.

### Starvation / collapse: what the paper says and does
- Definition (B.1): one or a few hypotheses win almost always, the others are rarely updated and the diversity shrinks. Measure: for each hypothesis k the count N_k of validation samples it wins; the negative entropy of this histogram (near-uniform = no collapse, one bin = maximal collapse).
- Observation: **no collapse** in the audio experiments for WTA or rMCL (Figure B.1: 20-hypothesis model on ANSYN, "high entropy"; no numeric entropy given). The paper puts this down to data variability and stochastic training (citing Ilg et al.). It says nothing about how the number of hypotheses affects collapse and **proposes no remedy of its own**; the remedies named are relaxed WTA and top-n WTA from the literature and the combinations of Section 4.4.
- Outliers (B.2, toy with bivariate Cauchy outliers at rho = 0.02): some hypotheses absorbed the outliers and the score heads gave them low scores, so the predicted distribution was not damaged; this holds when outliers are rare and far from the data.
- The paper does not use the word starvation; the closest passage is the class imbalance of the score heads (section 3.2). The failure "a hypothesis never wins, so its position never moves" is the one the aMCL paper describes (below).

### aMCL (briefly; digest)
- **Loss.** l = squared Euclidean distance. Boltzmann soft assignment q_T(k | x, y) = exp(-l(f_k(x), y) / T) / sum_s exp(-l(f_s(x), y) / T) (Eq. 5 / 17); per-sample loss L = sum_k q_T(k | x, y) * l(f_k(x), y) (Eq. 18, Appendix A.2) with q **detached** (stop-gradient), so every hypothesis receives a gradient weighted by q_T. T -> 0 gives hard WTA, T -> infinity gives uniform weights and every hypothesis goes to E[Y | x]. One target per (x, y) pair; the multi-target case is not discussed.
- **Theory.** aMCL is an entropy-constrained block-coordinate descent (deterministic annealing); each f_k is a soft barycentre of y under q_T; the hypotheses split into subgroups at critical temperatures; the **first critical temperature** is T_c(x) = 2 * lambda_max(C(x)), C(x) the covariance of Y given x (Eq. 14 - 15, squared L2). The final solution is WTA.
- **Schedules** (constant within an epoch). UCI: exponential T = T0 * rho^t, T0 = 0.5, rho = 0.95, 1,000 epochs, training **reverts to plain WTA when T falls below 5e-4**. Synthetic: exponential T0 = 0.6, rho = 0.99 (Figure 1); linear T = T0 (1 - t / t_epoch), T0 = 1.0, 1,000 epochs, 49 hypotheses (Figures 3 - 4: T of about 0.950, 0.256, 0.131, 0.001 at epochs 50, 745, 870, 1,000). Speech: "chunk-linear" (Eq. 43), T0 about 0.1, annealing switched off after t_max = 100 of 200 epochs (so the second half is plain WTA), Adam 1e-3, batch 2, 3 seeds, n = m = 3 outputs. T0 sweep in the phase-transition study: {0.1, 5, 23} with rho = 0.9 (curves only). No rule for choosing the schedule; the paper says it is coupled to optimiser and learning rate and leaves schedule design open.
- **Scores.** Appendix A.2 reportedly keeps the rMCL BCE loss with the hard Voronoi-cell indicator; the speech experiments state that no scoring model is used; a score target under soft assignment is not defined in the parts I could read.
- **Collapse.** MCL collapsed for some speech seeds (std 0.59 against 0.10 - 0.13 for PIT and aMCL on 2 speakers); in Figure 5 the unused hypotheses "stop receiving gradient and stay stuck at the barycentre"; annealing "does not solve" the collapse of relaxed WTA (Appendix B.3, no numbers). Relaxed WTA's cost: bias towards the global barycentre, worse distortion.

### 5.1 What the loss does with b = 2 children (derived)
Let a tip have m GT elements in Y_s (m = 0, 1, 2, ...). The four cases:
- **m = 0** (tip off a lane or lane ends): K+ empty, no position term, both scores are pushed to 0. This agrees with an end-of-lane tip and with the low weight our losses already give to tips that are not on a lane.
- **m = 1** (the common case): E2 = E1. The winner is the nearer child, the other child is the loser. Score target (1, 0). The position gradient reaches the winner only. The score head learns the binary question "which child is nearer", and the loser's score says **"redundant"**, which is the behaviour we want for a plain lane (a short graph has one child). If the two children are near-duplicates the winner flips from sample to sample and both scores learn about 0.5 (the sum stays about 1): a deterministic tie-break is needed (lower index, or the longer child, which is what `dup_label` does by rule).
- **m = 2, different winners** (a fork whose branches are separated by the children): K+ = both, score target (1, 1), each child is pulled to its branch. This is the only case in which a second child is trained to be needed.
- **m = 2, same winner** (shallow fork, or the second child sits on the wrong side): K+ = {A}, A is pulled between the branches (the sum of two plain distances is minimised anywhere on the segment joining them, the sum of squared distances at the midpoint), B gets target 0 and no gradient on its position; nothing moves B to the uncovered branch. This is the collapse case. Monitor: the fraction of fork tips with |K+| = 1.
No published run shows this behaviour for K = 2. The aMCL theory says why cooling helps: at high T both children are pulled to the mean of the GT set (for a fork to the midpoint between the branches) and separate only below T_c = 2 lambda_max(C). Derived arithmetic for a fork with two branch ends a distance d apart and probabilities p and 1 - p: lambda_max = p (1 - p) d^2, so T_c = 2 p (1 - p) d^2, d^2 / 2 for equal branches, in squared-distance units (m^2 here). For a unique GT continuation C = 0: there is no transition, both children stay together as duplicates for any T > 0 and rely on the final WTA phase and a duplicate rule to separate. The softmin must be formed from the **squared** distance (as in the paper) even if the gradient loss stays our smooth-L1 on the distance (`beta` 0.25, clamp 3 m).
Options to keep the second child alive, to be compared on the fork scene test T27 of the plans (the two children cover different branches in at least 95 % of 200 instances), cheapest first:
1. **Geometric separation by construction:** if child slot j can be tied to a sector of the teardrop (an anchor, as MultiPath / MTR do), the Voronoi cells are separated before training and WTA only chooses between sectors. Whether the builder allows this was not checked.
2. **Relaxed share** (the coded eps = 0.1): the non-winner is pulled towards the same lane; keeps it alive and produces duplicates. In the paper's own combination eps = 0.5 helps EMD and hurts oracle error strongly (RESYN D3 14.6 to 26.92).
3. **Annealed softmin (aMCL):** exponential or linear cooling on the squared distance. Published horizons are 1,000 epochs (UCI, synthetic) and 100 + 100 epochs (speech, annealing off after the first half). Our budget in iterations and the effect of a short cooling are unknown; end in a plain-WTA phase (the published runs do: floor 5e-4 on UCI).
4. A 2 x 2 closed-form assignment of the two branches to the two children (compare the two permutations) would force distinct winners, but it is a matching in disguise and conflicts with the standing rule; listed only so nobody proposes it again.

### 5.2 Responsibility score versus existence / validity (how to attach the validity head)
Three quantities that must keep separate heads (this is rule R2 and conflict 4 of the compatibility study):

| quantity | question it answers | target | depends on siblings? | where it lives now |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ray verification / hazard | does the lane exist along this ray sample | tgt (gd < 0.6 and direction agrees), cumulative-product weights | no | `l_ver` in `level_losses` |
| child validity nu | does this child's segment lie on a GT lane | `tgt_c` = end within 0.8 m of the downstream set and four chord samples on the lane (optionally minus a longer valid sibling: `dup_label`) | no (except the duplicate rule) | `child_nu`, `l_val` |
| rMCL responsibility gamma | is this child the nearest child of at least one GT element | K+ indicator from the distance matrix | **yes** | missing |

- Keep nu as it is; add gamma as a second independent sigmoid on the same trunk; both are trained against detached labels (no gradient through the label construction). Never softmax over the children, never sum to one: the raw multi-label form is what lets a fork carry two true children (target (1, 1)).
- A child's keep weight can then be pi = nu * gamma (product of two calibrated-by-construction BCE probabilities; the calibration of the product is untested, as for the hazard chain in the overview, open item 4). gamma would replace the rule-based `dup_label` ("a child on the chord of a longer valid sibling is the same hypothesis twice") by a nearest-child rule that needs no length convention.
- The two heads disagree in exactly two informative cases: nu = 1 and gamma = 0 is a **duplicate** (on the lane but not needed); nu = 0 and gamma = 1 is a **needed child in a wrong place** (a recall failure, the nearest child is still far from its branch). The paper has no distance gate for K+; the nearest hypothesis counts as a winner however far it is. For us a gate is advisable: count a child in K+ only if its distance to the winning GT element is below the radius the position loss already uses (`near_`, 2.5 m), otherwise a far-off child would be labelled "needed" and the score would mean only "nearest". This is my design choice, not from the paper.
- Use of the sum: sum of the raw gamma over the children minus one is a per-tip fork signal at no label cost (compare it with the junction head; do not replace the junction head before it has been measured); the sum near zero marks a tip that continues nowhere.

### 5.3 Matching-free implementation on a distance tensor (own formulation)
Given per tip: D[h, g] = distance (m) between child h (the segment from the tip to the child end, sampled) and GT element g, point-to-polyline in both directions as in our losses (child samples to the GT polyline, GT samples to the child segment, averaged); a validity mask M[g] for the m real elements.
```
w[g]    = argmin_h D[h, g]                 # label side, no gradient, ties to the lowest index
L_wta   = sum_g M[g] * rho(D[w[g], g])     # rho = the existing smooth-L1 (beta 0.25, clamp 3 m); gradient reaches the winning children
K_plus[h] = max_g M[g] * (w[g] == h) * (D[h, g] < r_gate)      # r_gate about 2.5 m, my choice
L_score = sum_h BCE_with_logits(s[h], K_plus[h])               # sum over the b children, averaged over valid tips
```
- Everything except the argmin index is a gather on D and a BCE: smooth in the child positions and in the logits, cost b x m entries per tip, negligible against 31 ms. The relaxed variant (eps share for the non-winners) or the aMCL variant (q = softmax(-D^2 / T) detached, L = sum_h q[h, g] rho(D[h, g])) replaces the first line and the second line without touching the score target.
- **Two ways to build the GT element set**, both without Hungarian matching: (A) branch polylines (needs a branch id per downstream GT point, not in the lines of `p16_loss.py` I read; derivable from the geodesic matrix and the fork landmarks), (B) **point targets**: the lane points of the downstream ring (10 - 16 m) as in the existing FC term, each point a target. In (B) E2 is exactly "every ring point pulls its nearest child end", which is what `l_cov` already computes (nearest child end per lane point, 1.2 m slack); the missing pieces are the score target from the same assignment, the extension from fork tips to all tips, and the weight normalisation (mean over the points of a branch, not a sum, otherwise long branches dominate).
- Monitoring that costs nothing and should be logged from the first run: winner share per child slot and the negative entropy of that histogram (the paper's measure, B.1), the fraction of fork tips with |K+| = 1, and the confusion matrix of nu against gamma.
