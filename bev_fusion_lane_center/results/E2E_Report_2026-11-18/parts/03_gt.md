# 3. Required ground truth

**In short.** nuScenes provides the HD map, the recorded poses and the camera and radar data, but no lane-graph labels. A generator derives them: directed centre lines with split, merge, stop and exit events, which lane the car really drives, what the cameras can see, and for junctions the zone, the arms and the valid entry-to-exit pairs. The same graph code makes the training targets and the scoring reference, so a number is always measured against the label it was trained on. The token head additionally needs the 2.0 m lattice table, a static assignment of tokens to GT nodes and the link targets (below). The picture shows the labels of one junction frame; the details, the checks and the limits follow.

### 3.0 A short guide: how the ground truth of a lane graph must look
*Written for anyone who starts from a lane graph and has to label (or generate) the data. Every item below is what this project's label holds, and what it bought; the numbers are the ones of sections 3.1 to 3.7.*

**1. Lines, with a direction.** One ordered polyline per lane, **in travel order** (the direction is part of the label: without it a reversed lane scores as correct), a point every 0.5 m, in the ego frame of the sensor time, in a fixed window (50 m around the car). The line is the lane **centre**, not the paint (map arcs can be 0.3 - 0.7 m off the paint; label one convention and keep it). Lines are cut at the window border and the cut end is marked as such.

**2. Points that must be annotated (events), each with a position and the directions in and out.** Where the topology changes: **split, merge, start and end of a lane** (a natural end, not a cut), **window crossing**, and at a junction the **stop** (end of the lane before the junction) and the **exit** (start of the lane after it). A lane that ends silently, with no event, is the most frequent error of a label; check that every natural end has one (here 0 of 5,755 without).

**3. Relations, not only lines.** Successor / predecessor of every piece (a piece changes its id only at a split or a merge), left / right / opposite neighbours, lane index and lane count, road id. The links are what the model must predict; the neighbours and the count are what an SD map can be matched with.

**4. Regions (polygons or masks), four kinds.**
(a) the **junction zone** and inside it the **conflict core** (where connectors of different maneuvers cross); (b) **road and intersection areas** and the **drivable boundary**; (c) **dividers and, where present, stop lines and crossings**, as evidence and not as truth (entries and exits without paint: about 61 % and 81 %); (d) a **visibility mask**: ground that at least one camera sees and no object hides. Without (d) a lane behind a truck is counted as a model error instead of an unobservable one.

**5. Junctions need pairs.** Annotate the **entry and exit points** with their heading, group them into arms with a rank across the arm, and give the **valid entry -> exit pairs**. One curve per true pair matches the junction at correlation 0.88, all geometrically possible pairs only 0.53; the order rule (pairs inside one maneuver keep their order) is a free consistency check on the label (1899 of 1899 maneuvers hold it).

**6. Labels that must never be an input.** The **tier** of a point (1: the lane the car really drives, 2: its neighbours, 3: the rest) reads the future path of the car; it weights the loss and gives the own-lane score, and nothing at inference may read it.

**7. Resolution and checks.** Decide the grid the model works on before labelling (here 2 m nodes; what it loses: topo F1 0.0190, a 1 m grid buys nothing) and score the representation against the raw label first, as a ceiling. Every change of code or parameters is a new label version with its own signature; a trained model names it.

**Minimum and extra.** The minimum for a lane graph is items 1 to 3 (directed lines, events, links). Item 5 is what lifts the junction (sharper end targets alone raised the keypoint F1 from 0.630 / 0.642 to 0.723 / 0.705), items 4 and 6 give the planner's own lane and an honest score, item 7 keeps the numbers comparable.

**Why this chapter comes first.** A lane-graph head learns only what its label says. nuScenes has no lane-graph label for a camera system: it has a map (lanes, connectors, dividers) and the ego pose. Everything the head is trained and scored on, **directed centre lines, which piece continues into which, where lanes split, merge or end, which entry of a junction meets which exit, which lane is the one the car drives**, has to be derived from the map first. The GT is therefore the largest hidden part of the work, and the numbers in this report are only as good as it is: the same graph code makes the training targets and the scoring reference (E346: lanes are exact, the remaining loss sits in connectors and junctions).

**The E2E view (what the token champion reads from this GT).** The champion's head sees the predicted nodes of the frozen lane head B' (2 m grid) as tokens and is trained against the same ground truth, in three forms (E365 card, section 1): (a) the GT node table on the **2.0 m lattice** (position, heading, flags: split / merge / stop / exit / start / end, piece, links with their chord length and fitted handle scales); (b) a **static assignment** of tokens to GT nodes (greedy by descending existence of B', nearest unassigned GT node within 1.5 m and heading cosine >= 0.5), which gives the node-type label (7 classes: none, lane point, fork, merge, junction entry, junction exit, boundary) and the existence target; (c) the **link targets**: for a source token the positives are every token within 1.5 m of a GT successor (the GT links are first contracted over GT nodes that no token covers), separately for short (chord <= 8 m) and long (> 8 m, junction) links. Matching inside the loss is allowed (D55). Nothing of this table is read at inference: the read-out writer takes positions and headings from the tokens only (a mutant test puts a GT field into the prediction and must fail). The token cache of the champion was built from the GT version with the signature `9ad1d4ce30` (part of the cache folder name `runs/e365_cache/be292_a_{0,1}_K512_9ad1d4ce30_v1`). How much of the GT the token set can reach (E365 manifest, VAL, B' seed 0): 94.3 % of the GT links have both ends covered and lie inside the admissible-pair gate; 0.952 of the GT nodes are assigned to a token.

### 3.1 What the GT must contain, and why the model needs it
| what the GT holds | why it is needed (what breaks without it) |
|---|---|
| **Directed centre lines**, ordered polylines per lane, one point every 0.5 m in travel order, in the ego frame of the keyframe (x right, y forward, rear axle at the front-camera time), window \|x\|, \|y\| <= 50 m, cut at the border | The product is a graph of lane centres a planner can follow. Direction is part of the label: without it a reversed lane scores as correct and the planner may drive against traffic. A fixed frame and window make every frame comparable and let the head work on a fixed grid |
| **Pieces and ids:** a piece is a chain of map lanes and connectors joined while every join is 1 in, 1 out; a new id only at a split or a merge | The head must deliver *lanes*, not map fragments. Fragment boundaries of the map (arc joints) are not lane boundaries; an id that changes only at a split / merge is what "one lane" means and what the section loss trains |
| **Events** at the joins: split, merge, cross, stop, exit, start, end, window (the polyline crosses the border) | These are the places where the topology changes. They get a node of their own with a type flag, so the head learns *where* a lane ends or forks (the weak point of the plain graph: 25 - 33 % of the end flags are still missing) |
| **Structure:** successor / predecessor, left / right / opposite neighbours, lane index and lane count, road id | Successors give the links the head must predict; neighbours, index and count give the map assignment (which map lane is this?) and the own-lane evaluation |
| **Junction layer** (lane side, D82): zone, conflict core, arms with lane ranks, entry and exit points, the valid entry -> exit pairs, the order rule | The plain graph hides which entry meets which exit. One curve per true pair matches the junction at correlation 0.88, all geometrically possible pairs only 0.53 (54 candidate pairs per frame, 12.7 true; E283). The pair head, the keypoints and the maneuver metric all need the pairs |
| **Relevance tiers** per point: 1 = the ego lane on the path the car really drives, 2 = its left / right neighbours (same direction, within 10 m), 3 = the rest | A planner needs the own lane first. Tiers give a weighted loss and a separate "own lane" score (E276). They read the future path of the car, so they are **labels only, never an input** |
| **Visibility** per point (`vis`, `occ`): seen by at least one of six cameras and not hidden by an annotated vehicle / pedestrian / two-wheeler box | Hidden or unseen ground cannot be learned from images; without `vis` a lane behind a truck is a false negative of the model instead of an unobservable one |
| **A fixed resolution of the label:** nodes on a 2 m grid (one per cell, event points win), junction maps at 1 m | Topo F1, lanes found, junction F1 and maneuvers are then all defined on one graph, and the head is a dense network on a grid. What the 2 m code loses is measured (section 1.7) |

### 3.2 What nuScenes gives and what it lacks
| nuScenes gives | nuScenes lacks, and how it was derived |
|---|---|
| map expansion layers `lane`, `lane_connector` (arc paths and connectivity), `road_segment` (`is_intersection`), `lane_divider`, `road_divider`, `drivable_area` | **No road graph:** the edge-line tokens of 0 of 3,662 lanes match a road block; in two of the four maps the road segments are only intersections. Roads, neighbours and lane counts are **derived from the centre lines** (`road_graph.py`) |
| connectors through junctions (about 80 - 84 % of the connectors lie in an intersection; 16 - 20 % are plain continuations) | **No boundary record:** the road boundary is the outline of the union of the drivable-area polygons |
| ego pose per sample, six camera calibrations, annotated 3D boxes | **No junction pairs as objects, no tiers, no visibility:** derived from the connectors, the future ego poses (25 m back, 55 m ahead) and the boxes |
| | **Centre line is not paint:** a map arc. Boston lines are 0.3 - 0.7 m off the paint in places; no correction exists |

### 3.3 How the GT is made: the recipe (code: `GtGenerator/`, lane GT `l6`)
Inputs per keyframe: the nuScenes map of the location, the CAM_FRONT ego pose at the keyframe, the CAM_FRONT ego poses around it (tiers), the annotated boxes (visibility). No image and no radar.
1. **Read the map layers** (`lane_graph.py`): lanes and lane connectors with their arc paths and connectivity. **Connectors are not added: nuScenes has them.** A connector counts as inside an intersection when at least half of its points lie in an intersection road segment.
2. **Join pieces:** chain map pieces while each join is 1 in, 1 out; the id of a piece is the token of its first map piece. Per piece the index ranges of its points per map piece (`segments`, with the intersection flag) are kept, so a consumer can drop connector points inside intersections.
3. **Transform and cut** with the ego pose into the lane frame, window \|x\|, \|y\| <= 50 m (`half = 50`), resample every 0.5 m in travel order. A part cut by the window at both ends is dropped when shorter than 2 m (`min_len`); a short part with a natural end stays, so links and events never dangle.
4. **Events:** split / merge where a join has several in or out; cross (n in, n out); start / end where the map has no predecessor / successor; window where the polyline crosses the border; **stop** = the end of a lane whose successor connector lies inside an intersection, **exit** = the start of a lane whose predecessor connector does (for several successors the stop is for the intersection branches only).
5. **Derive roads, neighbours, lane count and index** from the centre lines (shared dividers; check: of 730 lane pairs that share a divider node on nuScenes mini, 723 are found as right neighbours and none as left). Left / right / opposite are symmetric; `lane_index` 0 = leftmost.
6. **Lines and areas:** `lane_divider`, `road_divider` and the boundary cut to the window with corners (turning angle above 30 degrees over +-2 m); road and intersection areas as polygons, not dilated (the head rasterises them itself).
7. **Tiers and visibility** (`lane_tiers.py`, labels on the same graph): tier-1 = the lane the car really drives, from the CAM_FRONT ego poses 25 m before and 55 m after the keyframe (where the car stands or the sequence ends the path continues along the map, the straightest successor, to 55 m; `tier_src` 2); tier 2 = left / right neighbours of a tier-1 piece within 10 m of a tier-1 point; tier 3 = the rest. `vis` = at least one of the six cameras sees the ground point and no box hides it (the ground under a box is hidden; a rear box hides for the rear cameras). Parameters: `tier_back 25, tier_ahead 55, path_tol 1.5, path_cos 0.7, neighbour_tol 10, min_ahead 40, extend True, opposite_tier 3`. l6 decides the tiers on a wide window and carries them to the point, so no cut of the window changes a tier.
8. **Junction layer** (lane side, `training/grid/jgt.py`, D82; GtGenerator itself is not edited): an **entry** is the first point of a connector run, an **exit** its last; ends are clustered (1.5 m, 40 degrees); an **arm** is a single-linkage group of ends with headings within 25 degrees, at most 6 m apart along and 6 m across the heading (entries and exits separately); an entry arm and an exit arm with opposite headings (at least 155 degrees) and centres within 14 m are the same arm. The **zone** is the union of the connector runs grown by 1.8 m (closing), the **conflict core** is where connectors of different pairs cross, merge or diverge. **Order rule** (written before the numbers): pairs inside one (entry arm, exit arm) maneuver keep their order, entry rank i before i' implies exit rank j <= j'; where k entries meet k exits the solution is entry i -> exit i. It holds on 1899 of 1899 multi-pair maneuvers on VAL.
9. **Store and version:** `python -m GtGenerator generate-lane ROOT --rig lane_center` (`GtGenerator.generate_lane_gt`), resumable, written as `data/autogt/lane/<signature>/<token>.pkl.gz`. The signature is the hash of the six source files plus every parameter that differs from the defaults, so **any change of the code or a parameter is a new GT with its own folder**: a cache or a trained model always names the signature it was made with. Current: lane l6, signature 9ad1d4ce30, release v14, 34,149 keyframes (all of nuScenes trainval), generated in 28 min (D83); about 40 ms per keyframe on one thread.

### 3.4 The record of one keyframe (what the stored graph holds)
| field | content |
|---|---|
| `frame`, `half`, `location`, `sample`, `signature`, `params` | "lane" (x right, y forward), 50 m, map name, sample token, version hash, the parameters |
| `pieces[]` | `id`, `piece` (stable id of the piece), `kind` (lane / connector / mixed), `pts` (n, 2) float32 every 0.5 m, `start_open` / `end_open` (cut by the window), `length`, `segments`, `succ` / `pred`, `left` / `right` / `opposite`, `road`, `lane_count`, `lane_index`, and per point `tier` (uint8), `vis`, `occ`, `tier_src` |
| `events[]` | `type` (split / merge / cross / stop / exit / start / end / window), `pos`, `in` / `out` (piece ids), `dirs_in` / `dirs_out` (unit vectors), `tier`, `vis`; stop / exit also `on`, `s`, `n_succ` / `n_pred` |
| `roads[]`, `lines`, `areas` | roads and intersections of the window (derived), dividers and boundary with corners, road and intersection polygons |
| `ego_path`, `tier_info` | the driven path used for the tiers; metres per tier, visible metres per tier |
| junction layer (computed on top, `jgt.py`) | `zones`, `ent` / `ext` (x, y, tx, ty), arms with ranks, `pairs` (entry, exit, arms, ranks, turn, `driven`), `core`, `ent_vis` / `ext_vis`, `order` |

### 3.5 From the GT to training targets: by location, no matching
Every target is placed **by where it lies**, so no loss contains a matching step between prediction and GT (`qtargets.py`, `qjunction.py`):
- **Node of a 2 m cell:** per piece and half-open 2 m cell the point nearest to the cell centre; event points win their cell; at most one node per cell. Points of connector segments inside an intersection give no node; event points (stop, exit, split, merge, start, end, window) are always nodes. Flags (stop, exit, split, merge, start, end, win_in, win_out); events at the same place are one node with several flags.
- **Links:** a node to every node reached next along the points of the pieces (through the suppressed points and over the joins of the successors); a link longer than 8 m is a long link (a connector through a junction) with two learned handle scales. **Section start:** the first node of a piece (s = 1). The decode of these targets back to Hermite curves covers 99.98 % of the GT lane points within 0.3 m (the representation check).
- **Junction maps (1 m, 100 x 100):** entry / exit heat (Gaussian at the end points, sigma 1.5 m in E286, **1.0 m** in the champion, E307), heading (tx, ty) within 2 sigma of an end, junction zone and conflict core, 0.5 m masks of stop lines and pedestrian crossings (the map expansion's polygons, `data/prep/junction_paint.py`).
- **Pair targets:** the true entry -> exit pairs of `jgt.py`; the keypoints of the network are matched to the GT ends only inside the loss (4 m, 30 degrees, one to one), never in the delivered graph.
- **Simulated SD map** (only for the assignment head): the GT graph with a scene-constant translation (sigma 1 - 2 m), yaw error, thinned shape points, 5 % pieces dropped and 5 % wrong lane counts (`map_sim.py`).

### 3.6 How to know a rebuild is right: the checks
- **Self-tests** (`python -m GtGenerator selftest`; `lane_graph_selftest.py`): a made-up map with split, merge, stop, join and window cut; the points lie on the nuScenes centre lines (median 0.01 m, 99 % within 0.05 m both ways); the junction events rebuild the map's junctions (F1 0.99); neighbours against the map's divider tokens; the store (generate, resume, load, another parameter = another folder). Against the lane project's older graph code 47 of 107,538 points differ by more than 0.3 m (pieces shorter than 2 m).
- **Pinned digests:** `lane_l4_digest.json` (40 frames) and `lane_l6_digest.json`; a field-by-field comparison of 12 sequences against the previous version names every difference.
- **Junction layer:** the order rule holds 1899 / 1899 on VAL; 826 of 873 VAL frames have at least one GT maneuver, an entry -> exit connection ("junction frame, maneuver definition"; chapter 3 splits its situations by GT junction nodes instead); the pairs of l6 equal l4 on 417 / 417 of 440 checked VAL frames; every natural piece end has an event (0 of 5,755 without one).
- **Representation ceiling (E346, 873 VAL frames, "GT replay"):** the lane GT scored against the old graph GT gives topo F1 0.9858; its 2 m node + link code scored against the lane GT 0.9669 (-0.0190), lanes found -0.0146, junction F1 0.9535 against 0.9995; a 1 m grid gives 0.9693 (+0.0024, buys nothing). A network cannot beat the representation, so these are the ceilings the head numbers must be read against.

### 3.7 Limits and what they cost
| item | number (source) |
|---|---|
| Lane GT vs old graph GT | topo F1 0.9858 (E346 g1) |
| 2 m node + link code | topo F1 0.9669 (-0.0190), lanes found -0.0146, junction F1 0.9535 vs 0.9995 |
| 1 m grid | 0.9693: +0.0024, buys nothing |
| Node chains in the junction | 57.2 - 59.7 % of connectors collide: rejected |
| Entries / exits without paint | about 61 % / 81 % (E285): the paint is evidence, not truth |
| Ends with several partners | 27 % / 33 %: a one-to-one assignment fails |
| Cameras see no ground within about 4.5 m | `vis` is False there (not "hidden"; `occ` is False) |
| Largest gap in the map | 6.3 m; nuScenes intersection polygons cover a median 27 % of the road area |

- **Centre line is not paint:** the label is a map arc; lines can be 0.3 - 0.7 m off the paint, no correction exists. Scores near the border of a lane measure the map as much as the network.
- **Tiers are non-causal:** they read the future ego path. They weight the loss and select the own-lane score; nothing at inference reads them.
- **Junction smear (E283, D71):** the blur is the unknown entry-exit pair. The GT's flags are complete; the 25 - 33 % missing end flags are the head's defect.
- **A real SD map would lack** lane-level lines, connectors, tiers and visibility (reading, not measured). Ours is simulated from this GT with controlled errors; D82 asks for a road-level variant with lane counts only. Open at HEAD: `test_qtargets.py` is red (not caused by E346).

### 3.8 What changed in the GT during this work
| version | what changed | why |
|---|---|---|
| l3 | the base graph: pieces, ids, events, neighbours, roads, lines, areas | a lane graph from the map |
| l4 (2026-11-07, D59 / D60) | **tiers and camera visibility** added as labels; geometry identical to l3 (checked on every test keyframe) | the own lane first (E276); no loss on ground no camera can see |
| l5 (2026-11-07) | two rigs, one store each (Spurmitte on the GaussianCaR window, BEV Fusion rectangular); the Spurmitte rig equals l4 file by file | two systems, one generator |
| junction layer (D82, lane side, `jgt.py`) | **zone, arms, entry / exit points, valid pairs, order rule** computed on top of the graph; later the end-heat targets narrowed from sigma 1.5 m to **1.0 m** (E307) | the plain graph hides which entry meets which exit; sharper end targets raised the keypoint F1 of the maps head from 0.630 / 0.642 to 0.723 / 0.705 (E307) |
| l6 (D78, current since D83) | corrections: no repeated points, unit event directions, junction events at the branch end, every stop / exit with its in and out, better lane index and count for connector pieces; all 34,149 keyframes regenerated | cleaner directions and entry / exit points; the junction pairs equal l4's on 417 of 417 comparable frames (of 440 checked on VAL) |

Every change is a new signature (a new store folder); a trained model names the signature it was made with.
