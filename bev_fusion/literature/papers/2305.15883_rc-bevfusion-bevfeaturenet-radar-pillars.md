**What it does**: A radar branch that plugs into camera-only BEV detectors (BEVDet etc.). Five sweeps (300 ms) are aggregated; each point carries 9 features: x, y, RCS, v_d (radial velocity), t_s (timestamp offset), plus offsets to the pillar mean (x_c, y_c) and to the pillar centre (x_p, y_p). Points go into a BEV grid; up to B = 2000 non-empty cells with N_p = 10 points each, a simplified PointNet (1x1 conv, BN, ReLU) with C = 32 channels, max over points, scatter back, then a conv backbone. The page notes that nuScenes radar is 2+1D (no z, no reflectivity measure).

**Results that matter** (nuScenes val, BEVDet* camera-only to camera + BEVFeatureNet): mAP 0.350 -> 0.434, NDS 0.411 -> 0.525, mATE 0.660 -> 0.511, mAVE 0.918 -> 0.421 (-54 %), mAAE 0.260 -> 0.182. Latency on RTX 2080 Ti 122 -> 139 ms (+17 ms). It performs about as well as a hand-built RadarGridMap and is recommended for needing less hand-crafted input. No ablation of single attributes (RCS, Doppler, time) - the question we care about is NOT answered here.

**Transferable technique**: the exact per-point feature vector and its pillar sizes are a copyable spec (9 inputs, 32 channels, N_p = 10, B = 2000) with a small cost (+17 ms on an older GPU); the time offset t_s is the way to let the network undo motion of multi-sweep points. Augmentations on the radar grid (blur filter, Doppler skewing) are listed.

**Cost and fit**: small; pure BEV, no image dependency in the radar branch, so it fits a BEV grid at 0.5 m with CAM_FRONT only (the paper uses six cameras).

**Doubts**: gains shown against a weak 2D-augmented baseline; velocity gain (mAVE -54 %) tells the Doppler is used, but nothing separates RCS from Doppler. Ego-motion compensation of v_d is not stated.
