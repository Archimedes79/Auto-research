**The question of S3:** does ONE cross-attention block (the tokens of a frame attend to map tokens = samples every 2 m of the carriageway centre lines with position, the direction of travel and the number of lanes; a frame without a map attends to a learned 'none' key only) lift the E365 head, trained on corrupted maps (including absent, count-unknown and grossly wrong ones), over the same head without it? **The honest starting point:** E291 gave the junction pair head a GT-derived lane-count table at the junction ends (counts only, hard association): at most +0.0035 topo F1 with a CLEAN table, about +0.001 of it the count, wrong counts harmless, right ones not exploited. Here the map enters at the TOKENS (every token can look at the road under it) and carries geometry as well as counts: step 0 says the signal is larger than E291's anchor (within an existence bin the supported weak tokens are 2 - 4 times likelier to be novel nodes), but **the simulated map is derived from the very GT lane pieces that define 'true': a clean read is an optimistic bound, and the card claims nothing about a real SD map.**
