# 2112.04282 Radar occupancy prediction with lidar supervision, long range kept by a polar sliding window

**What it does**: A U-Net with equal input and output size predicts binary occupancy on a polar range-azimuth image of a scanning radar (Navtech CTS350x on Oxford Radar RobotCar, 4 Hz, 0.9 deg, 4.32 cm range bins, 163 m; own data with a CIR504-X, 500 m). Training uses only the first 52.5 m (400 x 300 crops) where lidar labels exist; at test time windows with stride 10.5 m slide outward over the full range image and are combined. Labels: lidar with ground removed, five scans per radar frame, lidar points kept within 50 m (Oxford) or 100 m (own), points whose radar power is below a threshold removed.

**Results that matter**: training on the polar image gives an average IoU "4.2 times better" than training on the Cartesian image outside the training range (as stated on the page; absolute IoU not extracted here). Radar odometry with the predicted occupancy: translation error 0.0783 to 0.0528 m/frame, rotation 0.0748 to 0.0692 deg/frame. Loss Tversky (alpha = beta = 0.5; 0.4 / 0.6 for the long-range inference), RMSprop 1e-3, 20 epochs, batch 10. Runtime not reported.

**Transferable technique**: in polar coordinates a ray-structured receptive field sees the same pattern at every range, so a network trained near the sensor extends to far range; in a Cartesian image it does not. For a front camera + radar BEV with a range-dependent footprint this argues for doing the radar spreading in a polar tensor and only then resampling to Cartesian.

**Cost and fit**: small U-Net; windows add a loop over range. Needs a dense polar power image (not in nuScenes).

**Doubts**: the "4.2 times" is a ratio of IoU values that may both be small; labels come from lidar and so are limited to what lidar sees (the paper itself says so); no uncertainty output; only scanning radar.
