## 1. Problem and claim
Point-cloud completion / generation networks predict a point set and compare it with a ground-truth set by Chamfer Distance (CD) or variants (HyperCD, InfoCD). These use nearest-neighbour matching: many predicted points can pile onto the same target in dense areas while sparse areas stay uncovered, and the index selection is a non-differentiable step. Earth Mover's Distance (EMD) enforces one-to-one matching but is cubic in the point count. APML builds a differentiable approximation of a one-to-one coupling: a bidirectional softmin on the pairwise distance matrix with a per-row temperature fixed analytically by a target minimum probability, then a few Sinkhorn-style row/column normalisations, and the loss is the plan-weighted sum of distances.

The claim that matters: against CD / HyperCD / InfoCD the EMD of the results drops in every row of Table 1 (15 to 81 % as stated in the text), F1 is similar or mixed, the cost is about 30 to 38 % more training time, and "no tuning of the Sinkhorn regulariser" is needed. Evidence is shape completion on ShapeNet / PCN and one WiFi-to-point-cloud generation task. Nothing is shown for lane graphs, thin structures, existence probabilities, or point sets of very different sizes.

## 2. Method as built

### 2.1 Inputs, outputs (digest)
- Predicted points X_hat [B, N, 3], ground truth X [B, M, 3] (the code takes any D; N and M may differ). Output: one scalar loss, mean over the batch.
- Cost matrix (E1): C[b,i,j] = Euclidean distance between predicted point i and target point j, shape [B, N, M]. No squared distance.

### 2.2 Equations and hyper-parameters (digest; numbering follows the paper's equation numbers as the tool returned them)
1. **Adaptive softmin of one cost vector c of length K** (applied per row with K = M, per column with K = N):
   - shift so the minimum is zero (c_tilde = c - min c);
   - gap g = (second smallest c_tilde) + delta, delta = 1e-6;
   - temperature (E2/E3): T = -log( (1 - p_min) / ((K-1) p_min) ) / g. This is the value for which the best entry has probability at least p_min even if all other entries sit exactly at the gap (my check: with the best entry at weight 1 and K-1 others at weight exp(-T g) the best probability is exactly p_min);
   - if the second smallest c_tilde is below eps_g = 1e-5 the vector is set to uniform 1/K (reported to occur in under 0.6 % of batches);
   - otherwise (E5) P_j = softmax(-T c_tilde)_j.
2. **Bidirectional symmetrisation (E6):** P1 = row-wise adaptive softmin of C (rows sum to 1), P2 = column-wise (columns sum to 1), P = (P1 + P2) / 2.
3. **Sinkhorn normalisation (E7, E8):** L_iter times: divide each column by its sum plus eps_stab, then each row by its sum plus eps_stab; eps_stab = 1e-8. The paper says the aim is row and column sums of 1 and mentions marginals 1/N and 1/M as an alternative. There is no entropic epsilon: the temperature takes that role and is derived from p_min; the paper presents this as removing a regulariser to tune, while p_min remains one hyper-parameter.
4. **Loss (E9):** L = (1/B) sum_b sum_i sum_j P[b,i,j] * C[b,i,j], the Frobenius inner product.
5. **Values:** p_min = 0.8 (stated as not tuned), delta = 1e-6, eps_g = 1e-5, L_iter = 10 in the experiments section but "e.g. 20" in the method section (inconsistent inside the paper), eps_stab = 1e-8. Evaluation F1 threshold tau = 0.01. The paper says the largest row/column probability typically rises to about 0.8 during training (an outcome, not a setting).
6. **Complexity statement (Table 3):** O(N M (d + L)) time, quadratic memory for the cost matrix (the Discussion gives about 1.8 GB for 16 k points; my arithmetic: 16384 x 16384 x 4 bytes is about 1.07 GB, so that figure presumably includes more than one buffer).

### 2.3 Low-density regions (digest)
No term weights low-density regions. The argument is qualitative: every row and column receives at least p_min on its best entry before balancing, so a sparse target point has its own column that is normalised to carry mass, and the row/column balancing stops one prediction from serving many targets. No proof, no measurement of coverage by range or density is given; the evidence is lower EMD and the visual spreading figures. Appendix D.3: fewer than 8 % of the pre-Sinkhorn entries exceed 1e-3 (over 90 % sparse), the authors propose a thresholded sparse format and FP16 (about 5 to 6 times less memory by their estimate) as future work; the repository has a CUDA sparse version (section 4).

### 2.4 Unequal counts, masses, outliers, gradients (digest)
- **Unequal N and M:** the formulation is rectangular; the paper has no experiment with N different from M and says nothing beyond that EMD implementations pad or sample. Because the iteration has no target marginals, rows and columns cannot both sum to 1 when N differs from M (see 2.5).
- **Masses / weights / background:** none. Every point has equal weight, no existence probability, no padding mask, no dummy row or column.
- **Outliers:** Table 3 rates CD high and APML "low-medium" sensitivity; no outlier experiment.
- **Gradients:** the paper has no gradient analysis, only the qualitative statements "smooth" and "stable". Whether the plan P is differentiated through is not stated in the text I saw; the released code detaches it (2.5).

### 2.5 What the reference implementation does (digest of the code, see section 4 for the licence)
- Cost by `cdist` in float32 (gradient flows here only). Temperature, both softmins and all Sinkhorn steps are computed without gradient, so P is a constant weight matrix and the loss gradient is the plan-weighted gradient of the distances with respect to the predicted points. Default 20 Sinkhorn iterations (paper experiments say 10).
- The temperature in the code, as described by the tool, uses -log((1-p)/(M-1)), without the factor p of E3. My arithmetic: the best entry's probability is then at least 1/(2-p), about 0.83 for p = 0.8, not 0.8. A small difference, but code and text are not the same formula.
- The tie check is on the second smallest value of the shifted row, so a row with two tied minima becomes fully uniform, not split between the two.
- The Sinkhorn loop in the code has no marginal targets; with N different from M the final column step leaves columns near 1 and rows not normalised, so the loss is roughly proportional to M (it is a sum over i, j, with no division by N or M).
- No mask argument; padded points are ordinary points. M = 1 fails (division by zero in the temperature).

### 2.6 Training recipe (digest)
Backbones use their official default configurations (FoldingNet, PCN, PoinTr; CSI2PC for the WiFi task) with only the loss swapped. Optimiser, learning rate, point counts in training and batch size are not stated except batch 128 and 150 epochs for the cost measurement (Table 2). Software PyTorch 2.5, Python 3.11, CUDA 12.8; hardware 4 nodes with 4 V100 (32 GB) each.

### 5.1 How the loss would sit on our vertex set (sketch)
- **What is the point set.** The loss compares two point sets of similar size. Our nodes are sparse; the existing graph loss in `p16_loss.py` already samples predicted edges (four samples per edge) for precision and takes, for every lane point, a soft minimum over valid edges for recall. So take predicted points = samples along the valid edges at about 0.5 m spacing (an assumption: N then lies in the same order as M), each with mass a_i = validity of the edge (product of its end validities), positions differentiable through the node positions.
- **Ground truth.** The dense 0.5 m lane points `g05` with mask `m05`, mass b_j = 1, optionally larger for range above 40 m (the far range is the gap, the weight is our choice and a spent knob, so use 1 in the first arm).
- **Background.** One extra column for the predicted rows (cost c_fp, about the 1.2 m match tolerance used elsewhere in the loss code; a free choice) and one extra row for ground-truth columns (cost c_miss, same order). Mass that goes to the background column is a false-positive price, mass in the background row a miss price. This is what the paper lacks and what makes unequal counts and existence probabilities well defined.
- **Plan.** Adaptive temperature per row and column of the augmented matrix as in E2 - E5, symmetrised, then 10 weighted Sinkhorn passes towards marginals (a for rows, b for columns, the background absorbing the surplus), no gradient through the plan (as in the reference code). The loss is sum(P * C) divided by the total mass (not by M as in the reference), so its scale does not change with the number of ground-truth points.
- **Gradient to validity.** The plan is detached, so validity gets a gradient only through the weights: the price form `a_i * (sum_j P_ij C_ij - c_fp * P_i,bg)` style terms or the existing validity price of the recall term. This must be written on the card; an unrolled differentiable plan is the alternative arm if memory allows.
- **Cost of the plan (all my arithmetic, assumptions N about 500 edge samples, M about 3000, batch 8).** Cost matrix 1.5 M floats per frame, 6 MB; with P1, P2, P and the iterations without gradient about 50 MB per frame; ten passes are elementwise sums and divisions. Expected well below the other loss terms; measure on the card. At N and M of 16 k (the paper's size) the matrix is 1 GB and above, which we do not reach.

### 5.2 Failure modes (reasoning, nothing of it is tested in the paper)
1. **The softmin is almost a hard nearest neighbour on a dense grid (inference from E2 - E5).** On a 0.5 m ground-truth grid the gap between the nearest and second-nearest ground-truth point of a predicted point is small, and the temperature is inversely proportional to that gap. With p_min = 0.8 and K = 4000 the factor in the numerator is about log(0.8 * 3999 / 0.2) = 9.7 (my arithmetic), so entries only a few gaps further away carry a weight like exp(-29) or less. P1 then behaves like a one-hot nearest neighbour, like Chamfer; what differs from Chamfer is the Sinkhorn balancing, not the softmin. Check on real frames how much mass spreads beyond three neighbours.
2. **Blurry matching.** The opposite case: if the second-nearest point is far (isolated sparse region, sparse rows) the gap is large and the temperature small, the distribution stays fairly soft, mass spreads over many far points, and the loss then pulls towards a weighted mean (blur) with little precision. A low p_min has the same effect. The analogue of "small epsilon blurs the plan" is therefore "large gap or small p_min blurs the row"; a high p_min gives the Chamfer-like hard assignment.
3. **Scale of the cost in metres.** P does not depend on the unit (the temperature scales with 1 / gap, so a rescaling of C cancels), but the loss value is linear in the unit and in the total mass, so lambda must be set in metres and per unit mass; the background costs c_fp and c_miss are the only places where metres enter the plan itself, and they fix the match tolerance. Clamp C (the existing code clamps distances at 3 m) so a stray far vertex does not dominate; the plan weights do not downweight outliers by distance (each row still puts about 0.8 on its nearest target), so the clamp or the background does that job.
4. **Position-only gradient.** A missing far lane produces no gradient unless some predicted point lies near it or the validity of an existing edge is driven by the weights. This is the same limit as the nearest-point recall term; the loss would not by itself find lanes the builder never proposes.
5. **Duplicates.** Balanced mass makes two stacked edges on one lane compete, which acts as a duplicate repulsion; it may interact with the existing 1.5 m repulsion term and the vertex-count penalty (target 14.3 per 100 m). Test both on and off.
6. **Ties and degenerate rows** (the uniform override, one-point sets, M = 1) are rare but need masks for padded ground-truth points: set masked costs high before the softmin, as in the existing `nearest` helper.

## 8. Testing recipe for us (proposal; the card with prediction and decision rule is written before the run)
- **Layer 0, self-test of the loss module:** (a) two identical point sets of N = M points on a line: loss close to zero and plan close to the identity; (b) N points all at one end of a line of M targets: the loss must exceed the Chamfer value of the same pair (the Chamfer value hides the uncovered targets less than the balanced plan does, check the direction numerically on the toy); (c) mask test: padded ground-truth points with a mask give the same loss as the unpadded set; (d) background test: one far false vertex pays about c_fp, one uncovered ground-truth point about c_miss, in units of mass; (e) rescaling the coordinates by 10 leaves P unchanged (E5) and scales the loss by 10; (f) gradient check on positions by finite differences with the plan frozen.
- **Layer 1:** reproduce one stored Q on the control through the unchanged path (the new term off). **Layer 2:** a shuffled-target arm (ground truth of another frame) must not improve R@1 far; null (weight 0).
- **Arms (equal budget, 2 seeds, VAL, matched frames, paired scene bootstrap):** A0 current loss; A1 current loss + APML term on edge samples, plan detached, weights 1; A2 as A1 with the background row and column and validity as mass; A3 the same term with plain nearest-neighbour matching (Chamfer on the same samples), to separate "balanced mass" from "another distance term"; A4 as A2 with a larger weight on ground-truth range above 40 m. p_min 0.8 first, the 0.9 variant only as a second row.
- **Prediction (a number, to be confirmed on the card):** R@1 far +0.02 to +0.04 and lanes found +0.02 over A0, Q within +-0.010; I give the combination "R@1 far at least +0.03 and Q not worse than -0.010" about one chance in four.
- **Decision rule:** KEEP if R@1 far improves by at least +0.03 over A0 and over A3 with the bootstrap interval excluding 0 on both seeds, Q is within the 0.010 band of A0 or better, and topo F1 does not fall by more than 0.010; TIE (take the cheaper, i.e. the existing loss) if R@1 far moves less than 0.03; CUT if Q falls by more than 0.010 or false lanes increase; INCONCLUSIVE adds a seed. Report training step time and the memory of the plan (training only; inference ms unchanged by construction, verify with the idle-GPU timing).

## 9. Novelty and relevance for our problem
The novelty is small in mathematics and useful in packaging: a per-row temperature computed from the gap between the two nearest candidates so that the best match gets a chosen minimum probability, a symmetrised softmin plan, and a few normalisation passes, with no entropic epsilon to tune. For us it is a matching-free, argmax-free coverage term that is cheap enough for training sets of thousands of points, which fits the far-range gap in principle, but the paper gives no evidence outside shape completion, no weights or background mass, and no ablation. The part worth taking is the adaptive temperature and the balancing of mass between predicted and target points; the weights for validity, the background row and column, and the choice of points (edge samples at 0.5 m) are our design. Expect a modest effect, since a position-only set loss cannot create a lane that the builder never proposes, and keep it as a watch item behind the vertex-proposal and survival work.
