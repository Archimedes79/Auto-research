How pose is made differentiable (summary): (1) direct regression head (BEV-Locator, MapLocNet): differentiable, but SegLocNet reports slow convergence and poor cross-city transfer; (2) softmax over a score volume / soft-argmax (OrienterNet, U-BEV, U-ViLAR, decoupled matcher): differentiable, yields a distribution whose entropy is a confidence; (3) homography + DLT (HOLO); (4) non-learned exhaustive correlation on a segmentation output (SegLocNet): no gradient through the pose, only through the segmentation loss. Typical benchmark init errors are +-30 m/+-30 deg (MapLocNet, HOLO, U-ViLAR relocalisation), +-100 m (U-BEV), +-32 m (SegLocNet), +-2 m/+-2 deg (fine HD tasks). Our 1-3 m / 2-6 deg is inside the "fine" regime, where SD maps (one line per road) fix lateral offset and yaw but leave the along-road offset weakly observable (HOLO, U-ViLAR, Decoupled paper all say so).
Not found: lane-marking/lane-count particle-filter matching to OSM from the last two years (only older work, snippet level); a pose head inside an online-mapping network that also outputs the vector map (SD-prior mapping papers only do implicit alignment: P-MapNet cross-attention, TrajTopo offsets, Driver2Map two-stage alignment [S]).

## Design implications for our architecture

1. Rough localisation = correlation of our perceived rough raster (road/drivable area, optionally lane-count-weighted) with the rasterised SD centre line (draw it as a band; SegLocNet used 10 m, tune 4-10 m [J]) over a small window (+-5 m, +-8 deg around the 1-3 m / 2-6 deg prior). SegLocNet shows this non-learned matching works on nuScenes SD and transfers across cities where regression heads fail. Translation and yaw share one score volume; use FFT or a decoupled search (yaw, then lateral, then longitudinal) as in the 53-hypothesis solver.
2. An SD map is a 1-D structure: lateral offset and yaw are observable on straights, the along-road offset only at junctions and curves (HOLO, U-ViLAR, decoupled solver). Estimate and report all three but gate the longitudinal component on an observability test (curvature or junction in the window); lane assignment needs lateral + yaw only.
3. Make the registration confidence an output: entropy or peak ratio of the score volume (U-ViLAR, OrienterNet). Uni-PrevPredMap shows a frame-level shift of 9 m (74.6 mAP) is no better than no map (74.0); our attribute stage must fall back to the prior-free graph when the score volume is flat or multimodal.
4. Differentiability is optional here. Because the pose search is small, keep it a non-learned module between two networks (perception head, attribute head); gradients flow through the segmentation loss as in SegLocNet. Direct regression heads (MapLocNet-style) are not needed for 1-3 m errors; BEV-Locator-style regression is only relevant after the rough step.
5. Attribute assignment = ONR formulation: lane -> SD road with topology constraints (beam search / HMM over road connectivity), then lane index by lateral order inside the carriageway after registration, lane count from the map only as a soft prior (OSM lane counts are correct in at most ~80% in one study, lit7). The ONR robustness numbers (NR-F1 77.5 -> 74.6 under 50% global shift) support that assignment degrades gracefully if the graph itself is good.
6. Extending short perceived lanes with map geometry is a prior-use problem, so apply the lit7 rule: only after registration passes the gate, and per-pixel gating or agreement checks (TrajTopo, NavMapFusion). Expect gains mostly at range, not near the ego.
7. Temporal fusion: warp evidence in the BEV raster (HRMapNet +30/-1 update, PredMapNet decay 0.9), then extract the graph; Can et al. found late merging of per-frame graphs gives +0.8 C-F against +6.1 for early BEV fusion. Track edges on top for IDs and consistency (GlobalMapNet: Hungarian association with 1 m lane buffer, in-place replacement).
8. Train with noisy history and history dropout: SQD-MapNet shows plain streaming lost 0.7 mAP while noise-trained streaming gained 4.0; Uni-PrevPredMap shows memory (+9.1) and map (+6.4) are complementary (+16.0 together), which matches our plan to bridge a camera loss with memory + SD map.
9. Do not assume a sensor failure is bridged for long: the only quantitative evidence (MapBench temporal StreamMapNet) keeps 37% of clean mAP after camera crash against 21% without temporal modelling. Measure our own bridging curve (mAP and on-route recall vs seconds of loss and vs odometry drift).
10. Evaluation stack: (a) lane detection and topology as lit7 (DET_l / TOP_ll / OLS-style) plus GEO / TOPO / APLS for the graph; (b) registration: R@1/2/5 m and 1/2/5 deg against the true pose (same convention as MapLocNet / OrienterNet, but state our init error); (c) assignment: an NR-F1-style length-aware score for on-route flag, lane index and lane count; (d) planner-facing: share of ground-truth path length covered within 1 / 2 m by a perceived on-route lane (own metric); (e) nuScenes L2 / collision only as a sanity check with ego status removed or reported separately.
11. Map content realism: ADASIS v2-like horizon = one line per carriageway, coarse lane count, no lateral position (lit7); the public v3 material only promises lane-level profiles, lane connectivity and alternative positions, with no public latency or error numbers. Simulate v2-like content and call it that.
12. Report three conditions separately (graph without map; + registration; + extension + memory), otherwise the SD benefit cannot be separated from the temporal benefit.

## What NOT to claim

- Do not claim we are the first to localise from BEV against an SD map (OrienterNet, U-BEV, MapLocNet, SegLocNet, HOLO, U-ViLAR) or the first to associate perceived lanes with SD roads (ONR). The open niche is the combination (lane graph + rough registration + attributes + temporal bridging, camera+radar) and the nuScenes-based E2E interface; even there I only checked arXiv-level coverage.
- Do not compare our recall numbers with the table above: init errors (+-30 m, +-100 m) and map types differ from our 1-3 m regime; use the table for failure modes and mechanisms only.
- Do not state ADASIS v3 position error, latency, update rate or a "recommended lane" field as facts; none was found. "Centimetre accuracy" is a vendor claim.
- Do not claim temporal fusion bridges camera loss or night in general: one model family (MapBench), 37% retention; BEVMapMatch is ~18 points lower at night even with 4 frames (R@1 m 26.11 vs 43.91 day).
- Do not claim radar-based SD-map localisation has a neural precedent: only radar-only Hong et al. (ICRA 2023, "up to 50%" lower position error, no absolute value read).
