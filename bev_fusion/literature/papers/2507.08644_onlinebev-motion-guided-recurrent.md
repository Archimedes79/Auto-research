**What it does**: one BEV feature map as memory, updated each frame; ego alignment, then a Motion-Guided BEV Fusion Network. A Motion Feature Extractor takes the difference between the current query and the historical query (FC layers + channel attention) and gives a motion context; Motion-Guided BEV Warping Attention (deformable cross-attention of Deformable DETR) uses it to predict the offsets and weights that align the history to the current BEV, 3 layers. A temporal consistency loss (HTC-loss, weight 2) applies two weight-shared heads to the current feature and the aligned history and takes the squared L2 distance of the two heatmaps, with the gradient stopped on the current branch.

**Results that matter** (nuScenes val, R50 256 x 704): SOLOFusion 17 frames 42.7 mAP / 53.4 NDS / 0.252 mAVE; OnlineBEV 44.4 / 54.5 / 0.244; with perspective-view pretraining 46.3 / 56.0 / 0.245. Ablations: motion-guided fusion 40.8 to 42.1 mAP, NDS 50.4 to 51.4, mAVE 0.296 to 0.287; HTC-loss 42.1 to 42.5, NDS 51.4 to 51.9, mAVE 0.287 to 0.280. 12.6 FPS against 13.8 for the single-frame baseline (79.3 ms, 205.7 GFLOPs).

**Transferable technique**: a self-supervised alignment signal without velocity labels: warp the history, ask the same head to give the same answer on the current and on the aligned history, stop the gradient on the current side. For us it is the label-free half of problem 3 as well (the aligned past should predict the present). The gain from motion-guided alignment is +1.0 NDS, not large.

**Cost and fit**: deformable cross-attention in a 3-layer stack; heavy for a 3060 at 0.5 m 120 x 104, though a conv version is conceivable.

**Doubts**: no object-wise measurement of the lag the alignment removes; gains over SOLOFusion are mixed with different training; no code; the page does not give the offset computation.
