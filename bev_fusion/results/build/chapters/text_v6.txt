@@@ system

<p>The system is drawn from the code (<code>bev_fusion/training/architecture.svg</code>, file map in <code>training/architecture.md</code>). A camera path and a radar path are brought to one BEV grid (120 x 104 cells of 0.5 m, x 0 - 60 m, y +-26 m),
fused, passed through a trunk and decoded by heads at any time offset dt (-0.5 / 0 / +0.5 s). Everything on the training path is dense and differentiable; box selection and line thresholding stay in post-processing.</p>
<table><tr><th>slot</th><th>what the full network uses</th></tr>
<tr><td>camera encoder</td><td>two frozen image encoders with cached outputs (DualBEV heads, stride 16; BEVFusion map-trained Swin-T, stride 8), two depth-weighted lifts merged by a 1x1 conv, 3 frames</td></tr>
<tr><td>camera memory</td><td>window of the last frames + a world-aligned static memory</td></tr>
<tr><td>radar encoder</td><td>delta-coded slot raster (3 exact slots per cell + remainder), lossless side loss that decays over the run; radar detections of 0.5 s compensated to now</td></tr>
<tr><td>fusion, trunk</td><td>concat fusion with modality dropout; DualBEV trunk (pre-trained, width 1.0) with a detail path</td></tr>
<tr><td>heads</td><td>5 area layers, 2 line layers + distance fields, dense object maps; object head with sign-free direction and the moving / static hurdle; radar auxiliary head</td></tr>
<tr><td>motion block</td><td>velocity field from two camera frames + the radar Doppler in a 3-level pyramid; the object head's box velocity is read from it; its gradient is clipped on its own</td></tr>
<tr><td>left out</td><td>radar memory with the Doppler static gate (an unlearned block in the small-set probe), lane branch, box refiner, centre-line head (no head design yet)</td></tr></table>

@@@ lane_51
<h3>5.1 What is measured, and why the numbers look the way they do</h3>
<ul><li><b>Ground truth.</b> The two line layers <code>lane_divider</code> and <code>road_divider</code> of the nuScenes map expansion (autogt v8) are drawn as lines one cell (0.5 m) wide in a bird's-eye grid of 120 x 104 cells (x 0 - 60 m ahead, y +-26 m). Pedestrian crossings are polygons.</li>
<li><b>Plain IoU of a 1-cell line is very harsh:</b> a line that is predicted one cell beside the true one scores zero. We therefore report the plain IoU (0 m) and two tolerant versions: a predicted cell within 0.5 m (or 1 m) of a true line cell counts as a hit, and symmetrically for the true cells. Some published map models (GaussianCaR) draw their divider labels about 1 m thick and score the plain IoU: that is a similar kind of generosity as our 0.5 m tolerance, but not the same number (section 6.4).</li>
<li><b>Zero-crossing F1 and field error.</b> The network predicts a signed distance field to the nearest line (and a probability). The line is read where the field crosses zero; <code>zc f1@0.5</code> is the F1 of that thin line against the true line within 0.5 m, <code>field MAE</code> is the mean absolute error of the distance field in metres.</li>
<li><b>Area.</b> Scored on the cells the front camera sees (about 62 % of the grid); the network also gets the radar, but a painted line is a camera feature.</li></ul>

@@@ lane_52_note
<p class=small>The 'prior' baseline (a fixed per-cell map of the training scenes) scores 0.000 on all three line / crossing layers, and its field MAE is 1.33 m against 0.57 m for the network: the numbers above are not what a lookup of typical road layout gives.</p>

@@@ weak

<ul>
<li>Car parks, pedestrian crossings and the lane divider are the weakest map outputs (IoU 0.27, 0.26 and 0.43 with a 0.5 m tolerance, 0.19 plain; the road divider reaches 0.57 with a 1 m tolerance); the baseline's train-to-val gap is large for them (carpark 0.17, lane divider 0.13, road divider 0.10); the data arm t17 (700 instead of 428 scenes) did not reduce the gap and gave no map gain, so the next levers are the features and the training schedule, not the amount of data alone.</li>

@@@ lane_reading
<h4>What the pictures show (four of the twelve frames were read in detail)</h4>
<p>The pictures above are drawn with the memory warmed up by the frames before (as in the videos). The IoU in each caption is the stored number of the evaluation, where every frame starts from an empty memory, so the number printed in the picture title can differ a little.</p>
<ul>
<li><b>Two ways to read the same output.</b> The IoU table of section 5.2 scores the probability layer of the lane divider (a thresholded region, with a tolerance of 0.5 m). The last error panel in every picture shows the thin line that is read from the predicted distance field, which is what a planner would use. On the 1000 validation frames that thin line has precision 0.85 and recall 0.10 (0.5 m tolerance; F1 0.19, the value in the table of section 4); on the easier 384 frames of the GaussianCaR comparison it is 0.81 / 0.14 / 0.24. It is rarely wrong, but it is often missing. The headline IoU is therefore the generous reading; the thin-line reading is the stricter one and the weaker one.</li>
<li><b>Best case (scene-0016, frame 22).</b> A straight road with one dashed centre line: the line is found along the whole stretch from about 6 m to 52 m (83 cells right, 0 false, 22 missed at the two ends). This is what the network does well: a clean, clearly painted line on a quiet road.</li>
<li><b>Median case (scene-0099, frame 36).</b> A wide city street with several painted lanes and many parked and moving vehicles. The camera picture shows the white and yellow lines clearly, but the thin line has 7 cells right, 13 false and 371 missed. Painted lines that vehicles stand on or partly cover are not recovered.</li>
<li><b>Worst case (scene-0017, frame 27).</b> An intersection: the map's divider segments lie mostly on the cross street 35 to 45 m ahead and on the far approach arms, and the network predicts no divider at all (0 right, 0 false, 144 missed). Far lines at a junction are a hard case.</li>
<li><b>Worst case (scene-0038, frame 31).</b> A curved single carriageway: the map has a divider line along the road (the last panel), but on the road surface in the camera picture I see no painted marking along the projected lines, and the network predicts none (0 right, 0 false, 69 missed). If that reading is right, the label asks for a line that the image does not show. That is the label problem the lane-probe experiment (t5) described, and no network can fix it from the image alone.</li>
</ul>
<p>So the lane divider is limited from both sides: by what the camera can see (hidden or unpainted lines) and by what the features resolve (thin, far lines). The stride-8 map-trained features helped most (section 5.4); a higher-resolution image side for thin lines (the stride-4 branch of t7) gave +0.04 to +0.07 on the old ground truth and has not been combined with the full network.</p>
