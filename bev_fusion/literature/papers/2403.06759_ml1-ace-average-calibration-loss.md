**What it does**: a differentiable auxiliary loss. Per image and per class the predicted probabilities are binned into M = 20 uniform bins; for every bin the mean predicted probability e is compared with the observed foreground fraction o; loss = average of |o - e| over bins and classes (marginal L1 average calibration error). Hard bins are directly differentiable through the mean prediction; soft binning uses a triangular kernel. Used as Dice + CE + ACE at 1:1:1; classes absent from the image get 0.

**Results that matter** (test sets, baseline Dice+CE -> hard / soft bins): macro-ACE ACDC 0.131 -> 0.102 / 0.078, AMOS 0.107 -> 0.103 / 0.087, BraTS 0.146 -> 0.132 / 0.102, KiTS 0.171 -> 0.131 / 0.109; micro-ACE BraTS 0.060 -> soft 0.025. Dice: hard bins 0.880 / 0.881 / 0.905 / 0.863 (baseline 0.880 / 0.883 / 0.905 / 0.859), i.e. unchanged; soft bins cost up to 1.7 points (BraTS 0.905 -> 0.888).

**Transferable technique**: for each channel and frame, 20 bins of the sigmoid output over cells with w > 0 (weights = our masks); o = weighted mean of the (soft) target in the bin - the soft-label extension is natural (the paper uses hard indicators); e = weighted mean of the prediction. Use the hard-binned variant. Known-answer test: p identical to a soft target gives 0; constant p = 0.9 over all-foreground cells gives |1 - 0.9| in the one occupied bin.

**Cost and fit**: a histogram per channel per frame (scatter-add), negligible. Binning discards spatial information, so it cannot fix local over-confidence at line edges; for rare classes (dividers 1-3 cells wide) most bins are nearly empty, so the term may be noise there - apply to areas and objects first. Whether it is needed: BCE on soft targets is a proper score; the region terms (IoU / Dice) are what push toward 0 / 1.

**Doubts**: medical 3D, 600-1250 epochs, V100 / A100; no ignore masks, soft labels or thin classes; the repo needs 24 GB VRAM for its own bundles (irrelevant for a port); two cheaper margin-based or SDF-based calibration losses (2209.09641, 2503.05107) compete.
