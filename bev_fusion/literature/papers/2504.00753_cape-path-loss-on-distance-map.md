**What it does**: the network predicts a distance-from-foreground map y_hat (not probabilities); L = MSE(y, y_hat) + alpha x L_CAPE (alpha value not given). For each connected pair of GT graph vertices: Dijkstra on the GT gives the GT path; each endpoint is moved to the local minimum of y_hat in a 7 x 7 window; the GT path rendered as a mask and dilated by 10 pixels restricts a second Dijkstra on y_hat^2-weighted cost; the loss of the pair is the predicted path cost sum y_hat(n)^2 (GT path cost is 0). So a gap in the predicted field (high y_hat on the way) is paid for in proportion to the squared distance values, long gaps more than short ones.

**Results that matter** (Table 1, Dice / APLS / TLTS): CREMI MSE 85.4 / 81.9 / 79.5, clDice 79.3 / 82.3 / 81.2, CAPE 88.7 / 93.6 / 93.5; DRIVE MSE 77.1 / 68.2 / 66.9, clDice 76.2 / 74.5 / 70.5, CAPE 80.3 / 80.2 / 76.4; Brain 3D MSE 75.2 / 83.9 / 75.2, CAPE 78.3 / 89.8 / 93.1. Perceptual loss gives the best Dice on two sets. No Skeleton Recall or Betti baseline; single runs, no variance.

**Transferable technique**: the principle "regress a distance map and penalise the predicted cost along the GT path" is differentiable in the values along a fixed path (the path itself comes from a discrete search on the prediction, i.e. an argmin step, which our fully differentiable training rule does not forbid on the loss side but does not like). A cheaper variant with our stored GT: the path is the GT polyline itself (known, no Dijkstra on the prediction): L = mean over GT vertices (sampled every 0.5 m) of huber(y_hat at the vertex) - this is already contained in our Huber term at near cells.

**Cost and fit**: CPU shortest paths per image; the paper gives no timing. Training schedules 10k-50k epochs on small sets.

**Doubts**: three small datasets, MSE baseline only (not our Huber or tolerant Dice), no soft labels, no ignore mask, no cost, fixed unablated constants (7 x 7, 10 px).
