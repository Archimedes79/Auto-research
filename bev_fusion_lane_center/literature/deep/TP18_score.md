## 1. Problem and claim

Input: six surround images plus an SD map (OpenStreetMap polylines with a class, e.g. road / sidewalk); output on OpenLane-V2 subset A (Argoverse 2): lane segments (centre-line plus left and right boundary, 10 points each), pedestrian crossings, road boundaries, 2D traffic elements, lane-to-lane and lane-to-traffic-element topology (the CVPR 2024 Mapless Driving / "Driving Scene Topology" task). Gap as the paper frames it: earlier SD-map methods (SMERF, TopoSD) encode the SD map for the BEV stage and have no temporal memory (Table I); challenge entries relied on large backbones and ensembles. The claim that matters: a stack of SD-seeded hybrid queries, one-to-many supervision, denoising, a distance term on the topology scores and ConvGRU fusion of BEV features lifts lane-segment detection from 32.3 to 44.0 (+36 %) on val and from 27.3 to 39.1 (+43 %) on test over LaneSegNet. The SD map is a perception prior (where lanes are); it carries no route, and misalignment or wrong maps are not studied.

## 2. Method as built

- **Pipeline / architecture** (shapes only where a page gave them; host values are from the LaneSegNet paper, digest, and are *assumed* to apply to SCORE):

```
6 images (down-scaled to 50 %) -> ResNet-50 + FPN -> BEVFormer encoder -> F_BEV
      (host: BEV 200 x 100 cells, +-50 m x +-25 m, 3 encoder layers, 256 ch; SCORE gives no grid or channel number)
SD polylines (class label) --[SMERF-style: encoded SD map cross-attends to BEV features]--> F_BEV enriched
F_BEV(t) + ego-warped state of t-1 --ConvGRU--> fused F_BEV(t) -> propagated to t+1   (stage 2 of training, 30 epochs)
queries q_ls = [ q_sd (50) | q_b (200 learnable) | q_dn (60, training only) ]
   reference points p_ls = [ p_hat_sd | p_b | p_hat_dn ]
   -> lane segment decoder (host: 6 layers: self-attention (8 heads), lane attention with 8 reference points taken
      from the boundaries predicted by the previous layer, FFN 512)  -> q_hat_ls (N_ls x C)
   -> per query: class, line types, centre-line + boundary offset (10 ordered 3D points), mask (dot product with BEV)
   -> topology: pair features (N_ls x N_ls x 2C) -> MLP -> sigmoid = A^S ; closed-form A^D from endpoint distance ;
      A = min(A^S + 0.8 A^D, 1)
road boundary decoder: 1 query per instance, 20 points per layer, reused as reference points of the next layer (multi-point attention)
traffic elements: 2D boxes on front-camera features, DN-DETR with YOLOv9 proposals as extra queries (not relevant to us)
```
- **Inputs, outputs.** Lane segment: three polylines of n_pts = 10 points in 3D (centre-line, left, right), lane type, line types; road boundary n_rb = 20 points; traffic element 2D box. Region x in [-50, 50] m, y in [-25, 25] m (OpenLane-V2 annotation). SD map: list of polylines with `points` and `category` (OpenLane-V2 `sdmap.json`: road, cross_walk, side_walk; the file is optional and exists only for subset A); SCORE additionally uses OSM maps because an earlier paper (SMERF, Luo et al.) found them better. The frame of the SD points (ego or city) and the source of the OSM data are not stated.
- **Queries.** 200 base queries, 50 SD-enhanced queries, 60 denoising queries (n_dn) during training. It is not stated whether the 200 includes the 50; by E2 the decoder sees n_sd + base + n_dn, so inference probably runs 250 queries (not confirmed).
- **Equations** (own numbering; the paper numbers (5), (6), (7) of E7 - E9 were seen only through the ar5iv digest):
  - E1 `q_hat_ls = LaneDecoder(F_BEV, q_ls | p_ls)`, in R^(N_ls x C); C not stated.
  - E2 `q_ls = concat(q_sd, q_b, q_hat_dn)`, `p_ls = concat(p_hat_sd, p_b, p_hat_dn)`.
  - E3 SD seed: `p_hat_sd,i = MLP(q_pos,i) + p_sd,i`. `p_sd,i` are points sampled from the SD edges and normalised to [0,1]: one point at the midpoint of every edge, then further points on each edge in proportion to its length until n_sd = 50 points exist (even spacing, no duplicate information); `q_pos,i` is the positional query, so the MLP term is a learned offset around the prior position. A self-attention mask between the SD queries and all other queries stops errors of the SD map (missing or wrong elements) from propagating; direction of the mask (one-way or both) is not stated. How `q_sd` itself is initialised (learned embedding or encoded from the SD point) is not stated.
  - E4 Denoising (DN-DETR style): for each of N_GT ground-truth lane segments (3 polylines x n_pts points), `dp_i = (dx, dy, dz)`, each component i.i.d. U(-1, 1); noisy reference points `p_hat_dn = lambda_dn (p_dn + dp_dn)`, lambda_dn in (0,1) (value not stated; as transcribed). Number of groups = floor(n_dn / N_GT). Masked attention keeps the groups apart from each other and from the other queries (no leakage of ground truth).
  - E5 `q_hat_dn = q_dn + MLP(p_hat_dn)`.
  - E6 `q_hat_te = TrafficDecoder(F_PV^0, q_te)`.
  - E7 (pair stacking) lane queries are repeated N_te times and traffic-element queries N_ls times, `Q_lste in R^(N_ls x N_te x 2C)`; `A^S_lste(i,j) = sigmoid(MLP(Q_lste(i,j)))`; the same for lane-lane, `A^S_lsls`.
  - E8 (6) distance term: `A^D_lsls(i,j) = 2 / (1 + exp(d_ij / alpha))`, with d_ij "the distance between the start/end point of lane segment i and j" (Euclidean or not, which frame, and whether end-to-start is meant are not stated).
  - E9 (7) `A_lsls(i,j) = min(A^S_lsls(i,j) + beta A^D_lsls(i,j), 1)`, alpha = 2.5, beta = 0.8, "determined empirically" (procedure not given; the note A16 says "tuned on validation", the page does not say so). Called a post-processing function; not stated whether it is also applied during training, and not stated whether L_top acts on A^S or on A. Computed from E8: A^D = 1.00 at d = 0, 0.80 at 1 m, 0.54 at 2.5 m, 0.24 at 5 m, 0.04 at 10 m.
  - E10 `L_ls = l_vec L_vec + l_seg L_seg + l_cls L_cls + l_type L_type + l_top L_top + l_o2m L_o2m + l_dn L_dn`; E11 `L_seg = l_ce L_ce + l_dice L_dice`; E12 `L_rb = l_vec,rb L_vec + l_seg,rb L_seg + l_cls,rb L_cls + l_o2m L_o2m`.
  - Metric: `OLUS = 1/5 [DET_ls + DET_a + DET_t + f(TOP_ll) + f(TOP_lt)]`, `DET_a = 1/2 [DET_ped + DET_b]`; the page does not define f (see 3.5: f(x) = sqrt(100 x) reproduces the table).
- **Losses.** L_vec: summed Manhattan (L1) distance on centre-line and both boundaries of matched pairs; L_seg: cross-entropy + Dice on masks; L_cls: classification (host: focal); L_type: cross-entropy on line types; L_top: focal loss on the lane-lane topology; L_o2m and L_dn: the same terms on the auxiliary queries. Weights (Sec. IV-B): lane head l_vec 0.025, l_seg 3.0 (l_ce 1.0, l_dice 1.0), l_cls 1.5, l_type 0.01, l_top 5.0, l_o2m 1.0, l_dn 1.0 (identical to the LaneSegNet weights where the host has the term); boundary head l_vec 0.0125, l_seg 1.5, l_cls 0.5 (l_o2m for this head not stated). **Assignment: Hungarian** for the one-to-one branch (host cost: focal class + line-type CE + L1 vector + mask, and the topology loss according to the host appendix); the o2m branch follows hybrid matching (Jia et al., H-DETR); denoising queries have known targets and need no matching.
- **One-to-many (what is and is not stated).** SCORE says only that o2m matching "following hybrid matching" is applied to lane segments and road boundaries with auxiliary loss weight 1.0 (lane head). K (copies of the ground truth), number of extra queries, cost, separate decoder or shared weights: not stated. In the cited H-DETR (digest): a second query group (1500 vs 300 on COCO) runs through the same decoder layers with shared weights, the ground truth is copied K = 6 times, the *Hungarian algorithm* matches the copies (so it widens the positive set, it does not remove matching), loss = one-to-one + 1.0 x one-to-many, self-attention masked between the two groups, only the one-to-one group is kept at inference (no NMS needed).
- **Denoising (cited DN-DETR, digest).** Noised copies of the ground truth are queries that reconstruct their own source; no bipartite matching on that part; training only; groups isolated by an attention mask; DN-DETR box settings lambda1 = lambda2 = 0.4, label flip 0.2, 5 groups. SCORE perturbs the reference points only (E4); no label noise is described.
- **Labels / ground truth.** OpenLane-V2 subset A map element bucket: per frame `lane_segment` with `centerline`, `left_laneline`, `right_laneline` ([n, 3] each), line types (0 none, 1 solid, 2 dashed), `is_intersection_or_connector`; areas (pedestrian_crossing, road_boundary); traffic elements; lane-segment topology matrices. Subset B (nuScenes) has no lane-segment annotation and is not used. Dataset resampling: about 96 % of training samples are straight driving and 4 % turns; samples with steering (wheel) angle above 7 degrees are oversampled (factor not stated).
- **Temporal module.** What is fused: the BEV feature map F_BEV only, not queries, not lane segments. A ConvGRU combines the current BEV feature with the previous fused one; the fused map is ego-motion compensated and passed on to the next step (StreamMapNet recipe; the method of compensation, ConvGRU kernel and hidden size, hidden-state initialisation at the first frame, number of past frames or sequence length, gradient into previous states: not stated). Training: first 30 epochs single-frame (warm-up), then 30 epochs with temporal fusion. The position of the ConvGRU relative to the SMERF-style SD cross-attention (before or after) is not stated.
- **Training recipe.** AdamW, cosine annealing, 8 NVIDIA Tesla V100, total batch 8, images at 50 % resolution, ResNet-50 + FPN; 30 + 30 epochs; YOLOv9 200 epochs. Learning rate not stated (host: 2e-4, 24 epochs). Host stack: Python 3.8, PyTorch 1.9.1, mmcv-full 1.5.2, mmdet 2.26.0, mmdet3d 1.0.0rc6.
- **Inference.** Not described: no score threshold, no NMS, no confidence rule on the page (searched, absent). DN queries are dropped at inference (DN-DETR convention, not stated by SCORE). The topology blend E9 is applied on the pair scores.

## 5. Transfer to our pipeline

| question | answer |

**Detailed answers to the paper-specific questions.**

1. *How the SD map enters.* Three separate paths: (i) 50 of the decoder queries get their reference points from SD edges (midpoint plus length-proportional samples, normalised BEV coordinates, plus a learned offset MLP(q_pos), E3); (ii) a SMERF-style cross-attention from the encoded SD map into the BEV features (the largest step, +3.7 DET_ls, +2.7 TOP_ll in Table II); (iii) a switch of the data source from the benchmark's SD annotation to OSM (+0.9, +0.6). The SD map is vector data with a class label (road, cross_walk, side_walk in the benchmark file); it is not rasterised in SCORE, and the page names no other attribute (no lane count, direction, speed class, no planned route). If SCORE re-uses SMERF's encoder (not stated), that encoder samples 11 points per polyline, adds sinusoidal embeddings and a one-hot road type, and runs a 6-layer transformer (from our SMERF note PL43, itself from memory). Misalignment: not handled. The only protection is the attention mask of E3 against missing or wrong SD elements; no experiment on noise, offset, wrong or missing maps is reported (the project page shows qualitative "ambiguity in the SD map" examples, no numbers). The pose is implicitly exact.
2. *Temporal module.* Fuses BEV features only, in a ConvGRU with ego-motion compensation, trained 30 + 30 epochs. It carries no queries, no lane segments, no validity; per-frame detections have no identity across frames and no consistency metric is reported (Fig. 3 is qualitative). Effect: DET_ls +4.0, DET_ped +2.2, TOP_ll +3.8 (Table II), but unmatched for epochs (3.3); no warm-up fails (31.1). Our own feature-level analogue gave +0.008 F1 (E39 / E41), which fits a strong frozen foundation: SCORE's host has weak camera-only BEV features (ResNet-50, half resolution, BEVFormer), so the headroom for temporal averaging is larger. Do not expect +4 on our side.
3. *One-to-many and denoising, and what survives without Hungarian.* Denoising survives completely: each noisy query has its own ground-truth target, no assignment, training only, isolated by an attention mask; but it gave no gain here (DET_ls 39.8 to 39.8, TOP_ll -0.2), so it is low priority. Its natural analogue in our builder is training the refiner on jittered ground-truth vertices (and jittered ego-warped previous vertices, which is the input of our time recursion); that needs no matching either. One-to-many (+1.6 DET_ls, +2.4 TOP_ll, the largest gain outside SD and time) widens the positive set of a one-to-one DETR; in its cited form it is *still Hungarian* on repeated ground truth. What carries over is the principle "several positives per target", which our pipeline already has: the dense heat map supervises every cell near a centre-line, and K38 (rMCL / relaxed WTA) supervises the b children of a tip. A matching-free o2m for the sparse stage would be "the k lowest-cost predictions per target get the loss" (not in the paper). The topology head is also matching-dependent in SCORE (adjacency of matched queries); ours has parent edges by construction.
4. *Distance blend (called GeoDist in our notes; the name is not in the paper).* E8 - E9 add a decaying function of the endpoint distance to the learned link score, clipped at 1; alpha = 2.5 m sets the length scale (half weight near 2.5 m), beta = 0.8 the strength. +1.6 TOP_ll (34.6 to 36.2) with DET unchanged. Our link experiments (E42 - E56) found learned link heads and TopoLogic-style scoring within 0.01 of the rules, so little is expected on the lane graph itself. The form is more interesting for the route head: score = learned route logit + beta x 2/(1 + exp(d / alpha)), d = distance of a vertex to the SD route polyline after registration, alpha set to the expected registration error (1 - 3 m). That gives an interpretable road-level prior that works when the cross-attention is unsure, and abstention can be read from "all d large". Closed form, differentiable except the clip (use a soft clip). Uncertain gain, because the cross-attention route head already learns the proximity (E104b).
5. *Which parts could serve a lane-level route attribute.* None of SCORE's heads predicts a route attribute; the SD map is used as a map of all roads. Two ideas follow from the construction. (a) *Provenance*: an SD-seeded query is born at a specific SD edge, so an association "perceived lane to SD edge" exists at birth (not used or evaluated by SCORE). In our builder, hypotheses seeded at SD-edge points could carry the edge id and its route flag as a prior route logit, with the learned head refining it per vertex; an SD edge is a road centre line and covers several lanes, so the seed gives a *road-level* prior and the lane level must come from perception (the fallback structure we want: road level from the seed, lane level from evidence, abstain when they disagree). Risk: seeds can create false lanes where the SD road has no drivable lane (the paper has no false-positive analysis; "better short than false" argues for seeding only rays that the dense head confirms). (b) *Mask*: the SD queries are isolated by an attention mask so that map errors do not flow into the others; this is the same idea as our wrong-map training (E87) and a hard structural alternative to it.
6. *SD map and temporal fusion together.* SCORE treats them as independent additive steps and never ablates them jointly. Mechanistically: the SD prior is re-injected from the current pose in every frame, the ConvGRU accumulates evidence in the ego-warped frame, so independent per-frame registration jitter is averaged out, whereas a frame-consistent offset is reinforced (our inference, not measured). For us the interaction is where the registration lives: a slowly varying offset between perceived graph and SD map should be a filtered state over frames, and the per-vertex route logit belongs in the vertex tokens that our time recursion carries, which a BEV-feature GRU cannot do (route is not in the BEV features). A 2 x 2 test (SD map on / off x time recursion on / off, under map offsets of 0 - 3 m) would measure what SCORE could not; it needs no new technique.
7. *Wrong or noisy SD map.* Not tested in SCORE. We have more evidence than the paper (section 7).
