**What it does**: EfficientNet-B4 features of 6 cameras are multiplied by a 48-slice depth distribution (2 - 50 m) and sum-pooled into a 200 x 200 BEV of 0.5 m cells (100 m x 100 m, the same grid as ours). Past BEV features are warped into the current frame with a spatial transformer using the past ego motion; the ego motion is also concatenated as broadcast channels because warping discards it. A 3D-conv network with skip connections gives a 64 x 200 x 200 spatio-temporal state; a convolutional GRU rolls it forward with a 32-d Gaussian latent (future distribution in training, present distribution at inference, KL between them). Heads: centerness, offset, segmentation and flow; flow-warped centers are matched across frames by the Hungarian algorithm to keep instance ids. Moving vs static: the dynamic part lives in the instance flow head; the static scene is only ego-warped.

**Results that matter** (nuScenes, 2 s ahead): FIERY IoU 59.4 / 36.7 and VPQ 50.2 / 29.9 (short / long range) vs a static model 47.9 / 30.3 and 43.1 / 24.5. Removing the ego-motion transformation causes a large drop (no number on the page); recursive prediction beats predicting all futures at once. About 8.1 M parameters, training about one day on 4 V100 32 GB, batch 12. No inference latency on the page.

**Transferable technique**: warp + ego-motion channels + 3D-conv over a short stack on a 200 x 200 0.5 m grid is a working recipe at our grid size; the flow head (per-cell offset to the instance centre of the next frame) is the object-level handle for moving content.

**Cost and fit**: The 3D-conv state of 64 x 200 x 200 is the temporal part; no cost figure for it. Needs trainable camera encoder and instance labels; future prediction is out of scope for us.

**Doubts**: No latency; the static model baseline is weak for far horizons; no lane or map output.
