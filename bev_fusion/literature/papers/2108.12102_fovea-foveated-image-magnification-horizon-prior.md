**What it does** [checked]: a differentiable resampling layer maps the high-resolution frame onto a small fixed-size canvas; background pixels give up space so salient regions get more pixels; a standard detector runs on the canvas and its outputs are mapped back to original coordinates by a differentiable backward mapping. The warp is separable along the two axes (each axis a 1D saliency, Eqs. 6-7) with an anti-cropping constraint (reflect padding). Saliency is a KDE over bounding boxes (Sec. 3.3): a dataset prior (fixed, "encodes small objects appearing near the horizon", "magnifies the horizon" in a figure caption) or a temporal prior (detections of the previous frame).

**Results that matter** [checked]: Argoverse-HD (1920 x 1200 native, default input scale 0.5x = 960 x 600), Table 1. Before fine-tuning, baseline 0.5x AP 21.5 (small objects 2.8) vs the dataset-KDE warp 24.1 (small 8.5, large 50.6 -> 48.3). After fine-tuning, baseline 24.2 (small 4.9) vs the best variant 28.1 (small 10.3, large 54.1); upper bounds 29.2 at 0.75x and 33.3 at 1.0x. So at the 0.5x pixel budget the warp recovers more than 3/4 of the gap to the 0.75x model. BDD100K without fine-tuning (Table 3): the temporal-prior variant 15.1 -> 16.4 AP (small 1.0 -> 2.1), the dataset-prior variant drops to 13.7: "fails to generalise". The abstract: small-object accuracy more than 2x, streaming AP 17.8 -> 23.0 (GTX 1080 Ti).

**Transferable technique**: a static prior is fine for ONE fixed camera but not across datasets; for our front camera on nuScenes the prior is stable, and a derived prior (the ground-plane projection of the BEV band, see the LZU note) avoids fitting it to boxes. The gain is largest for small objects; the price is paid by large objects only before fine-tuning (large-object AP 50.6 -> 48.3), after fine-tuning they gain too (50.9 -> 54.1). That is the property we need so that near lanes are not harmed by a strip, and it says the near side must be checked, not assumed.

**Cost and fit**: cheap resampling layer, fixed prior = cached table; nothing is learned for the dataset prior.

**Doubts**: 2D detection (boxes), Faster R-CNN family, not 3D, not lanes; Argoverse-HD and BDD100K are different cameras; the latency claim "about 22 ms" in the text conflicts with Table 1 (49.4 ms vs 51.2 ms, +1.8 ms); no distance breakdown; the dataset-prior variant did not generalise, so a prior must be recomputed per camera.
