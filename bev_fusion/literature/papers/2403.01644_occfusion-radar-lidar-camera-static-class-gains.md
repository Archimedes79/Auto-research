**What it does**: Fuses surround cameras, the five nuScenes radars and optionally lidar into a 3D semantic occupancy prediction. Radar points (position + vx, vy) are voxelised with voxel-mean offsets, a VoxelNet 3D backbone, average pooling along z gives a radar BEV feature, fused with the others by channel concatenation, a convolution and SENet-style channel reweighting. Losses: focal + Lovasz-softmax + geometric and semantic scene-class affinity, applied at four levels weighted 1 / 2^l. No radar-specific loss or auxiliary supervision (checked).

**Results that matter** (nuScenes val, SurroundOcc labels, IoU camera -> camera + radar): barrier 18.39 -> 20.46, manmade 11.49 -> 16.16, vegetation 18.47 -> 21.81, mIoU 18.88 -> 20.73 (Table I). Manmade (+4.7) and vegetation (+3.3) gain more than any dynamic class the page summary lists (motorcycle, car, truck, bicycle: +1.5 to +2.7); barrier (+2.1) is in the same band as those. Night and rain subsets: manmade +3.1 / +6.7, vegetation +4.2 / +3.4. On Occ3D labels the paper reports "a non-trivial performance degradation caused by merging radar" and gives the reason that radar is good at distant objects but "the perception range in Occ3D's label and the visibility mask limit its strength"; Occ3D camera + radar 38.26 mIoU against camera + lidar 46.79 (no camera-only row in that table).

**Transferable technique**: (1) the classes to put into a radar-visible stationary target are manmade, vegetation and barrier (largest radar-on gains); (2) do not supervise radar with a label that stops at 40 m or is masked by camera visibility; (3) a controlled radar on / off, per class, on the target layer is the test to run on our own network.

**Cost and fit**: a 3D voxel backbone is far too heavy for us; only the evidence is used.

**Doubts**: gains are of a camera model plus radar, not radar alone; no radar-only supervision tested; the Occ3D explanation is the authors' reading, not an ablation.
