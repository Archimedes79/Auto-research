**What it does**: LSS-type BEV detectors collapse the whole height axis into one BEV plane. BEV-SAN builds one BEV feature per height slice (the LSS lift is repeated per slice) and merges them with channel attention (SE) and a two-way transformer. Global slices are [-6,4], [-5,3], [-4,2] m; local slices are drawn from six candidate bins [-6,-3], [-3,-2], [-2,-1], [-1,0], [0,2], [-2,4]. The local bins are chosen from a LiDAR height histogram (points in BEV, counted along z): the page reports most points near -2 and 0 (nuScenes lidar frame, i.e. ground and about 2 m above it).

**Results that matter** (nuScenes val, detection): ResNet-50 no CBGS NDS 0.366 / mAP 0.310 against BEVDepth 0.336 / 0.296; with CBGS 0.482 / 0.351 against 0.470 / 0.341. Slices ablation (NDS): baseline 0.330, global only 0.343, local only 0.351, both 0.366. LiDAR-guided local slices 0.366 vs evenly spread 0.359. Cost: 15.4 FPS and 911 MB against BEVDepth 24.3 FPS and 870 MB (the LSS operation is repeated per slice). No ablation of the number of slices.

**Transferable technique**: (1) the height histogram of a LiDAR sweep is a cheap way to pick the heights of the pull lift: the peaks are the ground and the first metre or two above it, so a slice must be centred on the ground peak, not on a regular grid that happens to miss it (our finding: slices of Grid missed the road at z = 0). (2) +0.007 NDS only from the lidar guidance, +0.036 from having several height-specific features at all: keeping height as channels (our plan 2.1) is the supported choice.

**Cost and fit**: for our pull lift the per-slice cost is a gather, not a repeated LSS, so the slice count is cheap for us (8 slices x 64 channels, about 6 M multiply-adds).

**Doubts**: detection only; the motivation is object class heights, not thin surfaces (the page does not mention thin structures); no code; the NDS gain mixes more parameters and more compute.
