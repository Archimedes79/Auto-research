**What it does**: every polyline vertex is modelled as two independent Laplace variables (location mu, scale b); the regression loss is the Laplace NLL L_R = sum_i sum_j [ log(2 b_ij) + |v_ij - mu_ij| / b_ij ] (Eq. 2). No regulariser on b; stabilisers: regression weight cut to 0.03 (2 for StreamMapNet), lr 1.5e-4 (1.25e-4), gradient clipping at norm 3. The scales feed a downstream trajectory predictor.

**Results that matter** (nuScenes, map AP without -> with the NLL): MapTR 0.4488 -> 0.4525, MapTRv2 0.5540 -> 0.5592, MapTRv2-Centerline 0.4789 -> 0.4655, StreamMapNet 0.7789 -> 0.7043 (the paper labels two of these as gains; the raw numbers fall). Trajectory prediction miss rate 8-15 % lower with uncertainty for four of five pairings (e.g. HiVT + MapTR MR 0.0981 -> 0.0834; DenseTNT + Centerline MR 0.1520 -> 0.1593, worse); downstream training converges in up to 50 % fewer epochs.

**Transferable technique**: (1) the Laplace NLL (L1 residual / b + log 2b) as the heavy-tailed alternative to the Gaussian when the GT has outliers like our map-offset elements; (2) the practical stabilisers (low weight, clipping). (3) A per-cell scale on the distance field is the dense analogue of their per-vertex scale: the consumer (planner) can weight line evidence by it.

**Cost and fit**: free at inference (one extra output); unproven for dense fields. The evidence that the mean can degrade (StreamMapNet -7.5 AP points on the raw numbers, Centerline -1.3) is the reason to take beta-NLL (2203.09168) with b = 0.5, not a plain NLL.

**Doubts**: vector (matched) heads, not dense rasters; two of the four map-quality rows contradict the paper's claim of no degradation; no ablation of the stabilisers.
