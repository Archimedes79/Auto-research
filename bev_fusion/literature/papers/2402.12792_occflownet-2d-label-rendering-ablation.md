**What it does**: Occupancy volume rendered along rays through pixels that carry projected lidar depth and projected semantic labels; 32,768 random rays per frame, 50 proposal + 100 fine samples (nerfacc, Mip-NeRF style). Loss: MSE depth + cross-entropy semantics, rays weighted by log class frequency omega(c) = log(sum N_k / N_c); temporal rays from O = 3 past and future frames, dynamic objects filtered; training only, nothing at inference. A flow variant adds occupancy flow for dynamic objects.

**Results that matter** (Occ3D-nuScenes val mIoU, 2D labels only, Swin, 512x1408, 4 A100): semantics only 15.19; + depth 21.79; + temporal rendering 24.97; + dynamic filter and loss weighting 26.14. Time horizon O = 0..5: 21.79, 24.70, 25.19, 26.14, 25.89, 25.55 (peak at 3). Comparison: RenderOcc 23.93, OccNeRF 10.81, SelfOcc 9.30; with 3D labels as well RenderOcc 26.11 vs OccFlowNet-Flow 33.86; best 3D-only baselines CTF-Occ 28.53, TPVFormer 27.83. Speed not reported.

**Transferable technique**: the supervised-by-projection recipe is cheap: sparse lidar depth in the image (we may have radar-reflected or lidar-derived depth at train time only) is worth more than semantics (+6.6 mIoU), temporal rays another +3.2.

**Cost and fit**: needs lidar at train time (nuScenes has it); backbone and resolution large. The finding transfers: depth + temporal rays matter, class weighting is minor (+1.2).

**Doubts**: comparison with RenderOcc under its own settings drops to 23.31, so part of the gain is training setup; single run each.
