**What it does** [checked abstract]: argues that batch size and input resolution matter more than the lifting method, "a simple parameter-free lifter works well", and that adding radar substantially improves performance and narrows the gap to lidar systems. [digest from the repo page] The camera + radar model scores mean IoU 55.8 on trainval against 47.6 camera-only (README numbers, as stated there; split and class definition not checked). Radar run flags in the README: use_radar, use_metaradar, nsweeps 5, use_radar_filters off; what the metaradar option does is not on the page.

**Results that matter**: see above; the abstract has no numbers. BEVCar (existing note 2403.11761) lists Simple-BEV (ResNet-101) at 18 ms, 54.9 FPS on an A100 (checked there).

**Addendum 2026-10-25 (decoder scout, checked in https://arxiv.org/html/2206.07959)**: the radar is rasterised to the NEAREST cell of the grid (hard binning, no footprint); the 15 non-position fields of a nuScenes return become channels (R = 15); the radar BEV is concatenated with the camera BEV and compressed by ONE 3 x 3 conv, then a ResNet-18. Ablation, val IoU, baseline camera + radar 55.7: occupancy only 55.0 (full metadata +0.7); nuScenes outlier filter on 53.7 vs off 55.7 (+2.0 from keeping everything); 1 sweep 53.1 vs 3 sweeps 55.7 (+2.6; the text says 2.4). So a hard-binned image with attribute channels, one fusion conv and a ResNet-18 already gives the whole radar gain of this paper; there is no soft-footprint ablation in it.

**Addendum 2026-10-25 (lift code read, gap scout; checked in raw files of https://github.com/aharley/simple_bev through a page summariser)**: `train_nuscenes.py` sets Z, Y, X = 200, 8, 200 voxels over X, Z in [-50, 50] m and Y in [-5, 5] m (the scene centroid is 1 m below the reference camera, y pointing down), i.e. 0.5 m cells on the ground and only 8 height cells of 1.25 m each; images 448 x 800, batch 8 x grad_acc 5. `utils/vox.py::unproject_image_to_mem`: the voxel centres (integer memory index i maps to XMIN + (i + 0.5) * size, half-voxel shift explicit) are projected with the full pix_T_cam, validity = in front of the camera and inside the image with a half-pixel margin, one `F.grid_sample(..., align_corners=False)` (bilinear, zero padding) per camera, multiplied by the validity mask; no depth, no occlusion test: every voxel on a ray gets the same pixel value. Cameras are combined as a masked mean in `Segnet.forward` (`reduce_masked_mean`, mask = feature non-zero). So each 1.25 m thick voxel is one point sample at its centre. This is the 'single point per voxel' design our oracle test found blind to the road at z = 0 when no slice centre lies near the ground; in Simple-BEV the ground (about 1.5 m below the camera, derived) lies close to a voxel centre, which is a property of the chosen numbers, not of the method. Whether training compensates (the 1.25 m cells are blurred over height by the following ResNet) is not tested in the paper.

**Transferable technique**: the parameter-free sampling lift is the control of block 1: for the same trunk, compare (a) point sampling at the cell centre, (b) cone-footprint-weighted sampling, (c) LSS-type depth lift. The batch-size / resolution finding is a reminder to control for both when comparing lifts.

**Cost and fit**: 18 ms class speed with a small backbone; MIT code that can be read and reused.

**Doubts**: the lift detail and the radar rasterisation are not verified from a page I read; the README mean-IoU numbers are not defined on the page.
