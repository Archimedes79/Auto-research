## 1. Problem, chain and current best

Goal: a planner-ready lane-centre-line graph without an HD map, from camera + radar: lane centre-lines with forks and merges, direction, fused over time, attached to an ADASIS-like SD-map route (lane level, road level as fallback), with confidence. Very good AND very fast: quality Q at <= 14.3 vertices per 100 m and ms per frame on one RTX 3060 (idle GPU, batch 1, cached foundation features); fully differentiable training path (no argmax, matching or rule step); better short graphs than false ones. Data: nuScenes (non-commercial), 255 scenes, scene-disjoint TRAIN 8,414 / VAL 873 / TEST 833 frames, frozen GaussianCaR camera + radar features (cached), simulated SD map with controlled errors, exact ego poses.

Chain (code in `training/`): camera + radar -> GaussianCaR BEV features (frozen, cached) -> dense lane heads -> differentiable sparse graph builder D7 (seeds, rays, survival, children, junction head, time recursion) -> assembly (path union, fork pruning, numba) -> temporal memory (hold / learned gate) -> map-conditioned builder + registration / trust to the SD map -> route head along the edges -> planner consumer.

Metrics (`evaluation/evaluate.py <registry name>`): Q (S without the sparsity term), topo F1 (connectivity), lanes found, R@1 far (recall at 1 m beyond 40 m), J (split / merge F1 at 6 m), route success (2 seeds), ms per frame; S (composite, rewards sparsity) is kept for the history. Select on VAL only, TEST once per finished design, noise band 0.010, paired scene bootstrap.

## 3. What the numbers say overall

1. The frozen map-pretrained foundation carries the result: swapping it is worth +0.24 F1 (K1), far above any decoder or loss change (U-Net against FCN +0.09 ... +0.12); the dense head's ceiling is a seen-to-unseen scene shift of 0.048 (VAL) / 0.079 (TEST) in the features (K2).
2. In the builder the large effects are structural: teardrop sampling with survival read-out (+0.053 S, K12), fork pruning with the junction head (J 0.265 -> 0.409, Q 0.701 -> 0.735 at identical geometry but 13.5 -> 14.8 vertices, K18), the route head along the edges (route success 0.798 -> 0.889, K14), registration before using a map (0.640 -> 0.923, K5), the parameter-free spatial hold under sensor failure (night F1 +0.35, K10).
3. Speed came from engineering, not a different model: 24 ms (17.5 - 18.8 without time recursion) against 628 ms for the control at equal Q (0.735 / 0.737); the CPU assembly is the largest stage (K15, K25).
4. Sparsity is bought. At matched vertices recall over champion is a tie (+0.0025) and fast_recall leads fast by +0.0149, which against the equal-budget j1eq is again a tie (+0.0062; K19, K22), while the control still leads on coverage (lanes found 0.800 against 0.695 - 0.767; R@1 far 0.811 against 0.709 - 0.771) and connectivity (topo F1 0.763 against 0.737 - 0.742).
5. The recall weight is a coverage lever, not a Q lever: four paired seeds, lanes found +0.060, Q +0.006 (inside the band), Q -0.038 with three cameras off (one seed pair, E231.a; K19, K22).
6. Honest negatives, several closed: route loss through the builder (three times), planner loss into any module, Poisson set loss (-0.073 Q at matched vertices), range thresholds, larger beams, learned pair head, keep gate, growth family, fork-coverage loss, more training units, builder ensembles (J falls), exact-distance target (TIE) (K11 - K14, K20, K24).
7. Measurement lessons: seed SD of Q 0.0025 (the band 0.010 is about 4 SDs); every Q must name its vertex count; an out-of-bound training target in 51 % of the frames (0.7 % above 1 m) cost nothing measurable in the j1 fine-tune (two seeds, effects above about 0.01 - 0.016 excluded); ground-truth offsets do not cap Q; VAL has two cities and 22 scenes, so city-level rankings are weak; route-head seed spread 0.03 - 0.09. Loop 18: 7 of 12 written predictions hit (ledger loop table).
8. What the system is not: failures and the SD map are synthetic and the SD route equals the driven route, so exact-map numbers are upper bounds; assembly, anchoring, relocation, pruning and tau are inference rules (the end-to-end gap). TEST is logged for loops 16 and 18; earlier loops reported TEST numbers without a log.
9. Open gaps (PROBLEM.md): (1) coverage at range, lanes found -0.105 and R@1 far -0.10 against the control, loss weights spent; (2) topo F1 -0.022 (fork pruning gave +0.021); (3) no responsive planner-level metric (floor 0.27 m); (4) a map with 1 - 3 m error, registration recovers about half; (5) recall variants with cameras off (Q -0.038).
10. Loop 22 attacks gap 1 with changes of the existing chain (spur-kept clean-up, seed ring, two-seed union, rc2 at its own operating point; K26). Open decisions for Mirko: D4 (which fast variant), D25 (vertex-count sentence of PROBLEM.md), D18 (per-metric bands), D19 (machine lock).
