**What it does** [checked]: one weight per task, z = softmax(xi) with logits xi_1 = 0. The model step uses the weights c z_i / l_i on the task gradients, c = (sum_i z_i / l_i)^-1, i.e. it descends the z-weighted LOG losses with one ordinary backward of one scalar. The logits move after the step: xi <- xi - beta (delta + gamma xi), delta = A^T [log l_t - log l_{t+1}], A the softmax Jacobian: tasks whose log loss fell least get more weight. Motivation: one gradient step on min_z 1/2 ||J z||^2 (rows of J = grad of log l_i, the MGDA-like objective) using the observed change of log losses instead of the K gradients. Losses must be positive: shift by a known lower bound, l_i <- l_i - l_i* + eps (our group losses are sums of BCE, Dice, Huber, focal, L1: all >= 0). gamma (decay on the logits) is the only sensitive knob, default 0.001 in the paper, searched over {1e-4, 1e-3, 1e-2}, 0.01 for CityScapes; the paper names the dependency on gamma as its limitation [checked].

**Results that matter** [checked, ar5iv; lower Delta_m% is better]: NYUv2 (3 tasks, segmentation + depth + normals): linear scalarisation (equal weights) 5.59, PCGrad 3.97, CAGrad 0.20, Nash-MTL -4.04, FAMO -4.10. The first three rows equal the MTAN / PCGrad / CAGrad rows of the CAGrad paper, so the baselines are the same runs. FAMO per task: mIoU 38.88, pixel acc 64.90, depth abs 0.5474, normal mean 25.06. CityScapes (2 tasks): FAMO Delta_m 8.13 against Nash-MTL 6.82 and CAGrad 11.64 (provisional, table garbled), so FAMO is not the best there. Also tested on QM9 (11 tasks) and CelebA (40 tasks) [checked: named; numbers not readable]. A 2025 paper lists FAMO -4.10 beside GO4Align -6.08, FairGrad -4.66, LDC-MTL -4.40 on the same NYUv2 table (2502.08585, digest), so FAMO is no longer the top of the O(1) family.

**Transferable technique**: weights for the four groups (areas, lines, objects, motion) from the log-loss change, no per-group gradients. A drop-in for `task_weighting="kendall"`: `loss = famo.backward(group_losses)`, `opt.step()`, then `famo.update(group_losses_after_step)`. Do not stack it with Kendall (both are log-loss weightings; Kendall's optimum is s = log L).

**Cost and fit** [checked]: O(1) in the number of tasks (vectors of length K plus Adam state for the logits, lr 0.025). Per step in the official trainer: one extra FORWARD pass under `no_grad` on the same batch after the optimiser step, to get the new losses (the paper text itself does not state this; the repo does). Estimated +25-35 % step time [memory: forward is about a third of forward + backward]; the paper says "negligible overhead" in a chart without numbers. With our dt-weighted multi-time loss the extra pass must include all dt targets (or take only dt = 0 losses for the update: untested). Keep the stored previous loss detached so no graph is held. No extra GPU memory beyond the forward activations (freed at once under no_grad). Works with K = 4 (tested K = 2, 3, 11, 40).

**Doubts**: CityScapes is worse than Nash-MTL; no dense BEV or detection task; gamma sensitive; baselines of 2023 (equal-weight LS 5.59) are weaker than tuned scalarisation in later studies (see 2408.07985: equal weights -7.1 vs grid search -9.7 on the same benchmark with a different protocol); the effect of a dt-weighted, ramped-in loss (our warm-up changes L_g over time) on a log-loss-rate rule is untested.
