**What it does**: for each radar return the radial speed is measured; the tangential part is obtained in closed form from the optical flow at the point's pixel (RAFT), the known ego motion and camera poses, the radar position and the assumption of constant velocity over dt: the full 3D velocity in the camera frame comes from inverting a 3 x 3 matrix per point (their Eq. 14). Because a wide-beam radar projects wrongly onto the image, an association net (R2P, a 5-level U-Net, 64 channels, inputs image + radar depth map + optical flow, 8 channels) predicts for each of N pixels around the raw projection the probability that its flow gives the right velocity; labels exp(-E^2/c) with c = 0.36 on the velocity error, cross-entropy, accepted if the best score >= 0.3, otherwise the point is flagged occluded.

**Results that matter** (nuScenes, point-wise velocity error mean (std), m/s): full velocity 0.433 (0.608) with R2P, 0.577 (1.010) with the raw projection, 1.599 (2.054) for the radial-only baseline; tangential component 0.322 against 1.536. Object-wise velocity 0.451 against CenterFusion 0.826 on the same detections. Accumulation of up to 25 radar sweeps with the estimated full velocity stays most accurate; downstream box pose: centre error 0.834 against 0.997 m, IoU 0.546 against 0.462. Time: RAFT 0.447 s, R2P 2.58 ms, closed-form solve 0.249 ms per frame.

**Transferable technique**: radar velocity is under-determined by one return, but a second observation of the same 3D point (here the camera flow; for us possibly the second radar sweep or the camera BEV flow of problem 3) gives the other component by a small linear system per point. The learned part is the association, not the physics.

**Cost and fit**: RAFT at 0.447 s is out of budget; a flow restricted to the pixels near radar projections or our own BEV flow could replace it.

**Doubts**: constant-velocity and rigid-point assumptions over one interval; occluded points are lost; no code found.
