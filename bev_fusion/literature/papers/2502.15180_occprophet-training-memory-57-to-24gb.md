**What it does** [checked]: Observer (efficient 4D aggregation with tripling-attention fusion on 3D multi-frame voxels), Forecaster (conditional prediction of the future), Refiner; works on reduced-resolution features. Input 3 frames (2 past + present), output 4 future frames at 2 Hz = 2 s on nuScenes (0.8 s on Lyft at 5 Hz); 0.2 m voxels, 512 x 512 x 40, six cameras at 448 x 800. AdamW lr 3e-4, wd 0.01, batch 4 on 4 x RTX 4090; schedule, epochs and loss not given.

**Results that matter** [checked, Table 6 / 4 / 5]: against Cam4DOcc: parameters 82 M vs 370 M, training memory 24 GB vs 57 GB (58 % lower), FLOPs 1985 G vs 6434 G (69 % lower), 4.5 vs 1.7 FPS, future IoU 29.15 vs 27.98 (+4 %). Fine-grained: movable objects (GMO) future IoU 9.34 vs 9.20 at 2 s, static objects 24.56 vs 17.83. Ablation (ResNet-18, IoU): full 28.24; without Observer 27.25, Forecaster 27.50, Refiner 27.44, all three 26.07. The paper's own numbers differ between tables (GMO 15.38 vs 13.71; 28.24 vs 29.15).

**Transferable technique**: the efficiency argument for forecasting on a coarse grid: our 2D BEV at 0.5 m has about 840 times fewer cells than the 0.2 m voxel grid (512 x 512 x 40 = 10.5 M voxels against 120 x 104 = 12.5 k cells; arithmetic from the page's grid and ours), so the memory problem of this paper does not arise at the grid level; a "forecaster" that conditions on dt is our FiLM-on-dt wrapper.

**Cost and fit**: needs 24 GB of training memory at their resolution, which does not fit a 12 GB card without cutting resolution or batch; our 2D design avoids the issue.

**Doubts**: loss and schedule not described; table inconsistencies; the forecast gain over a static copy of the last frame is not shown on the page.
