### 3.19 Loop 13: verification blocks for graph hypotheses, trust and ego state at the planner (how the literature entered; what each element gave in our experiments)

Loop 13 asked three things: why the learned lane graph has weak junctions, whether a learned differentiable block can remove false pieces and junctions, and whether a learned consumer of the output graph can use trust signals. The works below were looked up for this loop (abstract pages, 2026-10-19; (abs) = abstract only, nothing from the full text); the last column is ours (nuScenes TEST, camera + radar BEV features, 21 scenes, simulated navigation map, synthetic failures; sections 7.37 - 7.40).

| Work | Idea | Where it entered | What came out in our experiments |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hu et al., Relation Networks for Object Detection (CVPR 2018) (abs) | an object relation module lets detected objects interact through appearance and geometry; used for recognition and for duplicate removal inside the detector | the piece and junction verifiers (E116, E113c) read the tokens of the trace network; we assume that the context between pieces is already inside the tokens (no geometry-only control, no relation module over the list was tested) | piece verifier AUROC 0.855 (TEST), junction verifier 0.844; the verifiers raise precision (+0.043) and the one-to-one precision of the junctions (0.30 / 0.34 to 0.42 / 0.48) at a loss of 0.05 - 0.08 recall (sections 7.37, 7.38) |
| Hosang et al., Learning Non-Maximum Suppression (CVPR 2017) (abs) | greedy NMS with a fixed threshold forces a trade-off between recall and precision; a network that takes the boxes and scores can replace it | the piece verifier is the counterpart for the duplicated parallel traces and false pieces of the traced graph | the piece verifier trades recall for precision: on clean frames F1 +0.009; under failure the effect depends on the failure (-0.095 to +0.110 F1), and a clean-trained network sees shifted probabilities in night and blur (section 7.38); we did not compare with a hand-built rule |
| SeqGrowGraph: Learning Lane Topology as a Chain of Graph Expansions (arXiv 2507.04822) (abs) | centre lines are re-segmented so that splits occur only at junctions, forks and merges; this reduces spurious split points | the diagnosis of E113b: the learned graph has 1.8 times as many junctions as exist | one-to-one junction precision 0.30 / 0.34 against 0.85 / 0.72 for the rule graph with key points; a third of the real junctions have traced branches but no junction; a connector head that joins piece ends and starts (AUROC 0.95) lowers the junction F1 at every threshold; a learned cut point (the counterpart of the rule key points) was not tested |
| Gu et al., Producing and Leveraging Online Map Uncertainty in Trajectory Prediction (CVPR 2024) (abs), see section 3.18 | map uncertainty is an input of a trajectory predictor for the other traffic participants and improves it | the route confidence and an ideal failure flag as inputs of the learned consumer (E115, E115b); our consumer predicts the ego path | without a fallback the consumer gains nothing from them; with the ego motion the route confidence lowers the error by 0.006 - 0.062 m in 11 of 12 groups (at most a small gain, the per-seed sign changes in seven groups), the ideal flag gives no gain under failure (section 7.40) |

Takeaway: the junction weakness of the learned graph is mainly a precision problem (1.8 times as many junctions as exist, one-to-one precision 0.30 / 0.34), with a third of the real junctions also lacking a junction; a simple connector head does not help. Two small differentiable verifier blocks on the tokens remove part of it on clean frames (junction F1 +0.05 / +0.08, precision +0.04). The piece verifier is the wrong block in night and blur unless it is gated or trained on failure frames; the junction verifier raises the junction F1 for most failure graphs, lowers it for the single night and blur frames and lowers route F in all nine failure cells (-0.010 to -0.023). The literature we looked up covers learned duplicate removal in detection and topology re-segmentation in lane graphs; a verifier on the tokens of a lane tracer, trained on the tokens of the train rows, was not found in these abstracts (this is not a claim of novelty).
