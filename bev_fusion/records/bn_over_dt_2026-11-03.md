# BatchNorm over dt (open finding #5), measured on `t16.full_s0` - 2026-11-03

A diagnostic: it feeds no verdict. Question of `open_findings_2026-11-02.md` #5: the `shared` BatchNorm of the semantic head (`sem_head.head.net.1`) and of the object head (`obj_head.head.shared.1`) is called three times per training step (dt -0.5, 0, +0.5), each time with its own batch statistics, so the running statistics that the evaluation uses at dt 0 are a mixture. Does that cost anything at dt 0?

**What was measured.** The EMA weights of `runs/t16_full_s0/ckpt_00006000.pt`, the dt 0 loss and the tolerant IoU (`evaluation/metrics.SegMeter`, whole grid, GT of the `cur` side only, the trainer's `forward_loss` path with the memory carried through the clips) on 75 clips of 3 keyframes = 225 frames of the trainer's own fixed 150-clip val subset (every second clip; the other 75 clips are the calibration set, no overlap), 25 batches of 3 clips. Three ways, the weights unchanged:

- **A** eval mode, the stored running statistics (the mixture);
- **B** the two BatchNorm modules in train mode: batch statistics of the dt 0 input of each batch of 3 samples, running statistics not updated (momentum 0; checked unchanged);
- **C** eval mode with the two modules' running statistics re-estimated from dt 0 inputs only (cumulative average over the calibration clips): the clean way to ask "what if the statistics were those of dt 0".

**Rule, written into the script before the numbers were read.** The mixture is negligible when, for B and C against A, (1) every dt 0 group loss differs by less than 2 paired standard errors over the 25 batches AND by less than 1 % of its value, and (2) the lane divider IoU@1 (tolerance 1 m) differs by less than the band 0.010 of the card.

Numbers: `bev_fusion/temp/bn_dt/measure.json` (scratch, not in git), made by `temp/bn_dt/measure.py` (213 s on the GPU; `wsl bash -c 'cd bev_fusion && PYTHONPATH=/mnt/c/Auto-research $HOME/miniconda3/envs/gcar/bin/python temp/bn_dt/measure.py --device cuda'`; the script is scratch and stays so, the tag of the next run that uses it promotes it).

The size of the mixture itself (eval forward, all three dts, calibration clips; mean over channels, `per_dt_input_statistics` in the json): the BatchNorm input of each dt against the stored running statistics - mean shift 0.05 - 0.08 running sigmas (sem: -0.5 0.055, 0 0.049, +0.5 0.059; obj: 0.084, 0.057, 0.062), variance ratio 0.86 - 0.96 (sem: 0.86, 0.93, 0.89; obj: 0.91, 0.96, 0.93). The dt 0 statistics differ from the stored mixture by 0.049 (sem) and 0.057 (obj) sigmas in the mean and have 0.93 / 0.96 of its variance. The dt-to-dt spread of the input variance is about +-4 % around its mean (sem, mean over channels of the variance: 7.56 / 8.17 / 7.80), smaller than the distance of every dt to the running variance (8.88; the variance ratios above say it is 4 - 16 % higher than that of any dt).

**Reading (by hand: the rule above applied to these numbers).**

1. B is not a measurement of the mixture: the statistics of a batch of 3 samples are noisy, and every map metric falls (drivable area IoU@1 -0.025, lane divider IoU@1 -0.0101, just outside the band). It shows the cost of small-batch statistics, nothing about dt.
2. C, the clean question, is inside the noise nearly everywhere: total -0.04 % (0.5 SE), lines -0.05 %, objects -0.29 % (1.8 SE), lane divider IoU@1 -0.0012 (band 0.010), IoU@0.5 unchanged, vehicle IoU unchanged. By the strict rule one place fails: `group.areas` +0.0045 = 3.0 SE (0.90 %, below the 1 % limit) - and in the direction of the mixture being better (dt 0 statistics make the areas worse: drivable area IoU@1 -0.0067). So the mixture is not shown negligible by the letter of the rule, but its cost is at most the sign-reversed 1 % of one group.
3. Not covered: this changes the statistics only, the weights stay those that were trained against the mixture; a BatchNorm trained per dt could differ. One checkpoint, one split, 225 frames; the object detection metrics (mAP, velocity) were not run, only the object head's loss.
4. A side finding: the stored running variance is 4 - 16 % above the variance of the dt inputs in an eval forward (every dt, the variance ratios above), a gap as large as or larger than the dt spread. Whether modality dropout (p 0.2) and the augmentation in training cause it was not tested.

**Recommendation.** A per-dt BatchNorm (or GroupNorm) is NOT worth a card now: re-estimating the statistics at dt 0 moves no map metric by more than 0.007 IoU and the lane divider by 0.001, and the one move beyond the noise is in the wrong direction. Leave finding #5 as "measured, small"; reopen it only if a dt != 0 output of t20 is much weaker than the same head at dt 0 (then the +-0.5 s statistics, which this measurement did not evaluate, are the first suspect). If BatchNorm statistics are ever looked at again, the train-versus-eval gap of point 4 is the larger lever.
