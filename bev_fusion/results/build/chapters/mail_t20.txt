2. YOUR QUESTION (t21): CAN THE TEMPORAL FUSION RUN WITH ONE BUFFERED FRAME?
Yes, at the quality of the three-frame window by the guards of the card. You were right that the network lifted three images per call. Three arms with ONE buffered frame were trained 1500 steps each from the t20 baseline and compared with an equal-budget control that keeps the three-frame window (paired scene bootstrap, one seed per arm, all verdicts "by hand: D51, one seed"):
- P (pair): the previous frame's camera BEV is the buffer; one new image is lifted per step.
- R (recurrent, "predicting"): the buffer is the previous fused state, moved to the present by the same splat and fused with the new frame; no older image is lifted again. Training carries the state from keyframe to keyframe (a chain of up to nine recurrent steps); the evaluation of a frame starts it cold; a stream carries it without limit.
- P4: P with the previous frame 0.333 s back instead of 0.167 s (see below).
Reading in words:
- By the point estimates no arm is beyond a guard of the card (velocity error at moving box centres within 0.10 m/s, mAP within 0.020, lane divider IoU@1 within 0.010): by the written rule all three are ties with the window. The intervals are wider than the points: on whole warm scenes the lower end of the lane IoU change reaches past the 0.010 band for P (-0.017) and P4 (-0.025), and the upper end of P's velocity change is +0.102.
- P and R lose about 0.05 m/s in velocity (separated from 0 by the scene resampling, inside the band; the card had predicted losses of 0.15 and 0.25). That is consistent with a halved time baseline of the velocity block, which was trained on 0.333 s and has no time input; it is not isolated. P4 keeps the 0.333 s baseline: velocity level with the window (+0.008 m/s, interval just containing 0) and better than P (-0.041, interval excludes 0).
- Cost, batch path on an idle GPU: 88.9 ms (P) and 90.0 ms (P4) against 102.5 ms for the window; R 110.2 ms (it unrolls three frames; the card had expected it dearer). The saving is in a stream with one new image per step: 57 ms against 85 ms at dt 0 (synthetic timing of the same modules; the window in that comparison lifts all its frames on every call, a window with cached lifted frames was not built).
- The stream equals the batch computation within about 5e-3 in the maps and 3e-5 in the velocity on one scene (identical for R), and the carried state drifts away from a cold start with time. The gain of a warm memory outside the camera view is the same in the window control, so it comes from the world memory, not from the new buffer.
- Honest costs: R loses a little on several outputs (road divider, vehicle and pedestrian AP at 1 m over the whole area); P4 has two separated losses on secondary lane metrics (the F1 of the lane-line zero crossings, -0.018 against the control, and on whole warm scenes the road divider IoU@1, -0.015), and its stream needs a buffer 0.333 s old (3 Hz, or a ring of two raw BEVs at 6 Hz), so it is NOT "one frame at 6 Hz". These are differences found among about forty metrics of one seed: hints, not findings.

3. WHAT t20 SAID (the redesign bundle, trained before t21)
The bundle (stride-4 lane branch L4, a 1 m centre-line head in the grid head, the correctness fixes, the dead radar channel removed) did NOT improve the lane layer of the full network: lane divider IoU@1 0.542 against 0.549 for t16.full_s0 (the card predicted 0.60: missed).
