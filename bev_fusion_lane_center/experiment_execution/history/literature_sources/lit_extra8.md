Takeaway: **adopted** in loop 8: the edge-classifier idea of SAM-Road (a transformer over the edges of a perceived graph gives a confidence per edge; E83), thresholds set for a stated operating point (E81; no junction recall at a fixed error budget was measured), the extension of occluded lanes with map geometry (E84), the route attribute on the lane as the planner-facing form of navigation (E83, E86). **Read but not used**: vertex NMS and endpoints as first-class vertices (the graph comes from the loop-4 line tracer cut into 6 m edges), min / product confidence propagation along edges (the mean dense probability is used), calibrated abstention in the sense of conformal or evidential outputs (only the keep and route probabilities with an ECE are reported), 20-point lane tokens (the consumer reads start, middle and end point). The literature did not provide a lane-graph paper with calibrated abstention, a perceived lane graph trained jointly with a planner, or lane-level route attributes per perceived lane from BEV.

Not claimed: joint training of a perceived lane graph with a planner (the design is a combination of known parts); that UMPE's gain comes from lane graphs; that aerial road-graph numbers hold for BEV; "command not mentioned" for a planner only means not found in the text read.

Takeaway: **adopted** in loop 8: a non-learned registration of the perceived lane points against the SD map with a small search window (E82; SegLocNet-style in spirit, with points and ICP instead of a raster correlation), temporal evidence on warped data (E85, training-free), the length- and assignment-aware view of the metrics (false lane length and path coverage instead of NR-F1). **Read but not used**: score-volume entropy as registration confidence, gating the along-road offset on observability, noisy-history training of the memory, NR-F1 and GEO / TOPO / APLS metrics. The literature did not provide a published route-following or lateral-deviation metric (the reference-path deviation of E86 is an own definition), a pose head inside an online mapping network that also outputs the vector map, or a camera-plus-radar BEV-to-OSM localisation.

Not claimed: being first to localise from BEV against an SD map or to assign perceived lanes to SD roads (the open niche is the combination, checked at arXiv level only); any ADASIS v3 position error, latency or recommended-lane field ("centimetre accuracy" is a vendor claim); general bridging of camera loss or night by temporal fusion (one model family, 37% retention; BEVMapMatch is ~18 points lower at night even with 4 frames, R@1 m 26.11 vs 43.91 day); a neural precedent for radar-based SD-map localisation (only radar-only Hong et al., ICRA 2023, "up to 50%" lower position error, no absolute value); unverified venues.
