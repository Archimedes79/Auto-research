**What it does** [checked]: Segments open space from the range-azimuth (RA) or range-azimuth-Doppler (RAD) tensor of a 77 GHz parking radar (SCORP dataset: 3193 train and 720 eval frames; 90 deg field of view, 15 m range, 0.12 m range bin). It compares a mask in the polar domain (RA-mask), a mask in the Cartesian domain (DoA-mask) and the three transfer cases: a network that must learn the domain change implicitly (polar in, Cartesian out), the same domain (Cartesian in, Cartesian out) and polar in, polar out. FCN-tiny, FCN and DeepLabV3+ are the networks. The paper says explicit conversion happens outside the network and does not name the interpolation; the implicit case has no dedicated learned layer.

**Results that matter** [checked, Mean-IoU, Table II]: RA input to Cartesian mask (implicit) 77.96 / 78.24 / 77.22 (FCN-tiny / FCN / DeepLabV3+); Cartesian input to Cartesian mask 79.00 / 80.75 / 78.05; RAD input to Cartesian mask (implicit) 73.24 / 78.05 / 73.92. Polar in, polar out: 81.99 / 82.59 / 81.14 (RA). Authors' conclusion: same domain in and out wins in all cases. FCN-tiny runs 324 FPS on an RTX 2080 Ti (checked, whole model, not the transform).

**Transferable technique**: do not make the CNN learn the geometry; keep the polar-to-Cartesian step a fixed table. Their loss from the implicit step is 1 to 3 IoU points for RA input (my reading of the table: the "same domain" rows are the explicit-conversion rows; the paper does not label them so). The polar-in polar-out numbers are on a different label, so they are not a like-for-like comparison.

**Cost and fit**: tiny; but spectra, not detections.

**Doubts**: one small dataset, one sensor, no seeds, no interpolation named, label domain differs between rows, so the "same domain wins" is confounded with the label.
