**What it does**: instead of a per-pixel depth distribution, it regresses a distribution over the height above ground for each pixel; with a known camera pose and the ground plane, height plus pixel determines the 3D point, so the lift is a fixed geometric function of the predicted height. The authors argue this is distance-agnostic and easier to optimise than depth for roadside cameras, where depth changes strongly with distance. Distribution parameterisation and speed are not on the abstract page.

**Transferable technique**: for an ego-vehicle front camera, a ground-plane prior gives the lane (height ~ 0) the exact depth d = h f / (v - v0) with no network; the network then only predicts height, which is non-zero only for objects. This is the ground-prior-plus-residual form the coordinator asked for; nothing here confirms it works on ego-mounted cameras (paper says BEV methods perform worse on roadside views, not the converse).

**Cost and fit**: LSS-class cost with a height bin axis; DAIR-V2X / roadside data, not nuScenes.

**Doubts**: abstract only; no numbers; roadside setting.
