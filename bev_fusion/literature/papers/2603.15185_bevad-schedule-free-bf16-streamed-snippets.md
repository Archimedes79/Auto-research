**What it does** [checked]: end-to-end driving (Bench2Drive 72.7 % success), BEV 100 x 100, BEVFormer-style encoder on a RADIO backbone with a LoRA adapter, DETR-style object decoder as the only perception supervision. Training: recurrent BEV generation is replaced by cached BEV features STREAMED from short episode snippets of n = 20 frames (about 2 s) fed in order, the memory reset periodically (Sec. 3.1, App. B.1); this "significantly improved runtime" (no number on the page). Optimiser: AdamW Schedule-Free, weight decay 0.01; two stages, a 4-epoch warm-up that trains the BEV backbone with perception supervision, then planning supervision with the BEV backbone frozen; 8 x A100-80GB, total batch 128, bfloat16 mixed precision (Sec. 3.2); appendix: batch 16 on a single A100-80GB. Random perturbation of all camera extrinsics (a random car-to-BEV transform) as augmentation. NAVSIM: 570 A100-hours, about 10 x less than ParaDrive (eight epochs).

**Results that matter**: no ablation of the optimiser or of bf16; the schedule-free + bf16 + staged-warm-up combination simply trains to the headline score. Numbers above are the only facts.

**Transferable technique**: three choices that fit one 3060: (1) bf16 autocast (no GradScaler needed for bf16 on Ampere, from memory; the PyTorch AMP page read does not address it); (2) Schedule-Free AdamW removes the need to fix the number of steps before the dt warm-up and the radar/camera stages are decided (see the note on 2405.15682); (3) streaming snippets of 2 s: a recurrent memory is trained on sequences of about 20 frames with a reset, not on a full drive.

**Cost and fit**: our trunk is smaller than theirs; 20-frame snippets at 12 Hz camera would be 1.7 s, matching the +-1.5 s dt range of plan 8.2.

**Doubts**: a planning benchmark, not BEV segmentation or detection; the snippet length and the batch sizes come from different places in the paper (main text 128, appendix 16 on one GPU); no detach statement; the lr is unknown.
