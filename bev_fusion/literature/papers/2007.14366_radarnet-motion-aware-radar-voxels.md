**What it does**: Lidar + radar detector. Radar targets are mid-level clusters (position, radial velocity, moving flag, timestamp); only 2D BEV position is kept. Early fusion rasterises each radar cycle into a BEV image with a three-valued motion-aware occupancy (0 empty, 1 any moving target, -1 all static) stacked over cycles and concatenated with the lidar voxels. Late fusion keeps the detection's direction and takes its speed from radar: the radial velocity is back-projected onto the detected motion direction, v_bp = v_r / cos(phi) (capped at 50 m/s), and a 5-layer MLP scores each detection-radar pair (dx, dy, dt, v_bp, box size, speed, direction) against a "no association" score of 1 under a softmax; trained without association labels.

**Results that matter** (nuScenes val, AP@2 m / AVE): cars lidar only 87.6 / 0.203; early fusion +0.3 AP, -2 % AVE; heuristic late fusion -9 % AVE; attention late fusion -14 % AVE; motorcycles -15 % AVE. On the authors' DenseRadar set the attention late fusion cuts ADVE by 19 %, AP gains grow with range (+0.3 / +0.5 / +0.8 AP at 0-40 / 40-70 / 70-100 m for early fusion). Ablation of single attributes is not given.

**Transferable technique**: the Doppler is used where it is geometrically meaningful (radial component, projected onto the expected direction of motion), not as a raw vx/vy image; a "no association" slot absorbs clutter and ghosts. The three-valued moving/static occupancy is a cheap channel that keeps the Doppler sign information of the static background (static returns are the lane-side structure we may want). The page names range/velocity aliasing and multipath but has no dedicated filter.

**Cost and fit**: the early part is a rasterisation (free). The late fusion is object-level velocity refinement and does not apply to lanes. Needs lidar for the published numbers.

**Doubts**: radar ego-motion compensation is not described on the page; the radar here is a different (denser) sensor in part (DenseRadar), so gains on nuScenes' five sparse radars are small (+0.3 AP).
