**What it does**: inverse perspective mapping (IPM) warps each camera image to the road plane with one homography from intrinsics, extrinsics and a planar road model. Their network (uNetXST, about 9.6 M parameters) applies these fixed homographies to encoder feature maps of each camera stream (spatial-transformer style) and fuses them; inputs 512x256, BEV ground truth about 70 m x 44 m.

**Along-ray distribution**: none; every pixel is assumed to lie on the ground (a delta distribution at the ground-plane intersection).

**Precompute versus runtime**: homographies precomputed; runtime is a warp (grid-sample). No timing in the paper.

**Flat-ground error**: the paper states IPM assumes a flat world, so vehicles and elevation changes appear distorted. Result on its synthetic validation set: raw IPM homography image mIoU 30.17 versus uNetXST 71.92 (the network learns the correction); without the IPM prior, uNetX* reaches only 45.95.
Derived (not from the page): a point at height z on a ray from a camera at height h is placed at distance d*h/(h - z) instead of d, so a 1 m high feature seen from a 1.5 m camera lands 3x too far; to first order a road pitch change alpha shifts the ground hit by d^2 * alpha / h, i.e. about 4 m at 20 m and 18 m at 50 m for 1 degree (h = 1.5 m).

**Results that matter**: see above; single-front-camera dataset (about 32 000 train frames, three classes) exists in the paper; training data synthetic (VTD simulator).

**Transferable technique**: use IPM as an input prior so the network only learns the residual (this is the ECM idea of PanopticBEV, 2108.03227); the warp is a fixed grid-sample.

**Doubts**: synthetic data; classes differ from ours; no timing.
