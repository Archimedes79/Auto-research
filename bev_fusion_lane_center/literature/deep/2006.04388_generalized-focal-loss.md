## 1. Problem and claim
Dense detectors (FCOS, ATSS) train a classification score and a separate quality score (IoU or centerness), and multiply them at test time, for ranking in NMS. Two defects: the two scores are trained apart but used together, and the quality score is supervised on positives only, so a negative can get a high quality score and outrank a true positive (Fig. 2 of the paper, digest). The paper merges both into one score: the classification output of the ground-truth class is trained towards a continuous target, the IoU of the predicted box with its ground-truth box, and towards 0 for negatives. A new loss, Quality Focal Loss (QFL), handles the continuous target. A second, independent part (Distribution Focal Loss, DFL) predicts each box edge as a discrete distribution. The claim that matters for us is the first: one score trained with a continuous target and a focal modulating factor, no extra branch, no extra inference cost, +0.7 to +1.9 AP in the paper's ablations (section 3).

## 2. Method as built

### 2.1 Pipeline
```
 image -> backbone + FPN -> shared head (convs) on every pyramid level
            cls branch : C sigmoid maps  (QFL; entry of the gt class = IoU(pred box, gt box), others 0)
            box branch : 4 x (n+1) logits, softmax per edge, expectation = distance  (DFL + GIoU)
 inference: score = sigmoid(cls)   -> NMS directly (no centerness / IoU multiplication)
```
Detector: ATSS (assigner and anchor setup cited, not described in the text read), also FCOS, FoveaBox, RetinaNet, SSD512 for Table 1(b). Input and output: COCO images, shorter side 800, longer at most 1333; boxes with scores (digest).

### 2.2 Equations (numbers as in the paper)
- E1 (Eq. 1) focal loss: `FL(p) = -(1 - p_t)^gamma * log(p_t)`; the alpha_t balancing term is dropped "for simplicity".
- E2 (Eq. 2) **Quality Focal Loss**: `QFL(s) = -| y - s |^beta * ( (1 - y) log(1 - s) + y log s )`, s = sigmoid output (per class), y in [0,1] the continuous target, beta >= 0. Global minimum at s = y (Appendix B / C). Special cases (my derivation from Eq. 2): y in {0,1} gives focal loss with gamma = beta and no alpha; beta = 0 gives plain BCE with a soft label; at y = 0 the factor is s^beta, at y = 1 it is (1-s)^beta.
- **Target y for the joint representation:** y = IoU between the predicted box and its matched ground-truth box, computed during training, for the entry of the ground-truth class at a positive location; y = 0 at negatives and for the other classes. The page text does not say whether the IoU is detached; the mmdetection head computes it on a detached prediction (section 4).
- E3 (Eq. 4-6) **Distribution Focal Loss**: each of four edge offsets is a distribution over n+1 bins with spacing Delta; softmax gives P, the edge value is the expectation sum P(y_i) y_i. `DFL(S_i, S_{i+1}) = -( (y_{i+1} - y) log S_i + (y - y_i) log S_{i+1} )`, with y_i <= y <= y_{i+1} the two bins around the continuous target; it is the cross-entropy part only, positives only, minimum at the linear-interpolation weights.
- E4 (Eq. 7) **GFL**: `GFL(p_l, p_r) = -| y - (y_l p_l + y_r p_r) |^beta * ( (y_r - y) log p_l + (y - y_l) log p_r )`; Appendix C: FL (Eq. 9), QFL (Eq. 10), DFL (Eq. 11) are special cases.
- E5 (Eq. 8) total loss: `L = (1/N_pos) sum_z L_Q + (1/N_pos) sum_z 1{c*_z > 0} ( lambda0 L_B + lambda1 L_D )`, L_B = GIoU loss, lambda0 = 2, lambda1 = 1/4 (averaged over the four edges); sums run over all locations of all pyramid levels, QFL on all of them, box terms on positives. Text of Section 3: the box terms are additionally weighted by the quality score, "following common practice in the official codes" (not visible in Eq. 8).

### 2.3 Losses, labels, training recipe (digest)
- QFL weight and beta: beta = 2 best (Table 1(c)); n = 16 bins, Delta = 1 default (Table 2(b), (c)).
- Normaliser: N_pos, the number of positive samples (Eq. 8).
- Ablation schedule 1x: 90 K iterations (about 12 epochs), no multi-scale, SGD momentum 0.9, weight decay 1e-4, batch 16, lr 0.01 divided by 10 at 60 K and 80 K iterations, ResNet-50 + FPN, COCO trainval35k (115 K images) for training, minival (5 K) for ablations, test-dev (20 K) for the main table. Main result schedule 2x (24 epochs) with multi-scale training. Hardware for FPS: one GeForce RTX 2080Ti, batch 1.
- Inference: score threshold 0.05, top 1000 candidates per level, per-class NMS at IoU 0.6, 100 detections per image.

### 3.5 Cost
Extra regression outputs (n+1 per edge) and the dropped quality branch leave FPS unchanged in Table 3 (19.4); the paper calls the cost negligible. QFL itself costs one power and one BCE per output element in training and nothing at inference.

### 3.6 Caveats
Single runs, no seeds or intervals in the digest; gains below about 0.3 AP (QFL over the IoU branch) cannot be separated from noise on this evidence. No gradient analysis, no stability discussion, no calibration measurement (no ECE, no reliability diagram): the paper shows a score/IoU scatter plot (Fig. 2(b)) only. No limitations section.

## 4. Paper versus code
From the mmdetection pages (digest; the original repository `implus/GFocal` returned 404 on three URLs):
- The quality target is the IoU of the decoded predicted box and the ground-truth box, computed on a **detached** prediction, with `is_aligned` pairwise IoU; background anchors get label = background and quality 0.
- QFL in code: start from a loss with target 0 for every class (BCE times sigmoid^beta), then at each positive overwrite the entry of the labelled class by BCE against the quality score times |quality - sigmoid|^beta; sum over classes; beta default 2.0. Reduction and `avg_factor` are handled by a wrapper not shown on the page.
- Normalisation differs from Eq. 8: the classification loss uses `avg_factor` = number of positive anchors (summed over images, mean-reduced across GPUs); the box and DFL terms are divided by a second normaliser, the sum of `weight_targets`, which is the maximum detached class sigmoid at each positive (clamped to at least 1). So the box terms are weighted by the predicted score; this is the "quality weighting" of the text.
- DFL weight 0.25, `reg_max` = 16 (17 bins), a fixed non-learned integral layer (softmax then expectation). No positives: losses are set to zero but kept in the graph.
- Not seen: whether the modulating factor |y - s|^beta is detached in code (Eq. 2 differentiated as written includes the factor; the page summary does not say), the GIoU loss weight in the config.

### 5.1 What we learn from the paper and from our own loss (my derivation, checked against the code above)
1. **Our loss is not minimised at p = y.** For a fixed target y (or for a binary label with probability q given the features) the expected loss of `loss_fn` with gamma = 2 and lam = wpos = 1 has the same form as the expected QFL for binary labels, `q (1-p)^2 (-log p) + (1-q) p^2 (-log(1-p))`. Its derivative at p = q is `2 q (1-q) log(q/(1-q)) + (2q - 1)`. At q = 0.25 this is -0.912 (my arithmetic), so the loss still decreases at p = 0.25 and the minimiser lies above 0.25; at q = 0.75 the sign flips. The minimiser is pulled towards 0.5. Effect: the heat value at a given distance from the line is not the Gaussian quality, and `lam` / `wpos` shift it further. The map used as "score" is therefore ranked correctly at best, not calibrated.
2. **QFL differs from our loss in one point:** full BCE with the soft label times |y - p|^beta, so the minimum is p = y exactly and the loss is zero there when the target is deterministic (factor zero). That is a toy test with a known answer (section 6).
3. **Caveat for calibration at range:** the "sigma = y" property needs y to be a deterministic function of the input. At range the features often do not determine y; the minimiser of the modulated loss is then not E[y | features]. Soft BCE (beta = 0) is a proper scoring rule and minimises at E[y | features]; beta = 2 gives the ranking gain the paper measures (Table 1(c): beta = 0 is 2.3 AP worse than beta = 2) but is not proper (my derivation; the known better calibration of focal loss in classifiers is **memory**, Mukhoti et al. 2020, not read). So calibration and ranking pull in different directions: sweep beta in {0, 1, 2} instead of fixing 2.
4. **Recall by loss weights vs. by threshold:** `lam` / `wpos` and the x10 positives move the minimiser, which buys lanes found and costs Q (PROBLEM.md: recall variants buy lanes found but not Q). With a loss whose output is calibrated, recall would be bought by lowering the verification threshold on a score that means something. This is a hypothesis (section 5.3), not a result.
5. **Normalisation:** the paper divides by the number of positives; with a Gaussian target there is no integer number of positives. Use the target mass `sum(y)` clamped to at least 1 (the mmdetection head normalises its box terms by the summed positive weights, section 4). Our mean over 40,000 cells times 100 is a fixed constant per batch; QFL summed over cells divided by sum(y) changes the effective weight of sparse frames. Gradient scale must be re-tuned (lr or loss multiplier) when swapping.
6. **Gradient at y = 0 and at s = y:** at y = 0 the loss is `s^beta * (-log(1-s))`; for beta = 2 the gradient vanishes smoothly for confident negatives. At s = y the factor |y - s|^beta is zero; for beta = 2 it is smooth ((y-s)^2); for beta = 1 the absolute value has a kink at s = y, and for beta < 1 the gradient diverges there. Use beta >= 2 or the squared form (my derivation; the paper has no gradient analysis).

### 5.2 Design sketch: continuous target for the lane-centre heat map
- **Target:** `y(x) = g(d(x)) * h(dtheta(x))`. d(x) = distance of cell x to the nearest ground-truth centre line (metres). g = the existing Gaussian `exp(-d^2 / (2 * 0.75^2))` (no change; this is already "distance-based quality"). dtheta(x) = angle between the predicted direction at x and the ground-truth tangent of the nearest line (lane direction, oriented). h in [0,1], e.g. `max(cos dtheta, 0)^k` with k = 1 (a choice, to be swept); the predicted direction is **detached** (as the IoU is in mmdetection) so the heat head is trained to predict "how good a seed is here, given what the direction head says", without a gradient into the direction head. Variant A (static): h = 1, only the loss form changes. Variant B (dynamic, direction-aware): as above, available only where the direction head exists (`with_dir`, `dir_loss`).
- **What it does not encode:** "whether the lane is really there" at range. If the annotation has a lane there, y is high whatever the sensors see, and the best the head can do is E[y | features]; that is the honest calibrated score. An existence term would need a visibility or coverage factor (for example from radar / camera coverage), not available in the paper and not designed here.
- **Loss:** `L = sum_x |y - p|^beta * BCE(p, y) / max(sum_x y, 1)`, p = sigmoid(logit), beta swept in {0, 1, 2}. No `lam`, no `wpos` (the recall trade-off moves to the threshold). Same network, same inference; only `loss_fn` and the target change.
- **ECE of the vertex score (needs a definition, not in the paper):** (a) ECE_q: bin the vertex scores in 10 to 15 bins; compare the bin mean of the score with the bin mean of the realised quality `y` of the same vertex against ground truth; (b) ECE_hit: compare with the fraction of vertices within a tolerance (for example 1 m, the metric's own matching tolerance in `evaluation/sparse_lanes`, to be read) of a true centre line. QFL targets (a), not (b); report both, and at range (> 40 m) separately. Strong simple baseline for any ECE claim: temperature scaling (or isotonic regression) of the **current** score on a split of VAL, because that gives calibration without a new loss.
- **Prediction (to be put on the card, numbers are my guesses before the baseline ECE is measured):** Q, topo F1 and lanes found within the noise band of the control (tie) for variant A with beta = 2; ECE_q at least 30 % lower in relative terms than the control for beta = 0 or 1, less so for beta = 2 (section 5.1, point 3); with the threshold lowered on the calibrated score, lanes found and R@1 far above the control at equal or better Q. The last part is the hypothesis that the paper does not test; expect it to fail if the lanes missed at range are missed because the features are empty, not because the score is mis-scaled.
- **Decision rule skeleton (written before the run, per CLAUDE.md):** layer 1 first, reproduce the stored heat-head score with the unchanged `loss_fn` and measure the control's ECE; then arms C0 current loss, A1 soft BCE (beta = 0, normalised by sum(y)), A2 QFL beta = 1, A3 QFL beta = 2, A4 = best of A1-A3 with the direction-aware target, C1 = C0 plus temperature scaling (strong simple baseline); 2 seeds each, VAL250 proxy then VAL with the paired scene bootstrap. KEEP if ECE_q falls by at least 30 % relative to C0 and is not worse than C1 on VAL, and Q and topo F1 stay within the 0.010 band (Q not lower by more than 0.005); a recall claim needs lanes found / R@1 far up with a bootstrap interval excluding 0 at Q within the band. TIE: take the variant that keeps the old loss (cheaper, nothing to maintain). INCONCLUSIVE: add a seed. Time: no extra inference cost; measure ms per frame only if the builder changes.

## Novelty and relevance for our problem
The novelty is the observation that a classification score and a localisation-quality score can be one output trained against a continuous label, together with a focal-type loss (QFL) whose minimum is the label; the box-edge distribution (DFL) is a separate, smaller contribution. The idea is incremental (sigmoid focal loss with a soft target and a different modulating factor) and its gain over a separate IoU branch is small (0.3 AP). For us the pixel-level form is almost already in place (soft Gaussian target, focal BCE, joint map), so the relevance is narrower than the `papers.csv` statement suggests: it exposes that our loss is not minimised at the target value, and it gives a clean replacement without the hand-set `lam` / `wpos`, a candidate for calibrated vertex scores (A8, F2). The paper itself never measures calibration and QFL with beta = 2 is not a proper scoring rule, so the calibration claim must be tested against temperature scaling, not assumed.
