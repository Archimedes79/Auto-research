**What it does** [checked abstract]: the particle filter (predict with a learnable motion model, update with a learnable measurement model, resample) written as a differentiable computation, trained for end-to-end state estimation instead of proxy losses for the sub-models; the recursion is an "algorithmic prior". Experiments (simulated and real, KITTI visual odometry): error reduced by about 80 % and better generalisation across robot policies than LSTMs. [memory] resampling is not differentiable in the weights path; the paper keeps the gradient through the particle states and uses a soft path for the weights - not verified.

**Transferable technique**: a justification for the architecture choice: a structured predict-and-update with learned models beats an unstructured recurrent net (their LSTM comparison); our TemporalPropagation (learned f_m, learned score, Doppler evidence in the update) is a predict step with a learned motion model; what the DPF adds is a learned measurement model that re-weights the particles each frame (our version: the fusion conv/GRU after the splat).

**Cost and fit**: single-object localisation with a few hundred global particles, not a grid with a velocity per cell.

**Doubts**: the abstract-level reading only; the resampling gradient and the cost for a dense grid are unknown here.
