**(c) Implications for our sparsification** (items marked "inference" are my reading, not a published result)

1. Make the vertex count a differentiable expected quantity with a target: closed-form expected L0 (sum of keep probabilities), expected kept edges, and a Lagrangian pull to a prescribed density (here the oracle's 14.3 per 100 m versus 22 now). The count term needs the task loss to balance it; PTDNet tunes two weights per dataset, CoFi uses a constraint instead of one weight. [SP01, SP02, SP03]
2. A step length from a dense verification profile can be supervised by a simplification oracle: PivotNet trains a pivot classifier on Visvalingam-Whyatt pivots, and MachMap reports > 93% fewer points with AP vs the original still 97-100% at 0.2-0.5 m. The DP (0.3 m) oracle is therefore an accepted kind of target. [SP20, SP22]
3. Check the oracle before judging 22 vs 14.3: DP does not minimise vertices; the Imai-Iri valid-link graph plus a shortest path does (under its own link test). Validity of chord (i,j) does not imply validity of (i,k) for k < j (inference), so greedy 'longest chord' can lose to the shortest path in the DAG of valid chords. Count the II vertices on the training lanes; use its path edges as DAG-merge targets. [SP26]
4. 'n endpoints + learned validity' exists in RNGDet (10 vertex queries per step, validity BCE, bipartite matching on distance, branch when several are valid); Co-DETR-type training gives many positives per GT and drops the extra branch at test. Train with every endpoint inside the tolerance as a positive and run a small n at test (inference). [SP24, SP13, SP14, SP15]
5. Duplicate control between hypotheses has three published forms: learned geometry-only re-scoring with one-to-one labels (GossipNet), score decay (Soft-NMS), or exact zeros in the normaliser (sparsemax/entmax; 1.5-entmax cut attended tokens from 24.25 to 5.55 on average). For endpoints on one ray the natural distance is the along-ray length (inference). [SP12, SP11, SP05, SP06]
6. Where selection works in practice it is hard and the scorer is trained by auxiliary supervision, not by gradient through the selection: BCE to an attention target (Sparse DETR), GT-box focal labels (Focus-DETR), BCE on kept voxels (SparseOcc), validity BCE (RNGDet), per-edge sigmoid (SAM-Road). A keep gate plus the expected-count penalty of item 1 fits this pattern. [SP16, SP17, SP19, SP24, SP27]
7. Over-complete first, prune late: SparseOcc cannot recover pruned voxels, Focus-DETR lowers the keep ratio layer by layer (0.5 down to 0.1). For ray hypotheses keep a generous n in training and at the first steps, then cut by validity and budget (inference). [SP19, SP17]
8. The size of the kept set can come from data statistics (SparseOcc: k from the largest occupied count per scale), from a predicted count (DQ-DETR), or from a class head (BeMapNet piece count). Oracle vertices per lane or per BEV area is the analogous statistic. [SP19, SP18, SP21]
9. Edge keep gate for the DAG merge: candidates by a fixed geometric rule (SAM-Road: 16 nearest within 64 px), labels by a graph-distance rule (BFS reaches the target without passing another target), score by a per-edge sigmoid, optionally an L0 gate with an expected edge count (PTDNet). The rule baseline is RoadTracer's merge (within 3D of a vertex, graph distance >= 6D). [SP27, SP03, SP23]
10. Wiring a sparse vertex set differentiably is done only in training: Sinkhorn there, exact assignment at inference, N fixed at 256 peaks (PolyWorld). Gumbel-Sinkhorn itself is in lit_extra15. [SP29]
11. Evaluate on a budget axis; the lane and road metrics opened ignore vertex count (OpenLane-V2 DET_l/TOP_ll, TOPO, APLS). Report (a) F1 versus vertices per 100 m by sweeping the keep threshold, (b) N ratio against the oracle and a C-IoU-style factor 1 - abs(N_A - N_B)/(N_A + N_B) on the lane-graph F1, (c) fork/merge accuracy with RoadTracer's junction metric (F_correct, F_error) or TOPO precision/recall, (d) DET_l/TOP_ll for comparability. [SP30, SP23, SP27, SP29]
12. Cost: hard sparsification pays only if the dense part dominates (Sparse DETR -38% computation, Focus-DETR ~-45% GFLOPs, SparseOcc 24.0 vs 6.3 FPS at equal RayIoU). Sample-by-sample checks along long rays are dense work too (rays x samples), so budget them against these numbers (inference). [SP16, SP17, SP19]

**(d) What no paper does that our design does** (among the pages opened here; absence is limited to this search)

- Step length = longest chord that stays within a tolerance of the lane, found by checking samples along a long ray: RoadTracer uses a fixed 12 m step (SP23), RNGDet regresses the next vertex without a tolerance rule (SP24), VecRoad's flexible step is unread (SP25), PivotNet/BeMapNet/MachMap fix the point count on the label side or with a class head (SP20-SP22). The closest items are the simplification oracles used to make labels (SP20, SP22, SP26).
- A vertex budget trained as an expected count in a lane or road graph: L0 and expected-count penalties appear for weights, heads, tokens and graph edges (SP01-SP04), but I found none applied to vertices or queries of a map or lane-graph model.
- One pipeline of n endpoint hypotheses per ray, learned validity, and a tree -> DAG merge with a learned edge keep: RNGDet has hypotheses and validity but no merge in the text (SP24), RoadTracer a rule merge without hypotheses (SP23), SAM-Road candidate edges and scores but vertices from NMS peaks and no step logic (SP27).
- F1 at an equal vertex budget for lane graphs: the lane and road metrics opened ignore vertex count (SP30, SP23, SP27); only the polygon work scores count (C-IoU, N ratio, SP29).
- Learned polyline simplification: the search returned only geometry papers (SP26); this is 'not found here', not 'does not exist'.
