**What it does** [checked]: replaces the schedule by a combination of iterate averaging and interpolation that follows from a theory unifying scheduling and averaging; no extra hyperparameter beyond those of SGD with momentum or Adam; "eschews schedules entirely". The abstract says schedules that do not need T are greatly outperformed by those that depend on T, and this method closes that gap. Schedule-Free AdamW was the core of a winning entry in the MLCommons 2024 AlgoPerf self-tuning track (abstract).

**Results that matter** [digest]: 28 problems from logistic regression to large deep nets, matches or outperforms heavily tuned cosine schedules (authors' evaluation, their cosine baselines); GPT-2 124M on OpenWebText: both Schedule-Free SGD and AdamW match or exceed cosine schedules of varying length. No BEV or detection result in the paper.

**Transferable technique**: our run length is not fixed: the loop restarts, the dt warm-up (20 % + 20 % ramp, plan 8.4) and the modality-dropout start depend on the total step count, and a cosine schedule would have to be re-planned at every extension. With Schedule-Free AdamW the weights used for evaluation are the averaged iterate (the optimiser keeps two sets; code: `optimizer.eval()` / `optimizer.train()` calls around evaluation and checkpointing, from memory of the README, not a page read), and a warm-up is still advised (from memory). BevAD trains with it (checked in 2603.15185).

**Cost and fit**: one extra copy of the parameters in optimiser state (small for our trainable parts); BatchNorm statistics must be handled with the averaged weights (from memory: one pass in train mode before evaluation), which interacts with our BN pitfalls (running_var derailment in the lane project).

**Doubts**: no vision-dense or BEV benchmark in the paper; authors' own baselines; our existing rules (clip, roll-back guard, lr <= 6e-4) were developed with AdamW + cosine/constant, a switch needs an equal-budget arm against it.
