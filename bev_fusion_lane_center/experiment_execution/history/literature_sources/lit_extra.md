### 3.10 Fine-tuning and weight-space methods (read for loop 6; abstracts checked 2026-10-15)

Takeaway: for a head that has to adapt to shifted inputs the literature offers a small set of cheap, well-studied levers - which layers to tune, how far to move from the starting weights, how to start a new output head, and how to combine weights afterwards. Loop 6 (E73) turns each of them into one controlled factor.

| work (venue, year) | core idea | reported result (author-reported) | how we used it (experiment ids E..) |
|---|---|---|---|
| LP-FT, Kumar et al. (ICLR 2022; arXiv 2202.10054) | Full fine-tuning can distort good pretrained features under a large distribution shift; fit a linear probe first, then fine-tune all layers | on 10 shift datasets LP-FT is about 1 % better in-distribution and 10 % better out-of-distribution than full fine-tuning (which loses about 7 points OOD against a linear probe) | E73 / E76: a map-trust head fitted on the frozen, finished network learns nothing (AUROC 0.50); trained jointly with the features it works and helps the main outputs |

### 3.11 SD-map priors, temporal memory and planner interfaces for online lane / map perception (read for loop 7; arXiv abstract pages checked 2026-10-15, numbers marked (html) from the arXiv HTML full text; datasets differ between papers)

Takeaway: a navigation (SD) map is a strong prior mainly at long range and for topology, but a shifted prior can cost most of the gain (TopoSD, Uni-PrevPredMap); the literature handles this with noise-augmented priors, prior dropout (the same network must work without the prior) and confidence gating, and it fuses time by warping the previous result with the ego pose. We found no paper on camera + radar lane graphs with temporal fusion and none that models ADASIS-like map content, and of the three planners we read (VAD, SparseDrive, DiffusionDrive) none consumes an explicit lane graph: the interface is confidence-filtered polylines or query features. Loop 7 (E77-E80) tests these ideas on the frozen camera-radar features.

### 3.12 What an ADASIS / navigation map contains and how good it is (read for loop 7; ADASIS v2.0.1.0 specification read in full, v3 from vendor documents)

Takeaway: an ADASIS v2 horizon is a one-dimensional path (offsets of 1 m, curvature and heading-change profiles, a coarse lane count, branch stubs with probabilities) - no lane geometry, no lane connectivity, no lateral position; v3 adds a lane model, but its field-level content is member-only or delayed and was not verified. Navigation-map accuracy is 'several meters' (vendor whitepaper; OpenStreetMap about 6 m mean displacement, consumer GNSS about 5 m), lane counts are not correct in more than about 80 % in the cities of one study (secondary source). We found no paper that simulates ADASIS-like content for learning; the SD-prior papers perturb with shifts of 0.25-5 m, rotations of 2-10 deg and 10-30 % dropout (TopoSD 5 m / 5 deg, Zhang et al. 0.25-2 m / 0-10 deg, SDTagNet 1 m / 2 deg, NavMapFusion 0-3 m). E77 builds the simulator from these facts.

| source (type) | content | value used in E77 |
|---|---|---|
| ADASIS v2.0.1.0 specification (read in full) | path / offset (1 m), curvature and heading profiles, lane counts in and against driving direction (0..6+, 0..2+), branch stubs with 5-bit probabilities, no lane geometry | one centre line per carriageway, lane-count channel, sparse shape points, route = most probable path; no lane geometry |
| TomTom / Elektrobit whitepaper; Haklay 2010; Cyprus OSM study; gps.gov | 'several meters'; OSM mean displacement about 6 m, 70 % within 6 m; consumer GNSS 4.9 m | map pose error sigma 1 / 2 / 3 m per axis (yaw 2 sigma deg) as the evaluation levels; 'ADASIS-like' lies between 2 and 3 m |
| Lyft study of OSM lane counts (secondary source) | lane counts do not exceed 80 % correct in the cities of one study | lane-count error probability 0.1 per road (own judgement; the study corresponds to 0.2-0.25, used in the poor variant) |
| SD-prior papers (TopoSD 5 m / 5 deg; Zhang et al., arXiv 2408.01471, 0.25-2 m / 0-10 deg; SDTagNet 1 m / 2 deg; NavMapFusion 0-3 m; Online Navigation Refinement dropout 10-30 %) | shifts up to 5 m, rotations 2-10 deg, dropout 10-30 % | training: map error 0.3-2 m (30 % exact), up to 40 % of the side roads dropped in half of the samples, wrong map 10 %, a poor-quality variant (shape tolerance 1-4 m, 25 % lane-count errors); evaluation: map error 1 / 2 / 3 m and an ADASIS-like cell (2.5 m, 5 % of the side roads dropped) |
